# Main Income Ideas - 2026-11-09 (Session 176)

## Who this is for
A 29-year-old software engineer with data analytics skills who wants to replace a full-time salary ($8-15K+/mo) within 12-24 months by building solo, in evenings and weekends, with no outside funding. Every idea below must have a realistic path to first revenue inside 90 days and a named, reachable distribution channel.

## Lens for this session: Alex Hormozi
Each idea is built from Hormozi's frameworks ($100M Offers / $100M Leads):
- **Value equation:** Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood of Success) / (Time Delay x Effort & Sacrifice). Each offer raises the top or shrinks the bottom.
- **Grand Slam Offer:** an offer so strong the buyer feels foolish saying no. Here that means performance-priced or guarantee-backed offers.
- **Core Four / speed-to-lead / LTV:CAC:** the data and automation gaps that small operators leave open.

Per standing feedback, none of these are compliance or regulatory tools. Each solves a revenue-leakage or growth problem.

## Ideas

### 1. Declined-Work Recovery Engine for Independent Auto Repair Shops

**Problem:** At an independent shop, a service writer recommends brakes, a belt or a fluid service, and the customer says "not today." That work is lost permanently, and nobody follows up. A typical shop declines $3-8K of recommended work per week, and the shop's own records hold it all.

**What to build:** Connect to shop-management exports (Tekmetric, Shopmonkey, Shop-Ware, CSV). Detect declined line items and run a timed text/email sequence tied to each item's urgency: a brake-pad wear photo, a "your inspection is still valid for 30 days" nudge, a financing link. Report recovered revenue per month.

**Grand Slam angle:** Sell it as "we only get paid on work we recover" with 15% of recovered declined-work revenue. This removes the shop's risk (higher perceived likelihood) and needs no effort from the shop (lower effort).

**Skill fit:** Data pipeline, messaging automation and attribution analytics.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends: CSV/API ingest, rules for declined items, SMS via Twilio, recovered-revenue dashboard.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days. Pilot free for two shops to prove recovery numbers, then convert.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** A shop recovers roughly $2,500/mo on average, so 15% is about $375/mo. 30 shops yields $11,250/mo. Add a $99/mo platform floor and 30 shops reach about $14K/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Performance fees scale with shop volume, and the case study ("recovered $31K in 90 days") is a repeatable sales asset.

**Biggest risk:** Attribution disputes and messy shop data. Mitigate with a conservative attribution rule: a recovered item counts only if it is invoiced within 45 days of the sequence's first message.

**Growth path:** Pilot shops, then 10 shops (about $4K/mo), then 30 shops. Next add tire/alignment cross-sell and then collision shops.

### 2. Speed-to-Lead Response System for Residential Solar & Roofing Installers

**Problem:** Contacting a web lead within 5 minutes converts many times better than waiting an hour, yet most small installers call back the next day. Leads bought from aggregators are expensive ($40-150 each) and go cold fast.

**What to build:** A lead-intake webhook (forms, aggregator emails, Facebook Lead Ads) that texts the lead within 60 seconds, qualifies by SMS (roof age, bill size, ownership) and books an appointment on the rep's calendar. It alerts the rep with a one-line summary and tracks speed-to-lead per rep.

**Grand Slam angle:** Offer "more booked appointments from the leads you already paid for, or the next month is free." The guarantee is measured against the baseline booking rate.

**Skill fit:** Webhooks, SMS flows, calendar APIs, conversion analytics.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends. Twilio, a calendar integration and a simple rep dashboard.

**Time to first $:** 30-60 days through Facebook installer groups and direct outreach to owner-operators.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $399/mo base + $2 per qualified appointment booked. An installer booking 80 appointments/mo pays about $559/mo. 22 installers x $559 = **$12,300/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every lead source the installer adds increases usage, and the per-appointment fee grows with their marketing spend.

**Biggest risk:** Lead-source integrations vary and SMS carrier registration (10DLC) takes time. Start with form and email-parse intake and complete carrier registration in week one.

**Growth path:** Solar and roofing, then windows/siding/HVAC replacement, then white-label for lead-gen agencies.

### 3. Maintenance-Plan Conversion Engine for Independent HVAC & Plumbing Companies

**Problem:** Recurring maintenance memberships are the most valuable asset a home-service company can own (Hormozi's "continuity offer"), but most techs never pitch them. A company with 3,000 past customers and 4% on a plan is leaving six figures of recurring revenue unclaimed.

**What to build:** Mine the job history for customers who qualify (system age, repeat repairs, no plan). Generate a personalized plan offer ("your 14-year-old furnace has been repaired twice") by text/email/mailer, and give techs an at-door script and tablet prompt. Track plan attach rate by tech and by month.

**Grand Slam angle:** The offer to the contractor is a done-for-you plan launch with a per-plan bounty of $25 per plan sold in the first 60 days.

**Skill fit:** Customer segmentation, templated messaging and cohort analytics.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends: ServiceTitan/Housecall Pro CSV import, segmentation, message generator, attach-rate dashboard.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249/mo software + $25 per plan sold. A company selling 40 plans/mo pays $1,249/mo at launch. A steadier 15 plans/mo is $624/mo. 18 companies x about $625 = **$11,250/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every plan sold is recurring revenue for the client, which makes switching off the tool unattractive.

**Biggest risk:** Owners resist the mailer and phone work. Mitigate by focusing on text/email and by pre-writing tech scripts.

**Growth path:** Plan launches, then renewal-rate tracking, then add-on offers (water heaters, air quality).

### 4. LTV:CAC Payback Dashboard for Local Service Businesses

**Problem:** Local operators (med spas, dentists, gyms, landscapers) spend on ads without knowing how long it takes a new customer to repay acquisition cost. Hormozi's rule is to recover CAC within 30 days so growth funds itself. Most owners cannot say what a customer is worth after 12 months.

**What to build:** Connect ad spend (Meta/Google), the booking/POS system and payments (Stripe/Square). Compute CAC per channel, 30/90/365-day gross-profit LTV per cohort, and payback period. Flag channels that fail the "pay back in 30 days" test and recommend where to shift budget.

**Grand Slam angle:** Price as a flat monthly fee, with a "find $2K/mo in wasted ad spend in 30 days or you don't pay" guarantee.

**Skill fit:** Direct data analytics: ETL, cohort analysis and dashboards.

**MVP scope:** 3-4 weekends: Meta Ads and Stripe/Square connectors, cohort table, payback chart.

**Time to first $:** 45-75 days. Run free teardown audits on 10 local businesses as the sales motion.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299/mo. 40 clients = **$11,960/mo**. Add a $1,500 one-time setup for 10 of them for another $15K up front.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** It is the analytics layer for ad agencies serving local businesses. An agency reseller license at $99 per client/mo is the scale path.

**Biggest risk:** Connector maintenance across many POS systems. Start with Stripe, Square and Google Sheets only.

**Growth path:** Audits, then 15 clients ($4.5K/mo), then 40 clients, then agency reseller tier.

### 5. Quote Follow-Up & Win-Rate Engine for Remodelers and Custom Fabricators

**Problem:** A remodeler sends a $40K quote and then follows up once, or never. Win rate on sent quotes is often 20-30%, and a few extra well-timed touches lift it meaningfully. Owners cannot tell which quote sizes, project types or sources actually close.

**What to build:** Ingest quotes from QuickBooks, Jobber or Buildertrend exports. Run a 5-touch sequence (day 2 recap, day 5 "common questions", day 10 case study, day 17 deadline on material pricing, day 30 close-the-file). Provide a win/loss view by project type, quote size and lead source, plus a one-tap "mark lost, reason" field.

**Grand Slam angle:** "If the engine doesn't lift your quote win rate within 90 days, you pay nothing." Baseline win rate is measured from the previous 6 months.

**Skill fit:** Messaging automation, funnel analytics and cohort comparisons.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days via remodeler Facebook groups and local builders' associations.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo + 1% of newly won quote value above baseline, capped at $500/mo per client. A client at about $450/mo x 25 clients = **$11,250/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Win-rate data per project type is useful to the owner for pricing, and the loss-reason data becomes a benchmark product.

**Biggest risk:** Owners keep quotes in many places. Start with email-sent quotes and a single forwarding address.

**Growth path:** Remodelers, then custom cabinetry, fencing, landscape design and signage.
