### 1. Medical Spa & Aesthetic Medicine Clinic Physician-Delegation, Supervision-Tier & Chart-Review Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Medical spas offering injectables, laser hair removal, and other cosmetic procedures performed by non-physician staff — registered nurses, physician assistants, nurse practitioners, and in some states licensed aestheticians — operate under state medical board rules requiring a supervising or "medical director" physician to hold a written delegation agreement or standing order specific to each procedure category, specifying exactly which credential level may perform which procedure and under what supervision tier: on-site supervision (the physician must be physically present), immediate-availability supervision (the physician must be reachable and able to respond quickly), or general supervision (a looser standard some states allow only for lower-risk procedures) — a spa that lets an injector perform neuromodulator treatments under general supervision in a state that requires immediate availability for that specific procedure is exactly the finding a state medical board complaint investigation is built to catch. Many states additionally require the medical director to conduct and document periodic in-person chart reviews and spot-check site visits — not a one-time contract signature — and a medical director who signs a delegation agreement and is never seen at the spa again, with no documented chart-review visits on file, exposes both the spa and the physician's own license to a medical board finding of inadequate supervision, a fact pattern regulators have pursued aggressively as medical spas have proliferated. A spa running several injectors across multiple procedure categories and a part-time medical director covering multiple locations manages per-injector delegation scope, supervision-tier compliance for each procedure performed, and medical-director chart-review visit documentation on a mix of a signed PDF contract and a front-desk appointment log, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific injector is actually cleared for a specific procedure under the state's required supervision tier before that appointment is booked.

**What to build:** A per-injector, per-procedure delegation-scope tracker mapping each staff credential against the state's required supervision tier for that specific procedure, with a "cleared to perform" gate blocking any appointment from being booked outside the injector's documented delegated scope; a medical-director chart-review and site-visit log with a due-date reminder tied to the state's required review frequency; and a multi-location supervision-coverage view for medical directors overseeing more than one spa site.

**Skill fit:** State medical board delegation-of-authority rules and supervision-tier definitions are public, structured regulatory data with concrete per-procedure and per-credential triggers, and the "cleared to perform" gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a fast-growing non-physician-injector niche this repo has never addressed and that shares no delegation mechanism with the ambulatory surgery center, urgent care, or stem-cell-clinic ideas already in this repo.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the per-injector/per-procedure delegation-scope gate, the medical-director chart-review log, and the multi-location coverage view. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent medical spas.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the American Med Spa Association (AmSpa) member directory and its annual conference reach exactly the small independent medical spas currently reconciling delegation agreements and supervision-tier compliance against a front-desk booking log with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per spa depending on injector count and procedure-category breadth. 22-28 spas × $429/mo avg = $9,438-12,012/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new injector hired reopens the per-procedure delegation-scope question from scratch, every new procedure category the spa adds to its menu requires its own supervision-tier determination, and the medical-director chart-review obligation recurs on its own fixed cycle for as long as the spa operates — a continuous, staffing-and-service-menu-driven compliance stream tied directly to the spa's growth.

**Biggest risk:** A spa already operating with an injector performing procedures outside their delegated scope has an underlying licensure exposure no tracking tool can retroactively cure. Mitigate by positioning the product explicitly as a forward-looking compliance aid and by qualifying leads toward growing spas expanding their injector roster or procedure menu, where the delegation-tracking genuinely earns its keep.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 spas ($5.1K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 20 spas ($8.6K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 22-28 spas plus a medical-board-facing delegation-and-supervision export spas can produce during a complaint investigation.

### 2. Commercial Truck Driving School FMCSA Training Provider Registry & Instructor-Qualification Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Commercial truck driving schools must register as a Training Provider on FMCSA's Training Provider Registry (TPR) under 49 CFR Part 380 Subpart F before a single student's training counts toward a valid CDL application, and every individual instructor delivering behind-the-wheel or classroom theory instruction must independently meet FMCSA's instructor-qualification standard — a specific combination of CDL-holding-period, driving-record, and (for behind-the-wheel instructors) recent driving-experience requirements the school must document and keep current for each instructor on staff — a school that lets an instructor's qualification file lapse, or that assigns a newly hired instructor to teach before their file is verified complete, has produced a batch of training records FMCSA can reject retroactively, invalidating every affected student's Certificate of Completion. The school's core deliverable is an electronic submission to the TPR of each graduate's training record — course completion, behind-the-wheel hours, and skills demonstrated — that must post to the registry before that student can even schedule their state CDL skills test; a submission delayed or rejected due to a data error is a student who shows up to their scheduled state skills test and gets turned away, a failure mode that destroys a school's word-of-mouth reputation in a business that depends heavily on it. Schools must also maintain their own state vocational-school licensure (a separate, state-level requirement layered on top of the federal TPR registration) and, for schools receiving federal financial aid referrals, VA/GI Bill approval with its own periodic re-certification cycle. A mid-size school running several instructors across rotating student cohorts manages per-instructor qualification-file currency, per-student TPR submission status, and state vocational license renewal on an admissions coordinator's spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific instructor's qualification file and a specific student's TPR submission are both actually complete before that student is marked graduation-ready.

**What to build:** A per-instructor FMCSA qualification-file tracker with a "cleared to instruct" gate blocking any instructor from being assigned a class or behind-the-wheel session until their file is complete and current; a per-student TPR submission tracker flagging any graduate whose training record hasn't yet posted to the registry; and a state vocational-license and VA/GI Bill approval renewal tracker.

**Skill fit:** FMCSA's Training Provider Registry submission format and instructor-qualification criteria are public, structured federal regulatory data with concrete per-instructor and per-student triggers, and the "cleared to instruct" gate plus the TPR submission tracker are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a CDL-training niche entirely distinct from every trucking-operator compliance idea already in this repo, none of which registers with FMCSA's Training Provider Registry or tracks an instructor-qualification file at all.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the instructor qualification-file gate, the per-student TPR submission tracker, and the state license/VA approval renewal tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent CDL schools.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Commercial Vehicle Training Association (CVTA) member directory and its annual conference reach exactly the small independent CDL schools currently reconciling instructor qualification files and TPR submissions on an admissions coordinator's spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per school depending on instructor count and student throughput. 22-28 schools × $429/mo avg = $9,438-12,012/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new instructor hired reopens the qualification-file verification from scratch, every graduating student generates its own TPR submission event regardless of how routine the cohort, and the state license and VA approval both renew on their own independent cycles — a continuous, cohort-and-staffing-driven compliance stream tied directly to the school's enrollment volume.

**Biggest risk:** A student turned away from their state skills test because their TPR record never posted is a severe reputational event for a school in a referral-driven business. Mitigate by making the TPR submission tracker a hard pre-graduation gate rather than an advisory flag, and by qualifying leads toward schools running multiple instructors and cohorts where the tracking genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

### 4. Indoor Firearms Range Operator OSHA Lead-Exposure Monitoring & Ventilation-Verification Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Indoor firearms range operators — commercial ranges, training facilities, and range/retail hybrid operations — carry a compliance obligation that has nothing to do with ATF licensing and everything to do with airborne lead: OSHA's lead standard at 29 CFR §1910.1025 requires periodic personal air-monitoring for every employee who works near the firing line, with both the monitoring frequency and the follow-up medical-surveillance obligation driven directly by each employee's measured exposure level relative to the action level and the permissible exposure limit — a range that lets a required monitoring round lapse, or fails to enroll an over-threshold employee in the mandated medical surveillance program (including blood-lead-level testing on a fixed periodic schedule), is exactly the finding an OSHA inspection is built to catch, and a training-heavy or high-round-count commercial range carries among the highest lead-exposure citation risk of any small business category OSHA regulates. Ranges must also maintain and periodically re-verify their ventilation system's actual measured airflow against NIOSH's published ventilation-design guidance for indoor ranges, since a system that has quietly degraded below its design airflow rate — a clogged filter, a fan losing efficiency — is the root cause behind the large majority of range lead-exposure citations, and a range that hasn't re-verified airflow since installation has no evidence the system is still doing its job. A range running several range safety officers and instructors across rotating shifts manages per-employee air-monitoring-and-medical-surveillance scheduling and ventilation-verification testing intervals on a facility manager's binder, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific employee's monitoring and medical-surveillance status, and the range's own ventilation performance, are both actually current before the next OSHA inspection.

**What to build:** A per-employee air-monitoring schedule computing each employee's next required test date from their prior exposure-level result, with an automatic medical-surveillance enrollment flag for any employee crossing the action level; a blood-lead-level testing tracker on its own periodic schedule for enrolled employees; and a ventilation-system airflow-verification tracker flagging the facility's next required re-test against NIOSH's design-airflow benchmark.

**Skill fit:** OSHA's lead-exposure monitoring thresholds and NIOSH's ventilation-design benchmarks are public, structured federal regulatory and standards-body data with concrete per-employee and per-facility triggers, and the exposure-driven monitoring-frequency calculator is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an indoor-range niche this repo has never addressed and that shares no regulator, standard, or exposure-monitoring mechanism with the FFL dealer or firearms-manufacturer ideas already in this repo.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the per-employee air-monitoring/medical-surveillance tracker, the blood-lead testing tracker, and the ventilation-verification tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent indoor ranges.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the National Shooting Sports Foundation's (NSSF) range-operator member segment and its annual range-safety seminar reach exactly the small independent ranges currently tracking air-monitoring schedules and ventilation testing on a facility manager's binder with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per range depending on employee count and facility size. 22-28 ranges × $429/mo avg = $9,438-12,012/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every employee's air-monitoring schedule recomputes on its own exposure-driven clock for as long as they're staffed, the ventilation-verification cycle recurs on its own periodic interval for as long as the range operates, and every new range safety officer or instructor hired reopens the monitoring-and-surveillance question — a continuous, staffing-and-facility-driven compliance stream tied directly to the range's operating hours and round count.

**Biggest risk:** A missed medical-surveillance enrollment for an over-threshold employee is a serious occupational-health failure, not a routine paperwork gap. Mitigate by making the exposure-driven monitoring and medical-surveillance triggers hard, unmissable alerts rather than advisory flags, and by qualifying leads toward higher-volume training-heavy ranges where the compliance calendar genuinely outgrows a facility manager's binder.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 ranges ($5.1K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 20 ranges ($8.6K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 22-28 ranges plus an OSHA-inspection-ready compliance export ranges can hand an inspector during a routine lead-exposure review.

## This Session's Pick: #4 (Indoor Firearms Range Operator) for the sharpest occupational-health stakes; #1 (Medical Spa) for the fastest-growing, most reachable buyer segment
