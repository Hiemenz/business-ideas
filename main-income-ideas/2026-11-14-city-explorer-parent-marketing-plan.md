# Marketing Plan: City Explorer for Parents of Young Kids (2026-11-14)

Companion to `2026-11-14-city-explorer-badges-app.md`. The customer is the **parent**. Kids are who the outings are for, not who uses the app. Parents sign up, plan, pay and check in. This plan covers how to find 300 parents on a free list, turn the best of them into paying members and win the family venues that fund the business.

## 1. The customer

**Primary persona: the tired planner.** A parent of a child aged 0-8, usually the one who carries the mental load of weekend plans. They want their kids out of the house and doing something good, and they are stuck on deciding, packing and getting there without a meltdown. On Friday they have nothing planned (the exact problem the app's rescue mode targets).

**Segments to test first (pick two):**
- **Babies and toddlers (0-4):** Short, nap-friendly outings, changing tables, stroller access. Highest pain, lowest confidence in going out.
- **Early school age (5-8):** Weekend boredom, rainy-day backups, activities that wear kids out.
- **Newcomer parents:** New to the city, no local network, the most grateful for recommendations.
- **Second parent or grandparent as helper:** The person who gets the plan forwarded to them. Make the plan easy to share.

**What they are really buying:** Not "ideas." They are buying a weekend that goes well and the relief of having it decided.

## 2. Positioning and message

**One-line position:** "Never be stuck on Friday again. We plan the weekend outing for your family, with the details checked."

**Value equation messaging (Hormozi):**
- **Dream outcome:** Weekends that feel like good family memories, not screens and boredom.
- **Likelihood:** Every place is checked for stroller access, changing tables, nap-friendly timing and open hours. Local parents' photos prove it.
- **Time delay:** A complete plan arrives Wednesday or Thursday, and a rescue list arrives Friday when you have nothing.
- **Effort:** One tap to lock it in, add to the calendar and text the other parent.

**Core promises to repeat:**
1. A ready plan, not a list of ideas.
2. Checked for little kids (strollers, naps, changing tables, quiet spots).
3. Friday rescue: three open-now options when you have nothing planned.
4. Free to start.

**Offer (Grand Slam):** "Get your family out of the house 4 weekends in 30 days, or your next month is free." Lead with the free weekly list, and offer the paid plan after the first lock-in.

**Message variants to test:**
- "It's Friday. You have no plans. Here's your rescue list."
- "The 15-minute weekend: a plan you can actually pull off with a toddler."
- "Stop Googling 'things to do with kids near me.'"
- "Made by a parent who got tired of Friday panic." (Use the founder's story, and only if it is true for you.)

## 3. The funnel

| Stage | What happens | Target (assumption) |
|---|---|---|
| **Reach** | A parent sees a post, a flyer, a partner mention or a friend's share | |
| **Sign-up** | Joins the free Thursday list with the 10-question intake | 8-15% of landing-page visitors |
| **Activation** | Taps "Lock it in" on a plan in the first 2 weeks | 40% of signups |
| **Follow-through** | Checks in after the outing | 55% of locked plans |
| **Paid** | Upgrades to the family plan at $59/yr after 2-3 good outings | 6-8% of active users |
| **Referral** | Invites another parent | 25% of activated users |

## 4. Lead magnets (what gets parents on the list)

1. **"The Rainy Day 10":** A free one-page guide to the 10 best indoor places in [neighborhood cluster] for under-5s, with parking and changing-table notes. Gated by email. Highest-value because it solves a real problem fast.
2. **"Friday Rescue Card":** A printable or phone-saved card that lists 5 reliable last-minute options (open late, no reservation, easy for kids). Share-worthy.
3. **"First 90 Days in [City] with a Baby":** For newcomers and new parents. Includes baby-friendly cafes, story times, parks and classes.
4. **Seasonal guide:** "Fall Family Bucket List: 20 things within 30 minutes" (apple orchards, pumpkin patches, leaf walks), released at the start of each season.
5. **Free first plan:** "Give us two minutes and we'll send you this weekend's plan." The intake itself is the lead capture.

**Rule:** Every lead magnet ends on the intake form and a promise of the Thursday plan.

## 5. Channels, in Hormozi's Core Four order

### A. Warm outreach (weeks 1-4, free, the first 100 parents)
- **Your own circle:** Message 40 parents you know (friends, coworkers, neighbors): "I'm building a weekend-plan service for parents of little kids. Would you try the first version and tell me what's wrong with it?"
- **Parent connectors:** Admins of local parent Facebook groups, moms' and dads' groups, baby-and-me class organizers, library story-time leaders, preschool directors and playgroup hosts. Offer them free premium and a say in the content. Ask for one introduction each.
- **Founding parents:** Recruit 25 "founding families" with free lifetime access to the paid features in exchange for a short call, honest feedback and a testimonial.
- **Script:** "Hi [name], I'm a parent in [neighborhood]. I'm testing a free Thursday text that plans your family's weekend with kid-ready details checked. Could I send you this week's plan? It takes two minutes and I'd love your honest feedback."

### B. Content (ongoing, builds in 4-8 weeks)
- **The Thursday plan itself is the product and the content.** Post each week's plan publicly (a web page and a social post) with a "get this in your inbox" link.
- **Where to post:** Local parent groups (follow the rules and ask admins before posting links), Instagram and Facebook, a neighborhood email newsletter, Nextdoor and local subreddits.
- **Formats that work for parents:**
  - Short videos: "3 things to do with a toddler this Saturday, all under 20 minutes away."
  - "Honest review" posts: what the changing table is like, where the stroller fits, how long you can stay.
  - Photo posts from members' hunts (with permission and no children's faces).
  - Seasonal lists and "rainy day" posts the night before bad weather.
- **Cadence:** 3 posts a week plus the Thursday plan. Batch on one weekday.
- **Search content (slower, lasting):** Pages titled like "Things to do with toddlers in [City]" and "Rainy day activities for kids in [City]," updated monthly. These bring in parents who are searching right now.

### C. Partnerships (weeks 3-12, the strongest local channel)
- **Libraries and children's services:** Offer the Rainy Day 10 as a handout and a QR poster at story time.
- **Pediatric and family-medicine offices, birth centers and new-parent classes:** A tasteful "settling in" card for new parents. Ask for a trial placement.
- **Preschools, daycares and parent-teacher groups:** A flyer in the newsletter or backpack, with a school-branded sign-up link and a small donation per signup if the school wants one.
- **Family venues:** Play cafes, farms, museums, indoor playgrounds and kids' classes. Offer a free featured slot in exchange for a link, a counter sign or an email mention. This is also how the venue pilot starts.
- **Family-oriented businesses:** Children's boutiques, family photographers, coffee shops near parks and family-friendly restaurants can co-promote.
- **Employers:** A weekend-plan perk for employees with young kids, pitched to HR or an employee resource group.
- **Referral reward to partners:** $5 credit or a donation per parent who signs up through them.

### D. Cold outreach (weeks 4-12, targeted and low volume)
- **Venue outreach:** 30 family venues visited in person with a printed sample report.
- **Parent-group admins:** Personal messages to 40 admins, asking for a permitted post, not spam.
- **Local press and bloggers:** Pitch one local-parent blogger and one local newspaper family section with a real hook ("The Friday rescue list").

### E. Paid ads (only after 30+ paying members and proven retention)
- **Test budget (assumption):** $300-500 for 4 weeks.
- **Targeting:** Parents within the neighborhood cluster, interests such as parenting, family activities and local events. Do not target children or use children's images in ads.
- **Offer in ads:** The free Rainy Day 10 or the Friday Rescue Card, never a hard sell on the paid plan.
- **Test:** 3 creatives x 2 audiences. Kill anything above $6 per free signup (assumption) after 25 signups.
- **Decision rule:** Scale only when cost per paying member is below one-fifth of annual revenue per member.

## 6. Referral loop

- **Plan share:** Every locked plan can be sent to the other parent, a friend or grandparent as a link that works without an account. The link shows the plan and a soft "get your own Thursday plan."
- **Parent pair bonus:** "Invite another family. You both get a free month."
- **Group plans:** Playgroup mode. Several families lock in the same plan, and the host gets a badge for organizing.
- **Moment to ask:** Right after a good outing check-in ("How did it go?" then "Want to send this to a friend?").
- **Testimonial prompt:** After the third outing, one message asking for a sentence and a photo of the thing (not the kids).

## 9. Metrics dashboard (review weekly)

Landing-page visits, sign-up rate, intake completion, Thursday open rate, plan-lock rate, follow-through rate, Friday-rescue use, weekends out per month per family, paid conversion, referrals per active family, cost per signup by channel, unsubscribe rate, venue pilot visits and revenue.

**Decision gates:**
- Week 4: under 60 list members means the message or channel is wrong. Interview 10 parents and rework.
- Week 8: plan-lock rate under 30% means the plan format needs work before any spending.
- Week 12: under 15 paying families means test price or the free-to-paid trigger.

## 10. Rules and trust

These are practical flags. Confirm local rules with a lawyer.
- **Parent-operated only:** No child accounts and no images of children in marketing. Ads target parents only.
- **Messaging consent:** Explicit opt-in for texts and email, an easy opt-out and registration for business texting where required.
- **Testimonials:** Written permission, and no children's names or faces.
- **Claims:** Say "checked for strollers and changing tables" only where it has been checked. A wrong claim on a family venue loses trust fast.
- **Social groups:** Follow each group's rules on self-promotion and ask admins first.
- **Safety:** Do not claim a place is "safe." Say what was checked and invite corrections.

## 11. Risks

- **Parents are busy:** Messages must be short and the plan must be complete.
- **Group-post fatigue:** Moderators may reject promotion. Lead with value and use admin permission.
- **Local scale:** The funnel only works with enough kid-ready places and parents in one cluster. Do not expand before density.
- **Seasonality:** Winter and summer plans differ, and bad weather can reduce outings. Use seasonal content and indoor backups.
- **Free-list trap:** Many parents love free and never pay. Use perks, family-plan tools (invites, calendar hold, rescue mode) and venue-funded revenue.

## 12. Open decisions

- Which neighborhood cluster and which two parent segments to start with.
- Whether to lead with text, email or both.
- Whether to use the founder's story in messaging.
- Whether to charge the family plan from week 9 or to wait for the month-4 checkpoint.
