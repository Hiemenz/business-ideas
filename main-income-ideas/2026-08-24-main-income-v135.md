### 1. NFA Title II Item Manufacturer (Class 2 SOT) ATF Form 2 & National Firearms Registry Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Small manufacturers of NFA Title II items — suppressors, short-barreled rifles and shotguns, and machine guns — hold an ATF Type 07 or Type 10 manufacturing Federal Firearms License layered with a Class 2 Special Occupational Taxpayer (SOT) registration under 26 U.S.C. §5801, a distinct annual special tax ($500 for a qualifying small manufacturer under the statute's reduced-rate threshold, $1,000 otherwise) that must be renewed every July 1 independent of the underlying FFL's own renewal cycle — an SOT that lapses even briefly makes every NFA item manufactured during the gap an unregistered firearm regardless of the FFL's own good standing. The core obligation that has no equivalent anywhere else in firearms manufacturing is the ATF Form 2 (Notice of Firearms Manufactured or Imported): every individual NFA item produced — each specific serial-numbered suppressor tube, each SBR receiver — must be reported on a Form 2 that adds that exact unit to the National Firearms Registration and Transfer Record (NFRTR) before it exists as a legal firearm at all, and a manufacturer who ships or even inventories a completed unit before its Form 2 has posted to the registry has created and possessed an unregistered NFA firearm, a strict-liability federal felony with no "we filed it a week late" defense. A manufacturer running several suppressor and SBR product lines through a growing production batch tracks per-unit Form 2 filing-and-registry-confirmation status, SOT renewal currency, and batch-to-serial-number reconciliation against ATF's own eForms confirmation on a spreadsheet and a folder of PDF confirmations, with no systematic gate confirming a specific serialized unit's Form 2 has actually cleared before that unit is marked shippable.

**What to build:** A per-unit Form 2 filing-and-NFRTR-confirmation tracker with a "cleared to ship" gate blocking any serialized unit from being marked shippable until its Form 2 confirmation number is on file; a Class 2 SOT annual renewal tracker independent of the underlying FFL's own renewal cycle; and a batch-to-serial-number reconciliation view cross-checking every unit produced in a manufacturing run against a confirmed, registry-posted Form 2.

**Skill fit:** ATF's Form 2/NFRTR filing requirement and the Class 2 SOT's separate annual renewal date are public, structured federal regulatory data with a concrete per-unit gating requirement, and the "cleared to ship" gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an NFA-manufacturing niche this repo has never addressed and that shares no filing type or failure mode with the standard Type 06/07 manufacturer or the FFL dealer/SOT-transfer ideas already in this repo.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the per-unit Form 2/NFRTR confirmation tracker with the ship-eligibility gate, the SOT renewal tracker, and the batch reconciliation view. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small NFA manufacturers.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the National Firearms Act Trade & Collectors Association (NFATCA) member directory and its annual conference reach exactly the small manufacturers currently reconciling per-unit Form 2 confirmations against a production batch with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per manufacturer depending on product-line count and monthly unit volume. 22-28 manufacturers × $429/mo avg = $9,438-12,012/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every unit manufactured opens its own independent Form 2/NFRTR confirmation cycle regardless of how routine the product line, the Class 2 SOT renews every July 1 on its own clock separate from the FFL, and every new product design the manufacturer develops multiplies the number of serialized units running through the registry-confirmation pipeline simultaneously — a continuous, per-unit compliance stream tied directly to the manufacturer's production volume.

**Biggest risk:** A unit shipped or inventoried as complete before its Form 2 posts to the NFRTR is an unregistered NFA firearm — a strict-liability federal felony exposure far more severe than a typical compliance lapse. Mitigate by qualifying leads for manufacturers running active, growing product lines where the per-unit registry-confirmation volume genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet, and by making the ship-eligibility gate an unmissable, hard block rather than a soft reminder.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10 manufacturers ($4.3K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 16 manufacturers ($6.9K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 22-28 manufacturers plus a distributor-facing registry-confirmation export manufacturers can hand a Class 3 dealer before a wholesale transfer.

### 2. Kosher-Certified Food Manufacturer & Processor Ingredient-Certificate, Mashgiach Supervision & Passover Recertification Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Food manufacturers seeking or maintaining kosher certification operate under private certifying agencies — the Orthodox Union (OU), OK Kosher Certification, Star-K, and Kof-K among the largest and most widely recognized — that require not just an annual facility audit but recurring, often unannounced on-site visits from a mashgiach (kosher supervisor) during actual production runs, since kosher status depends on real-time supervision of specific process steps — bishul yisroel supervised cooking, and kosherization (a specific heat- or steam-based cleaning procedure) of any shared equipment before it switches between a meat run and a dairy run — that a once-a-year paperwork audit alone cannot certify; a production run completed without the required mashgiach sign-off is exactly the gap a certifying agency's field representative is built to catch, and a lapsed relationship with the assigned mashgiach can suspend the certification symbol on every product the plant runs, not just the affected batch. Every formulation must also be classified meat, dairy, or pareve (neutral) and every sub-ingredient — gelatin, enzymes, emulsifiers, and flavor extracts, the same hidden-source categories that complicate any religious-dietary certification — must carry its own current kosher certificate from the supplier, since a single uncertified or misclassified ingredient can silently break a finished product's kosher status. **What to build:** A per-ingredient/per-supplier kosher-certificate tracker computing each certificate's expiration and flagging any formulation with a lapsed or missing sub-ingredient certificate; a per-production-run mashgiach scheduling and sign-off log enforcing a "cleared to produce" gate tied to meat/dairy/pareve classification and equipment-koshering confirmation between switched runs; and a Passover recertification cycle tracker managing the seasonal chametz-free ingredient review and dedicated production-run scheduling.

### 4. Autologous Stem Cell & Regenerative Medicine Clinic FDA HCT/P Minimal-Manipulation & Homologous-Use Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Clinics offering autologous stem cell and regenerative orthobiologic procedures — processing a patient's own adipose tissue or bone marrow and re-injecting it the same day for orthopedic, aesthetic, or wellness indications — operate in a narrow FDA regulatory lane defined by 21 CFR 1271.15(b)'s "same surgical procedure" exception and the minimal-manipulation/homologous-use test under 21 CFR 1271.10: a procedure that keeps the patient's own tissue minimally manipulated (no culturing, no significant structural alteration) and uses it for a homologous purpose (performing the same basic function it performed in the donor) can proceed without an IND or Biologics License Application, but a clinic that crosses either line — expanding or culturing cells, or marketing an orthopedic-derived cell product for an unrelated systemic or cosmetic claim — has manufactured an unapproved biological drug under 21 CFR Part 351, exactly the fact pattern behind the wave of FDA warning letters and federal injunctions this specific business model has drawn since the agency's regenerative-medicine enforcement-discretion period ended in 2021. Clinics must also track the physician's own state medical board scope-of-practice standing for performing the harvest-and-injection procedure and, in states requiring it, a specific informed-consent disclosure that the procedure is not FDA-approved for the marketed indication. A small clinic running a rotating patient schedule across knee, shoulder, and spine injection procedures manages per-procedure minimal-manipulation-and-homologous-use documentation, marketed-indication-to-informed-consent matching, and physician licensure currency with no dedicated compliance function beyond a clinical intake folder.

**Skill fit:** FDA's minimal-manipulation and homologous-use tests are public, structured regulatory criteria with a well-defined per-procedure classification question, and the classification-flag-before-scheduling pattern is the same deterministic "check the record against the rule before the action proceeds" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a same-day autologous-processing niche explicitly distinct from the allogeneic-donor Fertility Clinic idea and the cadaveric-donor Non-Reproductive Tissue Bank idea already in this repo, neither of which shares this clinic's donor-is-the-patient relationship or its minimal-manipulation/homologous-use classification question at all.

### 5. Cigar Bar, Hookah Lounge & Retail-Tobacco-Store-Exemption Operator Revenue-Percentage & FDA Blending Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Cigar bars, hookah lounges, and other on-premises smoking venues exist legally only because of a narrow exemption carved into a state or municipal Clean Indoor Air Act — such as Michigan's Dr. Ron Davis Smoke-Free Air Law's grandfathered cigar-bar carve-out or New York's Clean Indoor Air Act exemption for an establishment in continuous operation before a fixed date that derives a minimum statutory percentage of its gross annual revenue (commonly 10%) from tobacco-product sales — and a venue that quietly falls below that revenue threshold, whether from a slow tobacco-sales quarter or growing food-and-beverage revenue diluting the ratio, has silently converted itself into an illegal indoor-smoking establishment with no product ever having been mislabeled or improperly stamped; several jurisdictions require the venue to affirmatively certify its qualifying revenue percentage to the state or local health department annually, and a venue that can't produce that documentation on demand faces the exemption's immediate loss along with the fines that follow for every day of indoor smoking that occurred without it. A hookah lounge carries an additional, easy-to-miss trigger: any lounge that mixes or blends its own shisha flavor combinations on premises — rather than reselling a manufacturer's sealed product exactly as received — is itself legally a tobacco product manufacturer under FDA's deeming rule and must separately register that specific activity with FDA's Center for Tobacco Products, an obligation that has nothing to do with the venue's retail tobacco sales and everything to do with the physical act of combining tobacco products behind the bar.
