### 1. Portable Sanitation Company State Septage Hauler Permit & EPA Part 503 Waste Disposal Compliance
**Problem:** Portable sanitation companies that collect, transport, and dispose of waste from portable toilets, septic systems, and grease traps must comply with a multi-layer environmental regulatory framework. At the state level, most states require a separate septage hauler permit or waste transporter permit for each vehicle engaged in septage transport, with annual renewal, vehicle inspection requirements, and in some states, driver certification requirements. At the federal level, EPA's 40 CFR Part 503 establishes standards for the use and disposal of sewage sludge and septage — including land application (applying treated septage to agricultural fields as a soil amendment, which requires site-specific permits, buffer zone compliance, application rate calculations to avoid nitrogen over-application, and record-keeping of application dates, quantities, and field locations), beneficial use through composting (which requires meeting Part 503 Class A pathogen reduction and vector attraction reduction requirements), and disposal through alternative methods (land application to a permitted non-agricultural site, surface disposal, or incineration). Per-vehicle waste manifests must be maintained, documenting the origin, quantity, transport vehicle, and disposal destination for each load of septage transported. Small portable sanitation companies (5-30 vehicles, primarily rural and agricultural service areas) conduct land application of septage as their primary disposal method and face the most complex Part 503 compliance obligations — nitrogen loading calculations for each application site, Class B pathogen reduction documentation, and buffer zone records for each application event.
**What to build:** A portable sanitation company state permit and EPA Part 503 compliance management service — a vehicle permit and driver certification portfolio manager (tracking each vehicle's state septage hauler permit: permit number, renewal deadline, and any vehicle inspection dates — plus any state-required driver certification for septage transport, with alerts before each vehicle permit renewal deadline), a per-load waste manifest system (generating and archiving the manifest for each waste pickup and transport event: pickup location, waste type, volume, transport vehicle, and disposal destination — maintaining the manifests for the state's required retention period), a land application site compliance tracker (for each permitted land application site: site permit or landowner agreement, buffer zone documentation — distances to property lines, water bodies, and occupied structures — approved nitrogen loading rate, cumulative nitrogen applied in the current year, and application events with date, quantity, and GPS coordinates of application area), a Part 503 pathogen reduction documentation system (for septage applied to agricultural land under Class B pathogen reduction: documenting that the injected or incorporated septage meets the applicable Part 503 pH adjustment or heat treatment standard, with application method and post-application access restriction record), and an annual Part 503 compliance report generator (producing the required state annual septage land application report: aggregate quantities applied by site, nitrogen loading calculations for each site, and any buffer zone variance documentation — in the format required by the state environmental agency).
**Skill fit:** EPA 40 CFR Part 503 septage disposal standards and state septage hauler permit requirements are publicly available from EPA and each state's environmental agency — the nitrogen loading calculation methodology, buffer zone standards, and pathogen reduction documentation requirements are clearly specified. Building the vehicle permit tracker (a per-vehicle renewal calendar), per-load manifest system (a structured waste transport record), land application site compliance tracker (a per-site nitrogen loading calculator), Part 503 documentation system, and annual report generator are data management and calculation problems the builder's engineering and data analytics skills address directly. National Association of Wastewater Technicians (NAWT) and Pumper magazine's readership communities provide defined buyer communities.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a vehicle permit and driver certification portfolio manager, a per-load waste manifest system with retention archiving, a land application site nitrogen loading calculator with Part 503 buffer zone compliance, and an annual state septage land application report generator for 15 high-land-application states (TX, NC, PA, OH, IN, IL, MO, TN, GA, VA, WI, IA, KY, MN, SC); first 5-8 portable sanitation company clients through NAWT member outreach and Pumper & Cleaner Environmental Expo community while the nitrogen loading calculator is validated against real application site data and EPA Part 503 loading rate requirements.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through NAWT (National Association of Wastewater Technicians) member network and Pumper magazine trade community outreach (the concentrated professional communities for portable sanitation and septage hauler operators), state environmental agency septage management program referral networks (state program managers who administer septage land application permits see nitrogen loading tracking gaps and incomplete manifest records at every permit renewal and refer systematic compliance tools to haulers in their program), and portable sanitation equipment manufacturer referral networks (equipment manufacturers like Satellite Industries, PolyJohn, and Romtec see compliance challenges at their dealer customers and actively refer compliance management services to their customer base).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per portable sanitation company: $250-600/mo (depending on vehicle count and land application complexity — a 5-vehicle company with 2 land application sites: $250/mo; a 20-vehicle company with 8 permitted land application sites across multiple counties: $600/mo). Managing 30-50 company retainers at $375/mo average = **$11,250-18,750/mo**. Annual Part 503 compliance report preparation packages (one-time $300-700 per company for the annual state septage land application report) generate concentrated Q1 revenue. Reaching 30-50 company retainers requires 4-6 NAWT chapter relationships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Per-load manifests must be generated for every waste transport event — a 15-vehicle company performing 20 pickups per vehicle per week generates 300+ weekly manifest events. ### 2. High School Athletic Director NFHS Eligibility & State Athletic Association Compliance
**Problem:** High school athletic directors managing 15-25 sports programs with 200-800 student-athlete participants must maintain eligibility compliance for every student in every sport — a compliance obligation that is simultaneous, sport-specific, and academically gated. NFHS (National Federation of State High School Associations) eligibility rules establish baseline standards adopted by each state's athletic association, including: academic eligibility (passing a minimum number of credits in the preceding grading period — typically 5 of 6 classes), enrollment and attendance minimums, age limits (students must not have reached their 19th birthday before a specified date in the school year), amateur status, and maximum seasons of participation per sport. Transfer eligibility is particularly complex — a student who transfers from another school must demonstrate that the transfer was not athletically motivated (most states impose a waiting period or require a hardship waiver for transferring athletes). In addition, athletic directors must maintain current coach certifications with the state athletic association (first aid, CPR, concussion protocol training — required for every coach in every sport, with annual or biennial renewal), register each sport's roster with the state athletic association by the applicable sport season deadline, and submit physical examination certificates for each athlete before they participate in any practice or competition. Small high school athletic departments (managing these obligations across 15-25 sports with one or two athletic directors) do this work in a combination of paper files, a school information system, and direct communication with the state association — with no systematic deadline calendar.
**What to build:** A high school athletic director NFHS eligibility and state athletic association compliance management service — a student-athlete eligibility verification system (for each student-athlete: current academic credit load and prior term credits passed, eligibility status per NFHS and state standards, physical examination expiration date, age eligibility confirmation, and seasons of participation count per sport — with automatic eligibility status flags for any student approaching a participation limit or with expired documentation), a transfer eligibility tracking workflow (for each transferring student-athlete: prior school, transfer date, transfer type — intra-district vs. inter-district, reason for transfer, state association transfer waiver application status, and waiting period or eligibility determination — maintaining the documentation package required for transfer eligibility decisions), a coach certification compliance calendar (for each coach in each sport: first aid certification, CPR certification, concussion protocol training completion, and any state athletic association required coaching education — with expiration dates and renewal alerts across the entire coaching staff), a sport-season roster registration workflow (generating the state athletic association's roster registration for each sport season: eligible athletes with eligibility documentation confirmed, coaching staff with certification status confirmed, and submission deadline tracking for each sport's registration window), and an athletic physical examination tracking system (for each student-athlete: physical examination date, examining physician, and expiration — most state associations require a physical within 12 months of the first practice, and athletes whose physicals expire mid-season are ineligible until renewed).
**Skill fit:** NFHS eligibility standards are publicly available, and each state's high school athletic association publishes its specific eligibility rules based on the NFHS framework — building a state-by-state eligibility requirements database is a regulatory research problem. The student-athlete eligibility system (a per-student record with rolling eligibility status calculation), transfer eligibility workflow (a structured transfer documentation tracker), coach certification calendar, season roster registration workflow, and physical examination tracker are data management problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. NIAAA (National Interscholastic Athletic Administrators Association) member directory provides a defined buyer community.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a student-athlete eligibility status tracker with academic credit and physical examination currency, a coach certification compliance calendar for 15 high school sports, a transfer eligibility documentation workflow, and a sport-season roster registration checklist for 10 high-athletic-enrollment states (CA, TX, FL, OH, PA, IL, NY, GA, NC, MI); first 5-10 high school athletic director clients through NIAAA member outreach while the eligibility status tracker is validated against real school student-athlete roster data.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through NIAAA (National Interscholastic Athletic Administrators Association) member network and state athletic directors association outreach (the concentrated professional communities for high school ADs — NIAAA's annual conference is the primary industry event), state high school athletic association referral networks (state associations that administer eligibility compliance see transfer eligibility documentation gaps and coach certification lapses at every eligibility hearing and informally refer systematic compliance tools to member schools), and school district insurance provider referral networks (commercial liability insurers for school districts see eligibility and coach certification compliance gaps during risk assessments and refer compliance management tools to district risk managers).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per school athletic department: $200-500/mo (depending on program size — a 15-sport program with 250 student-athletes: $200/mo; a 25-sport program with 700 student-athletes and frequent transfers: $500/mo). Managing 40-60 school retainers at $325/mo average = **$13,000-19,500/mo**. State athletic association eligibility hearing preparation packages (one-time $200-500 per case when a student's eligibility is challenged) generate supplemental revenue. Reaching 40-60 school retainers requires 5-7 state athletic director association chapter relationships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Student-athlete eligibility must be verified each semester for every student in every sport — a school with 600 student-athletes across 20 sports verifies eligibility for 1,200 athlete-sport slots per year. Coach certifications expire on rolling schedules for every coach in every sport. Physical examinations expire annually for every athlete. Transfer eligibility determinations occur throughout the year as students transfer schools for all manner of reasons.
**Biggest risk:** A school that plays an ineligible student-athlete — because the physical examination had expired and the lapse was not caught — faces state athletic association sanctions including potential forfeiture of games won by that student-athlete's participation. ### 3. Consumer Product CPSC Safety Testing Program & Certificate of Conformity Compliance
**Problem:** Companies that manufacture or import consumer products subject to mandatory CPSC (Consumer Product Safety Commission) safety standards under the Consumer Product Safety Improvement Act (CPSIA) must maintain compliance with testing and certification requirements that vary by product type and target consumer. General children's products (intended for children 12 and under) must be tested by a CPSC-accredited third-party laboratory before sale, and a Children's Product Certificate (CPC) must be issued for each product based on the test results — the CPC must state the CPSC-required tests passed, the test results, the testing lab's name and contact information, and the product's production run. General products (not children's products) require a General Certificate of Conformity (GCC) based on either third-party testing or the manufacturer's own reasonable testing program. Testing must be repeated for production testing (periodic testing of products from ongoing production) at intervals required by CPSC rules or when a material change in the product occurs. Products in highly regulated categories — children's toys (ASTM F963), children's apparel (16 CFR Part 1610 flammability, 16 CFR Part 1501 small parts), children's furniture, juvenile products, and fireworks — have category-specific mandatory test standards. Small and mid-size consumer product companies (10-100 SKUs) frequently lack systematic tracking of: which products require which mandatory tests, whether current test reports cover the production run being sold, and when production testing intervals trigger a new round of required testing.
**What to build:** A consumer product CPSC safety testing and certification compliance management service — a product safety testing requirement database (for each product SKU: the applicable mandatory CPSC safety standards and test codes based on the product category and intended user age, whether the product is a general product or children's product, and the required testing frequency — initial testing before first sale and production testing intervals), a testing documentation tracking system (for each product's test report: the testing lab name and CPSC accreditation number, the standards tested, the test date, test results summary, and the production run covered — with alerts when a production test interval is due or when the current test report's coverage has expired for the current production run), a Certificate of Conformity generation and management system (generating the CPC or GCC for each product from the testing documentation: product description, applicable standards, test results, testing lab, and production batch identifier — maintaining the current CPC/GCC on file and generating updated certificates when new test reports are issued), a material change alert system (triggering a testing review when a product's material, component, or manufacturing process changes — CPSC requires new testing when a material change may affect compliance with an applicable standard), and a testing lab management system (maintaining the CPSC-accredited lab relationship for each product category: lab contact, accreditation scope and expiration, test turnaround times, and cost per test standard — for informed testing program planning).
**Skill fit:** CPSC mandatory safety standards and CPSIA testing and certification requirements are publicly available from CPSC's website — the product category-to-mandatory-test-standard mapping, CPC content requirements, and production testing interval rules are publicly documented. Building the product safety testing requirement database (a per-SKU testing requirement lookup based on product category and consumer age group), testing documentation tracker (a per-product test report expiration monitor), CPC/GCC generator (a template-based certificate generator), material change alert, and testing lab management system are data management and document generation problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. Toy Industry Association (TIA) and CPSC's small business resources provide access to the buyer community.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a product safety testing requirement database for the 20 most common CPSC-regulated product categories (toys, juvenile products, children's apparel, children's sleepwear, household furniture, fireworks, bicycles, helmets, bedding products, and others), a testing documentation tracking system with test report expiration alerts, a CPC and GCC generator, and a material change alert workflow; first 5-10 consumer product company clients through TIA member outreach and CPSC's registered importer community while the testing requirement database is validated against real product SKU portfolios.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through Toy Industry Association (TIA) and Juvenile Products Manufacturers Association (JPMA) member networks (the concentrated professional communities for children's product manufacturers — TIA's annual Toy Fair is the primary industry event), CPSC-accredited testing lab referral networks (SGS, Bureau Veritas, Intertek, and UL whose testing labs process CPSC mandatory tests for consumer product companies see testing program management gaps at every test order and actively refer compliance tracking tools to their recurring customers), and consumer product liability insurance broker referral networks (commercial liability insurers who write product liability coverage for consumer product companies require CPSC compliance documentation during underwriting and refer compliance management tools to companies that lack systematic testing records).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per consumer product company: $300-800/mo (depending on SKU count and product category complexity — a 15-SKU children's toy company: $300/mo; a 60-SKU company with mixed children's and general products across multiple regulated categories with ongoing production testing intervals: $800/mo). Managing 20-35 company retainers at $500/mo average = **$10,000-17,500/mo**. CPSC recall and enforcement response documentation packages (one-time $1,000-3,000 when a company receives a CPSC subpoena or Section 15 substantial product hazard notice) generate significant supplemental revenue. Reaching 20-35 company retainers requires 3-5 TIA and JPMA chapter relationships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Production testing intervals require ongoing periodic test submissions for every product in active production — a 40-SKU company with quarterly production testing intervals for children's products generates 160+ annual test submissions requiring documentation. CPC and GCC must be maintained current for every product being sold. Material changes to any product trigger an immediate testing review. CPSC enforcement activity targeting non-compliant children's products is increasing, creating consistent urgency for systematic certification documentation.
**Biggest risk:** A children's product sold without a current CPC — either because testing lapsed during a production run change or because a material change was not recognized as triggering a new test requirement — creates CPSIA civil penalty exposure (up to $120,000 per violation). Build the testing documentation tracker with a "currently selling" flag: any product marked as "in active distribution" without a current test report that covers its current production run triggers an immediate compliance gap alert that must be resolved before the product can continue to be listed for sale in the system.
**Growth path:** Side project → testing requirement database for 20 product categories, testing documentation tracker, and CPC/GCC generator deployed, first 5-10 company retainers through TIA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 10-15 company retainers, testing lab referral pipeline generating consistent new clients (~$4-7K/mo). ### 4. Professional Engineering Firm Multi-State PE License Portfolio & CPD Compliance
**Problem:** Professional engineering consulting firms that practice engineering in multiple states must maintain Professional Engineer (PE) licenses for each engineer who signs and seals engineering documents in each state where the firm provides services. Multi-state PE practice involves: obtaining a PE license in each additional state through comity or reciprocity (applying to each state's engineering licensing board with the engineer's primary state credentials, examination history, and experience documentation), completing state-specific continuing professional development (CPD) requirements for PE license renewal — most states require 15-30 PDH (Professional Development Hours) per renewal period, with some states requiring specific topic hours (ethics, sustainability, or topic-specific to the engineering discipline), and tracking renewal deadlines across all licensed states (renewal periods and deadlines vary by state from annual to triennial renewal, with late renewal penalties that can include license revocation). An engineering consulting firm with 15 engineers each licensed in 5-8 states manages 75-120 active PE licenses simultaneously, each with different renewal deadlines and CPD requirements. Managing this across a shared spreadsheet fails when engineers take CPD courses but don't centrally log their hours, when renewal deadlines are missed during busy project seasons, and when the firm expands into new states and needs to track comity application status for each engineer.
**What to build:** A professional engineering firm multi-state PE license portfolio and CPD compliance management service — a multi-engineer, multi-state PE license portfolio manager (for each engineer: all current state PE license numbers, license disciplines, renewal deadline by state, renewal period length, and any state-specific license conditions — with tiered alerts before each state's renewal deadline and a firm-level compliance dashboard showing every license's status), a CPD requirement tracking system (for each engineer by state: required PDH total for the current renewal period, CPD hours completed with course title and provider, hours by required category (ethics, technical, professional practice), and pacing alerts when an engineer is behind the completion rate needed to finish before renewal), a comity license application tracker (for engineers seeking new state PE licenses: tracking the application submission date, required documentation submitted, application fee paid, state board processing status, and license issuance confirmation — maintaining a complete application audit trail for each new state), a PE seal and signature compliance tracker (for each project requiring signed and sealed documents: confirming that the signing engineer has an active PE license in the applicable state with the appropriate discipline — preventing documents from being sealed by an engineer whose state license has lapsed), and a firm-level CPD completion and renewal dashboard (showing the firm's overall CPD completion rate across all engineers and states, any engineer with a renewal deadline in the next 90 days and their current CPD completion percentage, and any engineer with an expired or non-renewed license that prevents them from sealing documents in a given state).
**Skill fit:** State engineering licensing board renewal requirements, CPD/PDH requirements, and comity application procedures are publicly available from NCEES (National Council of Examiners for Engineering and Surveying) and each state's engineering licensing board — building a state-by-state PE renewal and CPD requirement database is a regulatory research problem. The multi-engineer, multi-state license portfolio manager (a per-engineer, per-state license database with renewal calendar), CPD tracking system (a per-engineer PDH log with category tracking), comity application tracker, PE seal compliance checker, and firm dashboard are data management problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. ACEC (American Council of Engineering Companies) member directory provides a defined buyer community.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a multi-engineer, multi-state PE license portfolio manager with renewal alerts for 50 state engineering licensing boards, a CPD tracking system with state-specific PDH category requirements, a comity application tracker, and a firm-level compliance dashboard; first 5-8 engineering consulting firm clients through ACEC member outreach while the multi-state license manager is validated against real firm engineer license portfolios.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through ACEC (American Council of Engineering Companies) member network and NSPE (National Society of Professional Engineers) member outreach (the concentrated professional communities for PE firm owners and firm administrators), state engineering licensing board CE provider referral networks (CE providers who sell PDH courses to engineers see license management challenges at every course signup and refer systematic license tracking tools to firms purchasing courses for multiple engineers), and engineering E&O insurance carrier referral networks (engineers' professional liability insurers who write E&O coverage for engineering firms require confirmation of current PE licensing for engineers signing project documents and refer license management tools to firms whose license tracking is manual).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per engineering firm: $300-900/mo (depending on engineer count and state diversity — a 5-engineer firm with 20 total state licenses: $300/mo; a 20-engineer firm with 120 total state licenses and complex CPD tracking: $900/mo). Managing 20-30 firm retainers at $550/mo average = **$11,000-16,500/mo**. Comity application coordination packages (one-time $200-500 per state for managing the comity application documentation and tracking for each new state an engineer wants to license in) generate consistent supplemental revenue as firms expand geographically. Reaching 20-30 firm retainers requires 3-5 ACEC chapter relationships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** PE license renewals stagger throughout the year across all engineers and all states — a 15-engineer firm with multi-state licenses has license renewal events throughout every month of the year. CPD hours must be accumulated continuously throughout the renewal period. ### 5. Medical Device Manufacturer FDA Quality System Regulation & MDR Adverse Event Compliance
**Problem:** Medical device manufacturers and contract manufacturers producing FDA-regulated devices (Class I, II, and III devices) must maintain compliance with FDA's Quality System Regulation (21 CFR Part 820 — now transitioning to ISO 13485-aligned requirements) and FDA's Medical Device Reporting (MDR) regulation (21 CFR Part 803). QSR compliance requires documented procedures covering: design controls (design history file for each device, with risk analysis, design verification and validation records), production and process controls (manufacturing procedures, incoming acceptance activities, labeling controls, and packaging validation), corrective and preventive action (CAPA records — documenting identified quality problems, root cause analysis, corrective actions taken, and effectiveness checks), complaint handling (every complaint received about a device must be logged, evaluated for reportability to FDA, and resolved with documented investigation), and device history records (maintaining records for each production lot including components used, manufacturing dates, and inspection records). MDR requirements mandate reporting to FDA within 30 days of becoming aware of information that reasonably suggests a device malfunction or adverse event that could cause or contribute to serious injury or death — and within 5 days for certain urgent events. Small medical device manufacturers (Class II devices, 10-50 employees) have the same FDA QSR and MDR compliance obligations as large device companies but without a dedicated quality and regulatory affairs department.
**What to build:** A medical device manufacturer FDA QSR and MDR compliance management service — a CAPA management system (for each identified quality problem or complaint: documenting the initial description, root cause investigation steps and findings, proposed corrective action, implementation target date and completion record, and effectiveness check — maintaining a CAPA log that FDA inspectors review during facility inspections), a complaint handling and MDR reportability assessment system (logging every complaint received about a device, documenting the MDR reportability assessment — does this complaint describe a malfunction or adverse event that could cause or contribute to serious injury, and is this a previously reported event — with a 30-day MDR reporting deadline tracker for each reportable complaint and automated escalation when the deadline approaches), a device history record maintenance workflow (for each production lot: documenting components and materials used with lot numbers, manufacturing dates, inspection records, and final release authorization — confirming traceability from each finished device back to its component materials), a design change control tracker (for each proposed change to a device's design, materials, or manufacturing process: documenting the change description, risk assessment, design verification or validation required, regulatory impact assessment — does this change require a 510(k) supplement or PMA amendment — and implementation authorization record), and an FDA inspection readiness dashboard (generating the document package that FDA investigators typically request during a Quality System Inspection Technique (QSIT) inspection: the 100 most recent CAPAs, complaint handling records for the prior year, current device history records, and the design history file for the most recently marketed device — organized for rapid retrieval during a facility inspection).
**Skill fit:** FDA 21 CFR Part 820 QSR requirements and FDA MDR requirements (21 CFR Part 803) are publicly available — the CAPA documentation requirements, complaint handling standards, MDR 30-day and 5-day reporting triggers, and device history record content are clearly specified in the regulations and FDA guidance documents. Building the CAPA management system (a structured quality problem documentation and tracking tool), complaint handling and MDR reportability assessment, device history record workflow, design change control tracker, and FDA inspection readiness dashboard are data management problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. Medical Device Manufacturers Association (MDMA) and AdvaMed member networks provide access to the buyer community.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a CAPA management system with effectiveness check tracking, a complaint handling log with MDR reportability assessment and 30-day deadline tracking, a device history record maintenance workflow for Class II devices, and an FDA inspection readiness documentation package generator; first 5-8 medical device manufacturer clients through MDMA member outreach while the CAPA management system is validated against real device company quality system documentation.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through MDMA (Medical Device Manufacturers Association) member network and Regulatory Affairs Professionals Society (RAPS) member outreach (the concentrated professional communities for medical device regulatory and quality professionals — RAPS' annual Regulatory Convergence conference is the primary industry event), FDA-recognized quality management system consultant referral networks (QMS consultants who help device companies prepare for FDA inspections see CAPA and complaint handling documentation gaps in every engagement and actively refer systematic QSR compliance tools to companies they've worked with), and medical device liability insurance broker referral networks (insurers who write product liability coverage for device manufacturers require evidence of QSR compliance as part of underwriting and refer systematic quality documentation tools to companies that cannot demonstrate a complete CAPA or complaint handling program).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per medical device manufacturer: $700-2,000/mo (depending on device class and quality system complexity — a single-device Class II manufacturer with a straightforward QSR program: $700/mo; a multi-device manufacturer with Class II and Class III devices, complex CAPA programs, and active MDR reporting volume: $2,000/mo). Managing 10-15 manufacturer retainers at $1,200/mo average = **$12,000-18,000/mo**. FDA inspection preparation documentation packages (one-time $2,000-5,000 when a manufacturer receives an FDA inspection notice) generate significant supplemental revenue. Reaching 10-15 manufacturer retainers requires 3-4 RAPS and MDMA chapter relationships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** CAPA records must be maintained and effectiveness-checked for every quality issue identified throughout the year. Complaint handling is triggered by every customer complaint — a device in active use generates complaints continuously. MDR reporting obligations arise from complaints with reportable adverse event characteristics. Build the MDR reportability assessment system with a hard deadline escalation: when a complaint is assessed as potentially reportable, a 30-day clock starts immediately, and the system escalates the open reportability assessment at days 20, 25, and 28 to the regulatory affairs manager and facility executive with increasing urgency.
