# Matchmaking Business: Friends, Marriage and High-Net-Worth Clients (2026-11-14)

A single matchmaking business built in three layers. One interest-and-values intake feeds all three, so the cheap tier builds the member pool and the expensive tiers bring the revenue per client. Pitched through an Alex Hormozi lens: Grand Slam Offer, value equation, risk reversal and warm leads first.

## Who this is for
A 29-year-old software engineer with data skills who wants to reach $8-15K+/mo building solo, in evenings and weekends, with no outside funding. Each layer needs a path to first revenue inside 90 days.

## The three layers at a glance

| Layer | Buyer | Price | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Interest-based friend matching | Adults who want real-life activity partners | $24-39/mo | Volume engine, fills the pool |
| 2. Marriage matching | Marriage-minded adults in one community | $299-499 intake + $99-149/mo + $1,500-3,000 success fee | High-ticket engine |
| 3. High-net-worth matchmaking | Founders, executives, physicians, newly wealthy, relocated families | $2,500-75,000 plus $10-50K success fee | Highest ceiling, slowest start |

Recommended order: start with layer 1 for cash and a member base, add layer 2 using its testimonials, and move to layer 3 once there is a track record.

## Layer 1: Interest-Based Activity-Partner Matching

**Problem:** Adults who move or change life stage struggle to find people who share a specific interest (pickleball, trail running, board games, language practice). Event apps list events but provide no one to go with, and few people show up alone to a group of strangers.

**What to build:** A paid, small-scale matching service, one city and one interest cluster at first.
- A 5-minute intake covers interests, skill level, schedule, location and goal (regular partner, small group or one-off).
- Each week, introduce 1-3 people or a pod of 3-4 with a suggested activity, time and place. Version one is you with a spreadsheet and a script, then software ranking.
- After each meetup, a text asks "Would you meet again?" Two yes answers create a standing pairing. A no feeds the matching model.
- Track meetups held, repeat pairings and 90-day retention.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Meet 3 people who share your interest within 30 days, or your next month is free." Likelihood is high because a human curates and the guarantee backs it. Time delay is short (first intro within 48 hours). Effort is low because you plan the first meetup.

**Skill fit:** Intake forms, matching logic, SMS and email automation, retention analytics.

**MVP scope:** 2 weekends for form, spreadsheet and manual intros. Automated ranking by weekend 4.

**Time to first $:** 14-30 days. Post in 3-5 local hobby groups and city subreddits, and offer 20 founding members a $19 first month.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $24/mo, or $39/mo for a premium pod tier. 350 members x about $29 = **$10,150/mo**. A $149 corporate plan for relocated employees adds a second line.

**Biggest risks:**
- **Cold start:** Matching needs enough people with the same interest nearby. Pick one dense city and 1-2 interests.
- **Safety and trust:** Use public first meetups, profile checks and a report button from day one.
- **No-shows:** A small recurring fee and a confirm-by-text step filter for commitment.

## Layer 2: Marriage Matching for a Specific Community

**Who it's for:** Serious, marriage-minded adults tired of swiping. Start with one community where demand and norms are clear (a faith community, an immigrant community, a professional niche such as nurses or engineers, or a city's 30+ crowd).

**What you do:**
1. A deeper intake covers values, worldview, kids, location, finances, family involvement, lifestyle and deal-breakers.
2. You personally review each profile, because the human matchmaker is the value.
3. Send 2-3 curated introductions a month, each with a "why you two" note.
4. After each date, collect feedback from both people, coach them and refine the next match.

**Pricing:**
- $299-499 intake and profile build
- $99-149/mo while actively matching
- $1,500-3,000 success fee on engagement or exclusive commitment

**Grand Slam angle:** "Three vetted introductions in 60 days, or the intake fee is refunded." The alternative, years of dating apps, costs a great deal in time and emotion, so buyers pay for someone to take the effort off them.

**Hormozi levers:**
- **Warm leads first:** Ask your network and community leaders (clergy, club heads, alumni groups) for introductions before any ads.
- **Lead magnet:** A free compatibility-snapshot quiz that also collects intake data.
- **Speed:** First introduction within 48 hours of signup.
- **Risk reversal:** Refundable intake fee and a success fee paid only on results.

**Income math (combined with layer 1):**
- 250 friend members x $29 = $7,250/mo
- 40 marriage-track members x $119 = $4,760/mo
- 1 success fee a month x $2,000 = $2,000/mo
- **About $14,000/mo** at maturity, with the marriage track giving roughly half the revenue from under 15% of members.

**Time to first $:** 14-21 days. Hand-match your first 10 clients free or at a steep discount in exchange for testimonials.

**Biggest risks:**
- **Cold start:** One narrow community in one city. A broad "everyone" product has too few good matches.
- **Gender balance:** Recruit the scarcer side first, with free or discounted membership.
- **Safety and trust:** Verify identity, use public first meetings and have a report-and-remove process.
- **Success-fee tracking:** Couples may go quiet once matched. Use a signed agreement and check-in dates.
- **Human time:** Hand-curated matching tops out around 40-60 active clients per person. Add a second matchmaker or lean on software ranking.

## Layer 3: Discreet Matchmaking for High-Net-Worth Individuals

**Core shift:** With HNW clients you sell privacy, curation and personal service, not an app. The matchmaker is a concierge, and one client can be worth more than 100 mass-market members.

**Buyers:**
- Busy founders, executives and physicians with no time to date who distrust apps.
- Newly wealthy people (exits, inheritance) wary that new contacts want their money.
- Relocated or international families seeking peers in a new city.
- Parents helping an adult child find a serious partner within the family's values.

**Offer ladder:**

| Tier | What they get | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Private friend circle | Hand-picked peer introductions around shared interests (sailing, collecting, philanthropy, golf, wine) and small curated dinners | $2,500-5,000/yr |
| Partner search | Confidential intake, vetted introductions, date coaching, a named matchmaker | $10,000-25,000 retainer for 6-12 months |
| Executive search for a spouse | Active outreach beyond your pool, background verification, discreet lifestyle brief | $25,000-75,000 plus success fee |
| Success fee (any tier) | Paid on a committed relationship or engagement | $10,000-50,000 |

**Grand Slam angle:** "Five vetted, in-person introductions in 90 days that match your written criteria, or we refund the second half of your retainer."

**Why HNW differs:**
- Price is not the objection. Trust is, so discretion, proof and referrals matter most.
- Verification is the product: identity, employment, marital status and reputation checks, done respectfully.
- 15-25 retainers a year can support a solo business.

**Income math (realistic):**
- 12 partner-search retainers x $15,000 = $180,000/yr
- 6 success fees x $20,000 = $120,000/yr
- 60 friend-circle memberships x $3,500 = $210,000/yr
- **About $510,000/yr (~$42K/mo) at maturity.** A realistic first-year target is far lower: 5 retainers plus 20 memberships is about $145,000.

**First 90 days (no funding):**
1. **Weeks 1-2:** Build a one-page offer, a confidential intake and a verification checklist.
2. **Weeks 2-6:** Get warm introductions through wealth advisors, family-office staff, private-club managers, executive coaches and divorce or estate attorneys. Offer each a referral fee.
3. **Weeks 4-10:** Take 3-5 pilot clients at a founding rate in exchange for an anonymized case study they approve.
4. **Weeks 8-12:** Host one invite-only dinner for 8-12 people as a lead magnet and live demo.

**Where your skills fit:** Build the back end: private CRM, encrypted storage, matching score over intake data, scheduling and a post-introduction feedback loop. The tech stays invisible and clients see white-glove service.

**Biggest risks:**
- **Reputation and trust:** One leak or bad introduction ends referrals. Use signed confidentiality terms, encrypted storage and strict access control.
- **Safety and liability:** Verify both sides, hold first meetings in public, have an attorney draft the client agreement and be clear that you introduce people without guaranteeing outcomes.
- **Credibility gap:** A 29-year-old with no track record will struggle to sell $15K retainers. Partner with or hire a respected, experienced matchmaker as the face, and run operations and technology for a revenue share.
- **Pool imbalance:** Recruit the scarcer side first, often free.
- **Success-fee leakage:** Use signed terms and scheduled check-ins.
- **Sales cycle:** Trust takes months. Keep the friend-circle tier to bring in revenue while retainers close.

**Caveat:** This is the highest-ceiling layer and the slowest to start. If cash is needed within 90 days, begin with layers 1 and 2 and use their clients and testimonials to move up.

## Shared growth path
One interest in one city, then more interests and cities, then employers, universities and new-resident programs, then a network of affiliated matchmakers sharing leads across cities. Later add concierge extras (event hosting, relocation help, travel companions), each priced separately. Interest-based matching can also expand into mentor pairing, co-founder matching and hobby-based travel groups.

## Variants
- Match people to local experiences (classes, tastings, volunteering) and take a booking commission.
- Match people to mentors or accountability partners in a skill such as coding, fitness or language learning.
- Match people to products or services through a curated concierge subscription.

## Go-to-Market: Building the Database and Getting People in the Door

Matchmaking has a cold-start problem: nobody joins an empty pool, and the pool stays empty until people join. Build it one narrow segment at a time, working Hormozi's Core Four (warm outreach, content, cold outreach, paid ads) in that order.

### Step 0: Pick one tight segment (week 1)
Choose a single city and a single community or interest, such as "pickleball players in [city], ages 28-45" or "single nurses in [city]." A narrow segment gives density, easy-to-find lead sources and a message that feels written for them. Do not launch to everyone.

### Step 1: Warm outreach (weeks 1-3, free, first 30-50 members)
- **Your own network:** Message 50 people personally: "I'm starting a small matching service for [segment]. Who do you know who'd want in?" Referral asks convert far better than cold asks.
- **Community connectors:** Contact club organizers, group admins, run-club leaders, church or community-center staff, gym owners and instructors. Offer free membership and a referral reward ($20 credit or cash per paying member).
- **Founding offer:** The first 20-30 members get $19 for life or a free first month, in exchange for honest feedback and a testimonial.
- **Fix the scarce side first:** If one gender or group is outnumbered, recruit that side first with free or discounted membership.

### Step 2: Lead magnets that fill the database (weeks 2-6)
- **Free compatibility quiz:** "What's your friendship style?" or "What are you really looking for in a partner?" It captures email and phone and doubles as intake data. Every taker becomes a database row.
- **Free first introduction:** One free match to try. People see the value after they have met someone.
- **Hosted meetups:** A free or $10 event such as a board-game night, a group run or a speed-friending evening. Collect contact details at the door, then offer matching to attendees. In-person events build trust, the main barrier here.
- **Waitlist:** A "join the [city] list" page with a live counter ("212 people on the list") for social proof, and a signal of when density is enough to launch.

### Step 3: Content (ongoing, works in 4-8 weeks)
- Post short, specific content where the segment already spends time: local subreddits, Facebook groups, Instagram and TikTok, Nextdoor and local Discords.
- Good topics: "How to make friends as an adult in [city]," "5 questions to ask on a first friend-date," and anonymized success stories.
- Every post ends with the quiz or waitlist link.
- Check each group's rules first. Some ban self-promotion, so ask admins or lead with value.

### Step 4: Partnerships (weeks 3-10)
- **Employers and HR:** Companies with many relocated staff want a retention perk. Offer a flat plan per employee.
- **Universities, alumni groups, new-resident programs, real estate agents and movers:** They meet people who just arrived and need friends. Offer a referral fee.
- **Venues:** Climbing gyms, cafes, breweries, board-game shops and run clubs can host events and send customers in exchange for foot traffic.
- **For the marriage and high-net-worth tiers:** Wealth advisors, family-office staff, private-club managers, executive coaches and divorce or estate attorneys meet buyers constantly. Offer each a referral fee and a confidentiality promise.

### Step 5: Referral loop (build in from day one)
- Give each member a personal invite link. "Bring a friend, you both get a free month" is simple and cheap.
- Ask for referrals at the moment of delight, right after a good meetup, through the post-meetup text.
- For marriage matching, one success story can bring 3-5 inquiries. Ask permission to share it anonymously.

### Step 6: Paid ads (only after the first 30-50 paying members)
- Use ads to scale something proven, not to find product-market fit.
- Run quiz or event-signup ads to a tight local audience on Instagram or Facebook, targeted by city, age and interest.
- Track cost per signup, cost per paying member and 90-day retention. If a member's lifetime value is $250 and acquisition costs $40, scale it.

### Database basics
- **Start simple:** Airtable, Notion or a spreadsheet plus a form tool. Move to a real database once there are a few hundred members.
- **Fields:** Name, contact, city, interests, skill level, availability, goal (friends, partner, marriage), source, status (lead, member, matched, active, churned) and consent.
- **Track the source of every signup** to see which channel works.
- **Use opt-in language** for texts and emails, and keep personal data secure. For the high-net-worth tier, use encrypted storage and strict access control.

### Milestones

| Stage | Goal | Timeframe |
|---|---|---|
| Waitlist | 100-200 signups | Weeks 1-4 |
| First paying members | 20-30 | Weeks 3-6 |
| Density check | 100+ active in one segment | Weeks 6-12 |
| First marriage-track clients | 3-5 | Weeks 4-10 |
| Referral share | 30%+ of new members | By month 3-4 |

### Common mistakes
- Launching in a whole country or to everyone, with no density anywhere.
- Running paid ads before the offer and matching quality are proven.
- Ignoring the scarce side of the pool.
- Collecting signups without making introductions. Match quickly, because early successes are the marketing.
