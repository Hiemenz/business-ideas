### 1. Batterer Intervention Program (BIP) Provider State Certification, Facilitator Curriculum-Hour & Court-Reporting Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Batterer intervention programs — the court-mandated, typically 26- to 52-week group programs a domestic-violence offender is ordered to complete as a condition of probation or a civil protective order — operate under a state probation department or domestic-violence-council program-approval framework specific to this service: California's Penal Code §1203.097 certified 52-week program standard, Texas's Battering Intervention and Prevention Program (BIPP) standards set by the Texas Council on Family Violence, Illinois's Partner Abuse Intervention Program (PAIP) certification through IDHS, and Florida's certified batterers' intervention program under Fla. Stat. §741.325 — each requiring an annual or biennial program-certification renewal tied to a specific curriculum (commonly a Duluth Model or Emerge-based curriculum), a documented facilitator-training-hour requirement per staff member, and ongoing facilitator supervision-hour logging. The single obligation with the sharpest consequence: every enrolled client's attendance has to be tracked session by session, and a client who misses a set number of sessions, or fails to complete the program by its court-ordered deadline, must be reported back to the referring court or probation officer within a fixed window — a program that lets a non-compliant client's status go unreported isn't just risking its own certification, it's leaving a judge or probation officer unaware that a domestic-violence offender has stopped attending a court-ordered program, a public-safety failure regulators and referring courts take seriously enough to pull a program's certification and referral pipeline entirely. A small BIP provider running several concurrent cohorts and a caseload of 40-120 active clients across multiple facilitators currently tracks program certification renewal, facilitator training-hour compliance, and per-client attendance-to-court-reporting on a program coordinator's spreadsheet and a stack of sign-in sheets, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific non-compliant client was actually reported to their referring court inside the required window.

**What to build:** A state program-certification and curriculum-training renewal tracker per facilitator; a per-cohort attendance log rolling up into a per-client completion-percentage view against the court-ordered deadline; and a non-compliance reporting tracker flagging any client who crosses the missed-session threshold, with a due-date countdown for the required report back to the referring court or probation officer and an archive of every report actually filed.

**Skill fit:** State BIP certification standards and each state's specific missed-session reporting threshold are public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-client, per-cohort trigger, and the non-compliance reporting gate is the same deterministic "flag it, don't just log it" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a court-ordered-program niche entirely distinct from the licensed clinical Substance Use Disorder Treatment Facility idea already in this repo, which dispenses controlled substances under a DEA registration and 42 CFR Part 2 confidentiality rules a BIP provider never touches at all.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the certification/training tracker, the per-cohort attendance log, and the non-compliance reporting tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small independent BIP providers.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — state domestic-violence coalition member directories (most states' coalition, such as the Texas Council on Family Violence or the California Partnership to End Domestic Violence, publish a roster of state-approved BIP providers) reach exactly the small providers currently reconciling attendance and court-reporting deadlines on a sign-in-sheet stack with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-399/mo base plus a per-facilitator add-on. Average client (4 facilitators) = $290/mo. 35-45 providers × $290/mo avg = **$10,150-13,050/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new cohort reopens the attendance-and-completion tracking obligation, every missed session reopens the non-compliance reporting clock, and every new facilitator a growing provider hires compounds the number of simultaneously tracked training-hour requirements — a continuous, caseload-driven compliance stream tied directly to court referral volume.

**Biggest risk:** A very small provider running a single cohort has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward providers running multiple concurrent cohorts across several facilitators and referring courts, where the cross-cohort reporting load genuinely outgrows a sign-in-sheet stack.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 providers ($4.4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 28 providers ($8.1K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 35-45 providers plus a court-facing completion-report export a program can generate the moment a probation officer requests a case update.

### 2. Court-Ordered DUI/DWI Education & Treatment Program Provider State License, Curriculum-Hour & Dual-Agency Completion-Reporting Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** State-licensed DUI/DWI education and intervention programs — the classroom and early-intervention coursework a first- or repeat-offense driver is ordered to complete before license reinstatement — operate under a state-specific licensing regime distinct from clinical addiction treatment: California DHCS-licensed DUI programs under Health & Safety Code Division 10.5, Chapter 9, requiring periodic site inspections and curriculum-hour documentation; Texas DPS-licensed DWI Education and DWI Intervention Programs under Education Code Chapter 1001; and Florida DHSMV-approved DUI programs under Fla. Stat. §322.292. Every program license renews on its own multi-year cycle contingent on a passing site inspection and current instructor certification, and every enrolled student's progress has to be tracked against the state-mandated curriculum-hour count for their specific offense tier (a first-offense student's required hours differ sharply from a repeat-offense student's). The obligation with the sharpest consequence is dual: on completion, the program must file a completion report with both the referring court and the state DMV or driver-license agency within a fixed window, since a license reinstatement is often gated directly on that DMV-side filing — a program that files late, or that reports a student complete without the full curriculum-hour count actually documented, is exactly the finding a state licensing-agency audit or a DMV cross-check is built to catch, and a pattern of reporting errors can suspend the program's referral eligibility entirely. A small program running several weekly class sections and a rolling enrollment of 60-200 active students currently tracks license renewal, per-student curriculum-hour completion, and dual court/DMV reporting on an office manager's spreadsheet and a folder of completion certificates, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific student's completion report was actually filed with both agencies before that student is marked closed.

**What to build:** A state program license and site-inspection renewal tracker with instructor-certification currency; a per-student curriculum-hour completion tracker split by offense tier; and a dual-filing completion-report tracker confirming a report was actually sent to both the referring court and the state DMV/driver-license agency within the required window, with an overdue-filing alert.

**Skill fit:** State DUI-program licensing statutes and their curriculum-hour-by-offense-tier requirements are public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-student trigger, and the dual-agency filing tracker is the same deterministic "confirm both records cleared" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an educational-and-early-intervention niche entirely distinct from the licensed clinical Substance Use Disorder Treatment Facility idea already in this repo, whose DEA-registration and 42 CFR Part 2 compliance core a classroom-based DUI program never holds at all.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the license-renewal tracker, the per-student curriculum-hour tracker, and the dual-agency completion-filing tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small independent DUI/DWI program providers.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — state-published lists of DHCS/DPS/DHSMV-licensed DUI program providers (every state maintains a public roster since courts and DMVs refer directly to it) reach exactly the small providers currently reconciling curriculum-hour tracking and dual-agency filing on a spreadsheet with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249-499/mo per program depending on weekly enrollment volume. 30-40 programs × $360/mo avg = **$10,800-14,400/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new enrollee reopens the curriculum-hour and dual-filing tracking obligation, the program license renewal recurs on its own multi-year inspection cycle, and every new weekly section a growing program adds compounds the number of simultaneously tracked completion deadlines — a continuous, enrollment-volume-driven compliance stream.

**Biggest risk:** A very small program running a single weekly class has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward programs running multiple weekly sections across a rolling caseload of 60+ active students, where the dual-agency filing load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 programs ($5.4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 26 programs ($9.4K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 30-40 programs plus a state-audit-ready completion-report export a program can produce the moment a licensing-agency site inspection arrives.

### 3. Air Charter Broker (Indirect Air Carrier) DOT Operator-Verification, Public-Charter Escrow & Disclosure Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent air charter brokers — who arrange private and group flights on behalf of clients without owning or operating any aircraft themselves — hold indirect-air-carrier economic authority under DOT regulation, which comes with two obligations most small brokerages track by memory rather than by system. First, a broker may never hold itself out as the operating air carrier: for every flight booked, the broker must verify the actual FAA Part 135-certificated operator's certificate and aircraft-insurance currency, and disclose to the client which certificated operator is actually performing the flight — a broker that books a client onto an operator whose certificate has lapsed, or that lets a client believe the broker itself is the carrier, is exactly the finding a DOT consumer-protection complaint investigation is built to catch. Second, any charter a broker markets and sells by individual seat to the general public — rather than chartering a whole aircraft to a single private group — falls under 14 CFR Part 380's public-charter rules, requiring a filed charter prospectus and passenger deposit funds held in a qualifying escrow account or bond until the flight is actually flown, since a broker that commingles or spends passenger deposit money before the flight occurs is committing exactly the violation Part 380's escrow requirement exists to prevent. A small brokerage arranging dozens of flights a month across a rotating roster of Part 135 operator partners currently tracks each operator's certificate/insurance currency and any public-charter escrow status on a booking agent's spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific booked flight's operator is currently verified and any public-charter deposit is properly escrowed before the booking is confirmed.

**What to build:** A per-operator-partner Part 135 certificate and aircraft-insurance currency tracker with a "cleared to book" gate; a per-booking client-disclosure record confirming the operating carrier was identified before the flight was confirmed; and a public-charter escrow/bond tracker for any charter sold by individual seat, gating a public-charter listing from going live until the prospectus filing and escrow account are both current.

**Skill fit:** DOT's Part 380 public-charter filing requirements and each Part 135 operator's certificate-currency status are structured, verifiable data with a concrete per-booking trigger, and the "cleared to book" gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a broker-side aviation-services niche entirely distinct from every FAA Part 135 charter-operator idea already in this repo, none of which involve a broker's own operator-verification-and-disclosure obligation or public-charter escrow requirement, since none of those ideas belong to a party that arranges flights without operating any aircraft of its own.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the operator-verification tracker, the per-booking disclosure record, and the public-charter escrow tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small independent air charter brokerages.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — the National Air Transportation Association's (NATA) air charter broker membership and the Air Charter Association's member directory reach exactly the small brokerages currently reconciling operator-certificate verification on a spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per brokerage depending on monthly booking volume. 22-30 brokerages × $420/mo avg = **$9,240-12,600/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every booking reopens the operator-verification-and-disclosure check from scratch, every publicly sold charter reopens the escrow/prospectus tracking obligation, and every new operator-partner relationship a growing brokerage adds compounds the number of certificates it has to keep current — a continuous, booking-volume-driven compliance stream.

**Biggest risk:** A broker working exclusively private whole-aircraft charters for a small client roster has no Part 380 public-charter exposure at all. Mitigate by leading with the operator-verification-and-disclosure tracker as the baseline value proposition for every brokerage, and treating the public-charter escrow module as a premium tier for brokers who do sell individual-seat charters.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10 brokerages ($4.2K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 18 brokerages ($7.6K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 22-30 brokerages plus a client-facing operator-verification certificate a brokerage can hand a corporate client's travel-risk-management team.

### 4. Pet Insurance Managing General Agent (MGA) State Appointment, Rate-Filing & Pet Insurance Model Act Disclosure Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Pet insurance managing general agents — companies that underwrite and administer pet insurance policies on behalf of an insurance-carrier partner rather than carrying the risk themselves — must hold a state Department of Insurance managing-general-agent appointment in every state they sell in, built on the NAIC Managing General Agents Act model, with each state's appointment renewing on its own cycle and requiring a current written agreement with the underwriting carrier on file. Every policy form the MGA sells requires its own state-by-state rate-and-form filing (some states require prior approval, others a file-and-use submission), and — since the NAIC's Pet Insurance Model Act began state-by-state adoption in 2022 — an MGA operating in an adopting state must issue a specific pre-sale waiting-period disclosure, a clearly defined pre-existing-condition exclusion notice, and, critically, a multi-year premium-increase history disclosure at every policy renewal, since the model act's renewal-disclosure requirement is the specific provision regulators use to catch an MGA quietly compounding rate increases without the transparency the act mandates. An MGA whose state appointment lapses in even one state, or whose policy form is sold without the required Model Act disclosures attached, exposes every policy sold under that gap to a state market-conduct exam finding and a potential unauthorized-insurance-practice violation. A small regional pet insurance MGA selling through several hundred independent veterinary-clinic and online-affiliate partners across a growing list of states currently tracks per-state appointment renewal, rate/form filing status, and per-policy Model Act disclosure attachment on a compliance officer's spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific state's appointment and filed rate form are both current before a new policy is sold there.

**What to build:** A per-state MGA appointment and carrier-agreement currency tracker; a per-state, per-form rate/form filing status tracker; a Pet Insurance Model Act disclosure-attachment checklist gating a policy issuance until the waiting-period, pre-existing-condition, and renewal premium-history disclosures are all confirmed attached; and a state-launch readiness gate blocking new policy sales in a state until appointment and filed rate form are both current.

**Skill fit:** State MGA-appointment statutes and the NAIC's published Pet Insurance Model Act text are public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-state and per-policy trigger, and the state-launch readiness gate is the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a pet-insurance-underwriting niche entirely distinct from the veterinary-practice billing and accounts-receivable-recovery ideas already in this repo, neither of which involves an MGA's own state appointment, rate filing, or Model Act disclosure obligations at all.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the state appointment tracker, the rate/form filing tracker, and the Model Act disclosure checklist. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small regional pet insurance MGAs.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — the North American Pet Health Insurance Association's (NAPHIA) member directory reaches exactly the small regional MGAs currently reconciling multi-state appointment and disclosure compliance on a spreadsheet with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $399-799/mo per MGA given the multi-state appointment and rate-filing stakes involved. 18-25 MGAs × $550/mo avg = **$9,900-13,750/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new state entered adds a fresh appointment-and-filing obligation, every policy issued reopens the Model Act disclosure check, and every policy renewal reopens the multi-year premium-history disclosure requirement — a continuous, state-footprint-and-policy-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the MGA's own growth.

**Biggest risk:** Pet insurance is a smaller, more concentrated market than most industries in this series, with a modest total number of independent MGAs nationally. Mitigate by pricing to reflect the real multi-state compliance stakes and by expanding into the growing list of Model Act-adopting states as a built-in growth driver.

**Growth path:** Side project serving 6-8 small MGAs → NAPHIA channel presence → 18-25 MGAs replaces-full-time-salary plus a carrier-facing compliance-audit export an MGA can hand its underwriting partner ahead of an annual program review.

### 5. Amphibious "Duck Boat" Tour Operator Dual USCG Certificate of Inspection & State Road-Vehicle Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Amphibious "duck boat" tour operators run a vehicle class that is simultaneously a vessel and a road vehicle, and has to satisfy two independent regulators at once. On the water side, following a series of fatal accidents, the U.S. Coast Guard now requires every amphibious passenger vessel to hold a current Certificate of Inspection (COI) under 46 CFR Subchapter T, incorporating post-2018 amphibious-specific reserve-buoyancy and canopy-removal or reconfiguration standards adopted specifically in response to those accidents, plus ongoing USCG marine-casualty reporting under 46 CFR Part 4 for any on-water incident, and the vessel's operator must hold a current USCG Merchant Mariner Credential appropriate to the route. On the road side, the same vehicle must separately carry a current state DMV/highway commercial-vehicle registration and pass a state safety inspection as a motor vehicle, and its driver must hold a state commercial driver's license valid for the vehicle class — meaning a single duck boat's compliance record depends on two entirely independent certifications staying simultaneously current, and a vessel dispatched with a lapsed COI, an unreported prior incident, or an expired state vehicle inspection is exactly the finding a USCG spot inspection or a state highway-patrol stop is built to catch, with either regulator able to ground the vehicle on the spot. A small operator running a fleet of 3-10 amphibious vehicles across a tourist season currently tracks each vehicle's COI renewal, state vehicle-registration/inspection status, and each operator's dual water-and-road credentials on a dispatcher's paper log, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific vehicle scheduled for today's tour is currently cleared on both its maritime and its road-vehicle certification before it leaves the dock.

**What to build:** A per-vehicle USCG Certificate of Inspection renewal tracker incorporating the post-2018 amphibious-specific inspection items; a per-vehicle state DMV registration and safety-inspection tracker; a per-operator dual-credential tracker (Merchant Mariner Credential plus state CDL); and a "cleared for today's tour" dispatch gate cross-referencing all three records before a specific vehicle and operator combination is confirmed for departure.

**Skill fit:** USCG's published Subchapter T inspection standards and each state's commercial-vehicle inspection requirements are public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-vehicle, per-operator trigger, and the dispatch gate is the same deterministic "block the departure until every record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a genuinely unique dual-regulator, dual-mode vehicle compliance surface that exists nowhere else in this repo.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the COI tracker, the state vehicle-registration/inspection tracker, and the dual-credential dispatch gate. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent amphibious tour operators.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — the Passenger Vessel Association's (PVA) member directory, which includes the amphibious-tour-operator segment specifically, reaches exactly the small operators currently reconciling dual maritime-and-road certification on a dispatcher's paper log with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249-499/mo per operator depending on fleet size. 20-28 operators × $360/mo avg = **$7,200-10,080/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every vehicle's COI and state inspection each recur on their own independent renewal clock, every operator's dual credentials renew on separate cycles, and every day's dispatch reopens the cleared-for-departure cross-check — a continuous, fleet-and-season-driven compliance stream.

**Biggest risk:** The amphibious tour-operator niche is genuinely small — only a few dozen operators run this vehicle class nationally following post-accident fleet reductions. Mitigate by pricing at a premium reflecting the real dual-regulator grounding risk, and by treating the dispatch-gate mechanic as directly reusable for any future operator running a mixed land/water fleet.

**Growth path:** Side project serving 5-6 operators → PVA channel presence → 20-28 operators replaces-part-time-to-full-time income, supplemented by a per-vehicle inspection-readiness export an operator can produce the moment a USCG or state inspector arrives dockside.
