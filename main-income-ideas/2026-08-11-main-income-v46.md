### 1. Home Health Agency Medicare Certification, OASIS iQIES Compliance & State HHA Licensure
**Problem:** Medicare-certified home health agencies (HHAs) providing skilled nursing, physical therapy, occupational therapy, and home health aide services to homebound Medicare beneficiaries operate under a multi-layer compliance framework administered simultaneously by CMS, state survey agencies, and state health departments. State HHA licensure: home health agencies must hold a state home health agency license issued by the state department of health, with annual renewal, a minimum administrator qualification requirement (most states require the HHA administrator to meet specific education and experience criteria), and a defined geographic service area. OASIS iQIES submission deadlines: for every Medicare-eligible patient, the HHA must complete and submit to CMS through iQIES (the internet Quality Improvement and Evaluation System) an OASIS outcome assessment at each clinically significant event — Start of Care (within 5 calendar days of the first skilled visit), Resumption of Care after a hospitalization (within 2 calendar days), and Discharge (within 5 calendar days of the final skilled visit). Late or missing OASIS submissions generate CMS iQIES warnings that affect the agency's HHCAHPS quality reporting and can trigger CMS focused review. CMS Conditions of Participation survey readiness: CMS requires Medicare-certified HHAs to comply with the Home Health Conditions of Participation (42 CFR Part 484), and state survey agencies conduct recertification surveys (typically every 36 months) to assess CoP compliance. Any deficiencies cited during a CMS survey must be remediated within the required plan of correction timeframe (typically 45 days for standard deficiencies). Small to mid-size HHAs (50-300 active patients) managing OASIS submission calendars for every patient episode, state license renewal, and CMS CoP documentation without a dedicated compliance coordinator miss OASIS deadlines routinely — late submissions are tracked and reported in the HHA's iQIES dashboard and visible to referral sources such as hospital discharge planners.
**What to build:** A home health agency Medicare compliance and state licensure management service — a state HHA license compliance calendar (tracking the agency's state HHA license number, annual renewal deadline, administrator qualification documentation, geographic service area attestation, and any state survey scheduling obligations — with tiered renewal alerts), an OASIS iQIES submission deadline tracker (for each active patient episode: patient name, Medicare beneficiary identifier, Start of Care date, the 5-calendar-day OASIS SOC submission deadline and submission confirmation date from iQIES, any Resumption of Care events with 2-calendar-day submission deadlines, and the anticipated discharge date with the 5-calendar-day discharge OASIS deadline — with a daily alert list showing every patient episode with an OASIS submission due within the next 3 business days that has not yet been confirmed as submitted), an iQIES submission status reconciliation (a weekly pull of the agency's iQIES dashboard data comparing expected submissions against confirmed submissions to identify any missing or rejected OASIS records before they age into a reportable late submission), a CMS Conditions of Participation documentation tracker (for each CoP domain: the relevant regulation reference, the agency's current compliance documentation — policies, procedures, personnel records, patient rights disclosures — and the last internal audit date confirming current compliance, generating a survey-readiness gap list ahead of any state survey), and a plan of correction management system (for any deficiency cited during a CMS recertification survey: deficiency citation number and CoP reference, required plan of correction elements, plan of correction submission deadline, implementation milestones, and corrective action documentation status — tracking that each cited deficiency is remediated within the required timeframe and documented for CMS review).
**Skill fit:** OASIS data set requirements and iQIES submission timelines are documented in CMS's OASIS Guidance Manual and iQIES User Guide. State HHA licensure requirements are available from each state's department of health home care licensing program. CMS CoP requirements are codified in 42 CFR Part 484. Building the state license calendar, OASIS submission deadline tracker, iQIES reconciliation, CoP documentation tracker, and plan of correction management system are data management problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. NAHC (National Association for Home Care and Hospice) and state home care association member networks provide defined buyer communities.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a state HHA license compliance calendar for the 25 highest-HHA-density states, an OASIS iQIES submission deadline tracker with patient episode management and daily alert generation for SOC, ROC, and discharge OASIS deadlines, a CMS CoP documentation tracker with the 2023 CoP domains organized as a compliance checklist, and a plan of correction management system; first 5-8 HHA clients through NAHC member outreach while the OASIS deadline tracker is validated against real agency patient episode volumes.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through NAHC (National Association for Home Care and Hospice) member network and state home care association outreach (the concentrated professional communities for HHA owners and administrators — NAHC's annual conference is the primary industry event), CMS accreditation organization referral networks (ACHC and The Joint Commission, which provide home health accreditation as a CoP survey alternative, actively refer compliance management tools to their HHA clients during accreditation onboarding), and hospital discharge planning department referral networks (hospital social workers and discharge planners who refer patients to HHAs track OASIS-based quality measures and actively route referrals to HHAs with documented iQIES compliance performance — creating a business development incentive for HHAs to maintain clean iQIES submission records).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per HHA: $350-800/mo (depending on active patient census and state regulatory complexity — a 75-patient census HHA in a single state: $350/mo; a 200-patient census HHA operating across 2 state license jurisdictions with active CMS CoP documentation management: $800/mo). Managing 20-35 HHA retainers at $550/mo average = **$11,000-19,250/mo**. **Why this can go beyond side money:** OASIS submission deadlines are generated by every patient start of care, every hospitalization-triggered resumption, and every discharge — a 100-patient census HHA with normal turnover generates 8-15 OASIS deadline events per week continuously. State HHA license renewal is annual. CoP documentation must be maintained and updated continuously as policies, personnel, and procedures change. A new administrator hire triggers administrator qualification documentation verification. Every new state in which the agency seeks to operate triggers a new state HHA license application. CMS surveys occur every 36 months on a rolling schedule — preparation begins 90 days before the anticipated survey window.

A lower star rating directly reduces referral volume from hospitals, the primary patient referral source for most HHAs. ### 2. DMEPOS Supplier Medicare PECOS Enrollment, Accreditation Renewal & Surety Bond Compliance
**Problem:** Durable medical equipment, prosthetics, orthotics, and supplies (DMEPOS) suppliers billing Medicare Part B — businesses providing wheelchairs, oxygen equipment, CPAP/BiPAP devices, prosthetic limbs, custom orthotics, diabetic supplies, and hospital beds to Medicare beneficiaries — must maintain three simultaneous CMS enrollment requirements that each have distinct renewal cycles and can independently trigger Medicare billing suspension if allowed to lapse. Medicare PECOS enrollment: DMEPOS suppliers must be enrolled in Medicare through PECOS (Provider Enrollment, Chain and Ownership System), with revalidation required every 3-5 years (CMS sends revalidation notices approximately 6 months before the due date, but suppliers who miss the revalidation window have their Medicare billing privileges deactivated). DMEPOS accreditation: CMS requires all DMEPOS suppliers (with limited exceptions for pharmacies and certain practitioners) to be accredited by a CMS-approved accreditation organization — the primary organizations are the Accreditation Commission for Health Care (ACHC), The Joint Commission, and the Board for Orthotist/Prosthetist Certification (BOC) for O&P suppliers. Accreditation surveys are conducted on a triennial (3-year) cycle, with an interim accreditation status review at 18 months. Suppliers must apply for accreditation renewal 6 months before the accreditation expiration date to ensure continuous accreditation coverage. CMS surety bond requirement: every enrolled DMEPOS supplier must maintain a $50,000 surety bond per enrolled location (physical address where DMEPOS is dispensed), payable to the Secretary of Health and Human Services, from a CMS-accepted surety bond company — and must notify CMS within 30 days of any change in surety bond carrier or bond cancellation. A lapsed surety bond results in automatic Medicare billing privilege revocation. Small DMEPOS suppliers (1-5 locations, serving 200-600 Medicare patients) managing PECOS revalidation cycles, accreditation renewal timelines, and per-location surety bond renewals with informal calendar reminders miss revalidation deadlines more frequently than any other Medicare supplier category — CMS deactivation notices to DMEPOS suppliers are disproportionately high relative to other Medicare-enrolled provider types.
**What to build:** A DMEPOS supplier Medicare enrollment, accreditation, and surety bond compliance management service — a Medicare PECOS enrollment tracker (for each enrolled DMEPOS supplier location: NPI number, Medicare supplier number (PTAN), enrolled location address, PECOS enrollment status, revalidation due date and the 6-month notice receipt date, and any open PECOS change of information requests — with a tiered alert beginning 9 months before the revalidation due date), a DMEPOS accreditation management system (for each accredited supplier location: accreditation organization, accreditation certificate number, accreditation expiration date, the 6-month-before-expiration accreditation renewal application deadline, the 18-month interim status review date, any corrective action plans issued during the last survey with remediation status, and the next survey scheduling status — with tiered alerts beginning 8 months before accreditation expiration), a surety bond compliance tracker (for each enrolled DMEPOS location: surety bond carrier name, bond number, bond amount — $50,000 per location — bond expiration date, bond renewal premium payment date, and the 30-day CMS notification obligation if the bond is changed or cancelled — with alerts beginning 90 days before bond expiration), a product category authorization tracker (DMEPOS suppliers must be enrolled and accredited for specific DMEPOS product categories — a supplier accredited for standard power wheelchairs cannot bill for custom power wheelchairs without the appropriate accreditation category; tracking the supplier's Medicare-authorized HCPCS product categories against the products they dispense prevents inadvertent billing for unauthorized product categories), and a Medicare billing suspension risk dashboard (a consolidated view of all PECOS revalidation dates, accreditation expiration dates, and surety bond expiration dates across all enrolled locations — ranked by days until expiration — giving the owner or compliance officer a single-screen view of the supplier's aggregate Medicare billing risk from enrollment compliance gaps).
**Skill fit:** DMEPOS Medicare enrollment requirements, accreditation standards, and surety bond requirements are documented in CMS's DMEPOS Supplier Standards (42 CFR Part 424, Subpart F), CMS Pub. 100-08 Medicare Program Integrity Manual, and each accreditation organization's DMEPOS standards guide. Building the PECOS revalidation tracker, accreditation renewal management system, surety bond tracker, product category authorization tracker, and billing suspension risk dashboard are data management problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. AAHomecare (American Association for Homecare) and AOPA (American Orthotic and Prosthetic Association) member networks provide defined buyer communities.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a DMEPOS Medicare PECOS enrollment tracker with revalidation cycle management, a DMEPOS accreditation management system with ACHC and Joint Commission survey cycle tracking, a surety bond compliance tracker with per-location bond management, a product category authorization tracker for the top 20 DMEPOS billing categories by Medicare volume, and a billing suspension risk dashboard; first 5-8 DMEPOS supplier clients through AAHomecare member outreach while the PECOS revalidation tracker and accreditation renewal system are validated against real supplier enrollment histories.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through AAHomecare (American Association for Homecare) member network and VGM Group member outreach (VGM is the largest DMEPOS buying group, serving 4,500+ member DMEPOS companies — VGM's annual Heartland conference is the primary industry event and VGM's member communications are the highest-reach channel in the DMEPOS industry), ACHC and Joint Commission DMEPOS accreditation client referral networks (accreditation organizations who work directly with DMEPOS suppliers on survey preparation see revalidation and bond compliance gaps at every accreditation renewal and refer compliance management tools to suppliers in their accreditation client portfolio), and Medicare billing software vendor referral networks (DMEPOS billing software vendors whose customers include thousands of DMEPOS suppliers see Medicare billing suspension events in their customer base and refer compliance management tools to suppliers with documented revalidation or accreditation gaps).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per DMEPOS supplier: $250-600/mo (depending on location count, accreditation complexity, and product category breadth — a 1-location oxygen and mobility equipment supplier: $250/mo; a 4-location supplier dispensing complex rehab power wheelchairs, custom orthotics, and prosthetics with multiple accreditation categories and per-location bonds: $600/mo). Managing 30-50 supplier retainers at $375/mo average = **$11,250-18,750/mo**. Medicare reactivation assistance packages (one-time $400-700 for DMEPOS suppliers whose Medicare billing was deactivated due to a missed revalidation and who need expedited reactivation coordination with CMS) generate event-driven supplemental revenue. Reaching 30-50 retainers requires engagement with VGM Group member communications and 3-4 ACHC/Joint Commission referral relationships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** PECOS revalidation cycles are 3-5 years per location but staggered — a 3-location supplier may have revalidation due dates distributed across different years. Accreditation renewal is triennial with an 18-month interim review. Surety bond renewal is annual for every enrolled location. ### 3. Mental Health Telehealth Provider Multi-State License, PSYPACT Compact & CAQH Credentialing Compliance
**Problem:** Licensed clinical social workers (LCSWs), licensed professional counselors (LPCs), licensed marriage and family therapists (LMFTs), and licensed psychologists operating telehealth private practices — serving clients in multiple states via video session platforms — face a multi-layer license and credentialing compliance burden that has grown significantly as telehealth client bases expanded across state lines following the 2020 telehealth expansion. Multi-state individual license compliance: therapists must hold an individual state license in every state where a client is physically located at the time of the telehealth session — not the therapist's home state. A therapist with clients in 8 states must maintain 8 active state licenses, each with state-specific CE requirements (most states require 20-40 CE hours per 2-year renewal cycle, with specific ethics CE hour minimums), independent renewal dates, and in some states, supervision hour requirements for license renewal at higher tiers. PSYPACT interstate compact participation: 42 states have joined PSYPACT (the Psychology Interjurisdictional Compact) as of 2025, allowing licensed psychologists who qualify for an E.Passport to practice in all PSYPACT member states without obtaining individual state licenses in each state — but qualifying for and maintaining the E.Passport requires tracking separately from individual licenses, and therapists must monitor new states joining the compact. CAQH ProView credentialing: therapists who bill insurance panels must maintain a current CAQH ProView profile — the primary credentialing data repository used by most major health plans for initial credentialing and re-credentialing. CAQH requires attestation every 120 days; an expired CAQH attestation suspends the therapist's profile and can delay insurance credentialing, blocking the ability to see new insurance-paying patients. Re-credentialing: most health plans require therapists to re-credential every 2-3 years; missing a re-credentialing notice results in removal from the panel and loss of in-network billing status for existing patients.
**What to build:** A telehealth therapist multi-state license and credentialing compliance management service — a multi-state individual license portfolio (for each state where the therapist holds a license: license number, license type, expiration date, CE hours required per renewal cycle with any ethics CE minimum, CE hours completed to date with provider and course name, and projected CE completion status — with tiered renewal alerts for the license expiration and a CE completion projection alert when the therapist is on pace to fall short), a PSYPACT E.Passport tracker (for licensed psychologist clients: E.Passport number, issuance date, PSYPACT renewal requirements, the current list of PSYPACT member states, and an alert when a state the therapist serves joins PSYPACT — enabling the therapist to drop an individual state license in favor of PSYPACT coverage and reduce individual license maintenance costs), a CAQH ProView attestation calendar (tracking the therapist's CAQH ProView ID, the 120-day attestation due date calculated from the most recent attestation, any data elements in the CAQH profile that are approaching expiration — malpractice insurance certificate, state license, DEA registration — and the attestation submission confirmation — with a 14-day and 3-day pre-attestation alert), a health plan panel credentialing and re-credentialing tracker (for each insurance panel the therapist is credentialed with: health plan name, credentialing effective date, re-credentialing cycle, anticipated re-credentialing application deadline, re-credentialing submission date, and panel status confirmation — with alerts 90 days before re-credentialing deadlines), and a new state license application tracker (for therapists seeking to add a new state license to expand their telehealth service area: application submission date, state licensing board, documents submitted, application status, and anticipated license issuance date — tracking the application through to approval).
**Skill fit:** State LCSW, LPC, LMFT, and psychologist licensing requirements and CE requirements are available from each state's professional licensing board. PSYPACT E.Passport requirements are documented on the PSYPACT website. CAQH ProView attestation requirements are documented in CAQH's provider guide. Health plan re-credentialing cycles are documented in each plan's provider manual. Building the multi-state license portfolio, PSYPACT tracker, CAQH attestation calendar, and panel credentialing tracker are data management problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. NASW (National Association of Social Workers), ACA (American Counseling Association), AAMFT (American Association for Marriage and Family Therapy), and APA (American Psychological Association) member networks provide defined buyer communities.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a multi-state therapist license portfolio for all 50 states and DC with license-type-specific CE requirements (LCSW, LPC, LMFT, psychologist), a PSYPACT E.Passport tracker with current member state list, a CAQH ProView attestation calendar with 120-day cycle management, and a health plan panel credentialing and re-credentialing tracker; first 5-8 telehealth therapist clients through NASW or ACA member outreach while the multi-state license portfolio and CAQH attestation calendar are validated against real therapist license situations.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through NASW (National Association of Social Workers), ACA, AAMFT, and APA member networks and continuing education provider communities (the professional associations for each therapist license type each maintain member communities with direct communication channels — CE providers who sell CE courses to therapists see license renewal tracking failures at every course enrollment and refer compliance management tools to their therapist customer bases), telehealth platform referral networks (SimplePractice, TherapyNotes, and TheraNest — the primary telehealth EHR and billing platforms for private practice therapists — have partner ecosystems and in-app resource directories where compliance tools are actively promoted to their therapist customer bases), and therapist Facebook group and Reddit community outreach (r/therapists, r/socialwork, and platform-specific Facebook groups for SimplePractice users are the primary peer communities where telehealth therapists share compliance questions and vendor recommendations — high concentration of the target buyer in a reachable peer community).

### 4. FDA Food Facility Registration, FSMA Preventive Controls Compliance & State Food Processor License
**Problem:** Food manufacturers, co-packers, specialty food producers, and commercial food processors — businesses that manufacture, process, pack, or hold food intended for human consumption in interstate commerce — face a multi-agency compliance framework administered by FDA at the federal level and by state departments of agriculture at the state level. FDA food facility registration: under the Bioterrorism Act and FSMA, food facilities (with limited exemptions for farms and retail food establishments) must register with FDA as food facilities, and must renew that registration biennially during the October 1 - December 31 registration renewal window in even-numbered years. Facilities that fail to renew their FDA registration during the biennial window are considered unregistered — a condition that can trigger FDA import alert or facility suspension actions. FSMA Preventive Controls for Human Food: food manufacturers covered by the Preventive Controls for Human Food rule (21 CFR Part 117) must maintain a written food safety plan that includes a hazard analysis identifying known or reasonably foreseeable biological, chemical, and physical hazards; preventive controls to significantly minimize or prevent each identified hazard; monitoring procedures for each preventive control; corrective action procedures; verification activities; and a supply chain program for ingredients with hazards controlled by the supplier. The food safety plan must be reviewed and updated at least every 3 years and reanalyzed whenever a significant change in the facility's activities creates the potential for new hazards. A Preventive Controls Qualified Individual (PCQI) must oversee the food safety plan — a requirement that small manufacturers frequently satisfy through a one-time PCQI training course without maintaining ongoing food safety plan update procedures. State food processor license: most states require food manufacturers to hold a state food manufacturer, food processor, or cottage food license issued by the state department of agriculture, with annual renewal, and annual or biennial state food facility inspections by state agriculture inspectors. License lapses or inspection deficiencies generate cease-and-desist orders from state agriculture departments.
**What to build:** An FDA food facility registration, FSMA food safety plan, and state food processor license compliance management service — an FDA food facility registration calendar (tracking the facility's FDA food facility registration number, the biennial registration renewal window — October 1 to December 31 of every even-numbered year — and any facility changes that require registration updates — change in facility name, address, owner, or emergency contact — with alerts beginning September 1 of each even-numbered year to initiate the biennial renewal process), a FSMA food safety plan status tracker (for each facility's food safety plan: date of most recent food safety plan review, date of most recent full reanalysis — required every 3 years — and any significant facility changes since the last review that may require triggered reanalysis — new product lines, new ingredients, new processing equipment, facility expansion — with alerts when the 3-year reanalysis deadline approaches and when any logged facility change is flagged as potentially triggering a reanalysis), a preventive control monitoring log (for each identified preventive control: the control type — process, allergen, sanitation, or supply chain — the monitoring frequency, the monitoring record template with required data fields, and the corrective action record for any monitoring deviation — with a monitoring log completion gate confirming all required monitoring records are entered for each production shift), a state food processor license compliance calendar (for each operating state: state food processor license number, annual renewal deadline, scheduled state food facility inspection date and most recent inspection result, any outstanding inspection deficiencies with required corrective action timeline, and the corrective action documentation status — with tiered renewal alerts), and a supplier food safety documentation tracker (for the facility's supply chain program: for each ingredient with a hazard controlled by the supplier, the supplier approval documentation — supplier food safety plan, audit results, COA review — the supplier approval status, and the re-approval due date based on the supply chain program's review frequency).
**Skill fit:** FDA food facility registration requirements are documented in 21 CFR Part 1, Subpart H. FSMA Preventive Controls for Human Food requirements are codified in 21 CFR Part 117 and extensively documented in FDA's guidance documents and the FSPCA (Food Safety Preventive Controls Alliance) PCQI training curriculum. State food processor licensing requirements are available from each state's department of agriculture. Building the FDA registration calendar, FSMA food safety plan status tracker, preventive control monitoring log, state license calendar, and supplier documentation tracker are data management and workflow problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. SNAC International (Snack Food Association), IFT (Institute of Food Technologists), and the Specialty Food Association member networks provide defined buyer communities.

### 5. Temporary Staffing Agency State Employment Agency License, Wage Bond & UI Experience Rate Management
**Problem:** Temporary staffing agencies — businesses that recruit, hire, and deploy temporary workers to client companies — face a multi-layer state compliance framework that is more complex than the compliance burden facing most other service businesses because staffing agencies are simultaneously the employer of record for every temporary worker and an unrelated business providing labor services to their client companies. State employment agency licensing: 16+ states require temporary staffing agencies (also called employment agencies or labor contractors in different state statutes) to hold state-issued employment agency licenses or temporary staffing agency registrations, with annual renewal, a designated responsible party, and in most states a surety bond or cash deposit guaranteeing wage payment to placed workers — bond amounts typically range from $10,000 to $50,000 per state. Agencies operating without the required license face state labor department enforcement action, injunctions, and per-day civil penalties. Wage and welfare bond compliance: beyond state employment agency license bonds, some states require separate wage bonds or wage guarantee bonds — bonds specifically protecting temporary workers from unpaid wages if the staffing agency fails to pay — with annual renewal by a state-approved surety bond carrier and prompt notification to the state if the bond is cancelled or reduced. State unemployment insurance (UI) experience rating: staffing agencies are particularly vulnerable to UI cost management failures because temporary worker turnover is inherently high — when temporary assignments end, workers frequently file for unemployment benefits, charging those claims to the staffing agency's UI account. UI contribution rates are experience-rated based on the staffing agency's claim history: a staffing agency with a high ratio of UI benefit charges to taxable payroll in a state will receive a high UI contribution rate in that state — sometimes 8-10% of taxable wages, compared to 1-3% for agencies that actively manage their experience rating. UI rate management requires tracking benefit charge notices promptly (disputing ineligible claims within the state's appeal window — typically 10-30 days), correctly classifying temporary worker separations, and ensuring that workers hired away from temporary assignments by the client company are properly processed as direct hires to avoid incorrect UI charge attribution.
**What to build:** A temporary staffing agency state license, wage bond, and UI experience rate compliance management service — a multi-state employment agency license portfolio (for each operating state: state employment agency license number, annual renewal deadline, responsible party designation, surety bond or cash deposit requirement — carrier, bond amount, and bond expiration date — and any state-specific reporting requirements such as annual payroll disclosure or placed worker count reporting — with tiered renewal alerts for each license and each surety bond), a wage bond compliance tracker (for states with separate wage bond requirements: wage bond carrier, bond number, bond amount, bond expiration date, and the state notification obligation for any bond cancellation or reduction — with 90-day and 30-day pre-expiration alerts), a UI benefit charge monitoring system (for each state where the agency has placed temporary workers: state UI account number, current UI contribution rate, most recent rate notice date and rate determination, a log of individual benefit charge notices received from the state UI agency with the claimant name, claim effective date, weekly benefit amount charged, and the agency's protest decision — whether the claim is eligible and properly charged or protestable — with a protest deadline tracker for each charge notice showing the state's appeal window), a UI protest filing tracker (for each benefit charge protest submitted: claimant name, protest submission date and confirmation, hearing date if applicable, protest outcome, and the benefit charge adjustment confirmation — maintaining a documented protest history to demonstrate the agency's active experience rate management), and a UI experience rate projection tool (for each state: the current UI taxable wage base, the current UI contribution rate, the year-to-date benefit charges against the agency's account, the projected year-end benefit charge ratio, and the projected contribution rate for the next rate year — enabling the agency to forecast UI cost exposure and make assignment management decisions that reduce high-charge-rate temporary worker terminations ahead of rate determination).
**Skill fit:** State employment agency licensing requirements and wage bond requirements are available from each state's department of labor employment services division. State UI experience rating systems and benefit charge protest procedures are documented in each state's UI employer handbook. Building the multi-state license portfolio, wage bond tracker, UI benefit charge monitoring system, protest filing tracker, and experience rate projection tool are data management and analytics problems the builder's engineering and data analytics skills address directly. ASA (American Staffing Association) member network provides the primary buyer community.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a multi-state employment agency license portfolio for the 16+ states with employment agency licensing requirements plus key non-licensing states for UI management, a wage bond compliance tracker, a UI benefit charge monitoring system with protest deadline tracking for the 20 highest-UI-cost states, a UI protest filing tracker, and a UI experience rate projection tool; first 5-8 staffing agency clients through ASA member outreach while the UI benefit charge monitoring system and experience rate projection tool are validated against real agency UI account histories.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through ASA (American Staffing Association) member network and staffing industry analyst community outreach (ASA's Staffing World annual conference is the primary industry event; Staffing Industry Analysts — SIA — publishes the primary trade research and maintains a vendor directory that staffing agency operators consult for compliance tools), state workers' compensation insurance carrier referral networks (workers' comp carriers who write staffing industry policies have direct insight into UI cost management challenges facing their staffing agency customers and refer UI management tools to agencies with high experience-rated premium histories), and staffing agency payroll processing company referral networks (ADP, Paychex, and staffing-specific payroll providers like Staffmark and TempWorks see UI benefit charge management failures in their staffing agency customer base and refer compliance tools through their partner marketplace programs).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per staffing agency: $300-800/mo (depending on state license footprint, temporary worker headcount, and UI activity volume — a 50-temporary-worker agency operating in 2 states: $300/mo; a 200-temporary-worker agency operating in 6 states with high-volume UI benefit charge activity requiring active monthly protest management: $800/mo). Managing 20-35 agency retainers at $525/mo average = **$10,500-18,375/mo**. UI protest filing assistance packages (one-time $200-400 for agencies that receive a large batch of UI benefit charge notices following a client layoff event and need rapid protest filing support for all protestable claims) generate high-value event-driven supplemental revenue. Surety bonds and wage bonds renew annually independently of license renewal dates.
