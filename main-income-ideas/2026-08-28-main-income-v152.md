### 1. Independent Audiology Practice State Board Licensure, Medicare Order/Referral Documentation & EHDI Newborn-Screening Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent audiologists hold state audiology board licensure — commonly under a combined Speech-Language Pathology and Audiology Board, but a distinct license category from both SLP and hearing-instrument-specialist/dispenser licensure — renewed on a biennial cycle requiring CE hours (commonly 20-30 hours per cycle) plus a state-mandated ethics component, alongside ASHA's Certificate of Clinical Competence in Audiology (CCC-A), maintained on its own three-year CE cycle independent of the state license. Traditional Medicare will not reimburse a diagnostic audiometric or vestibular-testing claim without a physician order or referral documented on file before the test is performed, and a claim billed without that order on record is exactly the finding a Medicare Administrative Contractor's post-payment audit is built to catch, triggering recoupment. Audiology practices that provide newborn hearing screening and diagnostic follow-up additionally report into a state Early Hearing Detection and Intervention (EHDI) program tracking system against the national 1-3-6 benchmark — screening completed by 1 month of age, diagnostic evaluation by 3 months, and early-intervention referral by 6 months — and a practice that misses an EHDI reporting deadline is exactly the gap a state EHDI coordinator's periodic data-quality review targets, risking removal from the state's referral network. Practices that program cochlear implants or other active hearing devices must also hold and renew manufacturer-specific certification (Cochlear, MED-EL, Advanced Bionics), each on its own separate cycle. A solo or small-group audiology practice currently tracks state license/CCC-A CE hours, per-patient physician-order documentation, and EHDI reporting deadlines on a front-office spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific diagnostic claim has an order on file, or a specific newborn's screening result was actually reported to the state before the 1-3-6 clock runs out.

**What to build:** A state license and CCC-A dual-CE-cycle tracker; a per-patient physician-order/referral documentation gate blocking a diagnostic claim from being marked billable until the order is confirmed on file; an EHDI newborn-screening and diagnostic-follow-up reporting-deadline tracker computing each infant's 1-3-6 benchmark dates and flagging any approaching or missed submission; and a manufacturer-specific cochlear-implant/device-programming certification renewal tracker.

**Skill fit:** State audiology board CE cycles, CMS's diagnostic-test order/referral billing rule, and state EHDI 1-3-6 reporting benchmarks are public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-patient trigger, and the order-documentation gate plus the EHDI-deadline tracker are the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance record clears / compute the deadline from the trigger event" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a diagnostic-audiology niche entirely distinct from the Hearing Aid Dispenser idea already in this repo, whose FDA device-labeling and trial/return-period compliance core governs hearing-aid *sales* with no diagnostic-order, EHDI-reporting, or device-programming-certification field at all.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the dual-CE tracker, the order-documentation gate, and the EHDI reporting-deadline tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent audiology practices.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the American Academy of Audiology (AAA) and Academy of Doctors of Audiology (ADA) private-practice sections reach exactly the small practices currently reconciling order documentation and EHDI deadlines on a front-office spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $99-199/mo per practice depending on provider count and newborn-screening volume. 60-80 practices × $150/mo avg = $9,000-12,000/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every diagnostic claim reopens the physician-order documentation question, every newborn screened opens its own independent EHDI 1-3-6 reporting clock, and every cochlear-implant patient added reopens the device-programming-certification question — a continuous, patient-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the practice's own caseload.

**Biggest risk:** A solo audiologist with a light Medicare caseload and no newborn-screening contract has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward practices running high diagnostic-Medicare volume or an active hospital/state newborn-screening referral relationship, where the order-and-EHDI load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 20 practices ($3K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 40 practices ($6K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 60-80 practices plus a MAC-audit-ready order-documentation export a practice can produce the moment a post-payment review arrives.

### 2. Certified Nurse-Midwife (CNM) Independent Practice & Birth Center State APRN Licensure, AMCB Certification & Collaborative-Agreement Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Certified Nurse-Midwives running an independent practice or a birth center hold Advanced Practice Registered Nurse licensure through a state Board of Nursing — not a midwifery board — renewed on a state-specific CE cycle, alongside national certification through the American Midwifery Certification Board (AMCB), maintained through AMCB's five-year Certificate Maintenance Program (CMP): a fixed set of continuing-education modules that, if not completed on schedule, requires the CNM to retake the national certification exam entirely rather than simply pay a late fee. In roughly half the states, full scope of practice — particularly prescriptive authority — requires a written collaborative or prescriptive-authority practice agreement with a physician, filed with the board and renewed on its own cycle, and a CNM who keeps prescribing after that agreement lapses (a collaborating physician retires or the practice relationship simply ends) has been practicing outside authorized scope for every prescription written in the gap, the same exposure a lapsed physician-agreement creates for a PMHNP. CNMs attending births outside a hospital setting must also maintain a current written hospital transfer agreement with a backup facility — a requirement of many state licensing regulations and of AABC accreditation for a freestanding birth center — and an out-of-date or unsigned transfer agreement discovered during a state licensing survey or an AABC re-accreditation review is exactly the deficiency that can suspend a birth center's ability to admit new clients. A CNM running an active birth caseload currently tracks APRN license renewal, AMCB CMP module completion, collaborative-agreement currency, and hospital transfer-agreement status on a personal spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming all four are simultaneously current before the next birth is scheduled.

**What to build:** A state APRN license and AMCB five-year CMP module tracker flagging any CNM approaching a CMP deadline without completed modules on file; a collaborative/prescriptive-authority practice-agreement tracker with a "cleared to prescribe" gate in restricted-practice states; and a hospital transfer-agreement currency tracker for out-of-hospital birth practices and freestanding birth centers.

**Skill fit:** State Board of Nursing renewal cycles, AMCB's published CMP module requirements, and hospital transfer-agreement expiration dates are public, structured regulatory and contractual data with a concrete per-CNM and per-facility trigger, and the prescriptive-authority gate is the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an APRN-licensed midwifery niche entirely distinct from the Licensed Midwife & Freestanding Birth Center idea already in this repo, whose NARM/CPM certification and midwifery-board licensure govern a completely separate non-nurse credential, regulator, and certifying body that shares no license category or CE-cycle structure with a CNM's nursing-board/AMCB framework.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the license/CMP tracker, the collaborative-agreement gate, and the hospital transfer-agreement tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent CNM practices and birth centers.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the American College of Nurse-Midwives' (ACNM) state chapter directories reach exactly the small independent CNM practices currently reconciling AMCB CMP deadlines and collaborative-agreement status on a personal spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $99-199/mo per practice. 55-70 practices × $150/mo avg = $8,250-10,500/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every birth attended outside a hospital reopens the transfer-agreement-currency question, the AMCB CMP cycle recurs on its own independent five-year clock, and every collaborating-physician change reopens the prescriptive-authority-agreement question — a continuous, caseload-and-relationship-driven compliance stream tied directly to the practice's own growth.

**Biggest risk:** A hospital-employed CNM isn't the buyer for an independent-practice compliance tool. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward independent and birth-center CNM practices running an active out-of-hospital or restricted-practice-state caseload, where the multi-agreement tracking load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 18 practices ($2.7K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 35 practices ($5.25K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 55-70 practices plus a survey-ready transfer-agreement-and-CMP export a CNM can hand a state board or AABC surveyor on arrival.

### 3. Correctional Healthcare Services Contractor NCCHC/ACA Accreditation, In-Custody Credentialing & Intake-Screening Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Small correctional healthcare contractors that staff county jails and small detention facilities with nursing and medical coverage under contract operate under National Commission on Correctional Health Care (NCCHC) accreditation standards — or a state Department of Corrections' equivalent standards — that require a documented receiving/intake health screening within a fixed window of a detainee's booking (commonly within 2 hours for the initial receiving screening and within 14 days for a complete history and physical), a sick-call response-time standard governing how quickly a detainee's medical request must be seen, and a documented medication administration record for every dose given in custody; a missed intake-screening window or an undocumented sick-call response is exactly the finding an NCCHC survey is built to catch, and is also the fact pattern behind Eighth Amendment deliberate-indifference civil-rights litigation that has produced large judgments against understaffed or under-documented correctional healthcare contracts. Every individual clinician — nurse, nurse practitioner, or physician — must hold a current state license in good standing *and* a separate, facility-specific in-custody credentialing file (background clearance, facility orientation, and privileging specific to that jail) before working an unsupervised shift, and a clinician placed on a shift before that facility-specific credentialing clears is exactly the gap a state DOC contract-compliance audit targets, with contract termination or non-renewal a real consequence. A contractor running coverage across several county jail contracts currently tracks per-inmate intake-screening deadlines, per-clinician state-license-and-facility-credentialing status, and sick-call response times on a shared spreadsheet and a shift log, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific detainee's intake screening actually completed on time or a specific clinician was actually cleared to work a specific facility's shift.

**What to build:** A per-inmate intake-screening deadline tracker computing each detainee's 2-hour receiving-screening and 14-day history-and-physical due dates from their booking timestamp, with a flag on any screening approaching or past due; a per-clinician state-license-and-facility-credentialing tracker with a "cleared to work" gate blocking any shift assignment until both the base license and the facility-specific credentialing file are confirmed current; and a sick-call response-time tracker flagging any medical request approaching its facility's response-time standard.

**Skill fit:** NCCHC's published intake-screening and sick-call response-time standards are public, structured accrediting-body data with a concrete per-inmate and per-clinician trigger, and the "cleared to work" credentialing gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a custodial-setting healthcare niche entirely distinct from every other clinical-practice idea in this repo, none of which tracks an intake-screening deadline clock, a facility-specific in-custody credentialing file, or a sick-call response-time standard of any kind.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the intake-screening tracker, the clinician credentialing gate, and the sick-call response-time tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small correctional healthcare contractors serving county jails.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the National Commission on Correctional Health Care's conference and member network, along with the American Correctional Association's vendor directory, reach exactly the small contractors currently reconciling intake-screening deadlines and in-custody credentialing on a shared spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per contractor depending on number of facility contracts and average daily population covered. 20-26 companies × $420/mo avg = $8,400-10,920/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every booking generates its own independent intake-screening deadline clock, every new facility contract reopens the in-custody credentialing question for every assigned clinician, and every sick-call request opens its own response-time countdown — a continuous, contract-and-population-driven compliance stream tied directly to the company's own growth.

**Biggest risk:** A contractor holding a single small-jail contract with a stable, low daily population has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward contractors running multiple facility contracts with higher average daily populations, where the intake-and-credentialing load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 8 companies ($3.4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 14 companies ($5.9K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 20-26 companies plus an NCCHC-survey-ready intake-and-credentialing export a contractor can produce the moment an accreditation review arrives.

### 4. Electric & Illuminated Sign Contractor State Sign-Contractor License, UL Field-Labeling & Wind-Load Permit Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Electric sign contractors installing illuminated channel-letter signs, monument signs, and LED displays for retail and commercial clients hold a state sign-contractor license — a distinct specialty category from a general electrical contractor license in states such as Florida, which licenses "Certified" and "Registered" Sign Contractors separately under DBPR — renewed on a state-specific cycle, plus a local electrical permit pulled fresh for every single installation. Every sign must carry a current UL (or ETL) listing before a local electrical inspector will approve it, and a sign that ships without a factory listing valid for its actual installed configuration, or one field-fabricated on site, requires a UL Field Evaluation and field label before final inspection — a sign installed and energized without that field-label documentation on file is exactly the finding a building department's final electrical inspection is built to catch, and can force a contractor to de-energize and re-submit a completed job. Larger pylon and monument signs additionally require stamped structural engineering calculations sized to the sign's height and area against the specific jurisdiction's wind-speed zone under ASCE 7 before a permit will even be issued, and a permit application submitted with generic or mismatched wind-load calculations for that jurisdiction is a rejected submittal that stalls a job before it starts. A contractor running installations across a multi-county metro area currently tracks state and local license status, per-job UL field-label documentation, and per-jurisdiction wind-load engineering packages on a paper permit folder, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific sign has both a current UL field label and wind-load calculations matched to the exact jurisdiction it's being installed in before the crew shows up.

**What to build:** A state sign-contractor license and local competency-card renewal tracker; a per-job UL/ETL field-label documentation tracker gating a sign from being marked ready for final inspection until the field evaluation is confirmed on file; and a per-jurisdiction wind-load engineering package tracker matching each pylon/monument sign's height and area to that municipality's ASCE 7 wind-speed zone before permit submission.

**Skill fit:** State sign-contractor licensing rosters, UL's field-evaluation program, and jurisdiction-specific ASCE 7 wind-speed zone maps are public, structured regulatory and standards-body data with a concrete per-job trigger, and the field-label-and-wind-load gate is the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a sign-specific installation niche entirely distinct from the Outdoor Advertising Company idea already in this repo, whose state sign-permit and federal Highway Beautification Act compliance core governs a billboard's location and size as an advertising *structure* with no UL field-labeling or wind-load-engineering mechanic at all, and from the generic Licensed Electrical Contractor idea covered elsewhere in this repo, whose NEC-code-cycle tracker has no field for a sign-specific UL listing or wind-load calculation of any kind.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the license tracker, the UL field-label gate, and the wind-load engineering package tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent electric sign contractors.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the International Sign Association's (ISA) member directory reaches exactly the small sign contractors currently reconciling UL field-label documentation and per-jurisdiction wind-load packages on a paper permit folder with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $129-249/mo per contractor depending on job volume and number of jurisdictions served. 45-60 contractors × $180/mo avg = $8,100-10,800/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every job installed generates its own UL field-label compliance event, every new jurisdiction a contractor expands into reopens the wind-load-engineering question, and every pylon or monument sign quoted reopens the structural-calculation question for that specific site — a continuous, job-and-territory-driven compliance stream tied directly to the contractor's own growth.

**Biggest risk:** A contractor doing only small, non-illuminated, pre-listed signage has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward contractors installing illuminated channel-letter and pylon/monument signs, where the UL-field-label-and-wind-load load genuinely outgrows a paper folder.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 contractors ($2.7K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 30 contractors ($5.4K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 45-60 contractors plus an inspection-ready UL-field-label-and-wind-load export a contractor can hand a building inspector on the spot.

### 5. State-Registered/Certified Interior Designer Title-Act Licensure, NCIDQ Certification & Stamped-Drawing Permit Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** In title-act and practice-act states — Florida, Louisiana, Nevada, and several others — a Registered or Certified Interior Designer credential requires passing the NCIDQ exam and state board registration renewed on a CE cycle that includes a dedicated life-safety and building-code hours sub-requirement tracked separately from general CE, and in practice-act states like Florida a registered designer holds actual permit-stamping authority: the ability to pull a building permit and stamp non-structural interior construction documents for commercial projects, but only within a specific scope threshold defined by statute. A designer who stamps a project after their registration has lapsed, or whose stamped drawings exceed the permitted scope (structural work, life-safety systems outside the designer's authorized boundary), is exactly the finding a plans examiner's permit review or a board complaint is built to catch, and a rejected or challenged stamped submittal can halt a commercial buildout mid-construction. Every stamped project must also document accessibility and means-of-egress calculations specific to the space's occupancy classification under whichever edition of the building code the local jurisdiction has currently adopted — a designer working across several counties can find one jurisdiction still enforcing an older code edition while an adjacent one has already moved to the newest cycle, and stamping to the wrong edition's egress-width or occupant-load formula is a failed plan review. A designer running several concurrent commercial stamped projects currently tracks state registration and CE-hour status, per-project scope-and-stamping eligibility, and per-jurisdiction code-edition and occupancy-classification calculations on a paper project binder, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific stamped set was produced while registration, scope, and code edition were all simultaneously in order.

**What to build:** A state registration and CE-hour tracker with a separate life-safety/code-hours countdown; a per-project stamped-drawing eligibility gate blocking a project from being marked ready-to-submit until registration currency and scope-threshold compliance are both confirmed; and a per-jurisdiction adopted-code-edition and occupancy-classification/egress-calculation reference tied to each stamped project's specific location.

**Skill fit:** State interior-design board rosters, NCIDQ's published CE requirements, and jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction adopted building-code editions are public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-project stamping-eligibility trigger, and the stamped-drawing gate is the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a title-act/practice-act licensure niche entirely distinct from the Independent Interior Designer client-project, vendor-purchase-order, and budget tracker referenced elsewhere in this repo, a workflow tool with no state-registration, stamped-drawing, or building-code compliance mechanic at all, and from the Architecture Firm idea already in this repo, whose NCARB certification governs a separate, broader-scope stamping authority under an entirely different licensing board.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the registration/CE tracker, the stamped-drawing eligibility gate, and the jurisdiction code-edition reference. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to registered interior designers doing commercial stamped work in practice-act states.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the American Society of Interior Designers (ASID) and International Interior Design Association (IIDA) chapters in Florida, Louisiana, and Nevada reach exactly the small practices currently reconciling stamped-drawing eligibility and code-edition compliance on a paper project binder with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $99-199/mo per designer depending on stamped-project volume. 65-85 designers × $140/mo avg = $9,100-11,900/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every commercial project stamped reopens the eligibility gate, every jurisdiction a designer works in with a different adopted code edition reopens the egress-calculation question, and every CE renewal cycle reopens the life-safety-hours question — a continuous, project-and-territory-driven compliance stream tied directly to the designer's own commercial-practice growth.

**Biggest risk:** A designer working only in a title-act-only state with no permit-stamping authority has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward registered designers doing commercial stamped work in practice-act states (Florida, Louisiana, Nevada), where the stamped-drawing-and-code-edition load genuinely outgrows a project binder.

**Growth path:** Side project → 18 designers ($2.5K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 40 designers ($5.6K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 65-85 designers plus a plan-review-ready stamped-drawing export a designer can hand a plans examiner the moment a permit review raises a question.

Correctional healthcare compliance stands out because the intake-screening deadline and in-custody credentialing gate are genuinely load-bearing rather than paperwork nicety — a missed receiving screening or a clinician working before facility-specific credentialing clears is the exact fact pattern behind deliberate-indifference litigation that has produced large judgments against small contractors, and NCCHC's conference and member network give direct, credible access to exactly the small companies juggling multiple county jail contracts with no dedicated compliance staff.

Independent audiology practice compliance has the most durable, well-organized distribution channel in this session: the American Academy of Audiology and Academy of Doctors of Audiology run active private-practice sections that reach solo and small-group practices directly, the order-documentation-and-EHDI-reporting mechanic recurs on every diagnostic claim and every newborn screened rather than on an occasional renewal date, and the profession's steady growth as an increasingly independent, diagnostic-first specialty gives a builder a durable tailwind that doesn't depend on any single regulatory change.
