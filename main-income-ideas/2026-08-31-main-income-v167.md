### 1. Employee Assistance Program (EAP) Provider CEAP Credentialing, Per-Employer SLA & Confidential Utilization-Reporting Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent EAP providers — companies contracting with employers to deliver short-term, confidential counseling assessment-and-referral services to employees and their household members — staff a rotating roster of affiliate counselors who must hold current Certified Employee Assistance Professional (CEAP) credentialing through the Employee Assistance Professionals Association (EAPA), renewed on a three-year cycle with a specific CE-hour split between clinical and EAP-specific competency domains, alongside each affiliate's own underlying state clinical license (LPC, LCSW, LMFT). Every employer contract carries its own negotiated Service Level Agreement — a guaranteed counselor-contact response time after a referral (commonly 24-48 hours for a standard case, immediate for a critical incident), a contracted session-count cap per presenting issue, and a Critical Incident Stress Management (CISM) on-call obligation for workplace crisis dispatch (a workplace death, a violent incident, a mass layoff) — and an EAP that misses a contracted response-time SLA on even one case is exactly the finding that costs it a renewal at the next contract cycle. Layered on top: every employer client requires a periodic aggregate utilization report (total cases, top presenting-issue categories, session volume) that must never expose which specific employee accessed the benefit, and any referral touching substance-use treatment triggers 42 CFR Part 2's stricter consent-and-disclosure rules on top of standard HIPAA — a report or referral note that lets identifying detail slip through is both a contract-ending confidentiality breach and, for the substance-use subset, a federal violation. A small EAP running 15-40 affiliate counselors across a book of 20-60 employer contracts currently tracks CEAP/license renewal, per-employer SLA response times, and aggregate-only utilization reporting on a program coordinator's spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific referral was dispatched to a currently credentialed counselor inside its contracted response window.

**What to build:** A per-affiliate CEAP credential and underlying state-license renewal tracker with a "cleared to accept referrals" gate; a per-employer-contract SLA tracker logging referral-to-first-contact response time against the contracted window, with an overdue-response alert; a CISM on-call roster scheduler for critical-incident dispatch coverage; and an aggregate-only utilization-report generator that rolls up case counts and issue categories per employer while structurally blocking any field that could identify an individual employee.

**Skill fit:** EAPA's CEAP renewal cycle and the SLA-response-time and aggregate-reporting mechanics are a deterministic deadline-and-rollup problem well suited to a developer's skill set, and the "block any individually identifying field from the employer-facing report" constraint is exactly the kind of data-model discipline this series builds repeatedly, applied to a workplace-benefits niche entirely distinct from every licensed-clinical-practice or treatment-facility idea already in this repo.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the CEAP/license tracker, the per-employer SLA response-time tracker, and the aggregate utilization-report generator. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small independent EAP providers.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Employee Assistance Professionals Association (EAPA) member directory and its regional chapter meetings reach exactly the small EAP providers currently reconciling CEAP renewal and per-employer SLA tracking on a spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249/mo base + $15/mo per affiliate counselor tracked. Average client (10 counselors) = $249 + $150 = $399/mo. 28-35 EAP providers × $399/mo = **$11,172-13,965/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every referral reopens the SLA response-time clock, every employer contract renewal cycle demands a fresh utilization report, and every new affiliate counselor a growing EAP onboards compounds the number of simultaneously tracked CEAP credentials — a continuous, contract-and-referral-volume-driven compliance stream.

**Biggest risk:** A very small EAP running only 3-5 affiliates and a handful of small-business contracts has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward EAPs running 10+ affiliates across multiple mid-size employer contracts, where the multi-SLA, multi-credential load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 providers ($6K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 28-35 providers ($11.2-14K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding an employer-facing self-serve utilization dashboard EAPs can white-label into their own client renewal pitch.

### 2. Flotation Therapy (Float Tank) Studio Public Bathing Facility Disinfection-Cycle & Water-Chemistry Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Flotation therapy studios operate float tanks or pods holding roughly 150 gallons of water saturated with up to 1,000 lbs of Epsom salt to reach the 25-30% density needed for effortless floating — a water chemistry so different from a standard swimming pool or spa that most state and local health departments regulate float tanks under a distinct "public bathing place" or specialty-spa provision requiring UV and/or ozone disinfection between every single session (not on a daily or weekly cycle like a pool), a filtration-turnover-time log matched to each tank's specific volume, and a per-session water-clarity and hydrogen-peroxide or bromine-residual check logged before the next client enters. A studio that lets a between-session disinfection cycle run short, or that can't produce a specific tank's turnover-time and residual-testing log during a health department inspection, is exactly the finding that can shut a tank down mid-week — a serious revenue hit for a studio running only a handful of tanks. Studios must also track each tank's salt-density calibration (a float solution that drops below its target density stops floating clients effortlessly and draws refund complaints) and a filter-and-heater maintenance interval per unit, since a studio running 3-8 tanks across a full daily booking schedule currently tracks disinfection-cycle completion, water-chemistry test results, and salt-density calibration on a front-desk binder next to each tank, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific tank is actually cleared for its next booked session before the next client walks in.

**What to build:** A per-tank between-session disinfection-cycle log (UV/ozone cycle completion, filtration turnover time) with a "cleared for next session" gate blocking a booking from being marked ready until the log is complete; a water-chemistry and salt-density calibration tracker per tank with an out-of-range alert; and a filter/heater maintenance-interval tracker per unit supporting a health department inspection on request.

**Skill fit:** State and local "public bathing place" disinfection-cycle rules and the specific between-session cadence float tanks require are public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-tank, per-session trigger, and the "cleared for next session" gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a wellness niche entirely distinct from the swimming pool/spa and tanning salon ideas already in this repo, neither of which shares a high-density brine water-chemistry model or a between-every-client disinfection cadence.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the per-tank disinfection-cycle log, the water-chemistry/density tracker, and the maintenance-interval tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent float studios.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Float Tank Association's member directory and annual Float Conference reach exactly the small studios currently tracking between-session disinfection cycles on a binder next to each tank with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149/mo base + $25/mo per tank tracked. Average client (5 tanks) = $149 + $125 = $274/mo. 35-45 studios × $274/mo = **$9,590-12,330/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every single session booked reopens the between-session disinfection-cycle requirement, salt-density calibration drifts continuously and needs regular recheck, and every new tank a growing studio adds compounds the number of simultaneously tracked disinfection-and-chemistry records.

**Biggest risk:** A single-tank studio has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need barely worth a subscription. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward studios running 3+ tanks on a full daily booking schedule, where the per-session cross-check genuinely outgrows a front-desk binder.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 studios ($4.1K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 35-45 studios ($9.6-12.3K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding a health-department-ready compliance export a studio can produce the moment an inspector asks.

### 3. Outsourced Medical Coding Company HIPAA Business Associate, AAPC/AHIMA Credentialing & RADV Audit-Readiness Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Small outsourced medical coding companies — assigning ICD-10-CM, CPT, and HCC risk-adjustment codes to clinical documentation on behalf of physician practices and Medicare Advantage health plans — operate as a HIPAA Business Associate under a signed BAA for every single client relationship, requiring the coding company to maintain its own written information-security safeguards and breach-notification procedure independent of any client's own HIPAA program. Every individual coder must hold and renew a current AAPC (Certified Professional Coder) or AHIMA (Certified Coding Specialist) credential on its own CE-hour cycle, and the company itself must operate a documented OIG seven-element compliance program — a designated compliance officer, a written code of conduct, ongoing auditing and monitoring, and a documented corrective-action process for detected coding errors — since the absence of a working compliance program is itself an aggravating factor in any government coding-fraud investigation. For coders working Medicare Advantage risk-adjustment charts specifically, a health plan client's own CMS Risk Adjustment Data Validation (RADV) audit depends entirely on the coding company's HCC assignments surviving chart-level scrutiny years after the fact, requiring a documented per-coder accuracy-audit sample rate and a maintained record of which specific coder touched which chart — a coding company whose sample rate lapses, or that can't reconstruct which coder assigned a disputed HCC code, exposes its health plan client to a payment clawback that traces straight back to the vendor and can end the contract on the spot. A small coding company running 10-30 coders across several physician-practice and health-plan clients currently tracks BAA currency, per-coder credential renewal, and RADV accuracy-audit sampling on a mix of a compliance officer's spreadsheet and individual coder files, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific chart was coded by a currently credentialed coder inside the client's required audit-sample rate.

**What to build:** A per-client HIPAA Business Associate Agreement currency tracker; a per-coder AAPC/AHIMA credential and CE-hour renewal tracker with a "cleared to code" gate; an OIG seven-element compliance-program checklist with a corrective-action log for detected coding errors; and a RADV accuracy-audit sampling tracker logging which coder touched which chart against the client's required sample rate, with an under-sampled alert.

**Skill fit:** AAPC/AHIMA credential-renewal cycles and CMS's RADV audit-sampling expectations are public, structured professional and regulatory data with a concrete per-coder and per-chart trigger, and the "cleared to code" gate plus the coder-to-chart accuracy-sampling log are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a health-information niche entirely distinct from the payer-credentialing idea already in this repo, which tracks a provider's own payer enrollment rather than a third-party vendor's coding-accuracy and BAA compliance.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the BAA tracker, the per-coder credential tracker, and the RADV sampling/coder-attribution log. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small outsourced medical coding companies.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the AAPC's business-member directory and the American Health Information Management Association (AHIMA) member network reach exactly the small coding companies currently reconciling coder credentialing and RADV sampling on a spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299/mo base + $18/mo per coder tracked. Average client (15 coders) = $299 + $270 = $569/mo. 20-26 companies × $569/mo = **$11,380-14,794/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every coder's AAPC/AHIMA credential renews on its own independent cycle, every new client relationship opens a fresh BAA-currency obligation, and every chart coded for a risk-adjustment client reopens the RADV accuracy-sampling requirement — a continuous, client-and-headcount-driven compliance stream.

**Biggest risk:** A coding company working only fee-for-service physician-practice claims with no Medicare Advantage risk-adjustment work has a lower-stakes RADV exposure. Mitigate by leading with the BAA-and-credential tracker as the baseline value proposition and treating the RADV sampling module as a premium tier for companies with health-plan clients.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10 companies ($5.7K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 20-26 companies ($11.4-14.8K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding a health-plan-facing audit-defense export a coding company can hand a client the moment a RADV audit letter arrives.

### 4. Independent RV/Motorhome Pre-Purchase Inspector NRVIA Certification, E&O Coverage & Inspection Report Standard-of-Care Tracker

**Problem:** Independent RV and motorhome pre-purchase inspectors — hired by a private buyer or seller, distinct from any dealership, to inspect a specific used unit before a private-party or dealer sale closes — hold National RV Inspectors Association (NRVIA) Level 1 or Level 2 certification, renewed through its own continuing-education cycle covering roof-membrane, slide-out-mechanism, LP-gas-appliance, and chassis/frame inspection standards, and must carry professional liability/errors-and-omissions insurance sized against the real financial exposure of a missed defect — an inspector who signs off on a unit with an undocumented roof leak or a slide-out mechanism about to fail faces a direct E&O claim from a buyer who relied on that report to close a five- or six-figure purchase. Every inspection report itself has to meet a defensible standard of care: a structured, multi-system checklist (roof membrane and seal condition, slide-out operation, LP gas and propane appliance function test, chassis and frame corrosion, electrical and plumbing systems) with dated photo documentation for every flagged item, since an inspector's entire professional defense if a buyer later disputes a missed defect rests on being able to produce the exact report delivered and the specific systems actually tested that day. An independent inspector running several inspections a week across a travel radius covering multiple dealer lots, storage yards, and private residences currently tracks NRVIA certification renewal, E&O policy currency, and per-inspection report completeness on a mix of a paper checklist and a phone's camera roll, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific delivered report actually covers every required system before it's sent to the client.

**What to build:** An NRVIA certification and CE-hour renewal tracker with an E&O insurance policy currency tracker; a per-inspection structured checklist covering every required system (roof, slide-out, LP gas, chassis, electrical, plumbing) gating a report from being marked "ready to deliver" until every section and its required photo documentation is complete; and a client-delivered-report archive supporting the inspector's own liability defense if a dispute surfaces months later.

**Skill fit:** NRVIA's published certification standards and inspection-checklist structure are public, structured professional data with a concrete per-inspection trigger, and the report-completeness gate plus the delivered-report archive are the same deterministic "block delivery until the record clears / keep a defensible archive" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an independent-inspection niche entirely distinct from the RV Dealer idea already in this repo, whose dealership-license, RVIA Seal, and warranty-reimbursement compliance core never involves a third party hired to inspect someone else's unit before a sale.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the certification/E&O tracker, the per-inspection structured checklist with photo-documentation gating, and the delivered-report archive. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent NRVIA-certified inspectors.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the National RV Inspectors Association's (NRVIA) own certified-inspector directory and its active inspector Facebook and forum community reach exactly the independent inspectors currently tracking certification renewal and report completeness on a paper checklist with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $79-149/mo per inspector depending on inspection volume. 90-120 inspectors × $105/mo avg = **$9,450-12,600/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every inspection booked reopens the report-completeness check, NRVIA certification renews on its own CE cycle, and a growing inspector's own delivered-report archive compounds every season as the RV resale market — which has grown steadily since 2020 — keeps generating new pre-purchase inspection demand.

**Biggest risk:** A part-time inspector doing only a handful of inspections a month has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward inspectors running a full weekly schedule across multiple dealer and private-sale referral relationships, where the per-inspection report-completeness discipline genuinely matters to their E&O exposure.

**Growth path:** Side project → 30 inspectors ($3.2K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 90-120 inspectors ($9.5-12.6K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding a buyer-facing shareable report-delivery portal inspectors can offer as a premium client experience.

### 5. Commercial Tire Retreader NHTSA Part 574 Tire Identification, FMVSS 117 Testing & Steer-Axle Restriction Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Commercial tire retreaders — buying back worn commercial truck-tire casings, buffing and re-bonding a new tread layer under heat and pressure, and reselling the finished retread to trucking fleets — must register with NHTSA as a tire brand-name owner under 49 CFR Part 574's Tire Identification and Recordkeeping rule, assigning and permanently marking its own Tire Identification Number (TIN) on every retreaded casing and maintaining the casing-to-TIN ownership record that both NHTSA and a future tire recall depend on entirely; a retreader that loses track of which TIN was assigned to which casing lot cannot participate in a recall notification at all, exposing the retreader to direct federal liability if a defective lot keeps circulating. Every retread production lot must also pass FMVSS 117's retreaded-tire performance testing — high-speed, endurance, and bead-unseating tests run against a documented sample from that specific lot — before the lot can be released for sale, and a lot sold without a passing, retrievable test record is exactly the finding a NHTSA compliance audit is built to catch. Layered on top, a retreader supplying fleet customers must track FMCSA's 49 CFR 393.75 restriction barring retreaded or recapped tires from certain steer-axle positions on buses and some truck configurations, since shipping a steer-axle-restricted retread to a fleet that installs it on the wrong axle position creates liability exposure for both the retreader and the carrier. A small retreading plant running several production lots a week across a rotating mix of fleet accounts currently tracks per-casing TIN assignment, per-lot FMVSS 117 test results, and axle-position-restricted shipment flagging on a shop-floor production log and a filing cabinet of test reports, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific lot shipped to a specific fleet actually passed its required test and isn't destined for a restricted axle position.

**What to build:** A per-casing TIN assignment and ownership-record tracker satisfying NHTSA Part 574's recordkeeping requirement, with a recall-ready lot-lookup view; a per-production-lot FMVSS 117 test-result tracker gating a lot from being marked "cleared to ship" until a passing test result is on file; and a per-shipment steer-axle-restriction flag warning a fleet customer's order away from any restricted axle position before the shipment ships.

**Skill fit:** NHTSA's Part 574 recordkeeping structure and FMVSS 117's test requirements are public, structured federal regulatory data with a concrete per-casing and per-lot trigger, and the "cleared to ship" gate plus the recall-ready TIN lookup are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a tire-manufacturing niche this repo has never addressed.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the TIN/ownership-record tracker, the per-lot FMVSS 117 test tracker, and the steer-axle-restriction shipment flag. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent commercial tire retreading plants.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Tire Industry Association's Retread Technology Center and the Tire Retread & Repair Information Bureau (TRIB) member directory reach exactly the small retreading plants currently reconciling TIN assignment and FMVSS 117 test records on a shop-floor log with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249-499/mo per plant depending on weekly production-lot volume. 25-32 plants × $360/mo avg = **$9,000-11,520/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every production lot reopens the FMVSS 117 test-and-release gate, every casing processed adds a fresh TIN-and-ownership record the plant carries for the life of that tire, and every fleet shipment reopens the steer-axle-restriction check — a continuous, production-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the plant's own throughput.

**Biggest risk:** A very small retreading operation running only a handful of lots a month has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward plants running multiple weekly production lots across several fleet accounts, where the per-lot, per-casing recordkeeping load genuinely outgrows a filing cabinet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 plants ($4.3K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 25-32 plants ($9-11.5K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding a fleet-customer-facing TIN lookup portal a retreader can offer as a value-added service during contract renewal.
