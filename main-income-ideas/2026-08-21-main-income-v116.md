# Main Income Ideas - 2026-08-21 (Session 116)

## Who this is for
You're 29, you write software and know your way around data — pipelines, scrapers, structured reference data, rules engines. You want to replace a full-time salary ($8-15K+/mo) within 12-24 months with something you build solo in evenings and weekends, no outside funding, no co-founder, and you want the first paying customer inside 90 days. The wedge in this series is always the same: small operators in a regulated industry who have non-optional government or accreditation-body obligations — licenses, permits, certifications, filings, inspections — and no compliance department to track them. You sell a monthly-retainer SaaS that turns their compliance binder into a living calendar with alerts and hard gates. The regulator (or accrediting body) creates the deadline; you sell the peace of mind of never missing it.

## Ground rules for every idea in this series
- Solo-buildable start.
- Realistic path to $8-15K+/mo
- Time to first dollar < 90 days.
- Named distribution channel
- No repeats. This session's five regulated industries are: (1) human composting (natural organic reduction) and alkaline hydrolysis death-care facilities, (2) non-reproductive tissue banks, eye banks, and organ/tissue recovery agencies, (3) environmental remediation consultants and Licensed Site Professionals operating under state brownfield/voluntary cleanup programs, (4) customs bonded warehouse and Foreign Trade Zone operators, and (5) direct-to-consumer alcohol shipping compliance for small wineries, breweries, and distilleries. Each is distinct from anything already in this repo:
  - **Human composting / alkaline hydrolysis** is an entirely new death-care process category this repo has never touched. Prior sessions cover traditional funeral homes, crematories, and cemeteries — all regulated under the FTC Funeral Rule and state funeral-board licensing for embalming, casket burial, and flame cremation. Natural organic reduction and alkaline hydrolysis are chemically and biologically distinct processes (a multi-week microbial composting cycle vs. a chemical dissolution process) governed by newer, separate statutory provisions with their own time-temperature/process-parameter thresholds and, for alkaline hydrolysis, a wastewater-discharge permit that flame cremation never requires — none of which any prior funeral/crematory/cemetery idea in this repo addresses.
  - **Non-reproductive tissue bank / eye bank / organ-tissue recovery agency** is distinct from the Fertility Clinic & Reproductive Tissue Bank idea (Session 65), which covers FDA HCT/P donor-eligibility and SART/NASS cycle-reporting specifically for donor sperm, eggs, and embryos in an IVF context. A non-reproductive tissue bank instead recovers and distributes cadaveric donor tissue — skin, bone, corneas, heart valves — for surgical transplant, operating under the same CFR part but an entirely different donor population, recovery-technician credentialing structure (AATB/EBAA accreditation rather than SART membership), and clinical use case that shares no accrediting body or workflow with the fertility-clinic idea.
  - **Environmental remediation consultant / Licensed Site Professional** is distinct from the asbestos/lead abatement contractor, mold assessment/remediation contractor, and environmental testing laboratory ideas already in this repo, none of which track a consultant's own phased site-closure obligations. An LSP or LEP is a credentialed individual professional whose personal certification substitutes for direct state agency sign-off on a contaminated-site cleanup — an entirely different licensing mechanism (privatized regulatory oversight) than a contractor's trade license or a lab's accreditation.
  - **Customs bonded warehouse & Foreign Trade Zone operator** is distinct from the Licensed Customs Broker idea (which covers a broker's CBP 19 CFR Part 111 license for filing entries on a client's behalf) and the Ocean Freight Forwarder & NVOCC idea (which covers a transportation intermediary's FMC operating authority). A bonded warehouse or FTZ operator instead runs the storage/zone facility itself — a 19 CFR Part 19 warehouse license or an FTZ Board grant — with its own per-lot statutory storage-period limit, bond-adequacy requirement, and annual zone-reconciliation report that neither a broker's document-filing practice nor a forwarder's booking business ever touches.
  - **DTC alcohol shipping compliance** is distinct from the Craft Brewery/Winery/Distillery TTB Production Permit & State ABC Manufacturer License idea already in this repo, which covers production-side licensing (what it takes to legally make and bottle alcohol). This idea instead covers the completely separate patchwork of interstate direct-to-consumer shipping permits, per-consumer annual volume caps, and destination-state excise-tax filings a producer faces only once it starts shipping product to consumers across state lines — a distribution-and-tax compliance problem no production-licensing idea in this repo addresses.

### 1. Human Composting (Natural Organic Reduction) & Alkaline Hydrolysis Death-Care Facility Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** States have legalized "natural organic reduction" (NOR, aka human composting) at a rapid clip since Washington became the first in 2019 — Colorado, Oregon, Vermont, California, New York, Arizona, Delaware, Maryland, and Nevada have followed — and "alkaline hydrolysis" (water cremation, or resomation) is legal in an even longer list of 20+ states. Facilities offering either process hold a state-issued NOR or AH facility license, typically from the same state funeral/cemetery board that licenses traditional funeral homes but under entirely separate statutory provisions with their own process-verification requirements. NOR requires documented time-temperature logs proving the composting vessel reached and sustained a minimum temperature (commonly modeled on PFRP-style pathogen-reduction standards) for a state-specified number of days across the full multi-week curing cycle, screening of the finished soil for foreign material (implants, prosthetics) before release, and chain-of-custody documentation from intake through final soil disposition. Alkaline hydrolysis facilities separately must log each cycle's temperature, alkali concentration, pressure, and duration against the state's minimum process parameters, and manage a wastewater-discharge permit with the local treatment authority for the process's sterile effluent — an obligation flame cremation never carries. A small operator entering this fast-growing niche is tracking a facility license, a wastewater-discharge permit, and standard death-care chain-of-custody paperwork for a process type that didn't exist as a licensed category five years ago, with no compliance software built for it.
**What to build:** A per-vessel/per-cycle time-temperature log for NOR that auto-flags any cycle failing to sustain the state's minimum temperature-duration threshold and blocks soil release until it clears; a per-cycle process-parameter log for alkaline hydrolysis (temperature, alkalinity, pressure, duration) checked against the state minimum; a chain-of-custody tracker from intake authorization through final disposition; and a facility-license and wastewater-discharge-permit renewal tracker.
**Skill fit:** Every state's NOR and AH statute specifies concrete numeric thresholds — exactly the deterministic "log the reading, block the release until the threshold is met" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a death-care process category with no incumbent software vendor because the regulatory framework itself is only a few years old.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the NOR time-temperature log with its release gate, the alkaline hydrolysis process-parameter log, and the chain-of-custody tracker for the earliest-legalizing states (Washington, Colorado, Oregon, New York, California). First 5-8 clients through direct outreach — the operator population is still small enough that founder-to-founder outreach works.
**Time to first $:** 30-60 days — the National Home Funeral Alliance and the broader death-care-innovation community, plus direct outreach to the small, well-known list of licensed NOR operators and alkaline hydrolysis providers, a tight-knit, publicity-savvy founder community that talks to each other and to trade press.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $300-600/mo per facility given the higher-stakes, lower-volume nature of the process versus traditional cremation. 20-30 facilities × $425/mo avg = $8,500-12,750/mo.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every case run through the facility generates a fresh time-temperature or process-parameter log, and every new state that legalizes NOR or AH (the list has roughly doubled since 2022) both creates new potential clients and requires the software to encode a new statute — a structural reason the product never becomes "finished."
**Biggest risk:** The operator population today is small (dozens, not thousands), capping near-term acquisition speed. Mitigate by covering both NOR and AH in one product, since many facilities offering one plan to add the other, and by positioning early to become the default system new facilities adopt as more states legalize.
**Growth path:** Side project → 8-10 facilities ($3.4-4.25K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 18-20 facilities ($7.65-8.5K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 25-30 facilities plus first-mover expansion into every newly-legalizing state.

### 2. Non-Reproductive Tissue Bank, Eye Bank & Organ/Tissue Recovery Agency FDA HCT/P & AATB/EBAA Accreditation Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Independent tissue banks, eye banks, and organ/tissue recovery agencies that recover, process, and distribute donated skin, bone, corneas, and heart valves for surgical transplant operate under FDA's Human Cells, Tissues, and Cellular and Tissue-Based Products regulation (21 CFR Part 1271) — applied here to cadaveric donor tissue rather than reproductive cells — with its own donor-eligibility determination requirement: a specific communicable-disease testing and screening panel, a next-of-kin risk-factor interview, and a documented eligibility determination that must be complete before any recovered tissue can be released for distribution. On top of the FDA framework, tissue banks and eye banks pursue accreditation from the American Association of Tissue Banks (AATB) or the Eye Bank Association of America (EBAA) — most hospitals and surgery centers won't accept graft tissue from a non-accredited bank — which layers its own recovery-technician certification, chain-of-custody, and processing-standard requirements on top of the FDA rules, renewed on a multi-year cycle with periodic on-site inspection. A small independent recovery agency or regional eye bank running a handful of recovery technicians and processing dozens of donors a month manages per-donor HCT/P eligibility documentation, per-technician AATB/EBAA certification currency, and chain-of-custody records with no dedicated compliance system — and tissue distributed without a complete eligibility determination on file, or recovered by a technician whose certification lapsed, is exactly the finding an FDA inspection or accreditation survey is built to catch.
**What to build:** A per-donor HCT/P eligibility-determination tracker with a hard release-block until the determination is complete; a per-technician AATB/EBAA certification and competency tracker gating recovery eligibility; a chain-of-custody log from recovery through processing to distribution, tagged per graft/lot; and an AATB/EBAA accreditation and FDA establishment-registration renewal tracker.
**Skill fit:** FDA's HCT/P donor-eligibility framework and AATB/EBAA's accreditation standards are public and structured, sharing the same "compute eligibility, gate the release" pattern this series builds repeatedly for biologics-handling businesses — applied here to a non-reproductive donor-tissue category no prior session has touched.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the donor eligibility tracker with the release gate, the technician certification tracker, and the chain-of-custody log. First 5-8 clients through AATB and EBAA member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the American Association of Tissue Banks (AATB) and Eye Bank Association of America (EBAA) both maintain member directories of independent, non-hospital-affiliated tissue and eye banks currently tracking this in spreadsheets.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $349-599/mo per bank depending on donor volume and technician count. 25-30 banks × $449/mo avg = $11,225-13,470/mo.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every donor recovery opens a fresh HCT/P eligibility-documentation event, and technician certifications recur on AATB/EBAA's multi-year cycle across every recovery and processing staff member — continuous, donor-volume-driven compliance activity tied directly to the bank's ability to legally distribute tissue.
**Biggest risk:** Tissue distributed without a complete eligibility determination is a direct infectious-disease transmission risk FDA pursues aggressively — mitigate by making the release-block the product's core, non-optional feature rather than an advisory flag.
**Growth path:** Side project → 10-12 banks ($3.5-4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 20-22 banks ($7.5-8.4K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 28-32 banks plus an AATB accreditation-survey-prep service upsell.

### 3. Environmental Remediation Consultant & Licensed Site Professional State Brownfield/Voluntary Cleanup Program Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Independent environmental consultants who investigate and remediate contaminated sites — closing out a leaking underground storage tank site, clearing a brownfield redevelopment parcel, managing a dry-cleaner solvent plume — operate under a state-specific credentialed-professional framework that, in several states, privatizes cleanup oversight entirely: Massachusetts requires a Licensed Site Professional (LSP) to certify site closure without direct state agency sign-off, Connecticut runs an equivalent Licensed Environmental Professional (LEP) program, and most other states run a Voluntary Cleanup Program (VCP) or brownfield program requiring specific state approval/registration and a defined phased sequence (Phase I/II Environmental Site Assessment, remedial action plan, no-further-action determination). Each credential carries its own renewal cycle, continuing-education requirement, and personal professional-liability exposure, since an LSP's certification is a legal attestation the state relies on instead of its own inspection. A small firm running 1-4 licensed professionals across 15-40 active cleanup sites tracks each site's regulatory phase and required deliverable deadline, each professional's credential renewal and CE-hour status, and state-specific VCP program deadlines — almost always in a spreadsheet, where a missed monitoring report or an expired credential creates both a regulatory finding and a professional-liability exposure.
**What to build:** A per-site regulatory-phase tracker (Phase I → Phase II → remedial action plan → monitoring → closure) with each phase's required deliverable and deadline; a per-professional LSP/LEP/VCP-registration credential tracker with renewal and CE-hour requirements; a periodic groundwater/soil-monitoring report calendar tied to each active site's permit conditions; and a closure/no-further-action document library for audit readiness.
**Skill fit:** State brownfield/VCP requirements and LSP/LEP credentialing rules are published, structured regulatory frameworks, and the phase-deadline computation plus credential-renewal tracking is the deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly — applied to a licensed-professional category distinct from the abatement-contractor and testing-laboratory ideas already in this repo, none of which track a consultant's own phased site-closure obligations or personal licensure.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the per-site phase tracker with deliverable deadlines, the LSP/LEP credential tracker, and the monitoring-report calendar for Massachusetts and Connecticut first, since both have the most codified credentialed-professional frameworks. First 5-8 firms through direct outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Licensed Site Professionals Association (LSPA) in Massachusetts and the Connecticut Association of Environmental Professionals both run active member directories and CE events reaching exactly this small-firm buyer.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249-499/mo per firm depending on active site count and number of licensed professionals. 30-35 firms × $349/mo avg = $10,470-12,215/mo.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every active cleanup site generates recurring monitoring-report deadlines for the life of the remediation, often years, and every credentialed professional's license renews on its own CE-tracked cycle — a firm's site portfolio and credential roster only grow over time.
**Biggest risk:** A missed monitoring report or an LSP certifying closure on an expired credential is a serious professional-liability event, not just an administrative lapse — mitigate with redundant renewal alerting well ahead of each deadline and a clear disclaimer that the tool tracks documentation, not technical remediation judgment.
**Growth path:** Side project → 10-12 firms ($3.5-4.2K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 22-25 firms ($7.7-8.7K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 30-35 firms plus expansion to additional states' brownfield/VCP programs.

### 4. Customs Bonded Warehouse & Foreign Trade Zone Operator CBP Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Small logistics operators who run a customs bonded warehouse (storing imported goods with duty payment deferred until withdrawal) or a Foreign Trade Zone site/subzone (a secured area treated as outside U.S. customs territory for storage, assembly, or manufacturing) hold a distinct CBP-issued authorization — a bonded warehouse license under 19 CFR Part 19, or an FTZ Board grant plus CBP activation — separate from a customs broker's document-filing license. A bonded warehouse operator must maintain a continuous bond sized to the warehouse's merchandise value, submit a perpetual inventory system reconciling every lot against CBP's records, respect the statutory 5-year maximum storage period per entry (goods not withdrawn or exported by then are considered abandoned), and undergo periodic CBP compliance reviews. An FTZ operator separately files admission and status documentation per shipment (Form CBP 214), maintains the zone's annual reconciliation report to the FTZ Board, and keeps its security and inventory-control procedures manual current. A small operator running one bonded warehouse or a single-user FTZ subzone tracks bond adequacy, per-lot storage-period countdowns, Form 214 filings, and the annual reconciliation report with no dedicated system — and a lot that quietly ages past the 5-year limit, or an inventory reconciliation that doesn't tie out to CBP's own numbers, is exactly what a compliance review is built to catch.
**What to build:** A per-lot storage-period countdown flagging any lot approaching the 5-year statutory limit; a continuous-bond adequacy calculator comparing current inventory value against the posted bond; a perpetual inventory reconciliation log tying every movement to CBP records; an FTZ Form 214 admission-and-status filing tracker; and an annual FTZ reconciliation-report generator.
**Skill fit:** 19 CFR Part 19's recordkeeping rules and the FTZ Board's annual reconciliation requirements are published federal regulation with concrete numeric thresholds — the deterministic "compute the deadline, flag the threshold breach" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a facility-license category distinct from the customs broker and freight forwarder/NVOCC ideas already in this repo.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the per-lot storage-period tracker, the bond-adequacy calculator, and the Form 214/annual-reconciliation tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small bonded-warehouse and single-user FTZ operators.
**Time to first $:** 30-60 days — the National Association of Foreign-Trade Zones (NAFTZ) and its regional chapters run member directories and an annual conference reaching exactly the small-operator segment that lacks in-house trade-compliance staff.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per facility depending on SKU/lot volume. 25-30 operators × $399/mo avg = $9,975-11,970/mo.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every inbound lot opens its own 5-year storage-period clock and every FTZ shipment generates a fresh Form 214 filing event — continuous, shipment-volume-driven compliance activity, with the annual FTZ reconciliation report recurring for the life of the zone grant.
**Biggest risk:** The buyer population is narrower than most ideas in this series. Mitigate by covering both bonded-warehouse and FTZ operator types in one product, since many small logistics operators run both authorizations at the same site.
**Growth path:** Side project → 10-12 facilities ($3-3.6K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 20-22 facilities ($6-6.6K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 28-32 facilities plus a CBP compliance-review-readiness audit service upsell.

### 5. Direct-to-Consumer Alcohol Shipping Multi-State Permit & Excise Tax Compliance Tracker for Small Wineries, Breweries & Distilleries
**Problem:** A small winery, craft brewery, or micro-distillery shipping directly to consumers across state lines — an essential revenue channel since the 2005 Granholm v. Heald decision opened interstate DTC wine shipping — faces a patchwork unrelated to the TTB production permit or state ABC manufacturer license this repo already covers: each destination state requires its own DTC shipping permit (roughly 47 states allow some form of DTC wine shipping, far fewer allow spirits, and rules differ sharply by beverage type), many states cap the annual volume a producer may ship to a single consumer household, every shipment must carry age-verification-on-delivery through a compliant common-carrier program requiring separate shipper enrollment, and the producer owes destination-state excise and sometimes sales tax on that state's own filing schedule — on top of its home-state and TTB reporting. A small winery or distillery selling DTC into 15-30 states tracks 15-30 permit renewal dates, 15-30 per-consumer volume caps that must be checked against cumulative shipment history before every order ships, and 15-30 excise-tax filing schedules — almost always in a spreadsheet, and a shipment that blows through a state's cap or ships into an unpermitted state is a violation regulators actively enforce and that can put the producer's home-state ABC production license at risk.
**What to build:** A multi-state DTC shipping permit tracker with renewal deadlines and required annual filings per state; a per-consumer, per-state cumulative-volume-cap checker that blocks an order from shipping if it would push a consumer over their state's annual limit; an excise/sales-tax filing calendar computing each state's next due filing from actual shipment volume; and a common-carrier-enrollment status tracker per state the producer ships into.
**Skill fit:** Each state's DTC alcohol-shipping statute, volume cap, and tax-filing schedule is published, structured regulatory data — assembling and maintaining a 50-state matrix is exactly the reference-data-and-rules-engine problem this series builds repeatedly, and the per-order volume-cap check against cumulative shipment history is a genuine data-engineering feature a spreadsheet cannot practically perform at order time.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the permit-renewal tracker, the per-consumer volume-cap checker for the top 15 DTC-shipping states, and the excise-tax filing calendar. First 5-8 clients through a wine-industry trade association.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — WineAmerica and state winery associations (many run DTC-shipping-compliance webinars for small members) are a direct channel, plus the American Craft Spirits Association for the smaller but fast-growing DTC-spirits-shipping segment.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149-349/mo per producer depending on number of states shipped into. 45-55 producers × $249/mo avg = $11,205-13,695/mo.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every DTC order triggers a fresh volume-cap check against a consumer's running annual total, and every state's excise-tax filing recurs on its own schedule — continuous, order-volume-driven compliance that scales with the exact revenue channel the producer is trying to grow.
**Biggest risk:** A shipment violating a destination state's cap or an unpermitted-state shipment can trigger a home-state ABC license action, not just a destination-state fine — mitigate by making the volume-cap block a hard stop at order time, since prevention protects the producer's core production license.
**Growth path:** Side project → 15 producers ($3.7K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 32 producers ($8K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 45-55 producers plus a common-carrier-enrollment concierge service upsell for producers entering new states.

## This Session's Pick: #5 (DTC Alcohol Shipping) for the widest buyer pool and clearest growth-aligned pitch; #1 (Human Composting/Alkaline Hydrolysis) for first-mover advantage in a brand-new category

DTC alcohol shipping compliance stands out because its buyer base — small wineries, breweries, and distilleries actively trying to grow their DTC channel — is both large and growing, and the pitch aligns perfectly with the client's own revenue goals: the more successfully a producer sells direct-to-consumer, the more they need this tool. WineAmerica's active member network and the state associations that already run DTC-compliance webinars for their members give a warm, pre-educated audience that understands the pain immediately.

Human composting and alkaline hydrolysis is the long-game bet. The buyer pool is small today, but it's the only regulated category in this entire repo that is younger than this project itself — no incumbent compliance vendor has built for it yet, and the list of legalizing states keeps growing. A builder who gets there first and keeps the software current as new states adopt NOR and AH statutes has a durable head start that's much harder to replicate in a mature, well-served regulatory niche.
