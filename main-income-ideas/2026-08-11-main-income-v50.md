### 1. Hospice Agency Medicare Certification, IDG Documentation & GIP Billing Compliance
**Problem:** Medicare-certified hospice agencies providing end-of-life palliative care to terminal patients and their families face a multi-layer CMS compliance framework centered on the Conditions of Participation (CoPs), Interdisciplinary Group meeting documentation, election and revocation of the hospice benefit, and level-of-care billing medical necessity — with the General Inpatient (GIP) level being the highest-risk billing area for Medicare hospice audits. IDG meeting documentation: Medicare requires hospice agencies to establish an Interdisciplinary Group (IDG) composed of at minimum a physician, a registered nurse, a social worker, and a pastoral or counseling service provider — and the IDG must meet to review and update each patient's plan of care at least every 15 calendar days. The IDG meeting notes must document the patient's current clinical condition, the patient's and family's goals, and any changes to the plan of care — and must be signed by all IDG members present. Late or incomplete IDG documentation is a primary CMS survey finding. Election of hospice benefit compliance: for each patient electing Medicare hospice, the hospice must obtain a signed hospice election statement on the first day of care, a physician certification of terminal illness (CTI) certifying a prognosis of 6 months or less if the illness runs its normal course, and for the third and subsequent 90-day certification periods, a face-to-face encounter with the patient by a hospice physician or nurse practitioner within 30 days before the start of the period. Missing or late CTIs and face-to-face encounters are the most common Medicare hospice overpayment triggers found in Office of Inspector General (OIG) audits. GIP level of care documentation: Medicare covers General Inpatient level of care (billed at a significantly higher daily rate than routine home care) only when the patient has acute symptoms that cannot be managed in a lower-intensity setting. CMS and RAC auditors scrutinize GIP claims — GIP days without documented acute symptom management clinical rationale generate automatic claim denials and overpayment demands. Small to mid-size hospices (50-200 census patients, 10-30 clinical staff) managing IDG meeting schedules, CTI and face-to-face encounter deadlines, and GIP documentation requirements across a changing patient census without systematic tracking generate recurring compliance gaps.
**What to build:** A hospice agency Medicare compliance management service — an IDG meeting scheduler and documentation tracker (for each active patient: the most recent IDG meeting date, the next IDG meeting due date calculated as 15 calendar days from the most recent meeting, the IDG meeting documentation status — draft, signed by all members, or missing a required IDG member signature — and any patients whose IDG meeting is overdue — with a daily alert list showing every patient whose next IDG meeting is due within 5 days and any patient whose IDG meeting is already past due), a certification of terminal illness and face-to-face encounter calendar (for each enrolled patient: the hospice election date, the current benefit period — initial 90-day, second 90-day, or subsequent 60-day periods — the benefit period end date, the CTI due date for the next benefit period, the face-to-face encounter due date for the third and subsequent benefit periods — calculated as no later than 30 days before the start of the period — and the face-to-face encounter completion status — with tiered alerts beginning 45 days before the face-to-face due date), a GIP level of care documentation log (for each patient receiving GIP level of care: the GIP admission date, the acute symptom management clinical rationale documented in the medical record, the GIP eligibility review date — GIP eligibility must be reassessed daily — the clinical basis for continued GIP need each day, and the GIP transition date with the documented clinical rationale for transitioning to a lower level of care — generating a daily GIP census with documentation completeness status for each GIP patient), a Medicare election statement compliance tracker (for each new patient admission: the hospice election statement signature date confirmed within the first day of care, the physician CTI completion date and certifying physician NPI, and any deficiency in the election documentation — missing patient signature, missing attending physician endorsement — with a 3-day alert if election documentation is incomplete after admission), and a PEPPER (Program for Evaluating Payment Patterns Electronic Report) analysis tool (CMS distributes quarterly PEPPER reports to hospices showing their billing patterns for target areas — GIP utilization rate, live discharge rate, continuous home care utilization — compared to national and jurisdictional medians; tracking the hospice's PEPPER metrics against the median flags billing patterns that indicate elevated audit risk before RAC or ZPIC auditors target the provider).
**Skill fit:** Hospice Conditions of Participation and IDG requirements are codified in 42 CFR Part 418. Medicare hospice benefit election and CTI requirements are documented in CMS Pub. 100-02, Chapter 9. GIP level of care documentation requirements are documented in CMS's hospice LCDs (Local Coverage Determinations). PEPPER report interpretation is documented in the TMF Health Quality Institute's PEPPER User's Guide. ### 2. Motor Carrier FMCSA Operating Authority, DOT Safety Rating & Driver Qualification File Compliance
**Problem:** For-hire motor carriers operating commercial motor vehicles (CMVs) in interstate commerce — trucking companies, flatbed carriers, refrigerated carriers, and passenger motor carriers — must maintain active FMCSA operating authority and driver qualification file compliance under a regulatory framework administered by FMCSA (Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration) that generates both periodic filing obligations and continuous driver records management requirements. FMCSA operating authority (MC number): carriers must hold active FMCSA operating authority, register with the Unified Carrier Registration (UCR) system annually, file MCS-150 Motor Carrier Identification Reports biennially (or when the carrier's information changes), and maintain active FMCSA BOC-3 designation of process agents in every state where the carrier operates. Lapses in UCR registration or BOC-3 designation can result in FMCSA placing the carrier's operating authority Out-of-Service, which prohibits the carrier from dispatching any CMV in interstate commerce until the authority is reinstated. DOT Safety Fitness Determination and SMS monitoring: FMCSA's Safety Measurement System (SMS) calculates Behavior Analysis and Safety Improvement Categories (BASICs) scores for each carrier based on roadside inspection results, crash data, and investigation data — carriers with elevated BASIC percentile scores in categories such as HOS Compliance, Driver Fitness, or Vehicle Maintenance receive FMCSA intervention letters and may receive an Unsatisfactory Safety Fitness Determination (SFD) if the safety data deteriorates — an Unsatisfactory SFD prohibits interstate operations. Driver qualification file compliance: under 49 CFR Part 391, carriers must maintain a driver qualification file for each CDL driver containing: a completed application for employment, a motor vehicle record (MVR) from each state where the driver held a CDL in the past 3 years (obtained within 30 days of hire and annually thereafter), the driver's CDL copy, a medical examiner's certificate with current FMCSA Medical Examiner Registry verification, road test certificate or equivalent, and previous employer safety performance history records. Incomplete DQ files discovered during an FMCSA compliance review generate acute violation findings.
**What to build:** A motor carrier FMCSA operating authority and driver compliance management service — an FMCSA operating authority compliance calendar (tracking the carrier's USDOT number and MC number, UCR annual registration due date and state of registration, MCS-150 biennial filing due date and most recent filing confirmation, BOC-3 process agent designation status in each operating state and any updates required by state of operation changes — with tiered renewal alerts for UCR registration and MCS-150 filings), a DOT SMS monitoring dashboard (pulling the carrier's FMCSA SMS BASIC percentile scores from the FMCSA SAFER system on a monthly basis: HOS Compliance, Fatigued Driving, Driver Fitness, Controlled Substances/Alcohol, Vehicle Maintenance, Cargo-Related, and Crash Indicator percentile scores — with threshold alerts when any BASIC score enters the intervention alert range and a month-over-month trend view showing whether scores are improving or deteriorating), a driver qualification file tracker (for each CDL driver: employment application on file, CDL copy and expiration date, medical examiner's certificate expiration date with FMCSA National Registry verification status, annual MVR pull date and result, road test certificate, previous employer safety performance history inquiry responses received, and DQ file completeness status — with a daily alert list for drivers with medical certificates expiring within 60 days, drivers whose annual MVR is due within 30 days, and any new hire DQ files not yet complete), a drug and alcohol testing program compliance tracker (FMCSA requires carriers to maintain a DOT drug and alcohol testing program — tracking the consortium/TPA the carrier uses, the carrier's random testing pool, the annual minimum random testing rate for drugs and alcohol, tests conducted year-to-date against the required minimum, and any post-accident testing events with the triggering accident date and test completion within required timeframes), and a FMCSA compliance review readiness report (a pre-review self-audit generating the carrier's complete DQ file status for all drivers, SMS BASIC score history, UCR and MCS-150 filing status, drug and alcohol program documentation, and vehicle inspection and maintenance records — organized in the format FMCSA compliance reviewers use during an on-site compliance review).
**Skill fit:** FMCSA operating authority requirements, MCS-150 filing requirements, and driver qualification file requirements are codified in 49 CFR Parts 390-396. UCR registration requirements are codified in 49 CFR Part 367. FMCSA SMS BASIC score methodology is documented in the FMCSA SMS Methodology document. Building the operating authority compliance calendar, SMS monitoring dashboard, driver qualification file tracker, drug testing program tracker, and compliance review readiness report are data management and analytics problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. ATA (American Trucking Associations) and OOIDA (Owner-Operator Independent Drivers Association) member networks provide defined buyer communities.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build an FMCSA operating authority compliance calendar with UCR and MCS-150 cycle management, a DOT SMS BASIC percentile score monitoring dashboard with monthly score tracking and intervention threshold alerts, a driver qualification file tracker with all 49 CFR Part 391 required elements and expiration date management, a DOT drug and alcohol program compliance tracker with annual minimum random testing rate management, and an FMCSA compliance review readiness report; first 5-8 motor carrier clients through OOIDA member outreach while the driver qualification file tracker and SMS monitoring dashboard are validated against real carrier fleet situations.

### 3. Mortgage Loan Originator Multi-State NMLS License, SAFE Act CE & State Mortgage Regulator Examination Readiness
**Problem:** Individual mortgage loan originators (MLOs) who originate residential mortgage loans at independent mortgage brokerages, mortgage bankers, and correspondent lenders — working under their employer's company license but holding individual MLO licenses through NMLS — face a multi-state licensing compliance burden that has intensified as remote work has enabled MLOs to originate loans in states beyond their primary market. Individual MLO NMLS licensing: MLOs must hold a state MLO license in every state where they originate residential mortgage loans, with each state's license requiring: a completed NMLS application with credit report and background check, a sponsorship from a state-licensed mortgage company, a surety bond (in some states), and in most states passing the NMLS National Test with Uniform State Content (UST) and the state-specific test (where applicable). License renewal is annual in every state, requiring online renewal through NMLS, payment of the state license renewal fee, and confirmation of the annual CE requirement. SAFE Act annual continuing education: the SAFE Act requires every licensed MLO to complete at least 8 hours of NMLS-approved continuing education annually before renewing their license, including 3 hours of federal law, 2 hours of ethics (including fraud, consumer protection, and fair lending), 2 hours of non-traditional mortgage lending standards, and 1 hour of undefined instruction — plus state-specific CE hours required by individual states beyond the federal 8-hour minimum. Failure to complete the required CE before the annual license renewal deadline results in license expiration. MLOs who originate in multiple states may have CE requirements totaling 12-20 hours across the state-specific requirements stacked on top of the federal 8-hour baseline. State mortgage regulator examination readiness: in states with state-chartered mortgage company examination programs (conducted by the state department of financial institutions or banking), MLOs employed at state-licensed mortgage companies may be subject to state examination review of loan files for TRID (TILA-RESPA Integrated Disclosure) compliance, anti-predatory lending compliance, and loan origination fee disclosure accuracy.
**What to build:** A mortgage loan originator multi-state NMLS license and continuing education compliance management service — a multi-state NMLS license portfolio (for each state where the MLO holds an active license: state, NMLS license number, license status, annual renewal deadline, renewal fee, and sponsoring company sponsorship status — with tiered renewal alerts beginning 60 days before the annual renewal deadline and a sponsorship status alert if the MLO's sponsoring company's license in any state lapses), a SAFE Act CE tracker (tracking the MLO's NMLS CE completion for the current license year: federal baseline hours completed by category — federal law, ethics, non-traditional lending, undefined — plus any state-specific CE hours required for each licensed state, CE provider course completion confirmations from NMLS's CE Completion Roster, projected total CE completion against the combined federal plus state-specific requirement, and CE completion deadline — December 31 for most states — with alerts when CE pace falls below projected completion and a final 14-day pre-deadline alert), a state-specific CE requirement map (for each state where the MLO is licensed: the state-specific CE hours required beyond the federal 8-hour minimum — which vary from 0 additional hours in many states to 4-8 additional state-specific hours in states like California, New York, and Texas — and any state-specific CE topic requirements such as state law updates or state-specific ethics modules), an NMLS annual renewal checklist (for each state license renewal: the renewal application status on NMLS, CE completion confirmation, credit report update if required by the state, and annual certification attestation — with a pre-renewal audit confirming all renewal requirements are met before the MLO submits each state's renewal to avoid renewal deficiency notices), and a state mortgage regulator examination readiness log (for each originated loan file: TRID disclosure accuracy — Loan Estimate issued within 3 business days of application, Closing Disclosure issued 3 business days before consummation — the loan origination fee against the applicable state fee cap, and any changed circumstance documentation for re-disclosed Loan Estimates — maintaining a self-audit of originated loan files that can be presented during a state examination without unresolved TRID violations).
**Skill fit:** SAFE Act MLO licensing requirements and CE requirements are codified in 12 USC 5104 and implemented through NMLS. State-specific MLO license requirements and additional CE requirements are documented in each state's residential mortgage lending act and NMLS state licensing requirements. TRID requirements are codified in 12 CFR Part 1026 (Regulation Z). Building the multi-state NMLS license portfolio, SAFE Act CE tracker, state-specific CE requirement map, NMLS annual renewal checklist, and state examination readiness log are data management problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. NAMB (National Association of Mortgage Brokers) and MBA (Mortgage Bankers Association) member networks provide defined buyer communities.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a multi-state NMLS license portfolio for all 50 states and DC with state-specific annual renewal deadlines and fees, a SAFE Act CE tracker with federal baseline plus state-specific CE requirement management, a state-specific CE requirement map for the 25 highest-MLO-density states, an NMLS annual renewal checklist, and a TRID disclosure accuracy loan file log; first 5-8 MLO clients through NAMB member outreach while the multi-state license portfolio and CE tracker are validated against real MLO licensing situations.

### 4. Self-Funded Employer Health Plan TPA State Registration, ACA 1094-C/1095-C Filing & ERISA SPD Compliance
**Problem:** Third-party administrators (TPAs) that administer self-funded employer health plans — providing claims processing, network access, and plan administration services to employers who bear their own health insurance risk rather than purchasing fully-insured group health coverage — face a multi-layer compliance framework that touches state insurance regulation, federal ACA employer mandate reporting, and ERISA plan document requirements. State TPA registration: 42+ states require TPAs administering employer health plan benefits to register with the state department of insurance, with annual renewal, surety bond or letter of credit requirements, and in many states audited financial statement submission demonstrating minimum net worth. Operating as a non-registered TPA in a state that requires registration creates state insurance law enforcement exposure for the TPA and potentially for the employer clients whose plans are being administered without a registered TPA. ACA Forms 1094-C and 1095-C compliance: applicable large employers (ALEs — those with 50+ full-time equivalent employees) must file ACA employer mandate reporting annually with the IRS — Form 1094-C (transmittal) and Form 1095-C (individual statement for each full-time employee) — and distribute Forms 1095-C to all full-time employees by the annual employee furnishing deadline (January 31 of the following year) and file with the IRS by the paper filing deadline (February 28) or electronic filing deadline (March 31). TPAs who administer self-funded employer plans often take on the 1094-C and 1095-C preparation obligation as a value-added service for their ALE employer clients — but missed IRS filing deadlines generate per-form IRS penalties of $310-620 per form (with a maximum annual penalty of $3.9M for very large employers). ERISA summary plan description currency: ERISA requires every self-funded employer health plan to maintain and distribute a Summary Plan Description (SPD) describing the plan's benefits, exclusions, claim procedures, and appeal rights — and the SPD must be updated and redistributed within 210 days after the end of the plan year in which a material modification occurs. Outdated SPDs — which fail to reflect benefit changes, network changes, or prescription drug formulary updates — are a primary DOL audit finding for self-funded employer plans.
**What to build:** A TPA state registration, ACA reporting, and ERISA SPD compliance management service — a multi-state TPA registration portfolio (for each state where the TPA operates: state insurance department registration number, annual renewal deadline, surety bond carrier and bond amount and expiration date, audited financial statement submission deadline and filing confirmation, and ### 5. Dialysis Facility Medicare Certification, ESRD Quality Incentive Program Data Submission & State License
**Problem:** Medicare-certified end-stage renal disease (ESRD) dialysis facilities — both hospital-based and freestanding dialysis clinics — that provide outpatient hemodialysis and peritoneal dialysis treatment to Medicare ESRD patients face a compliance framework with direct Medicare payment consequences tied to quality reporting performance. Medicare certification: ESRD facilities must be certified by CMS through state health department survey assessing compliance with the ESRD Conditions for Coverage (42 CFR Part 494), with any Conditions Not Met requiring a 90-day corrective action submission. State dialysis facility license: dialysis facilities must hold state-issued dialysis facility or end-stage renal disease facility licenses in most states, with annual renewal and state health department inspection compliance. ESRD Quality Incentive Program (QIP): CMS's ESRD QIP is the payment incentive program that adjusts Medicare dialysis payments based on quality performance — facilities that score below the Total Performance Score (TPS) minimum threshold receive a Medicare payment reduction of up to 2% applied to every Medicare ESRD claim for the entire following payment year. QIP measures include clinical topics (Kt/V adequacy for in-center hemodialysis, hypercalcemia rates, long-term catheter use rates) and reporting measures (patient experience via ICH CAHPS survey participation, clinical depression screening documentation, NHSN bloodstream infection data submission). NHSN bloodstream infection data submission: all dialysis facilities must submit monthly bloodstream infection and access-related bloodstream infection data to the CDC's National Healthcare Safety Network (NHSN) Dialysis Component — with a monthly data submission deadline of the 15th of the following month. Missed or late NHSN submissions generate QIP reporting measure penalties in the TPS calculation. Water treatment system compliance: dialysis water must meet AAMI (Association for the Advancement of Medical Instrumentation) standards with documented water quality testing — monthly chemical analysis, quarterly microbiological testing, and annual microbiological testing of the entire water system — and documented corrective actions for any out-of-specification results. Water quality documentation failures are the most common ESRD facility survey deficiency.
**What to build:** An ESRD dialysis facility Medicare compliance management service — a CMS ESRD Conditions for Coverage compliance tracker (tracking the facility's CMS certification number, most recent state health department survey date and any Conditions Not Met with required corrective action submission deadline and corrective action documentation status, and the 90-day corrective action submission window — with tiered alerts for corrective action deadlines), a state dialysis facility license calendar (for each operating state: state license number, annual renewal deadline, most recent state inspection date and result, any outstanding deficiency findings with required plan of correction, and any state-specific water quality or staffing requirements beyond CMS Conditions for Coverage — with tiered renewal alerts), an ESRD QIP performance monitoring dashboard (for the facility's current QIP performance year: each clinical and reporting measure in the QIP, the facility's most recently available performance data for each measure, the measure's achievement and improvement point thresholds, the facility's projected TPS based on current performance, and whether the current projected TPS falls above or below the payment reduction threshold — enabling the facility administrator to see the payment impact risk from current clinical and reporting performance before the QIP payment year begins), an NHSN dialysis data submission calendar (for each month's NHSN Dialysis Component submission: the data collection period, the 15th-of-following-month submission deadline, the NHSN submission confirmation, and any NHSN data validation errors requiring correction — with a monthly alert 7 days before the submission deadline confirming the monthly data is collected and the submission is queued), and a water treatment compliance documentation log (for the facility's water treatment system: monthly chemical analysis results with AAMI limits comparison, quarterly microbiological testing results, annual system microbiological testing results, any out-of-specification results with corrective action documentation, and the preventive maintenance service log for the water treatment system components — with alerts for upcoming monthly, quarterly, and annual testing due dates based on the prior test date).
**Skill fit:** ESRD Conditions for Coverage are codified in 42 CFR Part 494. ESRD QIP measure specifications and TPS calculation methodology are documented in the CMS ESRD QIP Final Rule and QIP Technical Notes. NHSN Dialysis Component data submission requirements are documented in the CDC NHSN Dialysis Component Protocol. AAMI water quality standards for dialysis are documented in AAMI TIR11 and ANSI/AAMI RD52.
