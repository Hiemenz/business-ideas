### 1. Auto Transport Broker & Carrier FMCSA Operating Authority, Bonding & Carrier-Vetting Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Companies that broker or physically haul vehicles — via car-hauler trailer — on behalf of customers relocating a car, dealerships repositioning inventory, or auction houses moving purchased vehicles must hold FMCSA operating authority: a "Property (except Household Goods)" broker authority (MC number) under 49 CFR Part 371 if arranging transport through subcontracted carriers, backed by a $75,000 BMC-84 surety bond or BMC-85 trust fund, or a motor carrier authority if hauling with its own car-hauler trucks — each requiring its own BOC-3 process-agent designation filed in every state the company operates in. Every broker must confirm the subcontracted carrier's operating authority is active, its cargo/auto-hauler insurance meets FMCSA minimums, and its safety rating is acceptable before dispatching a vehicle to that carrier — assigning a car to a carrier whose authority has lapsed or insurance has expired is exactly the finding an FMCSA compliance review targets, and it exposes the broker to full liability for a vehicle damaged in transit with no valid coverage standing behind it. Brokers and carriers alike must renew Unified Carrier Registration (UCR) annually based on fleet size, and every vehicle transported requires a signed Bill of Lading paired with a pre-transport photographed condition inspection to resolve pickup/delivery damage disputes — a dispute a broker without a documented pre-transport photo record simply loses. A small auto transport broker dispatching dozens of vehicles a week across a rotating pool of 30-80 subcontracted carriers currently tracks each carrier's authority/insurance currency, its own UCR renewal, and per-vehicle inspection documentation on a dispatcher's spreadsheet, with no systematic check confirming the carrier assigned to today's vehicle is actually authorized and insured before the car is loaded.

**What to build:** A carrier-vetting database cross-referencing each subcontracted carrier's FMCSA authority status, insurance-certificate currency, and safety rating (via FMCSA's public SAFER lookup), with a "cleared to dispatch" gate blocking a vehicle assignment to a carrier whose authority or insurance has lapsed; the broker's or carrier's own operating-authority, BMC-84/85 bond, and UCR renewal tracker; and a per-vehicle Bill of Lading and photo-condition-inspection log.

**Skill fit:** FMCSA's SAFER system publishes carrier authority and insurance status through a public lookup, making the carrier-vetting gate a direct data-integration problem, and the operating-authority/bond/UCR renewal tracker is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a vehicle-transport niche entirely distinct from the interstate household-goods moving broker idea already in this repo, whose valuation-election-and-booklet framework never touches a single titled vehicle's own pre-transport condition inspection.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends: the carrier-vetting/SAFER-lookup gate, the operating-authority/bond/UCR renewal tracker, and the per-vehicle BOL/condition-inspection log. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small independent auto transport brokers.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Intermodal Auto Transporter Conference of America (IATCA) and the Auto Haulers Association of America member directories reach exactly the small brokers and owner-operators currently reconciling carrier authority and UCR renewal on a dispatcher's spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-399/mo per broker depending on monthly vehicle volume. 35-45 brokers × $290/mo avg = $10,150-13,050/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every vehicle dispatched reopens the carrier-vetting check, UCR renews annually against fleet size, and every new carrier added to the broker's rotating pool compounds the number of simultaneously tracked authority-and-insurance records — a continuous, dispatch-volume-driven compliance stream.

**Biggest risk:** Large national broker platforms (Central Dispatch, Super Dispatch) already serve bigger operators. Mitigate by explicitly targeting the small independent brokers those platforms' pricing and complexity price out.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 brokers ($4.4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 35-45 brokers ($10.2-13.1K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding an insurer-facing damage-dispute export built from the photo-inspection log.

### 2. Real Estate Pre-Licensing & Continuing Education School State Course-Provider Approval & Completion-Filing Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Real estate schools offering state-mandated pre-licensing coursework (commonly 60-180 classroom hours depending on the state) and post-licensing continuing education must themselves hold state real estate commission approval as a course provider — a separate accreditation from any individual agent's own license — renewed on its own cycle and requiring every instructor to hold current instructor certification, every course's curriculum to be re-submitted and re-approved whenever the state updates its required-topic list (a fair-housing-law update, an agency-disclosure rule change), and — critically — every completed course to be filed electronically with the state real estate commission within a fixed window (commonly 10-30 days) so the student's hours actually count toward their license application or CE renewal. A school whose course approval lapses, or that fails to file a class roster's completion hours before the state's window closes, generates exactly the finding a state real estate commission audit is built to catch — and a student whose class was never properly filed can be blocked from renewing their own license, a failure that ends a school's referral relationship with the brokerages that send it students. A small independent real estate school running several concurrent pre-licensing and CE sections across in-person and online formats currently tracks per-course state approval status, per-instructor certification currency, and per-class completion-filing deadlines on an office manager's spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific class roster was actually filed with the state before the window closed.

**What to build:** A per-course state approval and curriculum-resubmission tracker flagging when a state's required-topic list changes; a per-instructor certification-currency tracker; and a per-class-roster completion-filing deadline tracker with a "filed" confirmation gate and an overdue-filing alert.

**Skill fit:** State real estate commission course-provider approval requirements and completion-filing formats are public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-course and per-class trigger, and the filing-deadline gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a real estate education niche entirely distinct from every real estate agent/broker license-compliance idea already in this repo, none of which involve a school's own course-provider accreditation or its class-roster completion-filing obligation to the state.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends: the course-approval tracker, the instructor-certification tracker, and the completion-filing deadline gate. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small independent real estate schools.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Association of Real Estate License Law Officials (ARELLO) and state real estate educator association member directories reach exactly the small schools currently reconciling course approval and completion filing on a spreadsheet with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-399/mo per school depending on course-section volume. 40-50 schools × $280/mo avg = $11,200-14,000/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new course section reopens the completion-filing deadline, every state topic-list update reopens the curriculum-resubmission question, and a growing school running more sections compounds the number of simultaneously tracked filing deadlines.

**Biggest risk:** Large national real estate education platforms (Kaplan, Colibri) dominate the online-only segment. Mitigate by targeting small in-person and hybrid schools tied to local brokerages, where the multi-format, multi-instructor filing load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 schools ($4.2K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 40-50 schools ($11.2-14K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding a brokerage-facing student-progress dashboard schools can offer as a referral incentive.

### 3. Adult Traffic & Defensive Driving School State-Approved Course Provider & Court/DMV Completion-Filing Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Traffic schools offering court-ordered or DMV-approved defensive-driving courses for point reduction, ticket dismissal, or insurance-discount eligibility — a fundamentally different audience and regulatory relationship from a teen driver's-education school — must hold state approval as a traffic-violator-school course provider (administered by the state DMV in some states, by the county court system in others, such as California's Traffic Violator School licensing under the DMV's Occupational Licensing division), with every instructor separately certified and every course's curriculum matched to the state's minimum-hour and topic requirements. Every completed course triggers an electronic completion-certificate filing directly to the DMV or to the specific court that ordered the class — filed within a fixed window, commonly 30-60 days — since a student's ticket dismissal or point reduction depends entirely on the school's filing actually reaching the right court or DMV record before that student's own compliance deadline. A school operating court-ordered classes across multiple counties has to track which court each student's citation came from, since courts commonly maintain their own separate approved-provider list and filing format distinct from the DMV's own list — a school approved by the DMV but not by a specific county's traffic court can't file a completion for a student whose citation came from that county, a gap that isn't obvious until the filing bounces back. A small traffic school running online and in-person sections across several counties currently tracks per-student citation-source court, completion-filing deadlines, and multi-jurisdiction approved-provider status on an office spreadsheet, with no systematic check confirming a specific student's completion was actually filed with the correct court or DMV before their compliance deadline passed.

**What to build:** A per-jurisdiction (DMV and individual court) approved-provider status tracker; a per-student citation-source-to-filing-destination matcher ensuring a completion is routed to the right court or DMV; and a completion-filing deadline tracker with an overdue-filing alert before a student's compliance window closes.

**Skill fit:** State DMV and county traffic-court approved-provider lists and filing formats are public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-student, per-jurisdiction trigger, and the citation-source-to-filing-destination matcher is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an adult traffic-violator-school niche entirely distinct from the non-commercial driving school/driver's-education idea already in this repo, whose single-state-DMV filing model has no multi-court routing problem at all, and from the CDL ELDT idea, which files to an entirely separate federal registry.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends: the approved-provider tracker, the citation-to-court matcher, and the filing-deadline tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent traffic schools.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the American Driver and Traffic Safety Education Association (ADTSEA) and state traffic-school-operator associations reach exactly the small schools currently reconciling multi-court filing deadlines on a spreadsheet with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149-299/mo per school depending on monthly student volume and number of jurisdictions filed into. 45-55 schools × $220/mo avg = $9,900-12,100/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every enrolled student reopens the citation-to-court filing question, and every new county a growing school expands into adds a fresh approved-provider relationship it has to track and maintain.

**Biggest risk:** Large online traffic-school platforms (Aceable, iDriveSafely) dominate the DMV-approved online segment. Mitigate by targeting small schools handling court-ordered, multi-jurisdiction filings these national platforms don't specialize in.

**Growth path:** Side project → 18 schools ($4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 45-55 schools ($9.9-12.1K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding a court-facing bulk-filing export a school can hand a clerk's office directly.

### 4. Commercial Air Tour Operator FAA Part 136/NPATMA Flight-Allocation & Air Tour Management Plan Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Helicopter and fixed-wing air tour operators flying commercial sightseeing tours — over the Grand Canyon, Hawaii's volcanoes, or other national-park-adjacent airspace — operate under 14 CFR Part 136's commercial air tour reporting rule and, for tours near a national park, the National Parks Air Tour Management Act (NPATMA), which requires an FAA/National Park Service-approved Air Tour Management Plan (ATMP) or interim operating authority capping the exact number of commercial air tour flights an operator may conduct annually over specific park airspace — a hard flight-count quota an operator must track cumulatively across the season, since exceeding the allocated flight count is a federal violation distinct from any ordinary Part 135 air-carrier finding. Operators flying under an ATMP or interim authority must also fly aircraft meeting the "quiet technology" noise-reduction standard where required, and every quarter must file a Part 136 report to the FAA disclosing the exact number of tour flights conducted — a filing separate from, and in addition to, the operator's ordinary Part 135 Operations Specifications compliance. A small air tour operator running several aircraft through a national park's flight-allocation season has to track its real-time flight count against the park-specific annual cap, confirm each aircraft in its fleet meets the applicable quiet-technology requirement before it's assigned to a park-airspace tour, and file the quarterly Part 136 report on time — an operator that loses track of its flight count and exceeds the ATMP's annual allocation risks losing its operating authority for that specific park entirely, the single most damaging outcome for an operator whose business is built around that exact route. A small operator running 3-6 aircraft across a single park's tour season currently tracks cumulative flight counts, quiet-technology aircraft assignment, and quarterly Part 136 filing deadlines on a dispatcher's spreadsheet, with no systematic alert before a specific park's annual flight allocation is about to be exceeded.

**What to build:** A per-park cumulative flight-count tracker computing remaining allocation in real time against every tour flown, with an alert well before the annual cap is reached; a per-aircraft quiet-technology-compliance flag gating an aircraft from park-airspace tour assignment until its noise-reduction status is confirmed; and a Part 136 quarterly filing tracker with a report-generation tool rolling up the quarter's flight count.

**Skill fit:** FAA Part 136 and NPATMA's published flight-allocation and quiet-technology requirements are public federal regulatory data with a concrete per-flight, per-park trigger, and the cumulative-allocation tracker is the same deterministic "compute the constraint from the trigger event" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a commercial-air-tour niche entirely distinct from the Part 135 on-demand air charter operator idea already in this repo, whose Ops Specs/duty-time framework has no park-specific flight-allocation quota or quiet-technology-aircraft requirement of any kind.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends: the flight-count tracker, the quiet-technology aircraft flag, and the Part 136 quarterly filing tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small air tour operators flying national-park-adjacent routes.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — the United States Air Tour Association (USATA) member directory and National Air Transportation Association (NATA) sightseeing-operator contacts reach exactly the small operators currently tracking flight-allocation counts on a spreadsheet ahead of every FAA quarterly filing.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per operator depending on fleet size and number of park routes flown. 22-30 operators × $420/mo avg = $9,240-12,600/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every flight flown reopens the cumulative-allocation count for its specific park, the quiet-technology gate applies to every aircraft assignment, and the quarterly Part 136 filing recurs on its own independent clock regardless of season — a continuous, flight-volume-driven compliance stream with real license-continuation stakes.

**Biggest risk:** Air tour operations are concentrated in a small number of parks with an ATMP or interim authority in place, capping the total addressable operator count. Mitigate by proving the product out in the highest-volume markets (Grand Canyon, Hawaii) first, then expanding to every NPATMA-regulated park route.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10 operators ($4.2K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 22-30 operators ($9.2-12.6K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding a Park Service-facing compliance export an operator can produce the moment an ATMP renewal review comes up.

### 5. OSHA Outreach Training Provider DOL Authorization, Trainer-Card Issuance & OTI Database Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Companies and independent instructors delivering OSHA 10-hour and 30-hour Outreach Training courses — the construction and general-industry safety courses many employers require before a worker can be dispatched to a jobsite — must operate as an authorized trainer under the OSHA Outreach Training Program, administered through one of OSHA's regional OSHA Training Institute (OTI) Education Centers, which requires every individual trainer to hold current Outreach Trainer authorization (renewed every four years through a trainer update course) and to submit every completed student roster to OSHA's official card-issuance system within the program's required documentation window so the student's DOL-recognized completion card is actually issued — a course taught by a trainer whose authorization has lapsed, or a roster never submitted, produces a class of students who never actually receive a legitimate OSHA card despite having sat through the training, a failure that surfaces only when a student's card is rejected at a jobsite gate check months later and traces straight back to the training provider. Authorized trainers must also maintain the specific curriculum-hour breakdown OSHA's Outreach Program mandates (a fixed minimum number of hours on mandatory topics like fall protection and hazard communication, with the remainder as elective topics an employer can customize), and a trainer running dozens of classes a year for construction contractors and industrial employers has to track curriculum-hour compliance per class alongside trainer-authorization renewal and roster-submission deadlines. A small OSHA training provider running several instructors and a full calendar of onsite and public classes currently tracks trainer-authorization currency, per-class curriculum-hour compliance, and roster-submission-to-card-issuance status on an office spreadsheet, with no systematic check confirming a specific class's roster was actually submitted and cards issued before students show up at a jobsite expecting a valid card.

**What to build:** A per-trainer OSHA Outreach authorization and four-year renewal tracker with a "cleared to teach" gate; a per-class curriculum-hour compliance checklist matched to OSHA's mandatory/elective topic split; and a roster-submission tracker confirming each completed class's student roster was actually submitted to the card-issuance system, with an overdue-submission alert.

**Skill fit:** OSHA's published Outreach Training Program requirements, trainer-authorization renewal cycle, and curriculum-hour rules are public, structured federal data with a concrete per-trainer, per-class trigger, and the roster-submission tracker is the same deterministic "confirm the filing actually happened" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a workplace-safety-training niche entirely distinct from every clinical or trade-licensing-school idea already in this repo, none of which involve a DOL-recognized card-issuance system or an OTI Education Center's trainer-authorization framework.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends: the trainer-authorization tracker, the curriculum-hour checklist, and the roster-submission tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent OSHA Outreach training providers.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the OTI Education Center network's own authorized-trainer contact lists and the American Society of Safety Professionals (ASSP) member directory reach exactly the small providers currently reconciling trainer authorization and roster-submission deadlines on a spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $18/mo per authorized trainer tracked. Average client (6 trainers) = $199 + $108 = $307/mo. 32-40 providers × $307/mo = $9,824-12,280/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every class taught reopens the curriculum-hour and roster-submission check, trainer authorization renews on its own four-year clock per instructor, and every new trainer a growing provider adds compounds the number of simultaneously tracked authorization records.

**Biggest risk:** A very small provider running one instructor and a handful of classes a year has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward providers running 3+ trainers and a steady calendar of contractor-facing classes, where the multi-trainer/multi-class load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 providers ($4.6K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 32-40 providers ($9.8-12.3K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding a contractor-facing bulk-verification tool letting a general contractor confirm every worker's OSHA card is legitimate before jobsite access.
