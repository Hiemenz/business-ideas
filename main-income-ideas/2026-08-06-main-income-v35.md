### 1. Dry Cleaning & Commercial Laundry EPA NESHAP Solvent Compliance & State Environmental Permit Management
**Problem:** Dry cleaning facilities using perchloroethylene (perc, PCE) — the dominant dry cleaning solvent historically — or hydrocarbon solvents are regulated under EPA's National Emission Standards for Hazardous Air Pollutants for Dry Cleaning Facilities (40 CFR Part 63, Subpart M for perc dry cleaners). Federal NESHAP compliance requirements for perc dry cleaners include: monthly leak detection inspections of all machine seals, gaskets, hoses, and fittings using a halogenated hydrocarbon detector (equipment and operator method specified by the standard), documenting each inspection result and corrective action taken for any leak found, maintaining a machine solvent consumption log (annual perc usage determines the facility's NESHAP source classification and compliance tier), and following machine maintenance and repair procedures when components fail. Many states have adopted NESHAP requirements into their state air quality permit programs and imposed additional requirements — California's perc dry cleaner regulations are the most stringent, with active phase-out requirements in several localities. Small independent dry cleaners (single-location operations) are the most compliance-challenged segment: they have the same EPA NESHAP documentation obligations as large dry cleaning chains but with no environmental compliance staff, and EPA enforcement sweeps of dry cleaners frequently find inspection and recordkeeping deficiencies at independent locations.
**What to build:** A dry cleaning EPA NESHAP and state environmental permit compliance management service — a monthly leak detection inspection log (capturing each month's inspection date, inspector name, machine components inspected, any leaks detected with component location and description, and corrective action taken and completion date — formatted to satisfy EPA NESHAP recordkeeping requirements), a solvent consumption tracking system (logging monthly perc or hydrocarbon solvent purchases and machine fills to calculate annual solvent usage and confirm the facility's NESHAP source category — major source vs. area source — which determines the applicable compliance tier and any annual performance testing requirements), a state environmental permit compliance calendar (tracking the facility's state air quality permit renewal deadline, any state-required reporting due dates, and any permit conditions specific to the state program — with alerts 90/60/30 days before each compliance deadline), a machine maintenance and component replacement log (documenting machine seal and gasket replacements, refrigeration system repairs, solvent recovery drum changes, and any machine service events that NESHAP requires to be recorded), and a compliance documentation package generator (producing the NESHAP required 3-year inspection record package on demand for EPA or state agency inspection, organized by compliance year and machine component).
**Skill fit:** EPA NESHAP 40 CFR Part 63 Subpart M requirements, inspection procedures, and recordkeeping specifications are publicly available in the federal register and EPA guidance documents — building a compliance checklist and inspection log template from these sources is a regulatory research problem. The monthly inspection log (structured per-component inspection records with date, result, and corrective action), solvent consumption tracker (running annual usage calculation for source category determination), state permit calendar, and maintenance log are data management problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. Drycleaning & Laundry Institute (DLI) member directory provides a defined buyer community.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a monthly perc leak detection inspection log with EPA NESHAP-compliant field structure, a solvent consumption tracker with annual usage calculation and source category alert, a state permit compliance calendar for 10 high-dry-cleaner-density states (CA, TX, FL, NY, NJ, PA, IL, OH, GA, NC), and a 3-year inspection record package generator; first 5-8 dry cleaning business clients through DLI member outreach and dry cleaning industry association networks while the inspection log is validated against EPA's NESHAP recordkeeping requirements.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through Drycleaning & Laundry Institute (DLI) member network and regional dry cleaning association outreach (the concentrated professional community for dry cleaning business owners — DLI's annual trade show is the primary industry event), dry cleaning equipment distributor referral networks (distributors who sell and service dry cleaning machines encounter NESHAP compliance gaps at every installation and maintenance visit and refer compliance tracking services to their customers), and environmental consulting firm referral networks (EHS consultants who audit dry cleaning facilities for EPA compliance refer ongoing monitoring and recordkeeping services to facilities that need continuous support after an initial audit).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per dry cleaning facility: $150-350/mo (depending on facility size and state complexity — a single-machine perc dry cleaner in a state with standard NESHAP requirements: $150/mo; a multi-machine dry cleaner in California with state-specific perc phase-out compliance requirements: $350/mo). Managing 50-80 facility retainers at $250/mo average = **$12,500-20,000/mo**. EPA NESHAP compliance audit preparation packages (one-time $300-600 per facility when EPA or state agency announces an inspection sweep) generate concentrated supplemental revenue. Reaching 50-80 facility retainers is achievable given an estimated 20,000+ perc dry cleaning locations in the US — a very small market penetration fraction.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** EPA NESHAP monthly inspections are required every calendar month for every perc dry cleaning facility — a 3-year inspection record must be maintained continuously and produced on demand during EPA inspections. Annual solvent consumption must be tracked for source category confirmation. State permit renewals are annual. EPA enforcement sweeps of dry cleaning facilities in specific regions generate concentrated demand for compliance documentation reviews that the existing client base can be upsold rapidly.
**Biggest risk:** An EPA inspector who finds a gap in the monthly inspection log — a single month where no inspection was documented — can cite the facility for a recordkeeping violation even if inspections were actually conducted. Build the monthly inspection log with a calendar-month completion requirement: the system generates a prompt at the beginning of each month for the inspection to be completed, and the dashboard displays any month within the current 3-year record window that has no completed inspection entry.
**Growth path:** Side project → monthly inspection log and solvent consumption tracker for 10 target states, first 5-8 facility retainers through DLI outreach. ### 2. Marine Dealer USCG Vessel Documentation, State Watercraft Titling & Dealer License Compliance
**Problem:** Marine dealers selling new and used recreational and commercial boats must navigate compliance across three distinct regulatory layers that interact at every transaction: USCG National Vessel Documentation Center (NVDC) federal documentation for qualifying vessels (generally vessels 5 net tons or larger operated on navigable waters of the US — a threshold that includes most boats 26 feet and longer), which requires the dealer to assist buyers with documentation applications and maintain their own dealer abstractor relationships for title searches; state watercraft registration and titling for vessels that don't qualify for USCG documentation (most boats under 26 feet are state-titled and registered), with title transfer procedures varying significantly by state (some states title through the DMV, others through the state fish and wildlife or marine department) and varying timeframes for temporary registration permits that allow a boat to be operated before permanent registration is complete; and state marine dealer licensing (required in most states, with annual renewal, surety bond requirements varying by state from $10,000 to $100,000, display lot or business address requirements, and in some states, a separate license for used boat dealers vs. new boat dealers). Small marine dealerships (selling 50-300 boats per year across multiple brands) process a complex mix of new boat sales (requiring manufacturer's certificate of origin, state certificate of title, and/or USCG documentation application), used boat purchases and resales (requiring lien searches, title transfers, and prior USCG documentation abstracts), and trade-in processing (requiring simultaneous payoff of any existing marine lien and new title issuance).
**What to build:** A marine dealer compliance management service — a per-transaction title and documentation workflow manager (tracking each boat sale from contract to title completion: vessel HIN, make, model, year, transaction type — new vs. used vs. trade-in — buyer's registration/documentation choice, documents required for the applicable state or USCG process, documents received and verified, and title application submission status), a state watercraft title transfer tracker (for each state where the dealer transacts: the applicable title transfer procedure, required documents, fees, and processing timeframe — with alerts when a temporary registration permit is approaching expiration before permanent registration is complete), a USCG documentation workflow manager (for vessels qualifying for federal documentation: tracking the buyer's documentation application preparation, abstract search results, any pending maritime liens, and NVDC processing status), a state dealer license compliance calendar (tracking the dealer's license renewal deadline, required annual renewal documents, surety bond expiration and renewal, and any state-required continuing education for license renewal), and a lien and financing verification tracker (documenting that each used boat sale has been verified for prior liens through a state title search or USCG abstract search, protecting the dealer from downstream liability when a boat is sold with an undisclosed prior lien).
**Skill fit:** USCG NVDC documentation procedures, state watercraft title transfer requirements, and state marine dealer licensing statutes are publicly available from each agency's website — building a state-by-state transaction compliance database is a regulatory research problem. The per-transaction title workflow manager (a structured deal file tracking documents received and submitted), state title tracker, USCG documentation workflow, dealer license calendar, and lien verification tracker are data management and compliance calendar problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. National Marine Manufacturers Association (NMMA) and Marine Retailers Association of the Americas (MRAA) member directories provide defined buyer communities.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a per-transaction title and documentation workflow manager for the 15 highest-boat-density states (FL, TX, MN, CA, MI, WI, SC, OH, NY, NC, GA, IN, LA, TN, MO), a state watercraft title transfer tracker with temporary registration permit expiration alerts, a USCG documentation workflow manager, and a dealer license compliance calendar; first 5-8 marine dealer clients through MRAA member outreach while the transaction workflow manager is validated against real dealer title processing workflows.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through Marine Retailers Association of the Americas (MRAA) member network and state marine dealer association outreach (the concentrated professional community for marine dealers — MRAA's annual dealer conference is the primary industry event), marine finance company referral networks (lenders like Essex Credit, Southeast Financial, and Medallion Bank who provide marine loans encounter dealer title deficiency issues in every transaction and refer title compliance management services to their dealer clients), and marine dealer management software referral networks (DMS platforms like IDS (Integrated Dealer Systems) and Lightspeed serve as integration partners for transaction-level compliance tracking modules that complement existing DMS workflows).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per marine dealer: $400-900/mo (depending on transaction volume and state complexity — a 75-boat/year single-state dealer: $400/mo; a 250-boat/year multi-state dealer with USCG documentation transactions and complex state title mix: $900/mo). Managing 20-30 dealer retainers at $600/mo average = **$12,000-18,000/mo**. Per-transaction title audit packages (one-time $100-250 per deal for lien search verification coordination and documentation review) generate significant supplemental revenue proportional to transaction volume. Reaching 20-30 dealer retainers requires 3-4 MRAA regional chapter relationships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every boat sale generates a title and documentation compliance event — a 150-boat/year dealer has 150 per-transaction compliance events annually, each requiring correct document sequencing and deadline management. State dealer license renewals are annual. USCG documentation is a federal requirement that runs in parallel with state registration. Marine financing lenders are increasingly requiring documentation of lien search completion before funding, creating external demand for the lien verification tracking component.
**Biggest risk:** A used boat sold with an undisclosed prior lien — because the dealer didn't complete a lien search before acquiring the boat — creates downstream liability for the dealer when the lienholder repossesses the vessel from the new owner. ### 3. Freight Broker FMCSA Operating Authority, Surety Bond & Carrier Compliance Verification Management
**Problem:** Property freight brokers — companies that arrange transportation of cargo by connecting shippers with motor carriers — must maintain FMCSA Operating Authority (a property broker MC number requiring Form OP-1, a BOC-3 Process Agent designation, and continuous $75,000 surety bond or trust fund filing with FMCSA). The surety bond (typically a BMC-84 surety bond or BMC-85 trust fund instrument) must remain continuously in force — any lapse in bond coverage results in immediate automatic revocation of the broker's Operating Authority, which prevents them from legally brokering freight. Beyond their own licensing, freight brokers must verify the compliance of every motor carrier they use to transport freight: each carrier's FMCSA Operating Authority (property carrier MC number in Active status), the carrier's cargo insurance certificate (minimum $100,000 cargo insurance, higher for household goods), the carrier's commercial auto liability insurance certificate (minimum $750,000 for general freight, higher for hazmat), and the carrier's SAFER safety rating (brokers face significant liability exposure when tendering freight to a carrier with an Unsatisfactory or Conditional safety rating). Small freight brokerages (processing 50-500 loads per month) often handle carrier onboarding with a paper file and a manually reviewed FMCSA SAFER lookup — a process that doesn't systematically verify carrier credential currency before each load tender, creating liability exposure when a carrier's insurance lapses or rating changes between loads.
**What to build:** A freight broker FMCSA and carrier compliance management service — a broker operating authority and bond compliance monitor (tracking the broker's FMCSA MC number status, BOC-3 designation confirmation, and surety bond expiration date — with alerts 90/60/30 days before bond renewal and automatic FMCSA authority status checks to confirm Active status), a carrier compliance database and verification workflow (for each carrier in the broker's approved carrier list: MC number, DOT number, cargo insurance certificate with expiration date, commercial auto liability certificate with expiration date, SAFER safety rating, and a carrier agreement status — with automated expiration alerts for each carrier's insurance certificates and a quarterly SAFER rating re-verification), a load tender compliance gate (before a load is tendered to a carrier, the system checks that carrier's current credential status in the compliance database — confirming all insurance is current, Operating Authority is Active, and safety rating is Satisfactory or Not Rated — and flags any carrier whose credentials need renewal before the load is dispatched), a carrier onboarding workflow (collecting and documenting all required carrier credentials during initial onboarding — MC number, DOT number, W-9, insurance certificates, signed carrier agreement, and any carrier-specific special requirements), and a compliance audit trail generator (producing a per-carrier credential history showing every version of each carrier's insurance certificates and safety ratings, with timestamps — providing documentation of broker due diligence if a load is involved in a cargo claim or accident).
**Skill fit:** FMCSA surety bond requirements, carrier Operating Authority verification procedures, and SAFER safety rating system are publicly available through FMCSA's licensing and operations programs — the regulatory framework is well-documented. The broker authority and bond compliance monitor (a status tracker with renewal calendar), carrier compliance database (a per-carrier credential store with expiration tracking), load tender compliance gate (a pre-dispatch credential check), and audit trail generator are data management and compliance calendar problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. FMCSA's SAFER API (available at safer.fmcsa.dot.gov) provides programmatic access to carrier safety data, enabling automated carrier credential verification.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a broker operating authority and bond compliance monitor with FMCSA status alerts, a carrier compliance database with insurance certificate expiration tracking, a load tender compliance gate with carrier credential pre-check, and a carrier onboarding document collection workflow; first 5-8 freight brokerage clients through Transportation Intermediaries Association (TIA) member outreach while the carrier credential tracking system is validated against real brokerage carrier approval workflows.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through Transportation Intermediaries Association (TIA) member network and 3PL broker community outreach (the concentrated professional community for freight brokers — TIA's annual conference is the primary industry event), freight broker surety bond provider referral networks (surety companies and agents who write BMC-84 bonds see bond management and carrier vetting gaps at every renewal and refer compliance management services to their broker clients), and freight broker technology platform referral networks (transportation management systems like Aljex, McLeod, and Tai TMS serve as integration points for carrier compliance modules that work alongside the broker's existing TMS).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per freight brokerage: $400-1,000/mo (depending on carrier pool size and load volume — a 50-carrier-pool brokerage processing 100 loads/month: $400/mo; a 300-carrier-pool brokerage processing 500 loads/month with complex carrier onboarding volume: $1,000/mo). Managing 18-25 brokerage retainers at $600/mo average = **$10,800-15,000/mo**. Carrier onboarding credential review packages (one-time $50-150 per carrier for initial credential verification and onboarding documentation) generate significant supplemental revenue at high-volume brokerages actively growing their carrier network. Reaching 18-25 brokerage retainers requires 3-4 TIA regional chapter relationships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new carrier added to a broker's approved carrier list generates a carrier onboarding compliance event. Every carrier's insurance certificates expire annually or semi-annually — a 200-carrier approved list generates 200-400 insurance expiration events per year requiring re-verification. The broker's own surety bond renews annually. FMCSA's recent increases in broker liability enforcement (including broker liability for cargo claims when carrier vetting was inadequate) have created strong demand for systematic carrier compliance documentation.
**Biggest risk:** A freight broker whose own surety bond lapses — even for a short period — has their FMCSA Operating Authority automatically revoked, preventing them from legally brokering any freight until the bond is reinstated. Build the broker bond compliance monitor with a 90-day advance alert and a daily status check in the 30 days before the bond's expiration date, with escalating alerts that escalate to a phone notification (not just email) if the bond is within 7 days of expiration without confirmed renewal documentation in the system.
**Growth path:** Side project → broker authority and bond monitor, carrier compliance database with expiration tracking, and load tender compliance gate deployed, first 5-8 brokerage retainers through TIA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 10-12 brokerage retainers, carrier onboarding packages generating supplemental revenue (~$4-7K/mo). ### 4. Tax Preparation Firm IRS AFSP, State Non-Credentialed Preparer Registration & CE Compliance Management
**Problem:** Tax preparation firms that employ non-credentialed tax preparers (preparers who are not CPAs, EAs, or attorneys) must track each preparer's annual IRS Annual Filing Season Program (AFSP) participation: completing 18 hours of IRS-approved continuing education annually (including a required 6 hours of federal tax law update, 10 hours of additional federal tax law topics, and 2 hours of ethics), consenting to Circular 230 requirements, and obtaining an IRS Record of Completion — which allows the preparer to represent clients before the IRS at the audit and appeals levels during the tax year of completion. Without AFSP completion, a non-credentialed preparer loses limited representation rights and cannot accompany clients to IRS examination appointments. Several states impose separate non-credentialed tax preparer registration requirements: California requires registration with CTEC (California Tax Education Council — 20 hours of annual CE including a 15-hour California-required curriculum), Oregon requires preparer registration with the Oregon Board of Tax Practitioners, Maryland requires registration with the Maryland DLLR, and New York requires registration with the New York State Department of Taxation. Small and mid-size tax preparation firms (5-50 preparers at one or multiple locations) face significant AFSP tracking complexity: managing 15-40 individual preparers' CE completion, verifying that CE was completed with an IRS-approved CE provider, and confirming that each preparer's Record of Completion was obtained before the filing season cutoff (December 31 of the CE year) so they retain representation rights for the following filing season.
**What to build:** A tax preparation firm AFSP and state preparer compliance management service — a preparer AFSP tracking system (for each non-credentialed preparer: AFSP participation status for the current CE year, CE hours completed by category — federal tax law update, additional federal topics, ethics — CE provider name and IRS-approved provider confirmation, and Record of Completion status), a state preparer registration compliance calendar (for preparers in CTEC, Oregon BTP, Maryland, or New York jurisdictions: tracking the state registration renewal deadline, state CE requirements by subject area, registration fee payment status, and state registration certificate currency), a CE hour completion gap monitor (showing each preparer's current year CE hours by required category and the remaining hours needed by December 31, with alerts when a preparer is behind the pace needed to complete before the filing season cutoff), a firm-level compliance dashboard (showing the firm's overall AFSP completion rate for the current year — number of preparers with completed Records of Completion vs. pending — and any preparer whose credentials would lapse before the next filing season starts), and a preparer PTIN verification tracker (each tax preparer must maintain a current IRS PTIN — Preparer Tax Identification Number — renewed annually by December 31; the system tracks each preparer's PTIN expiration and generates renewal prompts before the PTIN lapses).
**Skill fit:** IRS AFSP requirements and state preparer registration requirements are publicly available from the IRS AFSP program page and each state's preparer registration agency — building a compliance database from these sources is a regulatory research problem. The preparer AFSP tracking system (a per-preparer CE completion record with category tracking), state registration calendar, CE gap monitor, and PTIN tracker are data management and compliance calendar problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. IRS AFSP participation data is accessible through the IRS public AFSP participant directory, enabling verification of Records of Completion.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a preparer AFSP tracking dashboard with CE category progress monitoring and Record of Completion status, a state preparer registration compliance calendar for CTEC (California), Oregon BTP, Maryland, and New York, a firm-level compliance rate dashboard, and a PTIN expiration tracker; first 5-10 tax preparation firm clients through National Association of Tax Professionals (NATP) member outreach and H&R Block franchisee and similar franchise community outreach while the AFSP tracking system is validated against real firm preparer rosters.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through National Association of Tax Professionals (NATP) and National Society of Accountants (NSA) member networks (the concentrated professional communities for tax preparation firm owners and office managers), IRS-approved CE provider referral networks (companies like NATP, AICPA, and The Income Tax School that sell AFSP CE courses encounter firms struggling to track CE completion for their preparers and actively refer tracking management services to their firm clients), and tax franchise association networks (franchise associations for Jackson Hewitt, Liberty Tax, and independent franchise tax offices encounter AFSP tracking gaps across their franchisee bases and value firm-level compliance visibility tools).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per tax preparation firm: $200-600/mo (depending on preparer count and state complexity — a 10-preparer firm in a standard federal AFSP state: $200/mo; a 40-preparer firm in California with CTEC state registration for California-resident preparers and federal AFSP for out-of-state preparers: $600/mo). Managing 30-50 firm retainers at $350/mo average = **$10,500-17,500/mo**. Filing-season AFSP audit preparation packages (one-time $200-400 per firm for a complete firm-level AFSP compliance report as the filing season opens) generate concentrated Q4 revenue. Reaching 30-50 firm retainers requires 3-5 active NATP and NSA chapter relationships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** AFSP is an annual CE requirement for every non-credentialed preparer in every tax preparation firm in the country — a new compliance cycle begins January 1 of every year. PTIN renewal is annual for every preparer. State preparer registration renews annually. The filing season creates an extremely concentrated urgency window (October-December, when AFSP must be completed before the December 31 cutoff) — making the product's value undeniable to any firm that missed a preparer's AFSP completion the prior year.
**Biggest risk:** A preparer who is incorrectly shown as AFSP-complete in the tracking system — because the firm tracked hours from a non-IRS-approved CE provider — may begin representing clients at IRS examinations without valid representation rights, creating liability for the firm. Build the CE hour entry system with an IRS-approved provider verification check: CE entries require the provider name and the provider's IRS-approved CE provider number (published in the IRS CE provider database), and the system warns if the entered provider number doesn't match a known approved provider.
**Growth path:** Side project → AFSP preparer tracking dashboard with CE category monitoring, state registration calendar for CTEC/Oregon/Maryland/NY, and PTIN tracker deployed, first 5-10 firm retainers through NATP outreach. Replaces-part-income → 15-20 firm retainers, filing-season compliance packages generating concentrated Q4 revenue (~$4-7K/mo). ### 5. Cemetery & Funeral Home Pre-Need Trust Account and State Licensing Compliance Management
**Problem:** Funeral homes and cemeteries that accept advance payment for future funeral services (pre-need funeral service contracts) must comply with state pre-need licensing and trust requirements — a regulatory framework entirely separate from at-need funeral service requirements and administered by a completely different state agency (typically the state insurance department, state funeral regulatory board, or state banking department). Pre-need compliance obligations include: maintaining a state pre-need license or registration that requires annual renewal, depositing a required percentage of consumer pre-need contract proceeds into a pre-need merchandise trust or services trust account (typically 75-110% of the contract amount depending on the state and contract type), maintaining trust account records documenting each consumer's pre-need contract balance, trust deposit history, and accrued investment return, making required annual disclosures to trust beneficiaries (in many states, an annual statement must be sent to each pre-need contract holder showing their contract balance and trust account performance), filing annual pre-need trust account reports with the state regulatory agency (typically including trust account balance, the aggregate value of outstanding pre-need contracts, and trust investment portfolio documentation), and maintaining performance bonds or alternative financial assurance instruments in states that require them. Small funeral homes (performing 100-400 funerals per year) that offer pre-need planning may have 200-1,000 active pre-need contracts simultaneously — each requiring accurate trust deposit tracking, annual statement generation, and trust account balance reconciliation against outstanding contract liabilities.
**What to build:** A funeral home and cemetery pre-need trust and state license compliance management service — a pre-need contract and trust deposit tracking system (for each active pre-need contract: contract holder name, contract date, services and merchandise covered, contract amount, required trust deposit percentage per state law, trust deposit confirmation with deposit date and trust account identifier, and current trust account value including accrued interest), a state pre-need license compliance calendar (tracking the funeral home's or cemetery's pre-need license or registration renewal deadline, annual report due date, performance bond expiration and renewal, and any state-required trust account audit completion dates — with tiered alerts before each deadline), an annual trust beneficiary statement generator (producing the required annual statement for each active pre-need contract holder: the contract balance, trust deposit history, accrued interest, and a summary of the services and merchandise covered — in the format required by the state's pre-need statute or regulation), a state annual pre-need report preparation workflow (assembling the required annual report to the state agency: aggregate pre-need contract liability balance, aggregate trust account value, trust investment portfolio documentation, and a reconciliation showing that trust assets meet or exceed the required percentage of aggregate contract liability), and a pre-need contract transfer and cancellation tracker (when a pre-need contract holder moves to a different state or cancels the contract: documenting the refund or transfer calculation per state law, the trust fund withdrawal authorization, and the written refund or transfer confirmation — providing a complete paper trail for each contract termination event).
**Skill fit:** State pre-need statutes and regulations, trust deposit percentage requirements, annual report formats, and annual beneficiary statement requirements are publicly available from each state's pre-need regulatory agency — building a state-by-state pre-need compliance database is a regulatory research problem. Managing 25-35 location retainers at $450/mo average = **$11,250-15,750/mo**.
