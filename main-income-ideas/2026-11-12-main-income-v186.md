# Main Income Ideas - 2026-11-12 (Session 186)

## Who this is for
A 29-year-old software engineer with data analytics skills who wants to replace a full-time salary ($8-15K+/mo) within 12-24 months by building solo, in evenings and weekends, with no outside funding. Every idea below must have a realistic path to first revenue inside 90 days and a named, reachable distribution channel.

## Lens for this session: Alex Hormozi
Built from the $100M Offers / $100M Leads frameworks:
- **Value equation:** Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood of Success) / (Time Delay x Effort & Sacrifice).
- **Grand Slam Offer:** performance-priced or guarantee-backed.
- **Pricing power and partners:** raise prices without losing customers, and borrow another business's audience through cross-promotion.

Per standing feedback, none of these are compliance or regulatory tools. Each solves a revenue-leakage or growth problem.

## Ideas

### 1. Price-Increase Rollout Engine for Recurring-Service Businesses

**Problem:** Pest control, lawn care, pool service and cleaning companies have not raised prices in two or three years because the owner fears losing customers. Costs rise 5-8% a year, so margins erode. A 6% increase on 400 customers at $90/mo is about $26K a year, if rolled out well.

**What to build:** Import the customer list and tenure. Segment customers (long-tenure, new, price-sensitive signals such as late payments) and schedule a staged increase. Generate the notice with a reason and a value reminder (a free add-on or loyalty credit for hesitant customers), send it by email and SMS and route any "cancel" replies to the owner with a save script. Report net revenue lift versus cancellations.

**Grand Slam angle:** "We take 10% of the first year's added revenue from the increase. If it costs you more in cancellations than it earns, you pay nothing."

**Skill fit:** Segmentation, messaging and a net-lift report.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days through route-business owner groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Added revenue of $26K a year gives a 10% fee of $2,600 per client, about $217/mo, plus a $49/mo platform fee. 45 clients x $266 = **$11,970/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Each year brings another increase cycle, and the data on who churns informs pricing for new customers.

**Biggest risk:** Owner nerves. Show the expected net lift from a small pilot batch before sending everything.

**Growth path:** Route businesses, then gyms, studios, memberships and small SaaS.

### 2. Cross-Promotion Network for Non-Competing Local Service Businesses

**Problem:** A mover, a cleaner, a painter and a landscaper all serve the same new-homeowner, but each fights for that customer separately. Owners swap referrals informally with two or three friends at best, and no one tracks results.

**What to build:** A private network where non-competing local businesses in a metro (one per trade) trade referrals and joint offers. The tool matches businesses, creates co-branded offers ("moving? get 15% off a move-out clean"), tracks referral codes and shows each member how many leads and jobs came in and went out.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Ten qualified referrals from partners in 90 days, or the first quarter is free."

**Skill fit:** Matching, referral tracking and offer pages.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends for one metro.

**Time to first $:** 45-75 days, since the network needs a founding group of 15-20 businesses.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $99/mo. 115 members across metros = **$11,385/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Each new trade makes the network more valuable to everyone else, and the referral data keeps businesses in it.

**Biggest risk:** Cold start. Recruit 15 businesses in one metro by offering founding-member pricing and run the first joint offer yourself.

**Growth path:** One metro, then ten, then home-buyer concierge offers sold to realtors.

### 3. Project-Update Portal for Remodelers and Custom Builders

**Problem:** A homeowner in the middle of a 10-week renovation calls the contractor every few days asking "where are we?" Each call interrupts work and signals worry. Unanswered worry becomes a bad review, and the contractor cannot ask for a referral from someone who felt in the dark.

**What to build:** A simple portal for each project: the site lead posts a photo and a one-line update in 30 seconds, the client sees a timeline, the next milestone and upcoming decisions. At completion, the tool requests a review and referral automatically. Track update frequency and client check-ins.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Cut 'where are we' calls by half in 60 days, or the first month is free."

**Skill fit:** Mobile photo upload, client portal and automated review/referral requests.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-60 days through remodeler owner groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $99/mo + $15 per active project. A contractor with 6 active projects pays $189/mo. 60 contractors x $189 = **$11,340/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** The portal collects reviews and referrals at the peak of client satisfaction.

**Biggest risk:** Competition from construction-management software. Differentiate on a 30-second update flow for small crews.

**Growth path:** Remodelers, then custom home builders, landscapers, solar installers and pool builders.

### 4. Table-and-VIP Upsell Engine for Small Venues, Breweries and Comedy Clubs

**Problem:** A small venue sells general-admission tickets and leaves money on the table. Groups would pay for reserved tables, bottle packages or early entry, but the venue never offers them, and walk-up buyers do not know they can pre-buy.

**What to build:** A layer on the venue's ticket page that offers table, VIP and add-on packages (food, drinks, meet-and-greet) at checkout, plus a pre-event email/SMS sequence to ticket holders with a limited-quantity upgrade. Show per-event upsell revenue and attach rate.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Free to install. We take 15% of upgrade revenue we add."

**Skill fit:** Ticketing integrations (Eventbrite, Tixr, Square), checkout upsells and messaging.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-60 days through venue and brewery owner groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** A venue running 8 shows a month adds $1,600 of upgrade revenue = $240/mo, plus a $49/mo platform fee, about $290/mo. 40 venues x $290 = **$11,600/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Revenue per ticket rises with each show and the upgrade data shows which events deserve premium offers.

**Biggest risk:** Dependence on ticketing platforms. Offer a standalone upgrade link as a fallback.

**Growth path:** Small venues, then theaters, festivals, race events and fitness events.

### 5. Trial-to-Paid Activation Coach for Indie SaaS Products

**Problem:** An indie SaaS with 400 trial signups a month converts 3% when activated users convert at 25%. The founder cannot see where trial users stall and has no time to build lifecycle emails.

**What to build:** Connect product events (via a small snippet or Segment-style webhook) and billing. Define the product's activation event, show where trial users drop off and send triggered nudges (a "finish setup" email, a founder-voice help message). Report activation rate and trial-to-paid conversion over time.

**Grand Slam angle:** "A 60-day pilot: lift your activation rate or pay nothing." The fee is a share of the incremental MRR.

**Skill fit:** Event pipeline, funnel analytics and lifecycle email.

**MVP scope:** 4 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 45-75 days through indie-hacker communities and SaaS founder groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149/mo. 80 products = **$11,920/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Founders keep the tool as their growth dashboard, and benchmarking activation across products becomes a data asset.

**Biggest risk:** Crowded space and tight founder budgets. Differentiate with an opinionated activation playbook for products under $20K MRR.

**Growth path:** Indie SaaS, then mobile apps, newsletters with paid tiers and course platforms.
