### 1. Craft Brewery & Distillery TTB Licensing and Label Approval (COLA) Management
**Problem:** Every craft brewery, cidery, meadery, and distillery that sells product across state lines or in interstate commerce must obtain federal approval for every label they sell — a Certificate of Label Approval (COLA) from the TTB (Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau). A craft distillery with 12 SKUs needs 12 approved COLAs; every reformulation, packaging change, or new seasonal release requires a fresh approval. TTB rejections are common (wrong mandatory language placement, missing net-contents statement, unapproved health claims) and each rejection delays a launch by 3-8 weeks. On top of COLAs, producers must maintain their federal Brewer's Notice or Distilled Spirits Plant permit, state manufacturer's license, and state distributor licensing registrations — each with its own renewal calendar and fee structure. Most small producers manage this entirely reactively (discovering a lapsed registration when a distributor flags a compliance hold), costing them distribution relationships and launch delays that hit revenue directly.
**What to build:** A licensing and COLA management service — a producer onboarding tool (cataloging all current federal and state registrations with expiration dates and renewal lead times), a COLA submission workflow (preparing each label application against TTB's mandatory label requirements for the product class, catching common rejection triggers before submission, submitting through TTB's COLAs Online portal, and tracking status through approval), a rejection response service (interpreting TTB deficiency notices and preparing corrected submissions), a state registration management workflow (maintaining the product registration or brand registration required by each state the producer ships into, which varies dramatically by state), and a renewal calendar with automated alerts — charged as a flat fee per COLA submission plus a monthly retainer for ongoing compliance management.
**Skill fit:** The compliance checklist automation (mapping each label's content against TTB's mandatory requirements by product class is a rule-set-applied-to-data problem), the registration renewal tracking, and the COLA submission status tracking are exactly the structured workflow and document management tools the builder constructs faster than a craft producer managing it on spreadsheets. The TTB regulatory layer is dense but well-documented; the 27 CFR Part 4 (wine), Part 5 (spirits), and Part 7 (malt beverages) label regulations are the complete rulebook and map cleanly into a label compliance checker.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a label compliance pre-check tool (flagging common TTB rejection triggers for one product class — start with distilled spirits since craft distilleries have the highest COLA rejection rates and the most complex mandatory requirements), a registration renewal tracker, and a COLA submission status dashboard; the first 3-5 producer clients are onboarded while the rejection pattern database is built from real TTB deficiency notice language.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through the American Craft Spirits Association member directory and newsletter (the primary trade association for small distilleries — their members are the exact target profile, and ACSA maintains a vendor directory where compliance services appear), state craft distillery guilds (nearly every state with active craft production has a guild that communicates regularly with members), and TTB-adjacent communities (craft spirits Facebook groups, r/mead, r/homebrewing communities where producers discuss compliance frustrations openly).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $150-350 per COLA submission (flat fee); $400-850/mo retainer per producer for ongoing compliance management (covering registration renewals, rejection responses, and new submission prep). Managing 15-20 active producer retainer clients at $550/mo average + COLA submission volume (each producer submits 4-8 new COLAs/year at $200 each = $800-1,600/producer/year in submission fees) = **15 retainer clients × $550/mo + $1,200 average annual COLA fees ÷ 12 = $8,250/mo + $1,500/mo = $9,750/mo** at 15 retainer clients. 20 clients pushes past $13,000/mo.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** The craft spirits and craft beer industries are both growing and fragmenting — more producers means more COLA submissions, more state registrations to manage, and more compliance complexity per producer. A producer who onboards their licensing management also routes every new SKU and seasonal release through the service for the life of the business, making each retainer client a compounding engagement rather than a one-time project. The rejection pattern database improves with every submission, creating a quality advantage over producers who submit without pre-checking (who learn TTB's preferences one expensive rejection at a time).
**Biggest risk:** TTB processing times are outside the service's control — a producer who needs a COLA approved for a launch date will be frustrated if TTB takes 60+ days even when the application is perfect. Set expectations upfront with a clear processing timeline disclaimer, and focus the service's value proposition on zero-rejection first submissions (faster net approval time even if individual processing time is fixed) rather than promising TTB turnaround speed.
**Growth path:** Side project → TTB COLA pre-check tool validated for one product class, first 3-5 craft distillery clients onboarded through ACSA network, first COLA approvals delivered. Replaces-part-time-income → 8-10 retainer clients, COLA rejection rate well below industry average (the core quality signal), state guild relationships generating referral flow (~$5-8K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 15-20 retainer clients, pre-check tool covering all three product classes (spirits, beer, wine), expansion to cideries and meaderies using the same compliance framework, pushing past $10-13K/mo.

### 2. Commercial Property Tax Assessment Appeal Service
**Problem:** County assessors routinely over-value commercial property — office buildings, retail centers, industrial warehouses, and small multifamily — often by 15-30% above supportable market value, generating inflated property tax bills that compound annually. A $2M commercial building assessed 20% above market at $2.4M generates $8,000-15,000 in excess annual taxes in a typical market. Property owners have the legal right to appeal their assessment to the county board of equalization (or equivalent body depending on state), but the appeal process — filing the formal protest, compiling the comparable sales or income-approach evidence, presenting at the hearing, and negotiating with the assessor's office — requires methodology knowledge and procedural familiarity that most small commercial property owners lack. A contingency-fee appeal service brings in no upfront cost to the owner and earns only when tax savings are achieved.
**What to build:** A commercial property tax appeal service — an assessment screening tool (comparing the county's assessed value against recent comparable sales data and the property's actual income, flagging likely over-assessment before committing to an appeal), a comparable sales analysis tool (pulling and normalizing comparable commercial sales from county transaction records and CoStar/LoopNet for the evidence package), an income-approach valuation builder (for income-producing properties, calculating a market-supported value using cap rate analysis), a formal appeal filing workflow (preparing the protest paperwork for each jurisdiction's specific appeal process), a hearing presentation package, and a post-appeal settlement tracking system — earned as a contingency fee of 25-35% of the first-year tax savings from any assessment reduction achieved.
**Skill fit:** The assessment screening, comparable sales normalization, and income-approach valuation are exactly the data engineering and analytics problems the builder solves fastest — pulling county transaction records, normalizing square footage and condition adjustments, and building cap rate models from income data are structured quantitative problems. The county appeal process is procedurally well-defined (filing deadlines, evidence requirements, and hearing formats are public) and learnable; the data quality of the evidence package is the primary determinant of success.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a screening tool that flags over-assessment probability from public county assessment data, a comparable sales analysis template for the first target county, and an appeal filing checklist for that county's specific protest procedure; first 3-5 appeals filed in one county while the comparable sales methodology is validated against assessor feedback.
**Time to first $:** 45-60 days (contingency fee is paid when the appeal resolves, typically 3-6 months after filing; price the first 2-3 appeals in a currently-open appeal window, since most counties have a 30-60 day annual window for protests) — distribute through commercial real estate CPAs (who file property tax returns and know which clients have the highest assessed values but rarely appeal assessments themselves), commercial property managers (who manage tax expenses on behalf of building owners and benefit from reducing that line item), and commercial real estate attorney offices.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Contingency of 30% of first-year savings. A $3M building assessed 25% above market ($3.75M assessed) with a 2% tax rate = $7,500 in annual excess taxes; 30% contingency = **$2,250 per appeal won**. Processing 6-8 appeals/month with a 70% success rate = 4-5 wins/month × $2,250 average contingency = **$9,000-11,250/mo**. Larger commercial properties (multifamily, industrial) generate $5,000-20,000+ per successful appeal. Building a consistent pipeline of 8-10 appeal filings per month requires only 3-4 active CPA referral relationships, each generating 2-3 referrals per quarter.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Each winning appeal generates a notification of changed assessment that the property owner sees annually — and a property that was successfully reduced can be monitored for re-assessment (which often occurs after a property sale or after a general reassessment cycle), creating a natural renewal event for each successful client 2-4 years after the initial appeal. Commercial CPAs who refer once and see a client's tax bill drop immediately become repeat referral sources for every other commercial property client they advise.
**Biggest risk:** Appeal windows are fixed annual deadlines in most jurisdictions — a county that closes its appeal window in May means all appeal opportunities for that year are lost if the screener isn't built and referral relationships aren't active before that window. Map the appeal deadline calendar for target counties before the first sales call, and concentrate initial prospecting in Q4-Q1 (when the next assessment notices are sent in most states) rather than building the referral pipeline after the window opens.
**Growth path:** Side project → first county's comparable sales methodology validated, first 3-5 appeals filed, appeal outcome data (assessor response patterns, hearing presentation feedback) used to refine the evidence package template. Replaces-part-time-income → 3-4 CPA referral sources active, 4-5 appeals/month filed, first contingency payments received (~$4-7K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 8-10 appeals/month, 2-3 county markets covered, larger commercial property pipeline (office, industrial) generating $5,000+ contingency fees per win, pushing past $10-12K/mo.

### 3. Independent Insurance Claims Adjuster Agency
**Problem:** When a major weather event (hurricane, hailstorm, tornado, wildfire) hits a region, insurance companies must inspect and adjust thousands of claims simultaneously — far exceeding the capacity of their staff adjusters. They engage independent adjusting agencies to deploy licensed independent adjusters who inspect damage, document findings, and write the adjustment report that determines what the carrier pays. The independent adjuster market is the opposite side of the transaction from public adjusting: these adjusters represent the insurance company, not the policyholder. Agencies that supply adjusters to carriers earn a per-claim fee from the carrier and pay adjusters a portion, keeping a spread. The demand is volatile but reliably intense: one Category 3 hurricane generates 100,000+ claims and requires a rapid surge of licensed adjusters; the agencies that maintain a large, licensed, deployable roster win the largest surge contracts. A small agency with a tight roster of highly-rated adjusters and fast deployment infrastructure can capture high-per-claim fees from carriers who need reliability, not the lowest cost.
**What to build:** An independent adjusting agency — an adjuster roster management system (tracking each adjuster's license status by state, line of authority — auto, property, casualty — training certifications, CAT deployment availability, and claim-handling performance ratings), a carrier vendor registration and relationship management workflow (registering as an approved vendor on each carrier's independent adjuster panel, which requires E&O insurance, state licensing, and roster documentation), a claim assignment and dispatch system (receiving CAT claim assignments from carriers with property address and claim file, assigning to the nearest available licensed adjuster, and tracking inspection completion and report submission deadlines), and a performance tracking and report quality review workflow (reviewing adjuster reports for completeness before submission to the carrier, catching coverage issues or documentation gaps that create re-inspection requests) — earning the spread between the carrier's per-claim fee and the adjuster's per-claim pay.
**Skill fit:** The roster management, deployment dispatch, and claim assignment tracking are exactly the kind of structured operational coordination problems the builder automates faster than an agency owner managing assignments by phone and email. The adjuster performance rating and report quality tracking systems directly apply data engineering skills to a domain where current agencies have no systematic quality control — creating a product quality argument for carrier vendor selection that pure staffing agencies can't make.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build an adjuster roster database with license tracking, a carrier assignment intake and dispatch workflow, and a report submission deadline tracker; initial adjuster roster built through NACA (National Association of Catastrophe Adjusters) membership directory and independent adjuster Facebook groups before the first carrier vendor registration is submitted.
**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — distribute through CAT adjusting staffing platforms (Xactware's Contractor Connection, Verisk's carrier tools, and dedicated CAT staffing agencies that sometimes subcontract overflow to smaller agencies), direct carrier vendor panel applications (most regional carriers and several national carriers post their independent adjuster vendor registration requirements publicly), and independent adjuster networking communities (where experienced adjusters who want agency-level volume without building their own carrier relationships are actively looking for reliable dispatch partners).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Carrier fee per claim: $250-600 per property claim (depending on claim complexity and CAT event severity); adjuster pay: $175-400 per claim; agency spread: $75-200 per claim. During an active CAT event, a 20-adjuster roster handling 10 claims each per week = 200 claims/week × $125 average spread = $25,000/week = **$100,000/mo during peak CAT deployment**. Non-CAT baseline with the same roster doing daily claims (water damage, fire, auto) generates 50-80 claims/week × $100 spread = **$5,000-8,000/mo steady state**, with CAT events providing 3-6 months per year of high-intensity surge income.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** CAT event income is seasonal and surge-based, but each well-executed CAT deployment builds carrier relationships that generate steadier daily-claims volume in the off-season — carriers who used the agency during a CAT event and received clean, on-time reports route their regular daily property claims to the same roster rather than going back to carrier staff adjusters. A roster of 15-20 consistently high-performing adjusters is the asset that generates both the CAT surge income and the steady-state income, and it compounds as adjusters who work well refer peers to the roster.
**Biggest risk:** CAT events are unpredictable in timing and geography — a roster of licensed adjusters in the Southeast generates nothing during a quiet Atlantic hurricane season. Diversify the roster's licensure geographically (adjusters licensed across multiple states) and across line of authority (property AND auto, so hail events generate both property and auto claims from the same event), reducing the geographic concentration risk that makes a purely regional roster vulnerable to a quiet year in one weather zone.
**Growth path:** Side project → adjuster roster of 5-8 licensed members, first carrier vendor panel registration submitted, first daily-claims assignment flow established as baseline income. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-15 roster adjusters, first CAT deployment during an active weather event, carrier relationships generating consistent daily assignment volume (~$5-8K/mo baseline). Replaces-full-time-salary → 20+ roster adjusters licensed across multiple states and lines of authority, 3-4 active carrier relationships generating daily-claims baseline, CAT surge events pushing monthly income to $50,000-100,000 during deployment periods, pushing past $15-20K/mo annual average.

### 4. Small Business 401(k) Plan ERISA Compliance Administration
**Problem:** Small businesses that sponsor a 401(k) plan for their employees face ongoing ERISA compliance obligations that most owners are not aware of and that their payroll provider and plan recordkeeper don't handle: annual Form 5500 filing with the Department of Labor (required for all plans with 100+ participants, and for smaller plans with certain characteristics), required annual participant notices (Summary Annual Report, fee disclosure under ERISA 404a-5, safe harbor plan notices), ADP/ACP non-discrimination testing (ensuring the plan doesn't disproportionately benefit highly compensated employees), plan document restatement every 6 years (required by the IRS), and correction filings when operational errors occur (late deposits, missed distributions). When these obligations are missed, the DOL imposes significant penalties — $250/day for late Form 5500 filings up to $150,000, plus potential plan disqualification. Plan recordkeepers (Vanguard, Fidelity, Guideline) handle investment management but explicitly disclaim responsibility for plan administration compliance — leaving small business owners with a compliance gap they don't know exists until a DOL audit surfaces it.
**What to build:** A 401(k) plan ERISA compliance administration service — a plan intake and compliance gap analysis tool (reviewing the plan document, prior Form 5500 filings, notice delivery records, and testing history to identify current compliance gaps and historical errors), an annual compliance calendar (tracking each plan's Form 5500 deadline, notice distribution deadlines, and testing cycle), a Form 5500 preparation workflow (gathering plan asset and participant data from the recordkeeper, preparing the Form 5500 and all required schedules, and submitting via EFAST2), a required notice generation and delivery tracking system (generating the SAR, 404a-5 fee disclosure, and any safe harbor notices and documenting delivery), an ADP/ACP test execution workflow (calculating the testing ratios from payroll data and the plan's census, identifying potential failures before the end of the plan year when they're still correctable without excise tax), and an error correction coordination service (preparing the VCP submission to the IRS when historical errors are identified) — charged as a monthly retainer per plan.
**Skill fit:** The compliance calendar management, Form 5500 data gathering and preparation, and ADP/ACP testing calculation are structured data pipeline problems — the builder can build a more reliable Form 5500 preparation workflow than the average TPA (third-party administrator) operating from PDF forms and manually re-entered census data. The ERISA regulatory layer (DOL Form 5500 instructions, IRS plan document requirements, and annual notice requirements) is dense but precisely defined, mapping cleanly into a compliance checklist and workflow system.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a plan intake and compliance gap analysis questionnaire, an annual compliance calendar, and a Form 5500 data collection template; the ERISA Institute or ASPPA (American Society of Pension Professionals and Actuaries) provides the regulatory training framework needed to understand Form 5500 preparation and ADP/ACP testing methodology in 40-60 hours of study.
**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — distribute through small business payroll companies (ADP, Paychex, and Gusto all offer 401(k) integrations but explicitly disclaim responsibility for plan administration compliance — a payroll company referral generates warm leads from businesses that have a 401(k) but may not know their compliance obligations), CPA firms with small business clients (who prepare the business tax return and are well-positioned to notice Form 5500 compliance gaps in their client's files), and ERISA attorneys who handle DOL audits and plan corrections (who actively need a referral for ongoing plan compliance administration after they resolve a client's audit).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $350-750/mo retainer per plan (depending on participant count and plan complexity; plans with 50-100 participants warrant higher retainers than micro-plans with 5-10 participants). Managing 20 active plans at $450/mo average = **$9,000/mo**; 30 plans = **$13,500/mo**. Reaching 30 active plans requires 4-6 active CPA referral relationships, each with 5-8 small business clients with standalone 401(k) plans — a realistic 12-18 month accumulation from consistent CPA outreach.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** 401(k) plans are sticky clients — a plan sponsor who has compliance administration off their plate does not voluntarily add it back; the retainer relationship lasts for the life of the plan, which in most small businesses means the life of the business. The DOL penalty structure creates urgency: a business that has missed 3 years of Form 5500 filings has $250/day in accruing penalties that stop accruing only when the plan gets compliant — making a compliance gap analysis that surfaces this problem the highest-value first deliverable in any client engagement.
**Biggest risk:** ERISA is a highly regulated field, and giving advice on plan compliance could be characterized as practicing law or acting as a plan fiduciary in states with strict definitions. Structure the service explicitly as a plan administration service (preparing documents and performing calculations) rather than legal or investment advice, and carry professional liability (E&O) insurance from the start; partnering with an ERISA attorney who reviews complex correction submissions provides the professional backstop for situations that exceed the service's scope.
**Growth path:** Side project → ASPPA or ERISA Institute training completed, first 3-5 plan compliance gap analyses delivered through CPA referrals, first Form 5500 and notice packages prepared. Replaces-part-time-income → 10-12 retainer plans, CPA referral pipeline generating 1-2 new plans/month, first ADP/ACP testing and correction coordination completed (~$4-7K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 25-30 retainer plans, CPA and payroll company referral channels both active, DOL audit representation capability added (highest-value engagement), pushing past $10-14K/mo.

### 5. USCG Vessel Documentation & State Boat Title Service
**Problem:** Recreational and commercial vessel owners who finance a boat purchase, operate in international waters, or use their vessel commercially (charters, fishing, towing) must obtain USCG Certificate of Documentation through the National Vessel Documentation Center (NVDC) — a federal registration separate from and in addition to state boat registration. Every documentation transaction — initial application, annual renewal, transfer of ownership, preferred ship mortgage filing (required by marine lenders to perfect their security interest), name or hailing port change, exchange of documentation type — requires specific NVDC forms submitted by mail or through the NVDC's cumbersome online portal, with processing times that can run 60-90 days for complex transactions. Marine lenders require documentation to be current before a loan can close; documentation delays kill boat purchase transactions. Additionally, the interaction between USCG documentation and state title (some states issue titles only to undocumented vessels; others issue both) creates a coordination requirement that most boat buyers and sellers navigate without any guidance, often incorrectly.
**What to build:** A vessel documentation preparation and filing service — a vessel eligibility screening tool (confirming the vessel meets USCG documentation eligibility requirements: five net tons minimum, owned entirely by US citizens, not already documented under another name), a transaction-type intake workflow (determining which NVDC forms and supporting documents are required for the specific transaction: initial documentation, renewal, transfer, mortgage filing, deletion, or exchange), a form preparation service (completing the NVDC application forms and compiling the supporting documentation package — bill of sale, builder's certificate, citizenship evidence, mortgage instrument), an NVDC status tracking system (monitoring processing status through the NVDC's vessel documentation portal and proactively following up on pending transactions), a state title coordination workflow (managing the interaction between NVDC documentation and the state title or registration requirement in the vessel owner's state), and an expedite escalation protocol (for transactions where a mortgage filing must be completed before a purchase closes) — charged as a flat transaction fee plus the NVDC filing fee pass-through.
**Skill fit:** The eligibility screening, form preparation, and status tracking are structured document management and workflow problems; the state-by-state interaction between USCG documentation and state title is a rule set (each state's position on documentation vs. title is defined in state boating statutes) that maps cleanly into a decision tree. The builder's data skills directly apply to building an NVDC status monitoring system that proactively tracks processing status rather than waiting for the NVDC to mail a response, which is how the current legacy service providers operate.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a vessel eligibility screener, a transaction-type intake questionnaire (covering the 6-8 most common NVDC transaction types), and a form preparation and document checklist tool for the highest-volume transaction (initial documentation and annual renewal account for the majority of NVDC volume); the first 5-10 transactions are managed manually while the form preparation workflow is validated against actual NVDC approvals and rejection notices.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through marine lenders (banks and specialty marine finance companies like USAA, Bank of the West Marine, and Trident Funding that require NVDC documentation as a closing condition and frequently have borrowers who need documentation completed on a specific closing timeline), boat brokers (who coordinate documentation as part of every vessel sale they close and actively maintain referral relationships with documentation services to protect their transaction timelines), and marina operators (who know which of their slip tenants are overdue for documentation renewal and who have established trust relationships with those tenants).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $125-275 per documentation transaction (flat service fee above the government filing fee pass-through). High-volume sources: a regional marine lender closing 20-30 boat purchase transactions per month refers 15-25 documentation transactions/month; a busy boat broker closes 5-10 sales/month, each requiring documentation transfer. Building referral relationships with 2-3 marine lenders and 3-5 boat brokers = 50-80 documentation transactions/month × $175 average service fee = **$8,750-14,000/mo**. Adding annual renewal reminders for the documented vessel database generates a compounding recurring revenue stream as the client roster of documented vessels grows.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every documented vessel is an annual renewal customer — USCG documentation requires annual renewal (or enrollment in the Simplified Measurement Renewal program), and a client whose initial documentation was handled correctly and painlessly routes their annual renewal through the same service indefinitely. The documented vessel database becomes a compounding asset: 500 documented vessels on annual renewal at $125 each = $62,500/year in renewal revenue alone, separate from new transaction volume, and growing as new transactions add to the renewal base.
**Biggest risk:** NVDC processing backlogs are outside the service's control — a marine lender whose borrower needs a documented title before a loan can close will be frustrated if NVDC takes 90 days even with a perfect application. Build a clear expectation-setting protocol for time-sensitive transactions (mortgage filings in particular can be expedited through specific NVDC procedures; document which transaction types have expedite options), and maintain a direct NVDC contact for status escalation so time-sensitive files can be flagged proactively rather than waiting in the queue.
**Growth path:** Side project → first 5-10 documentation transactions through marine lender referrals, NVDC form preparation workflow validated, annual renewal reminder system built and pointed at the growing documented vessel database. Replaces-part-time-income → 2-3 marine lender and boat broker referral relationships active, 25-35 transactions/month, annual renewal base of 100+ vessels generating recurring revenue (~$4-7K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 50-80 transactions/month, 500+ vessels on annual renewal, referral relationships with specialty marine lenders generating consistent new-transaction volume, pushing past $10-14K/mo.

## This Session's Pick: #5 First Dollar, #4 Best Compounding Base

**Fastest path to first dollar:** Idea #5 (USCG vessel documentation) has the most direct distribution path — marine lenders and boat brokers are easy to identify (they advertise publicly), have an immediate and recurring need for documentation services on every transaction they close, and represent a warm referral rather than a cold sales conversation (a marine lender who needs a borrower's documentation completed before a closing deadline will send that borrower to any documentation service that can reliably deliver on time). The 30-45 day first-dollar window is achievable because a single marine lender relationship generates 15-25 transactions/month, compressing the ramp from "first client" to "meaningful volume" compared to ideas where each client is worth one transaction.

**Best long-term compounding income structure:** Idea #4 (401(k) ERISA compliance) builds the most defensible recurring revenue base — a plan sponsor who gets compliant and has ongoing compliance administration handled does not voluntarily remove it, making each client a multi-year retainer. The DOL penalty urgency (accruing $250/day for late Form 5500 filings) creates the highest buyer urgency of any idea in this session, making the first engagement a revenue-generating gap analysis rather than a speculative pitch. CPA referral relationships are the most efficient distribution channel in the entire main-income series — CPAs work with the exact buyer profile and have established trust with clients who act on their recommendations.

**Recommended sequencing:** Launch #5 (vessel documentation) first — the transaction-fee model generates income within 30-45 days and the referral sourcing (marine lender cold outreach) can be executed in evenings. Begin the ASPPA or ERISA Institute training for #4 concurrently, targeting the first 401(k) retainer client 60-90 days after initial vessel documentation income begins. The two businesses require non-overlapping networks and non-overlapping daily work — vessel documentation peaks during spring/summer boating season, while 401(k) compliance work peaks in Q1 (Form 5500 deadlines) and Q4 (year-end testing and notice season), creating a natural complementary income calendar.
