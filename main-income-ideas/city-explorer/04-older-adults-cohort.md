## Older Adults Cohort: Staying Active, Connected and Out in the City

This expands the one-line "empty nesters and retirees" rows in the cohort table into a full cohort. Older adults are not one group. Needs shift a lot between 55 and 85, so the app splits them by life situation, not just age.

### Sub-groups

| Sub-group | Who | Main need |
|---|---|---|
| **Active 55-70** | Empty nesters, early retirees, still working part time | New things to do, a social life beyond work and kids |
| **Retired 65-80** | Flexible days, varied mobility and budgets | Weekday plans, companionship, routine and purpose |
| **Grandparents** | Spend time with grandchildren (any age 55+) | Outings that work for a child and an older body |
| **Living alone or widowed** | Often socially isolated | Company, a reason to leave the house, gentle ways to meet people |
| **Dating again** | Divorced or widowed and open to a new relationship | Low-pressure, public, safe ways to meet and go out |
| **Caregivers** | Looking after a spouse or parent | Short, nearby breaks and outings that include the person they care for |
| **Older (80+) and mobility-limited** | Need seating, step-free access and short trips | Easy, close and well-supported outings, often with a helper |

A person can belong to more than one (for example a retired grandparent who is dating again).

### What older adults actually want from the app
- **Something to look forward to** on the calendar each week.
- **Easy, no-surprise outings:** known seating, parking, restrooms and walking distance.
- **Company:** Someone to go with, or small friendly groups.
- **Respect:** No condescending "seniors" branding. Describe the activity, not the age.
- **Control and simplicity:** Large text, few steps and a human to call.

### Product adjustments for this cohort
- **Access first, not tucked away:**
  - Large-text mode, high contrast, simple screens with one choice per screen.
  - **Accessibility details on every idea:** Step-free entry, seating, walking distance in minutes, restroom availability, parking or drop-off point, noise level, and shade or indoor options.
  - **"Easy day" filter:** Under 20 minutes of walking, seating every few minutes, no stairs.
- **Weekday and daytime emphasis:** Morning and early-afternoon plans on Monday to Friday, when venues are quieter and cheaper. A Monday-morning message for weekday ideas.
- **Choose how to be reached:** Email, text, a printed monthly calendar mailed or picked up at a library or senior center, or a phone call. Do not assume a smartphone.
- **Pick-up and transit info:** Public transit route, a rideshare estimate, volunteer driver programs and a "ride with a friend" option. Transport is often the real barrier.
- **Simple confirm:** A one-tap or reply-YES lock-in, with a phone number that connects to a person for help.
- **Bring someone:** A one-tap invite to a friend, spouse or grandchild by text or a printed card.
- **Gentle companion matching (opt-in):** Meet others at an activity with similar interests, in small hosted groups, with a host present. Use the same mutual-consent approach as the matchmaking and connection cohorts.
- **Grandparent mode:** Outings for a grandparent and grandchild (a short walk and a story time, a train ride, a pottery drop-in), with age and energy filters for both. Include a "grandparent-friendly" tag and a "tired by 2pm" option.
- **Dating-again mode:** The dating track from the earlier section, with public venues, daytime dates, safety tips and no pressure to move quickly. Include the Level 1-4 conversation cards and easy exits.
- **Caregiver mode:** Short breaks near home and outings suited to both people, such as seated activities, quiet cafes and accessible gardens. Include respite resources.
- **Memory and meaning:** A "then and now" passport where older adults can photograph places and add short stories, such as "where I met my husband," which gives younger family members a way to join in.

### Badges and challenges (no childish tone)
- **Weekday Wanderer:** Outings on three different weekdays.
- **Local History:** Visit places with a story (a museum, a historic street, an old theater).
- **Garden and Park Walker:** A series of gentle walks.
- **Culture Circuit:** Concerts, matinees, galleries and talks.
- **Take Someone Along:** Bring a friend, spouse or grandchild on three outings.
- **Neighborhood Tour:** One place in each of several neighborhoods.
- **Seasonal series:** Spring gardens, summer concerts, fall color, winter indoor culture.
- **Photo hunt:** Gentle scavenger hunts at accessible places (find the carved animal, the oldest tree, the clock tower), with big, clear clues.
- Rewards: A local coffee, a museum pass, a concert ticket draw or a donation to a local cause in the member's name.

### Content ideas for this cohort
- **Gentle outdoors:** Botanical gardens, flat waterfront paths, bird-watching, scenic drives with stops.
- **Culture:** Matinee theater, symphony rehearsals, free library talks, art-museum seniors' mornings, historical-society tours.
- **Learning:** Community-college short courses, language groups, craft classes, cooking demonstrations.
- **Community:** Volunteer shifts, community gardens, church or club events, mentoring.
- **Social:** Tea dances, card or board-game groups, walking clubs, coffee mornings.
- **Food:** Lunch specials, farmers markets with seating, cooking classes.
- **Grandparent outings:** Story time, a short train or boat ride, a petting farm, an ice-cream walk.

### Business model for this cohort
- **Consumer plan:** The $39/yr individual plan, with a free tier that includes the weekly idea and a printed or phone option. Older adults will pay if the value is clear and the setup is easy. Offer annual payment by card or check.
- **Family-pays option:** Adult children buy a gift plan for a parent ("a weekly outing for Mom"), with the parent receiving the plan and the child receiving a gentle "she went to the gardens today" confirmation if the parent opts in. This is often the real buyer, so test it.
- **Venue side:** Museums, theaters, gardens, cafes and classes want weekday daytime visitors and will pay per redeemed visit or for featured slots. Older adults often have a loyal, repeat-visit pattern that venues value.
- **Community partnerships:** Senior centers, libraries, parks departments, community colleges, retirement communities, faith communities and area agencies on aging can distribute the printed calendar and host groups. Offer an organization license ($300-1,500/yr, assumption) that includes group tools and reporting.
- **Retirement communities:** A custom branded version for residents' off-campus outings ($300-800/mo per community, assumption).
- **Sponsors:** Local healthcare providers, pharmacies, financial planners, and hearing and vision services might sponsor a series. Check rules for any health or financial advertising to older adults, and keep sponsorship clearly labeled and non-medical.
- **Insurers and health-plan wellness programs:** Possible later partners for "social connection" benefits. These programs are regulated, so check requirements before pitching, and treat this as a later-stage option.

### Marketing for this cohort
- **Offline matters:** Library, senior-center and community-college handouts, a printed monthly calendar, local newspaper listings, church bulletins, a talk at a senior center, and a table at a farmers market.
- **Through the family:** Marketing to adult children ("give Mom a reason to go out") on parent-focused channels, with a gift plan.
- **Word of mouth:** Walking-club leaders, class instructors and activity coordinators at retirement communities.
- **Hosted starter events:** A free monthly "Meet and Walk" or coffee morning at a partner venue.
- **Simple offer:** "Get out 3 times in 30 days, or your next month is free," with a phone line for sign-up.
- **Tone:** Warm, direct and respectful. Photos of real people having a good day, not stock images of "seniors."

### Safety, trust and scams (important)
- Older adults are frequent targets of scams. Never ask for sensitive data by text or call. Say so clearly in every message, and never include links that ask for passwords, payment details or personal identification.
- Use a recognizable sender name and a consistent look so real messages are easy to trust, and list a phone number.
- Keep payments simple, transparent and cancelable, with annual renewal reminders.
- Share-my-plan with a family member and an "I need help" button that calls a chosen contact.
- Public, accessible places and transit-friendly routes by default.
- Do not collect health information. Ask only about mobility needs in a general way ("seating, step-free, short walks") and keep it private and optional.
- **Not medical or social-work advice.** Link to local agencies and professional resources for loneliness, caregiver stress and emergencies.
- **Dating-again safety:** Public daytime venues, tips for meeting new people and a warning about romance scams, with a report option.

### Metrics
Outings per member per month, weekday share of outings, share reached by print or phone versus app, group-activity attendance, "took someone along" rate, grandparent-mode use, gift-plan purchases by family, retention at 6 and 12 months, venue visits, venue repeat-visit rate, scam or confusion reports (should be zero) and help-line call volume.

### Validation
1. **Weeks 1-3:** Partner with 2 senior centers or libraries. Distribute a printed one-page weekly outing list and a phone number. Measure sign-ups and calls.
2. **Weeks 3-6:** Interview 15 people across sub-groups (active retirees, grandparents, widowed, caregivers) to see which one has the strongest pull.
3. **Weeks 4-6:** Test a "gift for a parent" offer with 20 adult children and measure purchases and parent follow-through.
4. **Weeks 5-8:** Run two hosted group outings (a gentle walk and a coffee morning) and measure attendance and repeat sign-ups.
5. **Weeks 6-8:** Offer 5 venues a weekday pilot with redeemed codes.
6. **Gate:** Keep the cohort if at least 25% of contacted members go to an outing, group events fill and either gift plans or venue pilots show willingness to pay. If sign-up depends entirely on app use, fall back to the print-and-phone model with partner funding.

### Risks
- **Tech friction:** Many will not use an app. Offer print, phone and email so the product is not locked behind a smartphone.
- **Low direct payment:** Fixed incomes and free community offerings mean consumer revenue may be small. The adult-child gift, venues and organization licenses are likelier payers.
- **Reach and trust:** Older adults trust local institutions. Work through libraries, senior centers and faith communities, not ads.
- **Mobility variation:** A recommendation that suits a 60-year-old can fail an 82-year-old. Filters and honest accessibility details are critical.
- **Safety and scams:** One bad incident can end trust. Keep communication simple, consistent and verifiable.
- **Tone:** Avoid patronizing language. Describe interests and activities, not age.
- **Regulation:** Health, insurance and financial advertising rules can apply. Review before any such partnership.

### Open decisions
- Which sub-group to start with (this file suggests active retirees and grandparents first, then dating again and caregivers after the print-and-phone model works).
- Whether to build the print calendar and phone line before any app features.
- Whether to sell the gift plan to adult children as the first paid offer.
- Whether to pursue organization licenses with senior centers and retirement communities in the first 6 months.
