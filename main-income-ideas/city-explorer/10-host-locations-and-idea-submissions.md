# City Explorer: Host Locations for Matchmaking and Idea Submissions

[Back to the City Explorer overview](../2026-11-14-city-explorer-badges-app.md) | [Focus and 4-week plan](01-focus-and-4-week-plan.md)

Two linked ideas. First, City Explorer becomes the **location network** where matchmaking introductions, friend meetups and first dates happen. Second, **anyone can submit a place or an idea**, so the location database builds itself instead of relying on one person's research. Numbers marked (assumption) are estimates.

Related: [matchmaking business](../2026-11-14-matchmaking-business.md).

## Part 1: Host Locations for Matchmaking

### The problem
A matchmaker needs a steady supply of good places to send people: public, comfortable, easy to leave, quiet enough to talk and willing to host. Choosing a spot for every introduction wastes time and a bad spot ruins a first meeting.

### What a "host location" is
A venue that has agreed to be part of the matchmaking network and meets a set of standards. It is verified, tagged for what it suits and willing to welcome matched guests.

### Venue types and what they suit

| Venue type | Best for | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Independent cafes and tea rooms | First meetings, friend introductions, marriage-track intros | Public, short, low cost, easy to leave |
| Board-game cafes | Friend pods, early dates, group nights | Play breaks the ice |
| Breweries, wine bars and cocktail lounges (with a quiet hour) | Second dates, couples' circles, mixers | Social, relaxed, early-evening slots |
| Bookstores with cafes and galleries | Quiet first dates, interest-based friend matches | Built-in conversation topics |
| Climbing gyms, studios and class venues | Activity-based friend matching, active dates | Shared focus, less pressure to talk |
| Parks, gardens and walking routes | Daytime first meetings, friend walks, older-adult introductions | Free, open and safe by day |
| Restaurants with private or semi-private corners | Marriage-track dates, couples' dinners, premium clients | Privacy and a better experience |
| Hotel lounges and private clubs | High-net-worth introductions | Discreet, vetted, controlled setting |
| Community centers and libraries | Hosted meetups and workshops | Low cost, trusted, accessible |

### Host-location standards (checklist)
- **Public and welcoming:** Open to anyone, with staff present.
- **Easy exit:** Short stays work, and no minimum spend.
- **Conversation-friendly:** Noise level, seating and lighting suit talking. Record these.
- **Accessible:** Step-free entry, seating and restroom access.
- **Safe:** Well-lit, with transit or parking close by, and staff who know the venue is used for introductions.
- **Capacity for groups:** Can seat 2, 4 or 8, and take a small hosted meetup.
- **Reliable details:** Hours, prices and policies verified within the last 30 days.
- **Willing to host:** Agrees to the code of conduct and to a quick point of contact.

### Venue profile for hosting (extra fields)
Noise level (quiet, moderate, loud), best times, seating for 2 / 4 / 8, private or semi-private area, reservation needed, minimum spend, alcohol-free options, accessibility details, parking and transit, safety notes, staff contact, typical stay length and tags such as "first-date safe," "good for friends," "good for a couples' circle," "quiet enough to talk" and "kid-free."

### How a matchmaker uses it
1. Filter by area, time, vibe, group size and accessibility.
2. Pick a suggested venue, with one backup in a different part of town.
3. Send both people the plan: venue, time, how to find each other and the exit options.
4. After the meeting, each person rates the venue ("good for this kind of meeting?"). Ratings are private feedback for the matchmaker and aggregate for the venue.

### Why venues will host
- **Foot traffic at slow times:** Matchmaking meetings are a steady source of off-peak customers.
- **Group bookings:** Meetups bring 6-12 people at once.
- **Visibility:** A "Matchmaking Host Venue" badge in the app.
- **Perks:** Priority in Thursday plans and the venue's badge counts (see the business listings file).

### Business model for hosting
- **Free host listing** for any venue that meets the standards.
- **Host Partner** ($79-149/mo, assumption): Priority in matchmaking recommendations, a reserved table or hour on set days, an offer slot and a monthly report on matched guests who visited (aggregate counts only).
- **Hosted event fee:** For a matchmaking mixer or couples' circle night, a flat fee per event ($100-300, assumption) or a per-attendee share, paid by the matchmaker or by attendees through ticket sales.
- **Premium private venues:** For the high-net-worth tier, a vetted-venue list as part of the retainer, with no public listing and a referral fee to the venue or no fee at all, depending on the venue.
- **Bundles:** "Coffee and a walk" or "tasting for two" packages sold through the app with a revenue share.

### Safety and fairness
- Matched guests never get another guest's private details from the venue. Venues receive only a first name and time, if needed.
- Venues agree to a code of conduct and a way to report concerns.
- The matchmaker keeps a rotation of venues so no single venue is overloaded, and one bad experience triggers a review.
- Rules on paid placement from the business listings file apply: sponsored status is labeled and payment never changes ratings.

## Part 2: Idea and Location Submissions (Building the Database Together)

### The idea
Anyone can enter a place or an idea, which grows the database faster than one person can. Users, venue owners, matchmakers, local guides and parent-group admins all become contributors.

### Who can submit and what
| Contributor | What they submit |
|---|---|
| Users (members) | New places, date ideas, outing ideas, corrections, photos |
| Venue owners | Their own listing, events, offers and hosting details |
| Matchmakers and hosts | Venues that worked well for introductions, and venues that did not |
| Local guides and group admins | Seasonal lists, neighborhood tours, family or older-adult picks |
| Businesses (paid or free) | Events and offers |

### The submission form (short, under 2 minutes)
1. **Type:** Place, activity idea, event or correction.
2. **Name and location** (address or map pin).
3. **What is it?** One sentence.
4. **Best for** (choose any): solo, friends, couple, family with young kids, older adults, group, first meeting, matchmaking host.
5. **Vibe:** Quiet, lively, active, creative, outdoors.
6. **Cost:** Free, $, $$, $$$.
7. **Good for:** Rainy day, evenings, mornings, weekends, weekdays, seasons.
8. **Practical details (optional):** Stroller access, changing table, step-free entry, seating, noise level, parking, reservation needed.
9. **Why you like it** (one or two lines).
10. **Photo (optional):** Of the place or thing, not of people or children.
11. **Your relationship to it:** Visitor, owner, staff or just heard of it.
12. **Consent:** Agree that the content can be shown in the app (see rights note).

### The pipeline (submitted to live)

| Status | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Submitted** | Entry received, checked for duplicates and spam |
| **Needs details** | Missing hours, address or tags, and the submitter is asked |
| **Pending verification** | A volunteer or staff member checks hours, access and safety |
| **Live (unverified)** | Shown with a "community submitted" label |
| **Live (verified)** | Checked on a date, shown with a verified badge |
| **Host-approved** | Meets the matchmaking host standards |
| **Flagged or removed** | Closed, unsafe, inaccurate or against the rules |

### Keeping quality high
- **Duplicate check:** Match by name and address before accepting.
- **Verification:** Call or visit for hosting venues. For general places, check the website and recent photos. Require at least two confirmations from different members ("I've been here") before a place is marked verified.
- **Voting and confirmations:** Members can thumbs-up ideas, add "I went" check-ins and flag problems.
- **Freshness:** A place with no confirmation for 6 months is marked "needs recheck," and one flagged as closed is hidden.
- **Anti-spam:** Rate limits, new-account limits, and manual review for business self-promotion that looks like advertising.
- **Moderation rules:** No private homes, no unsafe places, no personal information, no photos of identifiable people or children, no hate or harassment.
- **Owner claims:** A venue owner can claim and correct a listing after verification. Owners cannot delete honest reviews.

### Rewards that make people contribute
- **Local Scout badges:** Bronze, Silver and Gold for submissions that get verified (for example 1, 5 and 20).
- **First Finder:** Credit for being the first to add a place that later becomes popular.
- **Points toward the paid plan:** A month free for 10 verified entries (assumption).
- **Leaderboard for neighborhoods, not people:** "Eastside has 48 verified places," which builds local pride without a public ranking of individuals.
- **Recognition:** Opt-in credit on the place page ("Found by Maria in Eastside").
- **Local challenges:** "Add 3 rainy-day places for toddlers this month," tied to gaps in the database.
- **Venue rewards:** A venue that completes its profile gets a free month of the featured listing.

### Gap-driven requests
The app finds where the database is thin (no quiet cafes in a neighborhood, no accessible entrances listed, no weekday senior options) and asks members for those entries as a challenge. This points contributors at the highest-value gaps.

### Keeping the database useful
- **Standard tags** so entries can be filtered (cohort, vibe, cost, accessibility, season, hosting).
- **Structured data over free text,** so matching works.
- **A "who verified this and when" record** on every place.
- **Change history,** so a bad edit can be undone.
- **Export and backup** so the data is yours.

### Rights, privacy and safety (practical flags, confirm with a lawyer)
- **Content license:** Submitters keep ownership but grant the app a license to store and display their text and photos. Say so on the form.
- **No personal data:** Do not collect or show home addresses, children's images or names, or private details about people.
- **Venue consent:** Venues can request correction or removal.
- **Accuracy claims:** Show "community submitted" until verified, and say exactly what was checked and when.
- **Business self-promotion:** Label submissions from owners as owner-submitted.
- **Reporting:** A visible report button and a quick response time.

### How to build it (no code first)
1. **Week 1:** A form (Tally, Google Forms or Typeform) feeding a spreadsheet or Airtable, with the status column above.
2. **Week 2-4:** Share the form with 20 test parents, 5 local guides and 10 venues. Verify the first 50 entries by hand.
3. **Later:** A simple in-app form, "I went" confirmations, badges and the gap-driven challenges.

### Metrics
Submissions per week, share verified, time from submission to live, duplicates caught, share of entries with accessibility tags, confirmations per place, contributors per month, repeat contributors, share of host venues rated "good" by matched guests and database freshness (places confirmed in the last 6 months).

### Risks
- **Quality and trust:** Unverified entries can mislead. Label them and verify the host venues personally.
- **Low contribution:** Most users never submit. Use gap-driven requests, rewards and direct asks of local guides.
- **Spam and ads:** Business self-promotion can flood the database. Use review and labels.
- **Moderation workload:** One person cannot check everything. Start small, recruit volunteer scouts and cap the open queue.
- **Safety:** A bad host venue is a real risk. Personally check every host venue before it goes into matchmaking.

### Open decisions
- Whether the host-venue network starts with 10 hand-picked places before opening submissions.
- Whether to reward contributors with plan credit, badges or both.
- Who verifies in the first month (you, or a volunteer scout team).
- Whether matchmaking hosts rate venues privately, publicly or in aggregate only.
