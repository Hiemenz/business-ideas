# City Explorer: Name and Tagline

[Overview](../2026-11-14-city-explorer-badges-app.md) | [MVP playbook](01-focus-and-4-week-plan.md)

**Status:** Tagline proposed. Name not chosen yet. Working name: **Weekendly** (recommended, not final). Nothing here has been checked for domains, app-store names or trademarks.

## Tagline (proposed)

**"Hang up and hang out."**

The joke: the phone is how you find the plan, and the plan gets you off the phone. It fits an app that sends one notification a week. Pitch line: "One ping, then hang up and hang out."

**Spelling:** Use "hang out" as two words (the verb). "Hangout" is the noun. Pick one style and use it everywhere.

**Tagline variants**
- Hang up and hang out. (keep as written)
- Hang up. Hang out. (shorter, with a beat)
- One ping. Then hang out. (names the weekly notification)
- Hang up and go out. (less of a pun, closer to the goal)

**Watch-outs**
- "Hang up" can sound like a phone-call app at first glance. Pair it with the name and a weekend-plan icon.
- "Hang out" can sound teenage to older adults and some parents. Test it with a few of them.
- Common phrases may already be used by other companies. Search the phrase together with the name before printing anything.

## How to choose

1. **Say it out loud.** If it is hard to spell after hearing it, drop it.
2. **Keep it short.** Two or three syllables, under 12 letters.
3. **Keep the future open.** If you add couples, older adults and families, avoid names that mean only parents or only kids.
4. **Check availability.** Search the exact name in the app stores, check a `.com` or `.app` domain, and run a trademark search for the name and the tagline.
5. **Test it.** Ask 10 parents: "If a friend told you about [name], what would you think it does?" Pick the name most people guess right.

## Open decisions

- Final name (recommended: Weekendly, alternate: Passport).
- Whether the tagline is one phrase or two short ones.
- Whether to test the tagline with older adults before committing.
