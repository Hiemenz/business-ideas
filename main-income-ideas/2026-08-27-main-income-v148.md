### 1. Independent Psychiatric Mental Health Nurse Practitioner (PMHNP) Practice Multi-State APRN Licensure, DEA Registration & PDMP Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent psychiatric mental health nurse practitioners — increasingly running solo or small-group telehealth practices prescribing stimulants for ADHD, antidepressants, and office-based buprenorphine for opioid use disorder — hold state Advanced Practice Registered Nurse (APRN) licensure that must be renewed on a state-specific cycle with continuing-education hours, and a PMHNP treating patients by telehealth across state lines must hold and track a current, separate APRN license in every state where a patient is physically located at the time of the session, since prescribing to a patient in a state where the license has lapsed is a practice-without-a-license violation the moment it happens. In roughly half the states — the "reduced" or "restricted" practice states under the American Association of Nurse Practitioners' own classification, including Texas, Florida, Georgia, and California's transitional framework — full prescriptive authority additionally requires a written collaborative practice agreement or prescriptive-authority agreement with a supervising or collaborating physician, filed with the board and renewed on its own cycle; a PMHNP who keeps prescribing after that agreement lapses (a collaborating physician retires, changes practices, or simply forgets to re-sign) has been practicing outside their authorized scope for every prescription written in the gap. Every controlled-substance prescription also carries its own federal and state layer: DEA registration must stay current (renewed every three years) and, since the MATE Act's 2023 training-attestation requirement, a PMHNP prescribing buprenorphine must have completed and be able to document the required substance-use-disorder training hours as a condition of that registration remaining valid, while most state boards separately mandate a Prescription Drug Monitoring Program (PDMP) query before writing or renewing a Schedule II-IV prescription — a query a busy solo prescriber juggling dozens of patients a week can skip under time pressure, and a missing PDMP query on a controlled-substance chart is exactly the finding a state board complaint or DEA audit targets first. A solo or small-group PMHNP practice running a multi-state telehealth caseload currently tracks state-by-state license renewal dates, the current status of a collaborating-physician agreement, DEA registration currency, and per-prescription PDMP query documentation on a personal spreadsheet and a mental checklist, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific prescription was written to a patient in a state where the PMHNP's license, collaborative agreement, and PDMP query were all simultaneously in order.

**What to build:** A multi-state APRN license tracker with CE-hour countdowns and a hard flag on any state where the license has lapsed relative to the practice's active patient roster; a collaborative/prescriptive-authority agreement tracker in restricted-practice states with a "cleared to prescribe" gate tied to the collaborating physician's current signed agreement; a DEA registration and MATE Act buprenorphine-training-attestation tracker; and a per-prescription PDMP query compliance log flagging any controlled-substance prescription written without a documented query on file before submission.

**Skill fit:** State board of nursing renewal cycles, DEA registration expiration data, and state PDMP query-mandate rules are public, structured regulatory data, and the prescribe-eligibility gate (license current in the patient's state, collaborative agreement current, PDMP query logged) is the same deterministic "block the action until every compliance record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a fast-growing telehealth-psychiatry niche entirely distinct from the Med Spa Physician Oversight idea already in this repo, whose collaborative practice agreement authorizes cosmetic laser and injectable procedures rather than psychiatric prescriptive authority, and from the Opioid Treatment Program and Substance Use Disorder Treatment Facility ideas, both of which govern a licensed facility's own SAMHSA/DEA registration rather than an individual APRN's multi-state license and per-prescription PDMP obligations.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the multi-state license tracker, the collaborative-agreement gate, the DEA/MATE Act tracker, and the per-prescription PDMP query log. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to solo and small-group telehealth psychiatric NP practices.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the American Psychiatric Nurses Association's (APNA) member directory and the American Association of Nurse Practitioners' (AANP) Psychiatric-Mental Health Special Interest Group reach exactly the small practice owners currently reconciling multi-state license and PDMP-query compliance on a personal spreadsheet with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $99-249/mo per practice depending on provider count and number of states licensed in. 55-70 practices × $175/mo avg = $9,625-12,250/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new state a telehealth practice expands into reopens the license-and-PDMP-rule question for that state, every collaborating-physician change reopens the prescriptive-authority-agreement question, and every controlled-substance prescription written generates its own PDMP-query compliance event — a continuous, patient-volume-and-footprint-driven compliance stream tied directly to the practice's own telehealth growth.

**Biggest risk:** A PMHNP practicing in a single full-practice-authority state with a small, stable patient panel has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward multi-state telehealth practices and practices prescribing buprenorphine or stimulants, where the license-and-PDMP load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

### 2. Excavation & Trenching Contractor OSHA Subpart P Trench Safety & One-Call/811 Damage-Prevention Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Excavation and utility-installation contractors digging trenches for water, sewer, gas, and electrical service lines operate under OSHA's excavation standard (29 CFR 1926 Subpart P), which requires a documented "competent person" — an individual capable of identifying existing and predictable hazards and authorized to take prompt corrective action — to inspect every excavation five feet or deeper before work begins each shift, after any rainfall or other event that could increase hazards, and throughout the workday, and to select a protective system (sloping, benching, shoring, or a trench shield) based on the excavation's actual soil classification (Type A, B, or C under the standard's own appendix) rather than a generic default; a crew that opens a trench without a current competent-person inspection on file, or with a protective system undersized for the soil actually encountered, is exactly the finding an OSHA inspector targets after a cave-in, and trenching fatalities remain one of OSHA's most aggressively enforced hazard categories with National Emphasis Program inspections specifically targeting open trenches. Layered underneath, every state's one-call/811 damage-prevention statute requires a locate ticket to be filed and the statutory waiting period (commonly two business days) to elapse before excavation begins, and the ticket itself expires after a set window (commonly 30 days) — a crew that digs before the waiting period clears, after the ticket has expired, or outside the ticket's marked boundaries is committing an independently citable statutory violation the moment a buried gas or electric line is struck, regardless of how the excavation itself was shored. A contractor running several concurrent trench jobs across a service area currently tracks competent-person inspection sign-offs, soil-classification-to-protective-system matching, and one-call ticket status and expiration on a paper site log and a stack of ticket printouts, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific open trench has a current inspection, a properly matched protective system, and a valid, unexpired locate ticket all simultaneously on file before a crew climbs in.

**What to build:** A per-excavation competent-person inspection log with a daily/shift/post-rain-event inspection requirement and a hard "no entry" flag until the day's inspection is confirmed; a soil-classification-to-protective-system matching tool flagging any excavation where the logged protective system doesn't meet the standard's minimum for its recorded soil type and depth; and a one-call/811 ticket tracker computing each ticket's statutory waiting period and expiration date, blocking a job from being marked "clear to dig" until the waiting period has elapsed and the ticket is still valid.

**Skill fit:** OSHA's excavation standard and its soil-classification appendix, along with each state's one-call statute and ticket-lifecycle rules, are public, structured regulatory data, and the "no entry until inspection, protective system, and locate ticket all clear" gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a specialty-trade niche entirely distinct from the Underground Utility Locating Contractor idea already in this repo, which marks buried lines under a locate-ticket response-window statute and never opens an excavation itself, and from the Structural Demolition and Structural Steel Erection Contractor ideas already covered, both governed by an entirely different OSHA subpart and hazard class (structural collapse and fall protection, not cave-in/engulfment).

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the competent-person inspection log, the soil-classification/protective-system matcher, and the one-call ticket tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent excavation and underground-utility-installation contractors.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the National Utility Contractors Association's (NUCA) national and state chapter meetings reach exactly the small excavation contractors currently reconciling competent-person inspections and one-call ticket status on a paper site log with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $129-229/mo per contractor depending on crew count and concurrent job volume. 50-65 contractors × $165/mo avg = $8,250-10,725/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every excavation opened generates its own competent-person inspection requirement for as long as it stays open, every job pulled reopens the one-call ticket question on its own independent clock, and every new crew added to the contractor's operation multiplies the number of simultaneously open trenches needing daily inspection — a continuous, job-and-crew-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the contractor's own growth.

**Biggest risk:** A contractor running one or two shallow, short-duration jobs a month has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward contractors running multiple concurrent crews on deeper utility-installation work, where the inspection-and-ticket load genuinely outgrows a paper site log.

**Growth path:** Side project → 16 contractors ($2.6K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 32 contractors ($5.3K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 50-65 contractors plus an incident-ready inspection-and-ticket export a contractor can hand an OSHA compliance officer on the spot.

### 3. NFIP Write-Your-Own (WYO) Flood Insurance Agency FEMA Training, Elevation-Certificate & Flood-Zone Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent property-and-casualty insurance agencies that write National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) policies through the Write-Your-Own (WYO) carrier arrangement sell and service federally backed flood coverage while remaining bound to FEMA's own Flood Insurance Manual rating rules rather than a private carrier's underwriting discretion, and most states and WYO carrier appointments now require agents to complete and periodically renew flood-specific training before they're authorized to bind or service NFIP policies — an agent whose flood training has lapsed but who keeps writing flood policies anyway is operating outside the WYO carrier's own appointment terms, a finding a carrier's periodic agency audit is built to catch. The Flood Disaster Protection Act's mandatory-purchase requirement forces the sharpest recurring workload: every federally backed mortgage on a structure in a Special Flood Hazard Area requires flood insurance determined by a Standard Flood Hazard Determination, and FEMA periodically updates its Flood Insurance Rate Maps (FIRMs) — when a map revision moves a property's flood zone, every affected policyholder's rating and, in many cases, mandatory-purchase status must be redetermined, and an agency that keeps renewing a policy on its old, pre-revision zone rating is exactly the gap a lender's post-map-change compliance review or a FEMA agency audit targets. Underneath that sits the Elevation Certificate (FEMA Form 086-0-33), still required for rating post-FIRM construction in a flood zone and for any Letter of Map Amendment or grandfathering election — a certificate that's missing, expired for the structure's current use, or never obtained before binding is one of the most common findings behind a mis-rated policy, and a mis-rated flood policy is a leading source of NFIP agent E&O exposure since an underpaid claim traces directly back to the rating error at the point of sale. A small independent agency carrying a book of 100-400 flood policies currently tracks flood-training renewal, elevation-certificate currency per insured structure, and flood-zone-redetermination status after each FEMA map update on the agency management system's general notes field, with no dedicated system cross-checking a specific policy's rating against its most current flood-zone and elevation data before each renewal.

**What to build:** A flood-training renewal tracker for every agent authorized to bind NFIP policies, tied to the specific WYO carrier appointment requiring it; a per-policy elevation-certificate tracker flagging any policy rated without a current certificate on file or with a certificate that predates a structural change; and a flood-zone redetermination workflow that flags every policy in an affected map panel the moment FEMA publishes a FIRM revision, generating a re-rating and mandatory-purchase-status review queue before the next renewal cycle.

**Skill fit:** FEMA's Flood Insurance Manual, the National Flood Hazard Layer, and FIRM panel revision publication dates are public, structured federal data, and the map-revision-triggered re-rating queue is the same deterministic "recompute the compliance status from the trigger event" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an insurance-distribution niche entirely distinct from the Surplus Lines Broker and other property/casualty license ideas already in this repo, none of which touches FEMA's flood-specific rating manual, elevation-certificate requirement, or map-revision redetermination mechanic.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the flood-training tracker, the elevation-certificate tracker, and the FIRM-revision redetermination workflow for the highest flood-policy-volume coastal and river-basin states. First 5-8 agency clients through direct outreach to independent agencies with an active flood book.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Independent Insurance Agents & Brokers of America's (the "Big I") flood insurance committee and the National Flood Association's member directory reach exactly the small agencies currently reconciling elevation-certificate currency and flood-zone redeterminations on a management system's notes field with no dedicated tracker.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $89-179/mo per agency depending on flood-policy count. 65-90 agencies × $130/mo avg = $8,450-11,700/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new flood policy written opens its own elevation-certificate-and-rating question, every FEMA map revision reopens the flood-zone redetermination question for every affected policy simultaneously, and every WYO carrier appointment renewal reopens the flood-training-currency question — a continuous, policy-count-and-map-revision-driven compliance stream tied directly to the agency's own flood book growth.

**Biggest risk:** An agency with a small, stable flood book in a single, rarely-remapped county has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward agencies in actively remapped coastal and river-basin counties with a larger flood book, where the redetermination load genuinely outgrows a notes field.

**Growth path:** Side project → 20 agencies ($2.6K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 42 agencies ($5.5K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 65-90 agencies plus a FEMA-audit-ready elevation-certificate-and-rating export an agency can produce the moment a carrier audit notice arrives.

### 4. Independent Contract Surety Bond Agency Underwriting-Authority, Power-of-Attorney & Miller Act Bond-Form Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent insurance agencies specializing in contract surety — bid, performance, and payment bonds for construction contractors bidding public and larger private work — hold a state property-and-casualty producer license with a surety line of authority, renewed on a state-specific cycle with continuing-education hours, and operate under individual underwriting authority letters issued by each surety carrier they represent, specifying the class of bond and the maximum penal sum the agency can bind on the carrier's behalf without referring the account to the home office; an agent who binds a bond above the carrier's letter authority, or under a carrier appointment that has since lapsed, has issued an obligation the carrier can refuse to honor, an exposure that surfaces only when a bond claim is filed and the carrier reviews the binding authority on file at the time of issuance. Every bond issued must carry its own executed power of attorney naming the specific signing agent, bond number, and penal sum — a bond delivered without a valid, matching power of attorney on file is not properly executed and can be challenged by the public entity or project owner requiring it — and before any bond is bound, the agency must collect a signed General Indemnity Agreement (and typically personal guarantees) from the contractor principal, since a bond issued without an indemnity agreement on file leaves the agency and its surety carrier with no contractual right of recovery if a claim is paid out. The federal Miller Act (40 U.S.C. §3131-3134) requires performance and payment bonds on federal construction contracts over $150,000 in a specific statutory form and penal-sum structure, and every state's own "Little Miller Act" mirrors this for state and local public works with its own bond-form language and percentage requirements that vary state to state — an agency that issues a bond using the wrong statutory form or penal-sum percentage for the specific public entity involved is exactly the gap a public owner's bond examiner or a Miller Act payment-bond claim surfaces, often months after the bond was written. A small surety agency running a book of 60-150 active contractor accounts currently tracks per-carrier underwriting authority letters, per-bond power-of-attorney execution, indemnity-agreement collection, and which statutory bond form applies to which public-works project on a shared spreadsheet and a filing cabinet of carrier correspondence, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific bond's authority, power of attorney, indemnity agreement, and statutory form were all simultaneously in order at the moment it was issued.

**What to build:** A per-carrier underwriting authority tracker recording each carrier's binding limits by bond class with a hard flag on any bond request exceeding current authority; a per-bond power-of-attorney execution log matching each issued bond to its POA number and penal sum; an indemnity-agreement compliance gate blocking a bond from being marked "issued" until a signed GIA is on file for the principal; and a state-by-state Miller Act/Little Miller Act bond-form reference flagging the correct statutory form and penal-sum percentage for the specific public entity and contract value involved.

**Skill fit:** Carrier underwriting authority structures, the Miller Act's statutory bond-form requirements, and each state's Little Miller Act text are public and carrier-published structured data with concrete per-bond triggers, and the issue-eligibility gate (authority current, POA executed, indemnity agreement on file, correct statutory form selected) is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a surety-specific insurance-agency niche entirely distinct from the Surety Bond Consulting & Application Service idea already in this repo — that idea is a financial-consulting/brokerage business serving the contractor as its client to help assemble underwriting financials and shop the surety market, and its own text flags producer licensure as an open question the operator must resolve; this idea instead serves the already-licensed surety agency itself, tracking the agency's own regulatory and carrier-contractual obligations as the party actually binding and issuing bonds — an entirely different buyer, business model, and compliance surface.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the underwriting authority tracker, the power-of-attorney execution log, the indemnity-agreement gate, and the Miller Act/Little Miller Act bond-form reference for the highest public-works-volume states. First 5-8 agency clients through direct outreach to independent contract surety agencies.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the National Association of Surety Bond Producers (NASBP) member directory reaches exactly the small surety agencies currently reconciling carrier authority letters and per-bond power-of-attorney execution on a shared spreadsheet with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $179-349/mo per agency depending on active bond volume and number of carrier appointments tracked. 40-55 agencies × $240/mo avg = $9,600-13,200/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every bond issued generates its own power-of-attorney-and-indemnity-agreement compliance event, every new carrier appointment reopens the underwriting-authority-letter question, and every public-works bid pursued reopens the statutory-bond-form question for that specific public entity — a continuous, bond-volume-and-carrier-count-driven compliance stream tied directly to the agency's own book growth.

Mitigate by qualifying leads toward agencies actively bidding public-works accounts across multiple carrier appointments, where the authority-and-bond-form load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

### 5. Fresh Produce Farm FSMA Produce Safety Rule Agricultural-Water, Worker-Training & Soil-Amendment Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Farms growing, harvesting, packing, or holding "covered produce" — fruits and vegetables typically eaten raw, such as leafy greens, tomatoes, and berries — above FDA's sales-volume threshold operate under the FSMA Produce Safety Rule (21 CFR Part 112), enforced through on-farm inspections conducted by the state Department of Agriculture under a cooperative agreement with FDA's National Produce Safety Program rather than by FDA directly, and a farm that can't produce clean records the moment an inspector arrives is exactly the finding that turns a routine on-farm inspection into an enforcement action. Agricultural water used for irrigation or post-harvest handling must be tested on a defined schedule for generic E. coli — untreated surface water sources in particular require an initial multi-year sampling baseline followed by ongoing annual testing — and a water source that fails the rule's microbial quality threshold requires a documented corrective action (treatment, a die-off waiting period, or discontinuing that water source for pre-harvest use) before the next harvest touches it; a farm that keeps irrigating from a source with no current test on file, or ignores a failed result, is exactly the gap a produce-safety inspection is built to catch. At least one supervisor must complete FDA-recognized Produce Safety Alliance Grower Training, and every worker who handles produce or food-contact surfaces needs documented annual hygiene training — a worker on the harvest crew with no training record on file is a citable finding regardless of how well that worker was actually trained verbally. Underneath both of those, biological soil amendments of animal origin — raw manure and, in some cases, compost — carry an application-to-harvest interval the farm must document for every field and planting, since harvesting before the required interval has elapsed after a manure application is one of the produce-safety inspection's highest-priority checks given the direct pathogen-transmission risk. A small produce farm running several fields across multiple crops and water sources currently tracks water-test results, worker-training completion, and soil-amendment application dates on a farm-office binder and a filing cabinet of lab reports, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific field's water source is currently passing, every harvest crew member has current training on file, and a specific planting's manure-to-harvest interval has actually elapsed before the crew starts picking.

**What to build:** A per-water-source testing tracker computing each source's required sampling frequency and flagging any source whose most recent result has aged past its testing window or failed without a documented corrective action; a per-worker training compliance log with a "cleared to handle produce" gate blocking any harvest-crew assignment until PSA grower training or annual hygiene training is confirmed current; and a per-field biological-soil-amendment tracker computing each planting's application-to-harvest interval from its logged manure or compost application date, flagging any field where harvest is scheduled before the interval has elapsed.

**Skill fit:** The Produce Safety Rule's water-testing frequency requirements, the Produce Safety Alliance's training-completion framework, and the rule's soil-amendment interval structure are public, structured regulatory data with concrete per-source, per-worker, and per-field triggers, and the harvest-eligibility gate (water source passing, crew trained, soil-amendment interval elapsed) is the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a farm-level food-safety niche entirely distinct from the USDA-Certified Organic Farm idea already in this repo, a voluntary labeling certification under a completely different agency and statute a farm can hold independent of its Produce Safety Rule status, and from the PACA-Licensed Produce Dealer idea already in this repo, whose compliance core is trust-preservation and prompt-payment obligations for trading produce rather than growing it.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the water-testing tracker, the worker-training gate, and the soil-amendment interval tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small produce farms ahead of their next state produce-safety inspection window.
