### 1. Debt Settlement & Debt Management Company State License & TSR Dedicated-Account Compliance
**Problem:** Debt settlement companies — firms that negotiate reduced lump-sum payoffs with a consumer's creditors — must hold a state debt adjuster, debt settlement, or debt management license (administered by the state banking or financial regulator, with several states banning the model outright, meaning a multi-state operator must maintain a state-by-state eligibility map rather than a single license), renewed annually with a surety bond sized to enrolled-client debt volume. The core federal compliance layer is the FTC's 2010 amendment to the Telemarketing Sales Rule (16 CFR § 310.4(a)(5)), which bans collecting any advance fee before a debt is actually settled and requires client funds to sit in an FDIC-insured dedicated account, held in the consumer's name, administered by an independent third party, with the consumer retaining withdrawal rights at all times — a structural requirement that differs fundamentally from a normal operating account and that state examiners and the CFPB specifically audit for. Fees may only be collected proportionally as each individual debt is actually settled, meaning every settlement event triggers a fee-calculation and dedicated-account disbursement that must reconcile exactly against the client's enrollment agreement. A small debt settlement firm running 200-800 enrolled clients across a dozen states manages state license eligibility, TSR dedicated-account reconciliation per client, and per-settlement proportional fee calculation with no dedicated compliance staff — and a TSR violation (charging a fee before settlement, or commingling dedicated-account funds) is a direct FTC and state AG enforcement target with per-violation penalties that have shut down firms outright.
**What to build:** A state licensing eligibility map (per state: licensed/prohibited/exempt status, license number and renewal date, bond amount — with tiered renewal alerts and an enrollment gate blocking new client sign-ups from a state where the firm isn't currently licensed); a dedicated-account reconciliation engine tracking each client's dedicated-account balance against enrolled debts, confirming funds are only disbursed as fees after a specific debt is actually settled (never in advance); a proportional fee calculator that computes the fee owed on each individual settlement event against the client's enrollment agreement terms; and a TSR compliance documentation binder (required pre-enrollment disclosures, dedicated-account administrator agreement, and settlement letters) exportable for a state exam or CFPB inquiry.
**Skill fit:** State debt settlement/debt adjuster statutes and the FTC's TSR debt relief provisions are public regulatory text, and the dedicated-account reconciliation and proportional fee calculation are exactly the kind of ledger-matching, rules-engine data problems a software engineer's background is suited to build once and apply across every enrolled client. The American Fair Credit Council (AFCC) provides a concentrated, identifiable buyer community of licensed debt settlement firms.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the state licensing eligibility map for the ~40 states that permit debt settlement, the dedicated-account reconciliation engine, and the proportional fee calculator; get the first 5-8 clients through AFCC member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — American Fair Credit Council (AFCC) member directory outreach, plus referral relationships with the independent dedicated-account administrators (companies like Global Client Solutions) that debt settlement firms are already required to use, who see which firms reconcile accounts manually and error-prone.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per firm: $350-800/mo (a small single-state firm with under 300 enrolled clients: $350/mo; a multi-state firm with 800+ enrolled clients across a dozen states: $800/mo). Managing 20-25 clients at $550/mo average = **$11,000-13,750/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new client enrollment adds a fresh dedicated-account ledger, every individual debt settlement triggers a proportional fee calculation and disbursement event, and state license renewals recur annually across every state the firm operates in — a continuous, per-transaction compliance stream tied directly to the firm's core revenue-generating activity.
**Biggest risk:** Disbursing a fee from a client's dedicated account before the specific debt it corresponds to was actually settled is a direct TSR violation that has triggered FTC enforcement action against debt settlement firms; the reconciliation engine's hard block on any fee disbursement not tied to a confirmed settlement event is the core protection.
**Growth path:** Side project → state eligibility map, dedicated-account reconciliation engine, and fee calculator deployed, first 5-8 firm retainers through AFCC outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-15 firm retainers, dedicated-account administrator referral relationship established. Replaces-full-time-salary → 20-25 firm retainers, pushing past $11-13.75K/mo.

### 2. Talent & Modeling Agency State Talent Agencies Act License, Client Trust Account & Child Performer Compliance
**Problem:** Talent and modeling agencies that procure employment for actors, models, and performers must hold a state talent agency license under statutes modeled on California's Talent Agencies Act (Labor Code §1700 et seq.) or equivalent state laws (New York's General Business Law Article 11 is the other major framework), which require the agency to file its standard client contract form with the state labor commissioner, maintain a surety bond, and — critically — hold any client funds received (commission payments, advances passed through from a booking) in a segregated client trust account rather than the agency's general operating account, with detailed accounting available to each client on request. Agencies representing minors face an additional compliance layer: state entertainment work permits are required before a minor can be booked for any job, and in states with Coogan Law-type protections (California and New York being the primary examples), a percentage of the minor's earnings (typically 15%) must be deposited into a state-mandated blocked trust account (a "Coogan Account") within a statutory window after the agency receives payment — a requirement wholly separate from and in addition to the general client trust account. An agency representing 50-150 talent, a meaningful share of them minors, manages license renewal, client trust account reconciliation across dozens of individual clients, work permit verification before each booking, and Coogan Account deposit deadlines with no dedicated back-office compliance function — and a labor commissioner audit finding commingled client funds or a missed Coogan deposit can suspend the agency's license outright.
**What to build:** A state talent agency license and bond tracker (license number, filed contract form status, renewal deadline — with tiered alerts); a per-client trust account ledger reconciling every payment received against commissions owed and pass-through amounts due each talent, with a hard block against any transfer that would commingle client funds into the agency's operating account; a minor-client work permit and Coogan Account compliance module that, before a minor is booked, confirms a current entertainment work permit is on file, and after each payment, calculates the statutory percentage due and starts a countdown to the state's mandated Coogan Account deposit deadline; and an audit-ready trust accounting export in the format the state labor commissioner requests during a license investigation.
**Skill fit:** State talent agency statutes, the specific Coogan Law deposit percentages and deadlines, and entertainment work permit requirements are public regulatory sources; the trust account ledger and Coogan deposit countdown are the same deterministic ledger-and-deadline pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an entertainment-industry back office that currently has almost no purpose-built compliance software. Association of Talent Agents (ATA) and National Association of Talent Representatives (NATR) provide a concentrated, identifiable buyer community.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the license/bond tracker, the client trust account ledger with commingling-prevention logic, and the minor work permit/Coogan Account module; get the first 5-8 clients through ATA/NATR outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — Association of Talent Agents (ATA) and National Association of Talent Representatives (NATR) member directory outreach, plus referral relationships with entertainment industry business managers who see agencies' trust accounting gaps firsthand.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per agency: $250-600/mo (a small boutique agency with under 40 talent: $250/mo; a larger agency with 150+ talent including a substantial minor roster requiring Coogan Account tracking: $600/mo). Managing 25-30 clients at $425/mo average = **$10,625-12,750/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every booking generates a new trust account transaction requiring reconciliation, every minor booking triggers a fresh Coogan Account deposit deadline, and talent roster turnover continuously adds new clients to track — a per-booking compliance stream, not an annual filing.
**Biggest risk:** Missing a statutory Coogan Account deposit deadline for a minor client's earnings is a labor commissioner violation with real reputational fallout in a small industry — the countdown-driven deposit alert, triggered automatically the moment a payment is logged, is the core protection.
**Growth path:** Side project → license tracker, trust account ledger, and Coogan Account module deployed, first 5-8 agency retainers through ATA/NATR outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 15-18 agency retainers. Replaces-full-time-salary → 25-30 agency retainers, pushing past $10.6-12.75K/mo.

### 3. Massage Therapy Establishment State Board Licensing & Human Trafficking Prevention Compliance
**Problem:** Massage therapy establishments must maintain two layers of state licensing: an establishment (business) license issued by the state massage board or, in states without a dedicated board, a local licensing authority, renewed annually or biennially and typically requiring proof of adequate liability insurance and a facility inspection; and individual therapist licensure for every practitioner on staff, requiring completion of a state-mandated minimum hours of massage therapy education (commonly 500-1,000 hours) plus, in most states, a passing MBLEx (Massage & Bodywork Licensing Examination) score, renewed on a 1-2 year cycle with continuing education requirements. Layered on top, a majority of states have enacted human trafficking prevention laws specifically targeting the massage industry (a response to trafficking activity historically concealed inside unlicensed "massage" storefronts) requiring establishments to post specific human trafficking hotline signage, and in a growing number of states, to document that all staff — not just therapists — completed state-approved human trafficking awareness training on a recurring schedule. A small multi-location massage therapy business (2-6 locations, 15-40 therapists) tracks establishment license renewal per location, individual therapist license and CE currency across a rotating staff, and human trafficking training completion for every employee with no dedicated compliance function — and a state board investigation finding an unlicensed therapist providing services, or missing trafficking-prevention signage/training documentation, risks both license suspension and, in several states, is now treated as a serious statutory violation carrying its own separate penalty track.
**What to build:** An establishment license portfolio per location (license number, insurance documentation, renewal deadline — with tiered alerts); an individual therapist license and CE tracker (state license number, MBLEx status, CE hours completed against the renewal requirement — with expiration alerts); a scheduling eligibility gate that blocks any therapist without a current license from being booked for a client appointment; and a human trafficking prevention compliance module tracking per-location signage posting confirmation and per-employee training completion against the state's required renewal cadence, with an audit-ready compliance export for a state board inspection.
**Skill fit:** State massage board licensing statutes, MBLEx requirements, and state human trafficking prevention mandates for the massage industry are public regulatory sources. Building the multi-location license portfolio, therapist credential tracker, and scheduling eligibility gate is a data management and rules-engine problem well suited to the builder's engineering background. The American Massage Therapy Association (AMTA) and Associated Bodywork & Massage Professionals (ABMP) provide a large, concentrated, identifiable buyer community.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the establishment license portfolio, the individual therapist license/CE tracker, the scheduling eligibility gate, and the human trafficking prevention compliance module; get the first 5-8 clients through AMTA/ABMP local chapter outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — American Massage Therapy Association (AMTA) and Associated Bodywork & Massage Professionals (ABMP) state chapter member outreach, both of which represent thousands of small massage therapy businesses actively navigating the recent wave of trafficking-prevention statutes.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per business: $150-400/mo (a single-location studio with 5-10 therapists: $150/mo; a multi-location business with 30+ therapists across several cities: $400/mo). Managing 45-60 clients at $275/mo average = **$12,375-16,500/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Therapist licenses renew on staggered cycles across a rotating roster, CE hours accumulate continuously, every new hire triggers a fresh credentialing event, and human trafficking training now recurs on a mandated schedule in most licensed states — a continuously active compliance surface, not a once-a-year renewal.
**Biggest risk:** A therapist providing services on a lapsed license — because the renewal date wasn't tracked against the actual practicing roster — is both a licensing violation and, in the current regulatory climate, an issue state boards actively investigate; the scheduling eligibility gate blocking any booking for a therapist without a current license is the core protection.
**Growth path:** Side project → establishment portfolio, therapist tracker, and scheduling gate deployed, first 5-8 business retainers through AMTA/ABMP outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 20-25 business retainers. Replaces-full-time-salary → 45-60 business retainers, pushing past $12.4-16.5K/mo.

### 4. Medical Waste Transporter & Biomedical Waste Hauler State Permit & DOT Infectious Substance Compliance
**Problem:** Companies that collect and transport regulated medical waste — sharps, pathological waste, and other biohazardous materials — from hospitals, clinics, dental offices, and veterinary practices must hold a state regulated medical waste transporter permit (administered by the state environmental agency or department of health, with permit and vehicle registration renewed annually) and comply with DOT hazardous materials transportation rules specific to infectious substances (49 CFR Part 173 Subpart I), which classify waste into Category A (highly infectious, requiring UN-specification packaging and placarding) and Category B, each with distinct packaging, labeling, and driver hazmat-endorsement requirements. Every load must be documented with a generator-to-disposal manifest or tracking form (state-specific, but functionally similar to a hazardous waste manifest) recording the waste's origin, quantity, transport vehicle, and the licensed treatment/disposal facility it was delivered to — a chain-of-custody record state inspectors audit specifically to confirm no medical waste was diverted to an unpermitted disposal site. Drivers must hold current hazmat endorsements and complete the biennial DOT hazmat training cycle, and vehicles require periodic decontamination documentation. A small medical waste hauler running 5-20 collection routes and a handful of drivers tracks state transporter permit renewal, per-load manifest completion and reconciliation against the destination facility's receipt confirmation, and driver hazmat endorsement/training currency with no dedicated compliance staff — an unreconciled manifest (waste picked up but no confirmed disposal receipt on file) is exactly the gap a state environmental audit is designed to catch.
**What to build:** A state transporter permit and vehicle registration tracker (per state, per vehicle: permit number, renewal deadline — with tiered alerts); a load manifest system generating the generator-to-disposal chain-of-custody record per pickup, with a reconciliation engine flagging any load picked up but lacking a confirmed disposal-facility receipt within the expected turnaround window; a driver hazmat endorsement and biennial DOT training tracker with dispatch-eligibility gating (a driver with a lapsed endorsement can't be assigned a route); and a Category A/B packaging compliance checklist per load type confirming the correct UN-specification packaging and placarding was used.
**Skill fit:** State medical waste transporter permit requirements and DOT's Part 173 Subpart I infectious substance rules are public regulatory sources, and the manifest reconciliation engine — matching pickup records against destination-facility disposal confirmations — is a direct data-reconciliation problem well suited to the builder's engineering background, the same pattern used successfully for hazardous waste and grain warehouse reconciliation elsewhere in this series. The Healthcare Environmental Resource Center and state medical waste hauler associations provide a reachable buyer community.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the transporter permit tracker, the load manifest and reconciliation engine, and the driver hazmat endorsement dispatch-eligibility gate; get the first 5-8 clients through direct outreach to regional medical waste haulers not affiliated with the large national players (Stericycle, Waste Management).
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — outreach through state medical/biohazardous waste hauler associations and referral relationships with independent medical waste treatment/disposal facility operators, who see which small regional haulers deliver loads with incomplete manifest documentation.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per hauler: $300-650/mo (a small hauler running 5-8 routes: $300/mo; a larger regional hauler with 20+ routes and multiple states: $650/mo). Managing 25-30 clients at $450/mo average = **$11,250-13,500/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every collection route generates fresh manifest and reconciliation events daily, driver hazmat training recurs on a biennial cycle across the fleet, and state permits renew annually per vehicle — a high-frequency, per-load compliance stream directly tied to the hauler's daily operations.
**Biggest risk:** A load picked up but never confirmed delivered to a licensed disposal facility — because the manifest reconciliation wasn't systematically checked — is the exact fact pattern a state environmental audit targets as potential illegal dumping; the reconciliation engine's flag on any unconfirmed load is the core protection.
**Growth path:** Side project → permit tracker, manifest reconciliation engine, and dispatch eligibility gate deployed, first 5-8 hauler retainers through direct outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-15 hauler retainers, disposal facility referral relationships established. Replaces-full-time-salary → 25-30 hauler retainers, pushing past $11.25-13.5K/mo.

### 5. Chiropractic Practice State Board License, In-Office X-Ray Registration & Medicare Enrollment Compliance
**Problem:** Chiropractic practices must maintain state chiropractic board licensure for every doctor of chiropractic on staff, renewed on a 1-2 year cycle with state-specific continuing education requirements (commonly including specific mandated topics like risk management or opioid/pain management CE in a growing number of states), and must operate strictly within the state board's defined scope of practice, which varies meaningfully by state (some states permit chiropractors to perform minor office procedures or order limited lab work, others restrict scope much more narrowly) — practicing outside scope is an active board enforcement target distinct from simple license lapse. Practices with in-office diagnostic X-ray equipment must separately register that equipment with the state's Bureau of Radiological Health (or equivalent agency) under state radiation control rules, which require periodic equipment calibration/inspection, a documented radiation safety program, and in most states specific technician certification or physician training hours for whoever operates the X-ray unit — a distinct registration and inspection cycle from the practice's chiropractic license itself. Practices billing Medicare (chiropractic is one of the few complementary/alternative fields with limited Medicare coverage, specifically for manual manipulation to correct subluxation) must maintain active PECOS enrollment and revalidate on CMS's standard cycle, with documentation requirements (X-ray or physical exam evidence of subluxation) specifically scrutinized in Medicare chiropractic billing audits, a segment CMS has flagged for elevated improper-payment rates. A small multi-doctor practice (2-6 chiropractors, 1-3 locations) tracks doctor license/CE renewal, X-ray equipment registration and inspection cycles, and Medicare PECOS revalidation with no dedicated compliance staff — a lapsed X-ray registration or an expired license discovered during a state board complaint investigation halts that provider's ability to treat patients immediately.
**What to build:** A doctor license and CE tracker (per chiropractor: state license number, renewal deadline, CE hours completed against required topics including any state-mandated specific-topic hours — with tiered alerts); an X-ray equipment registration and inspection tracker per unit (Bureau of Radiological Health registration number, calibration/inspection due date, operator certification on file); a Medicare PECOS enrollment and revalidation tracker with a documentation checklist reminder (subluxation documentation requirements) for claims billed under Medicare's limited chiropractic coverage; and a scope-of-practice reference tied to the practice's state confirming which services/procedures fall within the licensed scope, flagging anything that would require referral out.
**Skill fit:** State chiropractic board licensing rules, radiation control equipment registration requirements, and CMS PECOS enrollment data are public regulatory sources; building the multi-doctor license tracker, X-ray equipment registration/inspection tracker, and PECOS revalidation tracker is a data management and deadline-tracking problem well suited to the builder's engineering background. The American Chiropractic Association (ACA) and state chiropractic associations provide a concentrated, identifiable buyer community.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the doctor license/CE tracker, the X-ray equipment registration/inspection tracker, and the PECOS revalidation tracker; get the first 5-8 clients through state chiropractic association outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — American Chiropractic Association (ACA) state affiliate member outreach and networking at state chiropractic association annual conventions, where multi-doctor practice owners already discuss board CE requirements and Medicare audit risk.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per practice: $175-400/mo (a solo-doctor practice without in-office X-ray: $175/mo; a multi-doctor, multi-location practice with in-office imaging and active Medicare billing: $400/mo). Managing 45-55 clients at $275/mo average = **$12,375-15,125/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Doctor license and CE cycles stagger across a growing practice's provider roster, X-ray equipment inspection recurs on a fixed periodic cycle per unit, and PECOS revalidation and per-claim documentation checks recur continuously with every Medicare patient visit — an ongoing compliance surface, not an annual filing.
**Biggest risk:** Continuing to bill Medicare for manipulation services without the required subluxation documentation on file is exactly the pattern CMS's chiropractic-focused audits target for improper-payment recoupment — the documentation checklist reminder tied to every Medicare claim is the core protection.
**Growth path:** Side project → doctor license tracker, X-ray registration tracker, and PECOS revalidation tracker deployed, first 5-8 practice retainers through ACA state affiliate outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 20-25 practice retainers. Replaces-full-time-salary → 45-55 practice retainers, pushing past $12.4-15.1K/mo.

## This Session's Pick: #3 for Highest Ceiling and Largest Reachable Buyer Pool; #5 for Cleanest Recurring-Audit Hook

**Highest ceiling and largest reachable buyer pool:** Idea #3 (massage therapy establishments) combines the highest income ceiling in this session with by far the largest, most concentrated buyer community — AMTA and ABMP together represent tens of thousands of small massage businesses, and the recent wave of state human trafficking prevention mandates gives this product a second, fast-moving compliance hook on top of the existing dual-license requirement, at a price point that's an easy yes for even a single-location studio owner.

**Cleanest recurring-audit hook:** Idea #5 (chiropractic practices) stands out because it sits at the intersection of two independently strict audit regimes — state chiropractic board scope-of-practice enforcement and CMS's specifically-flagged elevated improper-payment scrutiny on chiropractic Medicare billing — giving the product two distinct, credible reasons for a practice owner to pay every month rather than one.

Idea #1 (debt settlement) has the sharpest single point of catastrophic failure in this session — a TSR dedicated-account violation can end a firm outright — but the buyer pool is smaller and more state-restricted than the other four ideas, making the ramp to 20-25 clients slower.
