# Main Income Ideas - 2026-11-11 (Session 184)

## Who this is for
A 29-year-old software engineer with data analytics skills who wants to replace a full-time salary ($8-15K+/mo) within 12-24 months by building solo, in evenings and weekends, with no outside funding. Every idea below must have a realistic path to first revenue inside 90 days and a named, reachable distribution channel.

## Lens for this session: Alex Hormozi
Built from the $100M Offers / $100M Leads frameworks:
- **Value equation:** Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood of Success) / (Time Delay x Effort & Sacrifice).
- **Grand Slam Offer:** performance-priced or guarantee-backed. Here the fee is a share of money the client was already owed.
- **Lead levers:** corporate and partner (B2B) lead sources, where one deal brings many customers.

Per standing feedback, none of these are compliance or regulatory tools. Each solves a revenue-leakage or growth problem.

## Ideas

### 1. Late-Delivery Refund Recovery for Small E-Commerce Stores

**Problem:** Carriers owe refunds for late deliveries and billing errors, but a store shipping 800 parcels a month never files the claims. Checking each tracking number against the carrier's service guarantee takes hours, so the money goes uncollected.

**What to build:** Connect to the store's shipping account (ShipStation, Shopify Shipping, carrier invoice exports). Scan shipments for late deliveries and invoice mismatches (wrong weight, wrong zone), file the refund claims through the carrier portal or API and track what is recovered. Show recovered dollars per month.

**Grand Slam angle:** "We only take 25% of what we recover. If we recover nothing, you pay nothing."

**Skill fit:** Carrier API integration, anomaly detection and workflow automation.

**MVP scope:** 3-4 weekends. Start with one carrier's invoice CSV and tracking data.

**Time to first $:** 30-60 days through e-commerce seller groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** A store shipping 800 parcels a month recovers about $350/mo. 25% = $88/mo. That is small per store, so target stores shipping 3,000+ parcels a month, which recover about $1,500/mo = $375/mo. 35 such stores = **$13,125/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Recoveries recur every month for as long as the store ships, with no work for the owner.

**Biggest risk:** Carrier rules change and some claims need manual filing. Keep the first version to the carriers with API access and send the rest through a manual claim queue.

**Growth path:** Small stores, then mid-size brands and 3PLs, then multi-carrier invoice auditing.

### 2. Zone-Based Pricing Optimizer for Small Courier and Local Delivery Firms

**Problem:** A small courier firm quotes by feel: a flat rate for "downtown," a guess for the suburbs. Some routes lose money because of traffic and distance, and others are underpriced against what customers would pay. The owner has years of delivery data in a spreadsheet and no analysis of it.

**What to build:** Import delivery history (pickup, drop-off, time, driver time, price). Compute cost per minute and margin by zone pair and time of day, show money-losing routes and suggest zone rates and surcharges (rush, after-hours). Give the dispatcher a quote tool that applies the rates.

**Grand Slam angle:** "We find at least 5% more margin in your delivery pricing or the first quarter is free."

**Skill fit:** Geospatial analysis, pricing logic and a quote widget.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 45-75 days through courier and delivery-business owner groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $179/mo. 65 firms = **$11,635/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Margin lifts show up in the owner's bank account, and pricing data improves as deliveries accumulate.

**Biggest risk:** Messy dispatch data. Offer a paid data-cleanup onboarding and begin with firms using one common dispatch tool.

**Growth path:** Couriers, then moving companies, limo and shuttle operators and food-delivery fleets.

### 3. Document-Chase Automation for Small Accounting and Bookkeeping Firms

**Problem:** A small firm needs bank statements, receipts and forms from clients, and staff spend hours emailing reminders and sorting what arrives. Work stalls until the last document comes in, and the firm cannot invoice or close the month. Clients ignore generic emails.

**What to build:** A per-client checklist of required documents, with a client portal link and a timed reminder sequence by email and SMS. Incoming files are auto-labeled and matched to the checklist. The firm sees a board of which clients are complete, partial or silent.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Cut document-chasing time in half within 60 days, or you pay nothing."

**Skill fit:** Portal, checklist logic, reminders and file classification.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-60 days through accountant and bookkeeper owner communities.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $99/mo + $2 per active client. A firm with 80 clients pays about $259/mo. 45 firms x $259 = **$11,655/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** It touches every client every month, so it becomes the firm's intake system.

**Biggest risk:** Competition from practice-management suites. Differentiate on simple setup, low price and an SMS-first reminder approach.

**Growth path:** Bookkeepers, then accountants, financial planners and immigration-paperwork consultants.

### 4. Corporate-Wellness Pitch Engine for Independent Gyms and Studios

**Problem:** Independent gyms rely on walk-ins and ads, while local employers with 50-300 staff would pay for on-site classes or discounted memberships. Winning one employer brings 20-60 members at once, but gym owners never prospect HR contacts.

**What to build:** A list of local employers by size and industry, with the HR or office-manager contact. A ready-made proposal ("lunchtime class series, $X per session") and a three-step outreach sequence. Track outreach, replies, meetings and signed corporate accounts, with a revenue-per-account report.

**Grand Slam angle:** "We book two employer meetings in 60 days or you do not pay."

**Skill fit:** Lead-list building, outreach templates and CRM.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days through gym-owner groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo + $150 per signed corporate account. A gym signing one account per quarter averages about $250/mo. 50 gyms x $250 = **$12,500/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Each employer account is recurring revenue and a reference for the next pitch.

**Biggest risk:** Employer sales cycles are slow. Offer a low-commitment pilot (four sessions) as the opening offer.

**Growth path:** Gyms, then yoga studios, physical therapists, massage clinics and healthy-meal providers.

### 5. Warranty-Claim Recovery for Independent Appliance and Electronics Repair Shops

**Problem:** Independent repair shops perform manufacturer-authorized warranty repairs but are paid only if the claim paperwork is complete and filed on time. Missing serial numbers, photos or parts return tags lead to rejected or never-filed claims, and the shop absorbs the cost of the repair.

**What to build:** A claim tracker: at job intake, capture serial number, purchase proof and photos with a checklist per brand. Track each claim's status, deadline and payment, flag claims missing a required item before the job closes and show unpaid claims by manufacturer.

**Grand Slam angle:** "We recover at least $3,000 in unpaid warranty claims in the first 90 days, or you pay nothing." The fee is 10% of recovered claims.

**Skill fit:** Intake checklists, document capture and payment reconciliation.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days through appliance-repair owner groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** A shop recovers $2,000/mo of previously lost claims = $200/mo, plus a $79/mo platform fee, about $280/mo. 45 shops x $280 = **$12,600/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Recovered cash is visible, and intake discipline reduces future losses too.

**Biggest risk:** Each manufacturer has its own claim process. Start with the three brands the most shops service.

**Growth path:** Appliance repair, then electronics, HVAC warranty work, auto glass and power-equipment dealers.
