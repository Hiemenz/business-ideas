### 1. Non-Consensual Tow & Vehicle Storage Facility (Impound Lot) Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Tow companies and vehicle storage facilities that perform non-consent tows — private-property tows, police-initiated impounds — operate under state VSF/impound licensing (such as Texas Occupations Code Chapter 2308) that requires a per-location storage-facility license and a per-truck operator permit, and every vehicle towed without the owner's consent triggers a statutory notification duty: the operator must mail notice to both the registered owner and any recorded lienholder, using the state's specified method and timing, before the vehicle can legally proceed toward a lien sale for unpaid storage fees. A lot that sells or auctions a vehicle before that notification window has fully run — or that can't produce proof the notice was actually mailed — is exactly the finding a state licensing-board complaint or a wrongful-lien-sale lawsuit is built to catch, and disputes over improperly noticed lien sales are a leading source of towing-industry litigation and license revocation. Every non-consent tow also requires a posted, capped daily storage rate and (in most states) a photographed intake condition record, since a rate charged above the statutory cap or a damage dispute with no intake photo on file is the other recurring complaint category regulators act on. A small towing operator running one or two impound lots and several trucks manages per-vehicle notification deadlines, lien-sale eligibility, and rate-cap compliance with a paper intake ticket and a filing cabinet of certified-mail receipts, with no systematic cross-check between a specific vehicle's actual notification date and whether today is the earliest day it can legally be sold.
**What to build:** A per-vehicle intake and notification tracker (tow date, owner/lienholder lookup, notice-sent date) with a "cleared for lien sale" gate blocking any vehicle from being scheduled for auction until the statutory notification window has fully expired; a rate-cap compliance checklist confirming charges never exceed the posted, state-capped daily rate; and an intake-condition photo log tied to each vehicle's record for damage-dispute defense.
**Skill fit:** State VSF/impound licensing statutes and their notification-timing requirements are public, structured regulatory text with a well-defined per-vehicle record model, and the lien-sale-eligibility gate is the same deterministic "block the action until the deadline clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a vehicle-custody trade most small tow operators currently track with a paper ticket and a stack of certified-mail receipts with no systematic link between a specific vehicle's notice date and its lien-sale eligibility.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the intake/notification tracker with the lien-sale gate, the rate-cap checklist, and the intake-photo log; land the first 5-8 operators through Towing and Recovery Association of America (TRAA) state affiliate outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — TRAA's state chapter directories and independent towing-operator Facebook groups, where small impound-lot operators already juggle per-vehicle notification deadlines and rate-cap compliance with no dedicated software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $279/mo base (up to 100 vehicles stored/month) + $2/mo per additional vehicle. Average client (250 vehicles/month, 150 over base) = $279 + 150×$2 = $579/mo. 16-20 operators × $579/mo = **$9,264-11,580/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every single non-consent tow reopens its own notification clock and lien-sale-eligibility check from scratch, with no vehicle ever inheriting compliance from the last one — a continuous, per-vehicle compliance stream tied directly to the operator's daily tow volume.
**Biggest risk:** A vehicle sold before its notification window expired, or one sold with no proof of mailed notice, is exactly the finding that triggers license revocation or a wrongful-sale lawsuit — the lien-sale-eligibility gate is the core protection, built to make the compliant path the only path the tool allows.
**Growth path:** Side project → intake/notification tracker, lien-sale gate, and rate-cap checklist deployed, first 5-8 operator retainers through TRAA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 10-13 operators. Replaces-full-time-salary → 16-20 operators, pushing past $9.26-11.58K/mo.

### 2. Asbestos Abatement Contractor NESHAP Notification & AHERA Certification Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Licensed asbestos abatement contractors operate under EPA's NESHAP Asbestos regulation (40 CFR Part 61 Subpart M), which requires a written notification filed with the state environmental agency or EPA regional office at least 10 working days before any covered renovation or demolition project begins — a hard filing deadline that doesn't bend for a client's rescheduled start date. A project that starts before its 10-day notification window has run is exactly the finding an EPA or state air-quality inspection is built to catch, and NESHAP violations carry federal civil penalties that can run into tens of thousands of dollars per day of noncompliance. Layered on top, AHERA (40 CFR Part 763) requires every supervisor and worker on an abatement crew to hold a current, annually renewed EPA-accredited training certificate specific to their role, and dispatching an uncertified worker onto a containment crew is a separate, frequently cited violation. Every containment area must also pass clearance air-sampling — an independent industrial hygienist's post-abatement air test coming back below the regulatory fiber-count threshold — before the space can legally be reoccupied, and a contractor who releases a space without a clean clearance result on file is exactly the gap a building owner's own liability review or a later EPA inquiry is built to catch. A small abatement contractor running several concurrent projects across a metro area manages per-project 10-day notification deadlines, per-worker AHERA certification currency, and per-project clearance-sample tracking with a project binder and no systematic alert distinguishing which project is cleared to start from which is still inside its mandatory notification window.
**What to build:** A per-project NESHAP notification tracker computing the 10-working-day filing deadline from the planned start date with a "cleared to start" gate blocking mobilization until the notification has been filed and the window has run; a per-worker AHERA accreditation currency tracker gating crew assignment; and a clearance air-sample tracker requiring a documented passing result before a project is marked ready for reoccupancy.
**Skill fit:** NESHAP's 10-day notification requirement and AHERA's accreditation-renewal cycle are public, structured federal regulatory text with a well-defined per-project and per-worker record model, and the start-eligibility gate plus the reoccupancy gate are the same deterministic "compute the deadline from the trigger event / block the action until the required result is on file" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an environmental-remediation trade most small contractors currently track in a project binder with no systematic cross-check between a specific project's planned start date and its actual filed notification.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the NESHAP notification tracker with the start-eligibility gate, the per-worker AHERA tracker, and the clearance-sample log; land the first 5-8 contractors through Indoor Air Quality Association (IAQA) member outreach and state asbestos-contractor licensee lists.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — IAQA's member directory and state environmental agencies' public licensed-asbestos-contractor rosters, where small abatement firms already juggle per-project notification deadlines and per-worker certification renewal with no dedicated compliance software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299/mo base + $25/mo per active project logged that month. Average client (10 projects/month) = $299 + $250 = $549/mo. 17-22 contractors × $549/mo = **$9,333-12,078/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new project reopens its own 10-day notification clock and its own clearance-sample requirement, and every worker's AHERA certification renews on an independent annual cycle — a continuous, per-project compliance stream tied directly to the contractor's job volume rather than a one-time licensing event.
**Biggest risk:** A project started before its NESHAP notification window has run, or a space reoccupied without a passing clearance sample, is exactly the finding that draws steep federal civil penalties — the start-eligibility gate and the clearance-sample requirement are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → notification tracker, AHERA gate, and clearance log deployed, first 5-8 contractor retainers through IAQA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 11-14 contractors. Replaces-full-time-salary → 17-22 contractors, pushing past $9.33-12.08K/mo.

### 3. Water Well Drilling Contractor License & Well Completion Report Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Licensed water well drilling contractors operate under state water well driller licensing statutes — such as Texas's Water Well Drillers/Pump Installers Act (Occupations Code Chapter 1901), administered by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation — that require a periodically renewed driller's license, a well construction permit filed with the state or local groundwater district before drilling begins in most jurisdictions, and a Well Completion Report (well log) filed with the state water resources agency within a statutory window after the well is finished, commonly 30-60 days, documenting drilled depth, casing specifications, geologic strata encountered, and static water level. A driller who finishes a well and lets the completion-report deadline pass, or who starts drilling without a matching filed construction permit, is exactly the finding a state groundwater regulator's audit is built to catch, and unfiled well logs are a chronic enforcement target because they're also the primary public data source regulators use to manage regional aquifers. A small well-drilling outfit running crews across several rigs and a rotating schedule of residential, agricultural, and light-commercial wells manages per-well permit status, per-well completion-report deadlines, and driller/pump-installer license renewal with a rig-side clipboard and a filing cabinet, with no systematic cross-check between a well finished last month and whether its completion report was actually submitted.
**What to build:** A per-well construction-permit tracker with a "cleared to drill" gate blocking a rig from being scheduled until a matching permit is on file; a per-well completion-report deadline tracker computing the filing window from the actual completion date with escalating alerts as it approaches; and a driller/pump-installer license renewal tracker.
**Skill fit:** State well-driller licensing cycles and completion-report filing deadlines are public, structured regulatory data with a well-defined per-well record model, and the drill-eligibility gate plus the completion-report countdown are the same deterministic "block the action until the permit clears / compute the deadline from the trigger event" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a groundwater-construction trade most small drillers currently track on a rig-side clipboard with no systematic alert when a finished well's completion-report window is closing.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the permit tracker with the drill-eligibility gate, the completion-report countdown, and the license renewal tracker; land the first 5-8 contractors through National Ground Water Association (NGWA) state chapter outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — NGWA's state chapter directories and public state driller-licensee rosters, where small drilling outfits already juggle per-well permit and completion-report deadlines with no dedicated compliance software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249/mo base + $15/mo per well completed that month. Average client (12 wells/month) = $249 + $180 = $429/mo. 22-28 contractors × $429/mo = **$9,438-12,012/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new well opens its own permit-eligibility check and its own completion-report filing clock, with no well ever inheriting compliance from the last one — a continuous, per-well compliance stream tied directly to the contractor's drilling volume rather than a once-a-year license renewal.
**Biggest risk:** A well drilled without a matching construction permit, or a completion report never filed within the statutory window, is exactly the finding a state groundwater audit is built to catch — the drill-eligibility gate and the completion-report countdown are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → permit tracker, completion-report countdown, and license tracker deployed, first 5-8 contractor retainers through NGWA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 14-17 contractors. Replaces-full-time-salary → 22-28 contractors, pushing past $9.44-12.01K/mo.

### 4. Structural Pest Control (Termite/WDO) Inspection & Treatment Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Structural pest control companies performing termite and wood-destroying-organism (WDO) inspections operate under state pest control boards — such as Florida's Structural Pest Control Act (Florida Statutes Chapter 482) — that require a licensed operator credential and certified-applicator status renewed on a continuing-education cycle, and every real estate transaction relying on a WDO inspection depends on the standardized NPMA-33 report being both accurate and current, since most lenders and title companies treat the report as valid for only a short window, commonly 30 days, before closing. A report issued outside that window, or by a technician whose applicator certification has lapsed, is exactly the finding a state board complaint or a failed-closing dispute is built to catch, and a pest control company whose reports keep expiring before closing dates burns through real estate agent trust fast. Every chemical treatment also has to be logged under FIFRA (7 U.S.C. §136) and state pesticide law — EPA registration number, application site, rate, and target pest — retained for a mandated period, and a company that can't produce that record for a specific treated property during a state inspection is exactly the gap regulators are trained to find. A small structural pest control company running several technicians across a metro area's inspection and treatment schedule manages per-technician license/CEU currency, per-property WDO report validity windows tied to closing dates, and pesticide application recordkeeping with a paper inspection pad and no systematic alert when a signed report is about to expire before its closing date.
**What to build:** A per-technician license and CEU-currency tracker with a "cleared to inspect/treat" gate; a per-property WDO report tracker computing each report's expiration relative to the buyer's closing date and flagging any report at risk of expiring first; and a FIFRA-compliant pesticide application log recording EPA registration number, site, and rate per treatment with the mandated retention period built in.
**Skill fit:** State structural pest control licensing cycles and FIFRA's application-recordkeeping requirements are public, structured regulatory data with a well-defined per-technician and per-property record model, and the report-validity tracker plus the practice-eligibility gate are the same deterministic "compute the deadline against a second date / block the action until the credential clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a licensed-pesticide-application trade most small companies currently track with a paper inspection pad and no systematic cross-check between a report's issue date and the closing date it has to survive until.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the technician license tracker with the practice-eligibility gate, the WDO report validity tracker, and the pesticide application log; land the first 5-8 companies through National Pest Management Association (NPMA) state affiliate outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — NPMA's state affiliate directories and local real estate agent referral relationships, where small pest control companies already juggle per-report expiration windows tied to closing dates with no dedicated compliance software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base (up to 50 WDO inspections/month) + $3/mo per additional inspection. Average client (120 inspections/month, 70 over base) = $199 + 70×$3 = $409/mo. 23-29 companies × $409/mo = **$9,407-11,861/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new WDO inspection reopens its own closing-deadline race, and every treatment logged adds to a FIFRA-mandated retention record that has to stay reconcilable — a continuous, per-inspection and per-treatment compliance stream tied directly to the company's real estate transaction volume.
**Biggest risk:** A WDO report that expires before the closing date it was meant to support, or a treatment with no FIFRA-compliant record on file, is exactly the finding that draws a state board complaint or costs a company its real estate referral relationships — the report-validity tracker and the application log are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → license tracker, report-validity tracker, and application log deployed, first 5-8 company retainers through NPMA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 15-18 companies. Replaces-full-time-salary → 23-29 companies, pushing past $9.41-11.86K/mo.

### 5. Commercial Blasting & Explosives Contractor ATF License & Seismograph Monitoring Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Commercial blasting contractors serving quarries, mines, and construction demolition sites operate under ATF explosives licensing (27 CFR Part 555), which requires a periodically renewed Type/user permit and a licensed magazine storage facility subject to its own inspection cycle, layered with individual state blaster certification for every technician actually detonating charges. A blast fired by an uncertified blaster, or a magazine storage facility running past its inspection window, is exactly the finding an ATF compliance inspection is built to catch, and repeated explosives-licensing violations are grounds for permit revocation — an existential risk for a contractor whose entire business depends on legally possessing explosive material. Separately, state mining and quarry regulators require every blast to be documented with seismograph or vibration-monitoring readings proving the blast stayed within the jurisdiction's peak-particle-velocity limit, plus a retained blast log recording date, location, charge weight, and scaled distance — a neighboring property owner's vibration complaint or a state mining inspector's audit is exactly the scenario this record is built to survive, and a contractor who can't produce a specific blast's monitoring data has no defense against a damage claim. A small blasting contractor running a rotating crew across several active quarry and construction sites manages per-blaster ATF/state certification currency, per-blast seismograph compliance, and magazine-storage inspection cycles with a site foreman's paper log and no systematic cross-check between a specific blast's monitoring reading and the jurisdiction's actual PPV limit.
**What to build:** A per-blaster ATF permit and state certification tracker with a "cleared to blast" gate blocking assignment of any technician whose credential has lapsed; a per-blast seismograph reading log comparing each result against the applicable PPV limit and flagging any exceedance; and a magazine storage facility inspection-cycle tracker.
**Skill fit:** ATF's explosives permitting cycle and state PPV-limit regulations are public, structured federal and state regulatory data with a well-defined per-blaster and per-blast record model, and the blast-eligibility gate plus the seismograph-compliance log are the same deterministic "block the action until the credential clears / cross-check the reading against the mandated limit" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a federally licensed explosives trade most small contractors currently track with a site foreman's paper log and no systematic comparison between a blast's actual monitoring data and the limit it has to stay under.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the blaster certification tracker with the blast-eligibility gate, the seismograph-compliance log, and the magazine-inspection tracker; land the first 5-8 contractors through International Society of Explosives Engineers (ISEE) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — ISEE's member directory and state mining/quarry blasting contractor associations, where small blasting crews already juggle per-blaster certification and per-blast monitoring records with no dedicated compliance software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $349/mo base (up to 20 blasts/month) + $12/mo per additional blast. Average client (35 blasts/month, 15 over base) = $349 + 15×$12 = $529/mo. 17-22 contractors × $529/mo = **$8,993-11,638/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every single blast reopens its own seismograph-compliance check and blast-log entry, and every blaster's ATF and state certification carries its own renewal risk — a continuous, per-blast compliance stream tied directly to the contractor's job volume rather than a once-every-three-years license renewal.
**Biggest risk:** A blast fired by an uncertified blaster, or a monitoring reading that exceeds the jurisdiction's PPV limit with no documented log to contest a damage claim, is exactly the finding that can revoke an ATF permit or cost a contractor a lawsuit — the blast-eligibility gate and the seismograph-compliance log are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → blaster certification tracker, seismograph log, and magazine-inspection tracker deployed, first 5-8 contractor retainers through ISEE outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 10-13 contractors. Replaces-full-time-salary → 17-22 contractors, pushing past $8.99-11.64K/mo.
