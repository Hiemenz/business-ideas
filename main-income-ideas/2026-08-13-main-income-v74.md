### 1. Commercial Drone/UAS Service Operator FAA Part 107, Remote ID & LAANC Compliance
**Problem:** Commercial drone service companies — roofing and insurance inspection firms, aerial mapping and surveying operations, real estate media companies, and ag-services operators — employ pilots who must hold an FAA Part 107 Remote Pilot Certificate, renewed through mandatory recurrent training completed every 24 calendar months; a pilot flying commercial operations on a lapsed certificate has flown outside FAA authorization entirely, invalidating the legal basis for every flight conducted during the lapse. Every drone weighing over 0.55 lbs used commercially must carry current FAA registration, and under the FAA's Remote ID rule (14 CFR Part 89, enforced since March 2024) must broadcast Remote ID — either built-in or via an external broadcast module — with a drone flown without current Remote ID compliance being an independently citable violation distinct from any pilot-certificate issue. Flights in controlled airspace (Class B, C, D, or surface E, common near any airport a roofing or real estate drone operator works close to) require LAANC (Low Altitude Authorization and Notification Capability) authorization obtained before each flight, or a standing FAA waiver for operations beyond standard Part 107 limits — night operations became standard under the 2021 rule change with anti-collision lighting, but flights over people, over moving vehicles, or beyond visual line of sight (BVLOS) still require a specific waiver on file — and a flight into controlled airspace without current LAANC authorization is exactly the finding an FAA ramp check or a client's insurance audit targets first. A small drone service company running 3-8 pilots and a growing fleet across dozens of monthly job sites manages per-pilot recurrency, per-drone registration/Remote ID compliance, and per-flight LAANC/waiver documentation with no dedicated compliance function.
**What to build:** A per-pilot Remote Pilot Certificate recurrency tracker computing each pilot's 24-month renewal deadline with a "cleared to fly" gate; a per-drone FAA registration and Remote ID compliance tracker; and a per-flight LAANC authorization/waiver log reconciling each job site's location against controlled-airspace maps before the flight is logged as compliant.
**Skill fit:** The FAA's LAANC facility maps, Part 107 recurrency cycle, and Remote ID rule are public, structured federal regulatory and airspace data well suited to a developer who can build a location-to-airspace-class lookup, and the fly-eligibility gate is the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a fast-growing drone-service trade most small companies currently track on a shared spreadsheet with no systematic cross-check between a pilot's certificate status and the airspace they're flying into.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the recurrency tracker with the fly-eligibility gate, the drone registration/Remote ID tracker, and the LAANC/waiver log; land the first 5-8 companies through AUVSI (Association for Uncrewed Vehicle Systems International) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — AUVSI member directory and regional drone-service-company networks (roofing/insurance inspection franchises, ag-drone dealer networks), where small operators already navigate LAANC authorization and Remote Pilot Certificate recurrency across a growing pilot roster with no dedicated compliance staff.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149/mo base + $15/mo per pilot tracked. Average client (5 pilots) = $149 + $75 = $224/mo. 40-50 companies × $224/mo = **$8,960-11,200/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new pilot hired opens its own 24-month recurrency clock, every drone purchased adds its own registration/Remote ID compliance record, and every flight in controlled airspace generates its own LAANC authorization event — a continuous, per-pilot and per-flight compliance stream tied directly to the company's flight volume and fleet growth.
**Biggest risk:** A pilot flying commercially on a lapsed Remote Pilot Certificate, a drone missing current Remote ID compliance, or a flight into controlled airspace without a current LAANC authorization on file is exactly the finding an FAA ramp check or a client's insurance audit is built to catch — the fly-eligibility gate and the LAANC/waiver log are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → Remote Pilot Certificate tracker, drone registration/Remote ID tracker, and LAANC/waiver log deployed, first 5-8 company retainers through AUVSI outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 18-22 companies. Replaces-full-time-salary → 40-50 companies, pushing past $8.96-11.20K/mo.

### 2. Telecommunications Tower Climbing & Wireless Infrastructure Company OSHA, NATE & FCC ASR Compliance
**Problem:** Companies that climb communication towers to install and maintain antennas operate under OSHA's fall-protection enforcement for communication towers (29 CFR 1926.502) — tower climbing carries one of the highest fatality rates of any U.S. occupation, and OSHA's tower-specific enforcement initiative targets exactly this hazard — and every climber must hold current NATE (National Association of Tower Erectors) climber certification, typically tracked across competent-person and rescue-certification tiers, renewed on a recurring cycle; a climber sent up a tower without current fall-protection and rescue certification on file is one of the most common findings behind an OSHA fatality investigation. Every tower over 200 feet — or a shorter tower near an airport — must carry current FCC Antenna Structure Registration (ASR) with painting and obstruction-lighting compliance verified against FAA Advisory Circular 70/7460, and the tower owner or servicing company must confirm the lighting is functioning and report any outage to the FAA within a defined window, commonly 30 minutes — an unreported lighting outage on a registered tower is a distinct FCC/FAA violation independent of any climbing-safety issue. Every structural modification adding new antenna loading must be verified against the tower's ANSI/TIA-222 structural rating by a qualified engineer before installation, and a tower loaded beyond its rated capacity without a current structural analysis is exactly the gap an FCC inspection or a carrier's own structural-audit program targets. A small tower company running several climbing crews across dozens of tower sites manages per-climber NATE/rescue-certification currency, per-tower ASR lighting-compliance and outage-reporting, and per-modification structural-analysis documentation with no dedicated compliance function.
**What to build:** A per-climber NATE certification and rescue-cert tracker with a "cleared to climb" gate blocking a climber from a job assignment once certification lapses; a per-tower FCC ASR lighting-status monitor with an outage-reporting-window countdown; and a per-modification structural-analysis (TIA-222 loading) document tracker.
**Skill fit:** The FCC's public ASR database, NATE's certification-tier structure, and TIA-222's structural-loading framework are public, checkable regulatory and certifying-body sources with a well-defined per-climber and per-tower record requirement, and the climb-eligibility gate plus the lighting-outage timer are the same deterministic "block the action / start the countdown at the trigger event" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a high-fatality-risk trade most small tower companies currently track on a shared spreadsheet with no systematic cross-check between a climber's certification status and the tower they're assigned to.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the NATE certification tracker with the climb-eligibility gate, the ASR lighting-status monitor, and the structural-analysis tracker; land the first 5-8 companies through NATE member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — NATE (National Association of Tower Erectors) member directory and regional wireless infrastructure contractor networks, where small tower companies already navigate per-climber certification currency and FCC ASR lighting-outage reporting windows by hand.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $18/mo per climber tracked. Average client (5 climbers) = $199 + $90 = $289/mo. 30-38 companies × $289/mo = **$8,670-10,982/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every climber's NATE certification and rescue-cert renew on their own independent cycles, every tower carries its own continuous FCC ASR lighting-compliance obligation regardless of climb activity, and every antenna-loading modification opens its own structural-analysis documentation event — a continuous, per-climber and per-tower compliance stream tied directly to the company's crew size and tower-site count.
**Biggest risk:** A climber sent up a tower without current fall-protection/rescue certification, or a registered tower's obstruction-lighting outage left unreported past the FCC's notification window, is exactly the finding behind an OSHA fatality investigation or an FCC enforcement action — the climb-eligibility gate and the lighting-outage timer are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → NATE certification tracker, ASR lighting-status monitor, and structural-analysis tracker deployed, first 5-8 company retainers through NATE outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 14-18 companies. Replaces-full-time-salary → 30-38 companies, pushing past $8.67-10.98K/mo.

### 3. Dry Cleaning Facility EPA NESHAP Subpart M & State Environmental Fund Compliance
**Problem:** Dry cleaning facilities using perchloroethylene (perc) operate under EPA's NESHAP Subpart M (40 CFR 63, Perchloroethylene Dry Cleaning Facilities), which requires a leak inspection on a fixed interval — weekly for most machine types — using a halogenated-hydrocarbon detector, with results logged and any detected leak repaired within a defined window, commonly 24 hours for a major leak component; an unrepaired or undocumented leak inspection is one of the most common findings an EPA or state air-quality inspection targets, and perc machines co-located in a residential building face additional, stricter operating requirements including a phase-out deadline. Beyond the federal rule, most states impose their own dry cleaner-specific environmental registration — commonly a per-facility annual fee funding a state drycleaning solvent cleanup or remediation trust fund (Florida's Drycleaning Solvent Cleanup Program and Texas's Dry Cleaner Environmental Response Fund are two well-known examples) — and a facility that lets this registration lapse loses access to the very state cleanup fund it may need if contamination is later discovered on the property, a consequence distinct from any federal EPA penalty. Every facility handling spent perc, still bottoms, and filter cartridges also carries RCRA hazardous-waste-generator recordkeeping obligations for that solvent-waste stream. A small dry cleaning operation running one or more plants manages the weekly leak-inspection log, per-state environmental fund registration renewal, and solvent hazardous-waste manifest documentation with no dedicated compliance function.
**What to build:** A weekly leak-inspection log with an interval engine flagging any missed or overdue inspection and a 24-hour repair countdown the moment a leak is detected; a state drycleaning environmental fund registration renewal tracker; and a solvent hazardous-waste manifest log.
**Skill fit:** NESHAP Subpart M's leak-inspection interval and repair window, and state drycleaning environmental fund registration cycles, are public, structured regulatory requirements with a well-defined per-machine record structure, and the leak-inspection interval engine plus the repair-window countdown are the same deterministic "compute the deadline from the trigger event" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a perc-equipment trade most small dry cleaners currently track on a paper log clipped to the machine with no systematic overdue-inspection cross-check.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the leak-inspection log with the interval engine, the environmental fund registration tracker, and the solvent waste-manifest log; land the first 5-8 facilities through DLI (Drycleaning & Laundry Institute) and NCA (National Cleaners Association) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — DLI and NCA member directories and state dry cleaner association meetings, where independent facility owners already navigate weekly leak-inspection logging and state environmental fund renewal with no dedicated compliance staff.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $179/mo base + $25/mo per dry cleaning machine tracked. Average client (2 machines) = $179 + $50 = $229/mo. 40-49 facilities × $229/mo = **$9,160-11,221/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every dry cleaning machine carries its own recurring weekly leak-inspection clock independent of production volume, every state environmental fund registration renews on its own annual cycle, and every solvent-waste pickup generates its own manifest-reconciliation event — a continuous, per-machine compliance stream tied directly to the facility's equipment count.
**Biggest risk:** A dry cleaning machine operated past a missed weekly leak inspection, or a detected leak left unrepaired past the federal 24-hour repair window, is exactly the finding an EPA or state air-quality inspection is built to catch — the leak-inspection interval engine and the repair-window countdown are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → leak-inspection log, environmental fund registration tracker, and solvent waste-manifest log deployed, first 5-8 facility retainers through DLI/NCA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 18-22 facilities. Replaces-full-time-salary → 40-49 facilities, pushing past $9.16-11.22K/mo.

### 4. Manufactured (Mobile) Home Dealer & Installer HUD Code, State Installer License & Wind-Zone Anchoring Compliance
**Problem:** Manufactured home dealers sell homes built to HUD's National Manufactured Housing Construction and Safety Standards (24 CFR Part 3280, the "HUD Code"), and every home must carry a verifiable HUD certification label (the red tag) and data plate before a sale can close — a home sold or installed without a verifiable current HUD label on file is one of the most common findings a state manufactured housing program audit targets. Most states require a dedicated manufactured home installer license — distinct from a general contractor license — for the technician setting up the home on-site, and installation must follow either the manufacturer's specific instructions or, absent those, HUD's Model Manufactured Home Installation Standards (24 CFR Part 3285), which govern anchoring, blocking, and tie-down specifications rated to the home's assigned wind zone; an installation whose anchoring plan doesn't match the home's rated wind zone is exactly the gap a state installer-license audit or a post-storm insurance investigation targets, since manufactured home wind-damage claims routinely trace back to non-compliant tie-down installation. Dealers must also hold a state manufactured home dealer license — renewed annually and commonly bonded — separate from any general motor vehicle or RV dealer license, and warranty claims for defects covered under the manufacturer's one-year HUD-mandated warranty must be tracked and resolved within the manufacturer's required response window or the dealer risks a state consumer-complaint escalation. A small dealer/installer running several installer crews across a multi-county territory manages per-home HUD label/data-plate verification, per-installation wind-zone anchoring compliance, and warranty-claim response-window tracking with no dedicated compliance function.
**What to build:** A per-home HUD label/data-plate verification tracker gating a sale from being marked closed until confirmed; a per-installation wind-zone anchoring-spec checklist matching the home's rated wind zone to the installer's actual tie-down plan; and a warranty-claim response-window tracker.
**Skill fit:** HUD's Part 3280/3285 standards and state manufactured home installer licensing requirements are public, structured federal and state regulatory sources with a well-defined per-home record requirement, and the label-verification gate plus the wind-zone anchoring checklist are the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance record clears / reconcile the plan against the requirement" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a housing trade most small dealers currently track on a paper sales folder with no systematic per-installation wind-zone cross-check.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the HUD label/data-plate tracker, the wind-zone anchoring checklist, and the warranty-claim tracker; land the first 5-8 dealers through Manufactured Housing Institute (MHI) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — Manufactured Housing Institute (MHI) member directory and state manufactured housing association meetings, where small independent dealers already navigate per-home HUD label verification and wind-zone installation compliance with no dedicated compliance staff.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $15/mo per installer crew tracked. Average client (3 crews) = $199 + $45 = $244/mo. 37-46 dealers × $244/mo = **$9,028-11,224/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every home sold opens its own HUD label/data-plate verification event, every installation opens its own wind-zone anchoring-compliance check, and every warranty claim opens its own response-window clock — a continuous, per-home and per-installation compliance stream tied directly to the dealer's sales and installation volume.
**Biggest risk:** A home installed with an anchoring plan that doesn't match its rated wind zone, or a home sold without a verifiable current HUD label on file, is exactly the finding a state manufactured housing program audit or a post-storm insurance investigation is built to catch — the label-verification gate and the wind-zone anchoring checklist are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → HUD label/data-plate tracker, wind-zone anchoring checklist, and warranty-claim tracker deployed, first 5-8 dealer retainers through MHI outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 16-20 dealers. Replaces-full-time-salary → 37-46 dealers, pushing past $9.03-11.22K/mo.

### 5. Egg Producer & Processor FDA Egg Safety Rule, Salmonella Enteritidis Testing & USDA Grading Compliance
**Problem:** Egg producers with 3,000 or more laying hens operate under FDA's Egg Safety Rule (21 CFR Part 118), which requires a written Salmonella Enteritidis (SE) prevention plan covering biosecurity and rodent control, and a defined environmental testing schedule per hen house — commonly at specific points in the laying cycle — that, if a test comes back positive, triggers a mandatory egg-testing regimen before eggs from that house can be sold for table use rather than diverted to pasteurization; a producer that misses a scheduled environmental test, or fails to divert eggs after a positive SE environmental result, is exactly the finding an FDA or state agriculture department inspection targets, and in the worst case precedes a Salmonella-linked recall. Producers selling under USDA's voluntary grading program — a near-requirement for most large retail buyers — must maintain current USDA grading-plant registration and per-lot grading documentation, and every producer must meet refrigeration requirements (eggs held and transported at 45°F or below within a specific window after packing) that a state or FDA inspector checks first during a facility visit. A small producer running several hen houses and a packing operation manages the SE environmental-testing interval calendar, per-house divert/hold status after a positive result, and refrigeration temperature-log completeness with no dedicated compliance function — and a hen house that missed its scheduled SE environmental test, or eggs shipped without confirming cold-chain compliance, is exactly the gap an FDA inspection or a recall investigation is built to catch.
**What to build:** A per-house SE environmental-testing interval tracker with a hard "divert to pasteurization" flag triggered automatically by a positive test result until clearance testing passes; a refrigeration/cold-chain temperature-log tracker; and a USDA grading-plant registration and per-lot grading documentation tracker.
**Skill fit:** FDA's Egg Safety Rule testing schedule and USDA's grading-program registration requirements are public, structured federal regulatory sources with a well-defined per-house and per-lot record requirement, and the environmental-testing tracker plus the automatic divert flag are the same deterministic "compute the deadline from the trigger event / block the action until the compliance record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an egg-production trade most small producers currently track on a paper log with no systematic cross-check between a house's test status and its eggs' sale eligibility.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the SE environmental-testing tracker with the divert flag, the cold-chain temperature-log tracker, and the USDA grading tracker; land the first 5-8 producers through United Egg Producers (UEP) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — United Egg Producers (UEP) member directory and state egg producer association meetings, where small independent producers already navigate SE environmental-testing intervals and USDA grading documentation with no dedicated compliance staff.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $25/mo per hen house tracked. Average client (4 houses) = $199 + $100 = $299/mo. 30-38 producers × $299/mo = **$8,970-11,362/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every hen house carries its own independent SE environmental-testing clock, a positive test triggers its own divert-and-clearance-testing chain, and every packed lot generates its own cold-chain and grading-documentation event — a continuous, per-house compliance stream tied directly to the producer's flock size and packing volume.
**Biggest risk:** A hen house that missed its scheduled SE environmental test, or eggs shipped for table use after a positive result without confirming the required divert-and-clearance sequence, is exactly the gap an FDA inspection or a Salmonella-linked recall investigation is built to catch — the environmental-testing tracker and the automatic divert flag are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → SE testing tracker, cold-chain log, and USDA grading tracker deployed, first 5-8 producer retainers through UEP outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 14-18 producers. Replaces-full-time-salary → 30-38 producers, pushing past $8.97-11.36K/mo.
