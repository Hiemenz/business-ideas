# Main Income Ideas - 2026-11-11 (Session 183)

## Who this is for
A 29-year-old software engineer with data analytics skills who wants to replace a full-time salary ($8-15K+/mo) within 12-24 months by building solo, in evenings and weekends, with no outside funding. Every idea below must have a realistic path to first revenue inside 90 days and a named, reachable distribution channel.

## Lens for this session: Alex Hormozi
Built from the $100M Offers / $100M Leads frameworks:
- **Value equation:** Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood of Success) / (Time Delay x Effort & Sacrifice).
- **Grand Slam Offer:** performance-priced or guarantee-backed.
- **Value ladder and ascension:** give every customer a clear next offer. Most small businesses sell one thing once and never present the next step.

Per standing feedback, none of these are compliance or regulatory tools. Each solves a revenue-leakage or growth problem.

## Ideas

### 1. Good-Better-Best Proposal Builder for HVAC and Roofing Replacement Sales

**Problem:** A rep arrives at a $9K furnace or roof replacement and hands over one price. A single price invites "let me think about it" and shopping around, while three options let the customer choose which version to buy instead of whether to buy. Most small companies lack a tiered proposal template and build quotes by hand in a document, which takes hours and delays the quote.

**What to build:** A tablet proposal builder: the rep enters a few job details and the tool generates a three-tier proposal (basic, recommended, premium) from the company's price book, with a financing-payment line, warranty and photos. The customer picks on the tablet and signs. Track which tier is chosen, close rate and average ticket per rep.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Raise your average ticket by 10% within 60 days, or the first quarter is free."

**Skill fit:** Pricing rules, proposal rendering, e-signature and rep analytics.

**MVP scope:** 3-4 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days through HVAC and roofing owner groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149/mo + $15/rep/mo. A 6-rep company pays about $239/mo. 50 companies x $239 = **$11,950/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** The price book and proposal history become the company's core sales system, and rep analytics reveals coaching opportunities.

**Biggest risk:** Competition from established field-service platforms. Differentiate on simplicity and a focus on replacement sales only.

**Growth path:** HVAC and roofing, then windows, siding, flooring and solar.

### 2. Next-Best-Offer Engine for Independent Salons and Barbershops

**Problem:** A stylist knows a client's last service but rarely offers the logical next one (color after a cut, a treatment after a color, a retail product for the home routine). Without prompts, a client's visit value stays flat. Independent salon owners have the service history in their booking software and no system to use it.

**What to build:** Import appointment history. Compute the most common next services and products by client segment. Show the stylist a pre-visit card ("Maria: last color 9 weeks ago, suggest gloss + take-home shampoo") and send a pre-visit text offering the add-on. Track attach rate and added revenue per stylist.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Free to start. We take 10% of the added add-on revenue."

**Skill fit:** Booking-system exports (Square, Vagaro, Fresha), association analysis and messaging.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-60 days through salon-owner Facebook groups and distributor-rep networks.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** A 5-chair salon adds $1,500/mo of add-on revenue, so 10% = $150/mo, plus a $49/mo platform fee, about $200/mo. 60 salons x $200 = **$12,000/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Higher revenue per visit compounds, and stylists keep using pre-visit cards because they raise their own tips.

**Biggest risk:** Stylists may resist scripted upsells. Frame it as a client-memory aid, not a sales script.

**Growth path:** Salons and barbers, then nail studios, lash studios, tattoo shops and massage clinics.

### 3. Group-Buying Co-Op for Independent Restaurants' Supplies

**Problem:** A single independent restaurant buys paper goods, cleaning supplies and staples at list price from a distributor, while chains negotiate bulk rates. Owners know they overpay but have no time to collect quotes or coordinate with neighbors.

**What to build:** A marketplace where restaurants in the same metro join a monthly group order. The tool aggregates quantities for common items, gets bulk quotes from 2-3 distributors and shows each restaurant its saving versus its current price. Each owner places an order in a few taps, with one delivery drop per area.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Save at least 8% on supplies or you do not pay," funded by a 2% fee on order volume.

**Skill fit:** Marketplace logic, quote comparison and order aggregation.

**MVP scope:** 4 weekends, starting with a single metro and a narrow list of items.

**Time to first $:** 60-90 days, since it requires both restaurants and a distributor partner.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** 2% fee on supply spend. A restaurant spends $6,000/mo on covered items = $120/mo. 100 restaurants = **$12,000/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** More members increase buying power, which attracts more members and improves discounts.

**Biggest risk:** Chicken-and-egg supply and demand. Start by negotiating with one distributor, and sign 15 restaurants from one neighborhood.

**Growth path:** One metro, then 5 metros, then bars, cafes and food trucks.

### 4. RFQ Response Accelerator for Small Industrial Distributors

**Problem:** A small industrial or janitorial distributor receives requests for quotes by email with a list of part numbers, and the sales rep spends 1-3 hours cross-referencing catalog, stock and price. Slow replies lose to the faster competitor, and the buyer often picks the first complete quote.

**What to build:** Parse RFQ emails and attachments (PDF, Excel), match line items to the distributor's catalog and stock, apply customer-specific pricing and draft a complete reply for the rep to approve and send. Track response time and win rate by customer.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Quote in 15 minutes instead of 2 hours, or the first month is free."

**Skill fit:** Document parsing, LLM matching, catalog data and email integrations.

**MVP scope:** 4 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 60-90 days through distributor trade groups and direct outreach to independent distributors.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $349/mo. 35 distributors = **$12,215/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Faster quotes win more business and the catalog-matching data improves with every RFQ.

**Biggest risk:** Messy catalog data. Offer a paid data-cleanup onboarding at $750.

**Growth path:** Industrial supply, then janitorial, packaging, electrical and plumbing distributors.

### 5. Unused-Package Re-Engagement Engine for Studios and Spas Selling Prepaid Packs

**Problem:** A yoga studio, massage clinic or spa sells prepaid packs (10 classes, 5 massages), and many customers leave sessions unused. Unused sessions seem like free money, but those customers will not buy another pack, and they stop recommending the business.

**What to build:** Track each customer's remaining sessions and last visit. When a customer has unused sessions and has been absent for 3 weeks, send a friendly nudge with a one-tap booking link, and offer a bonus (a free add-on) to restart. When a pack is nearly used, trigger a renewal offer. Report renewal rate and revenue from re-engaged customers.

**Grand Slam angle:** "We lift your pack renewal rate, or you pay nothing." The fee is $20 per renewed pack.

**Skill fit:** Booking-system integrations, messaging and cohort analysis.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days through studio-owner groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $79/mo + $20 per renewed pack. A studio renewing 15 extra packs pays $379/mo. 35 studios x $379 = **$13,265/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** The studio's renewal revenue rises directly, and it shows up in a single number.

**Biggest risk:** Studios on platforms that do not expose package data. Start with Mindbody and a manual CSV import.

**Growth path:** Yoga and fitness studios, then spas, chiropractors, tutoring centers and music schools.
