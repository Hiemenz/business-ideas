# Main Income Ideas - 2026-11-11 (Session 182)

## Who this is for
A 29-year-old software engineer with data analytics skills who wants to replace a full-time salary ($8-15K+/mo) within 12-24 months by building solo, in evenings and weekends, with no outside funding. Every idea below must have a realistic path to first revenue inside 90 days and a named, reachable distribution channel.

## Lens for this session: Alex Hormozi
Built from the $100M Offers / $100M Leads frameworks:
- **Value equation:** Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood of Success) / (Time Delay x Effort & Sacrifice).
- **Grand Slam Offer:** performance-priced or guarantee-backed.
- **Lead and cash levers:** list building from public triggers, deposits and pre-sales that pull cash forward, and plugging revenue the business already earned but never billed.

Per standing feedback, none of these are compliance or regulatory tools. Each solves a revenue-leakage or growth problem.

## Ideas

### 1. Change-Order Capture Tool for Remodelers and Small General Contractors

**Problem:** On a remodel, the homeowner says "while you're in there, can you also..." and the crew says yes. The extra work is rarely written up and priced, so it ends up as unbilled labor. Contractors commonly give away 3-8% of job revenue this way, and arguments at final billing damage the relationship.

**What to build:** A phone-first tool for the site lead: voice-note or photo the request, and the tool drafts a one-page change order with a price from the contractor's own rate sheet and sends it to the homeowner for a one-tap approval before the work starts. Roll approved changes into the invoice and show the owner a monthly "change-order revenue captured" total.

**Grand Slam angle:** "We capture at least 3% more revenue on every job or the first quarter is free."

**Skill fit:** Mobile web app, speech-to-text, document generation and e-approval links.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-60 days through remodeler Facebook groups and local builders' associations.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $129/mo. A contractor doing $1.2M a year captures about $36K, which easily justifies the fee. 85 contractors = **$10,965/mo**. Add a $29/mo per extra site-lead seat.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** It is tied to every active job, and the savings show up in a number the owner checks monthly.

**Biggest risk:** Crews will not use another app. Keep it to three taps and let the foreman text a photo to a number instead.

**Growth path:** Remodelers, then electricians, HVAC installers, landscapers and fence builders.

### 2. New-Business Trigger Lead Feed for Commercial Cleaners, IT Shops and Sign Makers

**Problem:** Vendors that sell to small businesses (janitorial, IT support, signage, uniforms, coffee service) get the best leads from businesses that just opened or just moved. Those businesses buy within weeks, but vendors find them by driving around or not at all.

**What to build:** A weekly feed of newly opened businesses, new commercial leases and new permits in the vendor's service area, from public listings and business-registry data. Each lead is matched to the vendor's service type, with a suggested first message and a one-click export to the vendor's CRM. Track contacts, replies and wins.

**Grand Slam angle:** "We deliver 50 fresh in-area leads a month, or you do not pay."

**Skill fit:** Scraping and normalizing public data, geographic filtering and light enrichment.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends for one metro, then expand.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days through small-business-service owner groups and local chambers of commerce.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149/mo per vendor per territory. 80 vendor subscriptions = **$11,920/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** The same data set serves many vendor types and many cities, and the feed gets more valuable as it grows.

**Biggest risk:** Data access varies by city. Start with 2-3 metros with open data portals and expand by demand.

**Growth path:** One metro, then 10 metros, then add enrichment (owner name, phone, email) at a higher tier.

### 3. Custom-Order Inquiry-to-Deposit Funnel for Custom Apparel and Embroidery Shops

**Problem:** A custom apparel shop gets a stream of quote requests ("100 shirts with our logo") that arrive by email, DM and web form. Each takes 20-30 minutes of back and forth to quote, many prospects ghost, and the shop starts work before taking a deposit.

**What to build:** A guided quote form that captures quantity, garment, decoration and deadline, applies the shop's price table to produce an instant quote with art-proof upload, and collects a 50% deposit by payment link. Follow up automatically on unpaid quotes and show the quote-to-deposit rate.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Instant quotes and deposits taken before work starts, or we refund the setup."

**Skill fit:** Pricing logic, forms, payment links and funnel analytics.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-60 days through screen-printing and embroidery shop owner groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $99/mo + 0.75% of deposits collected. A shop collecting $20K in deposits per month pays about $250/mo. 50 shops x $250 = **$12,500/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Shops see more orders and fewer unpaid jobs, so they stay.

**Biggest risk:** Complicated pricing tables. Start with the common 80% of orders and send the rest to manual quotes.

**Growth path:** Apparel shops, then trophy and engraving shops, sign makers, print shops and promotional-product dealers.

### 4. Limited-Run Pre-Order Drop Engine for Small Craft Food and Beverage Makers

**Problem:** A small hot-sauce, coffee, candy or brewery brand produces a batch, hopes it sells and often discounts leftovers. Pre-selling a limited run collects cash before production, tells the maker how much to make and creates urgency.

**What to build:** A drop page builder with a countdown, a quantity cap, waitlist capture and a post-purchase production-updates sequence. Email and SMS the maker's list at launch and again at the 48-hour mark. Show pre-order revenue versus production cost and suggest the next batch size from the waitlist.

**Grand Slam angle:** "A drop that pays for the batch before you make it, or you owe nothing." The fee is 5% of pre-order revenue.

**Skill fit:** Storefront integration (Shopify), campaign automation and basic forecasting.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days through craft-food maker communities and farmers-market networks.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** A maker running 1 drop per month with $5,000 of pre-orders pays 5% = $250/mo, plus a $49/mo platform fee, about $300/mo. 40 makers x $300 = **$12,000/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Cash-now drops improve the maker's cash flow, and the waitlist grows with each run.

**Biggest risk:** Makers with small lists see little revenue. Qualify for brands with at least 1,500 email subscribers.

**Growth path:** Craft food, then candles, apparel drops, small-press books and art prints.

### 5. Traffic-Based Staffing Optimizer for Independent Cafes and Small Retail

**Problem:** A cafe or boutique schedules staff by habit, so it is overstaffed on slow Tuesdays and short-staffed at Saturday rush, with lost sales and walkouts in the rush. Labor is the biggest controllable cost, and the owner has the data (POS transactions by hour) but no analysis.

**What to build:** Import POS sales by hour. Build a demand forecast per day-hour using weather, holidays and local events, and recommend a schedule showing target headcount per shift. Show the estimated weekly labor savings and the lost-sales risk in under-covered hours.

**Grand Slam angle:** "We save you at least 5% of labor or the first quarter is free."

**Skill fit:** Forecasting, POS integrations (Square, Toast export, Clover) and a schedule view.

**MVP scope:** 3-4 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 45-75 days through cafe, boutique and small-restaurant owner groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $99/mo. A cafe with $12K/mo of labor saves about $600/mo. 110 shops = **$10,890/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Forecast accuracy grows with history, and the savings are easy to prove with POS data.

**Biggest risk:** Staff scheduling is sensitive and managers worry about hours. Present it as a suggestion with manager override.

**Growth path:** Cafes and boutiques, then bakeries, ice-cream shops, bars and small grocers.
