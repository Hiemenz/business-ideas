### 1. Enrolled Actuary Pension Practice Joint Board CE, Schedule SB/MB & PBGC Premium-Filing Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Enrolled actuaries who sign the actuarial certification on defined-benefit pension plans hold a federal credential issued by the Joint Board for Enrolled Actuaries (not a state board), renewed via Form 5434-A and requiring 36 hours of continuing education across a rolling 3-year cycle with a mandated minimum of "core" versus "non-core" hours — an EA who lets the CE cycle lapse loses the authority to sign a Schedule SB (single-employer plans) or Schedule MB (multiemployer plans) actuarial certification, and a Form 5500 filed without that signature is an incomplete filing subject to IRS/DOL penalty assessments that can run into thousands of dollars per day. Every single-employer defined-benefit plan the actuary certifies has its own PBGC premium-filing deadline (the Comprehensive Premium Filing, due the later of 9.5 months after the plan year begins or a fixed date), and underfunded plans above a variable-rate-premium threshold trigger extra reporting; a small actuarial consulting shop serving 20-40 small-business DB plans currently tracks its own CE-hour accumulation, each client plan's Form 5500/Schedule SB-MB certification deadline, and each plan's PBGC premium due date on a personal spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific plan's certification is both signable (EA credential current) and filed before its own PBGC and IRS deadlines.

**What to build:** A Joint Board CE-hour tracker with a core/non-core split and a hard flag as the 3-year cycle approaches; a per-plan Form 5500 Schedule SB/MB certification-deadline tracker computing each plan's specific due date from its plan-year end; and a PBGC Comprehensive Premium Filing and variable-rate-premium threshold tracker flagging underfunded plans approaching extra reporting obligations.

**Skill fit:** Joint Board CE-cycle rules, ERISA plan-year-driven Form 5500 deadlines, and PBGC's published premium-filing due-date formulas are all public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-plan trigger — a deadline-computation-and-alert problem well within reach for a data-oriented developer with no actuarial credential of their own required to build it.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the CE tracker, the per-plan certification-deadline tracker, and the PBGC premium tracker. First 5-8 clients from the American Society of Pension Professionals & Actuaries (ASPPA, part of NAPA) enrolled-actuary member directory and the Conference of Consulting Actuaries member roster.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — a small, specialized market of solo and small-firm enrolled actuaries reached through ASPPA/CCA outreach rather than volume cold email.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $150-300/mo per actuarial practice depending on the number of DB plans under management. 40-55 practices × $220/mo avg = $8,800-12,100/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every plan under management carries its own annual Schedule SB/MB certification and PBGC premium cycle running independently of every other plan, and the enrolled actuary's own CE cycle recurs on a rolling clock for as long as they hold the credential — a continuous, plan-count-driven compliance stream tied directly to the practice's own book of business.

**Biggest risk:** A very small solo actuary managing only a handful of plans has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward small actuarial firms managing 20+ DB plans across multiple client sponsors, where the per-plan deadline load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 practices ($3.3K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 32 practices ($7K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 40-55 practices plus a PBGC-and-IRS-audit-ready certification export a firm can produce the moment a plan sponsor's filing is questioned.

### 2. Certified Water & Wastewater Treatment Plant Contract Operator Multi-System Certification & Responsible-Charge Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent contract operators who provide certified operation services to small municipal water districts, rural water associations, and small wastewater treatment plants — because those systems are too small to employ a full-time certified operator — hold a state-issued operator certification (commonly Class I through Class IV, graded to the size and treatment complexity of the system, renewed every 1-3 years via a fixed number of contact/training hours per grade), and for every single system under contract, the state drinking-water or water-quality agency requires the operator to be formally designated as the "operator of record" or "responsible charge" operator on file for that specific system — a designation that must be actively updated whenever a contract starts, ends, or the operator's own certification grade changes relative to what a given system requires. A contract operator serving 8-15 small systems simultaneously has to track their own certification renewal (often across grade levels if certified in multiple categories — water treatment, water distribution, wastewater treatment, wastewater collection), each system's designation-of-record status with the state agency, and the underlying monitoring/reporting schedule (Discharge Monitoring Reports for wastewater NPDES permits, routine coliform and chemical sampling for drinking water) that operating in responsible charge makes the operator personally accountable for — a state audit or an inspection finding an operator working "under" a system without a current designation on file, or a certification grade that has lapsed below what that system requires, is a licensing-board enforcement action against the operator personally, not just the system.

**What to build:** A multi-grade certification renewal tracker with contact-hour accumulation by category (water treatment, water distribution, wastewater treatment, wastewater collection); a per-system responsible-charge/operator-of-record designation tracker flagging any system where the state filing hasn't been updated to reflect a new or ended contract; and a per-system monitoring/reporting-schedule tracker (DMR due dates, routine sampling windows) rolled up across every system the operator is personally accountable for.

**Skill fit:** State operator-certification renewal rules, per-system operator-of-record designation requirements, and EPA/state DMR and sampling schedules are public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-operator and per-system trigger — the same deterministic deadline-and-designation-gate pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a personally-accountable multi-system operator niche entirely distinct from the Private Community Water System idea already in this repo, whose compliance core belongs to the system itself rather than an individual contract operator's own multi-system certification and designation status.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the multi-grade certification tracker, the per-system responsible-charge designation tracker, and the DMR/sampling-schedule rollup. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent certified contract operators.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — state Rural Water Association chapters (which run certification training and maintain contract-operator networks in nearly every state) and the American Water Works Association's (AWWA) small-systems section reach exactly the contract operators juggling certification and multi-system designation status with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $99-199/mo per contract operator depending on the number of systems under contract. 60-80 operators × $150/mo avg = $9,000-12,000/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new system contract reopens the responsible-charge designation question with the state agency, every certification renewal recurs on its own grade-specific clock, and every system's own DMR/sampling schedule runs independently — a continuous, contract-count-driven compliance stream tied directly to the operator's own client roster.

**Biggest risk:** An operator under contract to a single stable system has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward operators serving 8+ small systems simultaneously, where the multi-system designation-and-deadline load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 18 operators ($2.7K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 40 operators ($6K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 60-80 operators plus a state-audit-ready designation-and-monitoring export an operator can produce the moment an inspector asks who is in responsible charge.

### 3. Anesthesiologist Assistant (AA) Group Practice Multi-State Licensure, NCCAA Recertification & Supervision-Ratio Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Anesthesiologist assistants — mid-level anesthesia providers who, unlike CRNAs, may only practice under the continuous, documented supervision of a licensed anesthesiologist at a fixed maximum ratio (commonly one anesthesiologist to four concurrent AA/CRNA cases, though the exact ratio and even AA licensure itself varies significantly by state, with AA practice authorized in only about 20 states) — must maintain state licensure in every state where they practice, alongside national certification through the National Commission for Certification of Anesthesiologist Assistants (NCCAA) on a 6-year cycle that combines ongoing continuing medical education with a proctored recertification exam at the end of the cycle, a materially different structure than a simple CE-hours renewal. A group practice staffing AAs across several surgical facilities must document, case by case, that the supervising anesthesiologist's concurrent case count never exceeds the state-and-facility-specific supervision ratio — a ratio violation discovered during a payer audit or a malpractice claim's discovery process is treated as the AA having practiced outside authorized scope for every affected case, exposing both the AA and the supervising anesthesiologist to licensing-board action. Facility-specific clinical privileging for each AA also runs on its own renewal cycle per surgical center or hospital, independent of both the state license and the NCCAA cycle. A group practice running 6-15 AAs across multiple facilities currently tracks state licenses, NCCAA recertification-cycle progress, per-case supervision-ratio compliance, and facility privileging on a shared spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific scheduled case has a supervising anesthesiologist within ratio before the case is booked.

**What to build:** A multi-state license and NCCAA 6-year CME/recertification-exam cycle tracker per AA; a per-case supervision-ratio compliance log cross-referencing the supervising anesthesiologist's concurrent case count against the state/facility-specific maximum before a case is confirmed bookable; and a facility-specific clinical-privileging renewal tracker per AA per site.

**Skill fit:** State AA-licensure statutes, NCCAA's published recertification-cycle rules, and facility privileging cycles are public and contractual structured data with a concrete per-case and per-AA trigger, and the supervision-ratio gate is the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a physician-supervised anesthesia niche entirely distinct from the Independent CRNA Practice idea already in this repo, whose multi-facility privileging tracker has no supervision-ratio mechanic at all since independent-practice CRNAs in opt-out states aren't supervised.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the multi-state license/NCCAA cycle tracker, the per-case supervision-ratio log, and the facility privileging tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to AA group practices.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — the American Academy of Anesthesiologist Assistants (AAAA) member directory reaches exactly the small AA group practices currently reconciling supervision-ratio compliance and multi-state licensure on a shared spreadsheet, though the AA-authorized-state footprint (roughly 20 states) makes this a slower, more relationship-driven sales cycle than a nationwide license niche.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249-449/mo per group practice depending on AA headcount and number of facility contracts. 25-35 practices × $340/mo avg = $8,500-11,900/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every scheduled case reopens the supervision-ratio compliance question, the NCCAA 6-year cycle recurs independently for every AA on staff, and every new facility contract reopens the privileging question — a continuous, case-volume-and-headcount-driven compliance stream tied directly to the practice's own growth.

**Biggest risk:** The AA profession is concentrated in a limited number of states, capping total addressable market size relative to a nationwide niche. Mitigate by pricing per-AA-seat rather than flat per-practice, since the supervision-ratio and privileging load scales directly with headcount even within a geographically limited market.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10 practices ($3.4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 22 practices ($7.5K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 25-35 practices plus a malpractice-discovery-ready supervision-ratio export a practice can produce the moment a claim challenges a specific case's staffing.

### 4. International Board Certified Lactation Consultant (IBCLC) Private Practice IBLCE Recertification & Payer-Credentialing Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** International Board Certified Lactation Consultants hold a credential issued by the International Board of Lactation Consultant Examiners (IBLCE), renewed on a 5-year cycle that requires accumulating Continuing Education Recognition Points (CERPs) in specific required categories (including a fixed minimum of "L-CERPs" specific to lactation content) or, absent enough CERPs, retaking the full certification exam — an IBCLC who lets the cycle lapse loses the credential entirely rather than simply paying a late fee, and every insurance claim billed after that lapse is billed under a credential that no longer exists. Since the ACA classified lactation counseling as a covered women's preventive service, IBCLCs in private practice increasingly bill commercial insurance directly under CPT/HCPCS codes such as 99404 or S9443, but each payer separately credentials the IBCLC on its own 2-3 year cycle independent of both the IBLCE certification and any other payer's credentialing timeline, and a lapsed payer credential discovered mid-treatment-episode is a claim the payer can retroactively deny — a clawback that hits especially hard for a solo consultant whose entire caseload runs through two or three regional insurers. Some states have also begun licensing lactation consultants as a standalone regulated profession (Rhode Island, Georgia, and New Mexico among the first), adding a state-renewal layer on top of the national credential in those jurisdictions. A solo or small-group IBCLC practice currently tracks IBLCE CERP accumulation, per-payer credentialing renewal dates, and any applicable state license on a private spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific billed visit occurred while both the national credential and that specific payer's credentialing were simultaneously current.

**What to build:** An IBLCE 5-year CERP tracker with a category-specific (including L-CERP) progress breakdown and a hard flag as the recertification exam deadline approaches; a per-payer credentialing renewal tracker with a "cleared to bill" gate blocking a visit from being marked billable until both the IBLCE credential and that payer's credentialing are confirmed current; and a state-license tracker for the small but growing number of states that license lactation consultants directly.

**Skill fit:** IBLCE's published CERP-category requirements, ACA preventive-service billing rules, and payer credentialing-cycle data are public and contractual structured data with a concrete per-visit billing-eligibility trigger, and the "cleared to bill" gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a lactation-consulting niche entirely distinct from the Certified Nurse-Midwife idea already in this repo, whose AMCB/state-nursing-board framework governs a separate credential most IBCLCs never hold, with no CERP-cycle or ACA-preventive-billing mechanic of any kind.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the CERP tracker, the per-payer credentialing gate, and the state-license tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent IBCLC practices.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the United States Lactation Consultant Association (USLCA) member directory and state chapter meetings reach exactly the small IBCLC practices currently reconciling CERP accumulation and per-payer credentialing on a spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $69-129/mo per consultant. 90-120 consultants × $95/mo avg = $8,550-11,400/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every insurance-billed visit reopens the per-payer credentialing question, the IBLCE 5-year CERP cycle recurs on its own independent clock for as long as the consultant holds the credential, and every new payer relationship a growing practice adds compounds the number of simultaneously tracked credentialing records — a continuous, caseload-and-payer-count-driven compliance stream tied directly to the practice's own growth.

**Biggest risk:** A solo consultant working entirely private-pay with no insurance billing has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward consultants billing multiple commercial payers under the ACA preventive-service mandate, where the payer-credentialing load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 25 consultants ($2.4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 55 consultants ($5.2K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 90-120 consultants plus a payer-audit-ready credentialing export a consultant can produce the moment an insurer requests documentation on a clawback review.

### 5. Certified Rehabilitation Counselor (CRC) Vocational-Expert Practice State Workers'-Comp Roster & CRCC Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Certified Rehabilitation Counselors who testify as vocational experts in Social Security disability hearings and provide vocational rehabilitation services in workers'-compensation cases hold certification through the Commission on Rehabilitation Counselor Certification (CRCC), renewed on a cycle requiring 100 hours of continuing education over 5 years including a dedicated ethics-hours minimum, layered under a state professional counseling license in most states. On top of both of those, nearly every state workers'-compensation system maintains its own approved-vocational-counselor roster or panel — a separate registration, renewed on its own state-specific cycle (commonly 1-2 years), that a CRC must hold in every state where they accept workers'-comp-referred vocational rehabilitation cases, and a CRC who accepts a referral in a state where their roster registration has lapsed has accepted a case they weren't authorized to bill, a finding a state workers'-comp board audit is built to catch. Social Security Administration disability hearings additionally require a vocational expert to meet SSA's own qualification standards and appear on the local hearing office's roster of approved experts, a separate qualification track from both the state workers'-comp panel and the underlying CRC/state-counseling credential. A CRC accepting referrals across several states' workers'-comp systems and multiple SSA hearing offices currently tracks CRCC CE-hour accumulation, per-state workers'-comp roster renewal, and SSA hearing-office qualification status on a personal spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific case referral falls within a currently active roster registration in that specific state.

**What to build:** A CRCC 5-year CE/ethics-hour tracker with a hard flag at the renewal deadline; a per-state workers'-compensation approved-vocational-counselor roster tracker with a "cleared to accept" gate blocking a new referral from being logged until that state's roster registration is confirmed current; and an SSA hearing-office vocational-expert qualification-status tracker for CRCs testifying in disability hearings.

**Skill fit:** State workers'-comp board roster-renewal rules, CRCC's published CE requirements, and SSA vocational-expert qualification standards are public, structured regulatory and professional-standards data with a concrete per-referral and per-state trigger, and the "cleared to accept" gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a vocational-rehabilitation-expert niche entirely distinct from every clinical mental-health-licensure idea already in this repo, none of which tracks a state workers'-comp roster registration or an SSA hearing-office qualification status of any kind.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the CRCC CE tracker, the per-state roster gate, and the SSA qualification tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent CRC vocational-expert practitioners.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the International Association of Rehabilitation Professionals (IARP) member directory reaches exactly the small CRC practices currently reconciling multi-state roster registration and CRCC CE-hour status on a spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $79-149/mo per CRC. 70-90 practitioners × $115/mo avg = $8,050-10,350/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new case referral reopens the per-state roster-eligibility question, the CRCC 5-year cycle recurs on its own independent clock, and every additional state or SSA hearing office a CRC works adds its own roster/qualification variant to track — a continuous, caseload-and-jurisdiction-driven compliance stream tied directly to the practitioner's own referral footprint.

**Biggest risk:** A CRC accepting referrals only in a single home state has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward CRCs working multiple states' workers'-comp systems or testifying across several SSA hearing offices, where the roster-and-qualification load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 18 practitioners ($2.1K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 42 practitioners ($4.8K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 70-90 practitioners plus a state-board-audit-ready roster-status export a CRC can produce the moment a workers'-comp board questions a case's eligibility.

## This Session's Pick: #2 (Water & Wastewater Contract Operator) for the broadest, most reachable market; #4 (IBCLC Private Practice) for the fastest sales cycle

**#2 (Water & Wastewater Contract Operator)** stands out because nearly every small rural water district and small wastewater plant in the country depends on a contract operator rather than in-house staff, state Rural Water Association chapters exist in every state specifically to train and network these operators, and the personal-liability angle — a lapsed responsible-charge designation is the operator's own licensing exposure, not just the system's — makes the pitch land immediately with an audience that already thinks in terms of certification grades and renewal cycles.

**#4 (IBCLC Private Practice)** has the fastest, most direct distribution channel in this session: the United States Lactation Consultant Association's member directory and active state chapters reach solo consultants directly, the ACA's preventive-services mandate has pushed a growing share of this profession into insurance billing for the first time (creating fresh per-payer credentialing pain that didn't exist a decade ago), and the profession's continued growth as birth centers and hospitals increasingly refer out to private-practice IBCLCs gives a builder a durable tailwind.
