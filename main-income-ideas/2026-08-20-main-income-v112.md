### 1. Proposal Win Rate Analytics for Marketing and Creative Agencies
**Problem:** A marketing, design, or creative agency sends 15-40 proposals per month and has no systematic record of which ones they win, which they lose, and why. The owner knows their rough close rate by feel ("we close about a third of them") but has no data on whether that rate varies by industry, deal size, proposal length, how quickly the proposal was sent after the initial call, or how many follow-ups preceded the close. The result: the agency keeps pitching the same way regardless of outcome, because they have no signal for what's working. A creative agency with a 28% overall close rate that discovers they close 61% of proposals under $15K and 14% of proposals over $30K has immediately actionable information — they can productize a fixed-scope offering at the $15K price point, stop competing on large deals they lose most of the time, and double their revenue without changing their service.
**What to build:** A proposal win rate analytics tool — the agency logs each proposal (client industry, deal size, service type, date proposal was sent, days between initial contact and proposal sent, number of follow-ups before decision, and outcome: won/lost/ghosted). A win rate dashboard: overall close rate, close rate by deal size bracket (under $10K, $10-25K, $25-50K, over $50K), close rate by industry, close rate by service type, and close rate by proposal turnaround time (proposals sent within 24 hours of the call vs. 48-72 hours vs. 7+ days). A trends view: close rate by month over the last 12 months. A lost deal tracker: for lost and ghosted proposals, a required "reason" field (price, competitor, no decision, timeline mismatch) so the agency can see whether they're losing primarily on price or on scope. A follow-up timing analyzer: the relationship between number of follow-ups and close rate — do agencies that follow up 3 times close more deals than those that follow up once?
**Skill fit:** The data model is simple: proposals, outcomes, attributes. The win rate calculations are groupby aggregations. The trend chart is a time-series aggregation. The follow-up timing analysis is a correlation between follow-up count and close rate — basic descriptive statistics. A clean React dashboard with configurable filters. No APIs required for the core product — proposal data is entered manually. Optional HubSpot and Pipedrive integrations pull deal data automatically for agencies with CRM data.
**MVP scope:** 1 weekend — manual proposal entry form, win rate dashboard with breakdowns by deal size and industry, and lost deal reason tracking. An agency owner who discovers they close 58% of proposals in the $8-18K range but only 11% outside that range — and immediately restructures their sales process around that insight — has gotten more value from 2 hours of data entry than from any sales training.
**Time to first $:** 14-30 days — agency owners are among the most reachable buyers online. Agency Hackers, UGURUS, the 2X agency community, and marketing agency owner LinkedIn groups are all active and full of owners who talk openly about proposal conversion rates. A post showing a blinded screenshot of the win rate dashboard — "what I learned after logging 90 proposals" — generates hundreds of engagements from agency owners who immediately recognize the problem.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $79-199/month per agency. Average agency at $129/month. 80 agencies × $129/month = **$10,320/month**. Upsell: a proposal template performance module ($49/month add-on) — tracking which proposal templates (by section structure, length, and format) correlate with the highest close rates, so the agency converges on the proposal structure that wins most often.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new proposal logged adds another data point to the close rate analysis, making the patterns more statistically reliable. An agency with 18 months of proposal data has a sales intelligence baseline — "here's exactly what our best deals look like at the time of proposal" — that they can't reconstruct by memory and won't abandon.
**Biggest risk:** Manual data entry discipline — if the agency doesn't log proposals consistently, the analysis is incomplete. Mitigation: the entry form is a 60-second fill (8 fields, no free text required for the core data); and HubSpot/Pipedrive integrations eliminate the entry burden for agencies with CRM data in those systems. A weekly email digest ("you have 3 proposals pending an outcome — log them now") maintains data hygiene.
**Growth path:** Side project → manual proposal entry, win rate dashboard, and lost deal tracker live, first 10-20 agency clients through Agency Hackers community. Replaces-part-time-income → 30-50 agencies, add HubSpot and Pipedrive integrations, add the proposal template performance module. Replaces-full-time-salary → 80+ agencies, add a close rate benchmarking report (your close rate vs. anonymized peer agencies by service type and deal size bracket), expand to freelancers and independent consultants with identical proposal dynamics.

### 2. Churn Intervention for Subscription Box Companies
**Problem:** A subscription box company on Cratejoy or Shopify has 500-5,000 subscribers and loses 5-8% of them every month — a churn rate that, if unaddressed, means half the subscriber base turns over every 12-18 months. The company knows who churned (the cancellation webhook fires in their system), but by then it's too late. What they don't know is who is about to churn — the subscriber who has received 4 boxes, opened none of them in 3 weeks based on email engagement data, and has a billing date approaching in 9 days. That subscriber is a churn event waiting to happen, and a proactive outreach (a "how are you enjoying your box?" email with a 10% discount on the next shipment) sent before the billing date converts 20-30% of at-risk subscribers who would otherwise cancel. The signals are in the data: email open rate, click rate, support ticket history, social engagement, and purchase add-on behavior all correlate with retention, but no one has connected them.
**What to build:** A churn intervention tool for subscription box companies — integrating with Cratejoy and Shopify (both have APIs) for subscriber and billing data, and with Klaviyo or Mailchimp for email engagement data. The tool computes a churn risk score per subscriber from: email engagement in the last 30 days (opens and clicks relative to send rate), billing history (any failed charges in the last 3 months), subscription tenure (new subscribers in months 1-3 have higher baseline churn risk), and support ticket count (subscribers who contacted support are either highly engaged or about to leave — the combination with low email engagement distinguishes the two). Subscribers above a configurable risk threshold get enrolled in a proactive retention sequence starting 10 days before their billing date: a personalized email ("We noticed you haven't had a chance to dig into your last box — here's what's inside this month") and a 10% discount offer for subscribers who don't open the email. A retention dashboard: total subscribers at risk this billing cycle, intervention rate, conversion rate (at-risk subscribers retained after intervention), and monthly churn rate trend.
**Skill fit:** Cratejoy and Shopify both have REST APIs with subscription and billing data. Klaviyo has a strong API for email engagement metrics per subscriber. The risk score is a weighted combination of the input signals — solvable with a configurable weighted average rather than ML for the MVP. The retention sequence is a conditional email flow via Klaviyo or direct SendGrid. The dashboard is a standard retention analytics view with a cohort comparison.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — Shopify/Cratejoy integration for subscriber data, Klaviyo integration for email engagement, risk score calculation, and a risk dashboard with a manual "enroll in retention sequence" button per at-risk subscriber. The automated sequence trigger is V2. A subscription box founder who manually reviews the risk dashboard 10 days before billing and emails their top 20 at-risk subscribers — retaining 5 of them at $45/month each — has recovered $2,700/year per intervention cycle.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — subscription box founders are reachable through the Cratejoy community, the Shopify subscription app ecosystem, subscription box Facebook groups (Subscription Box Community — 10,000+ members), and ecommerce founder Slack communities. A post showing "we reduced monthly churn from 7.2% to 4.8% in 60 days" resonates immediately in any DTC or subscription community.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-499/month per company depending on subscriber count. Average company at $299/month. 35 companies × $299/month = **$10,465/month**. Upsell: a subscriber lifetime value segmentation module ($99/month add-on) — segmenting the subscriber base by predicted LTV (tenure trend + engagement score + add-on purchase history) so the founder can prioritize retention spend on their highest-LTV subscribers rather than treating all at-risk subscribers equally.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Billing cycles recur every month, automatically generating a new at-risk cohort for each intervention cycle. The churn risk model improves as subscriber-level outcome data accumulates — a company with 12 months of risk scores and actual churn events has a training dataset that makes the model measurably more accurate. Switching means losing that model and starting the accuracy accumulation over.
**Biggest risk:** Klaviyo API access and cost — Klaviyo's API tier that includes per-subscriber engagement data requires a paid plan. Mitigation: Mailchimp has a similar API surface and is more common among smaller subscription box companies; and the risk score can be computed from billing and tenure data alone (without email engagement) for a slightly less accurate but still useful V1.
**Growth path:** Side project → Shopify integration, Klaviyo email engagement, risk score dashboard live, first 5-8 subscription company clients. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-18 companies, add Cratejoy integration, add automated retention sequence trigger, add LTV segmentation module. Replaces-full-time-salary → 35+ companies, add a win-back campaign for already-churned subscribers (the same 3-touch sequence as the gym win-back tool, applied to lapsed subscription box customers), expand to SaaS companies with monthly billing and similar churn signal dynamics.

### 3. Client Onboarding Automation for Independent Financial Advisors
**Problem:** An independent financial advisor (RIA or independent broker-dealer rep) onboarding a new client collects a suitability questionnaire, a risk tolerance assessment, account application paperwork for each account being opened (IRA, brokerage, joint), beneficiary designation forms, an investment policy statement, and identity verification documents. This process takes 2-4 weeks of back-and-forth email, PDF attachments, and wet signatures mailed back and forth — not because the advisor wants it to be slow, but because there is no system. The advisor emails one document, the client fills it out incorrectly, it comes back, the advisor sends a corrected version, the client sends it to their spouse who fills out their section, the spouse's signature date is wrong. Every day a new account is delayed in opening is a day the client's assets aren't invested — and a day the advisor isn't collecting the AUM fee on those assets.
**What to build:** A client onboarding workflow tool for independent financial advisors — the advisor configures an onboarding packet for each account type (IRA, joint brokerage, trust account) once: suitability questionnaire, risk tolerance assessment, investment policy statement, and any custom forms. When a new client is added, the advisor selects their account type(s) and the tool sends the client a single secure link. The client completes each section in sequence on a mobile-optimized, step-by-step flow — plain-language questions with progress indication. E-signature via DocuSign for required signature fields. Identity document upload (driver's license, passport) with a secure S3 storage path. The advisor dashboard: every prospect in the onboarding pipeline, their completion percentage, the last item completed, and the document(s) still pending. An automatic nudge to the client when they've been inactive for 48 hours: "Hi [name], you're 60% done with your account setup — just a few more steps." A completion alert to the advisor when everything is ready for submission to the custodian.
**Skill fit:** DocuSign API for e-signature. Secure file upload with S3 and server-side encryption. The step-by-step form flow is a multi-page form with a completion state machine per client. The nudge email is a time-based trigger per client. The advisor dashboard is a pipeline aggregation. Compliance note: the forms themselves must be approved by the advisor's broker-dealer compliance department — the tool delivers the workflow, not the forms. The advisor uploads their own compliance-approved form templates.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — configurable onboarding packet, per-client link generation, step-by-step form flow with e-signature, and advisor dashboard with nudge automation. An advisor who goes from a 3-week average account opening time to 6 days — and whose clients comment on how smooth the process was — has a product that differentiates their practice from every advisor still emailing PDF attachments.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — independent financial advisors are reachable through the National Association of Personal Financial Advisors (NAPFA), XY Planning Network (a community of fee-only advisors serving younger clients — highly tech-forward and tool-receptive), and financial advisor communities on Twitter/X and Reddit. A post in the XY Planning Network community — "built an onboarding tool that cut my average account opening time from 3 weeks to 6 days" — generates immediate inquiries from advisors who are competing on client experience.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149-399/month per advisor depending on client volume. Average advisor at $249/month. 40 advisors × $249/month = **$9,960/month**. Upsell: an annual review preparation module ($79/month add-on) — automatically sending clients a pre-meeting questionnaire 2 weeks before their annual review (any life changes, new financial goals, concerns to discuss), so the advisor walks into every review meeting with the client's updated context rather than spending the first 15 minutes catching up.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new client the advisor onboards uses the tool. The onboarding packet configuration is built once and reused for every subsequent client. An advisor with 3 years of onboarding history in the tool has a client record system they trust and won't rebuild in a new platform.
**Biggest risk:** Broker-dealer compliance requirements — independent advisors affiliated with broker-dealers (as opposed to pure RIAs) need compliance department approval for any client-facing software. Mitigation: target fee-only RIAs first (the XY Planning Network is almost entirely fee-only RIAs), who have direct control over their software choices; broker-dealer reps are a secondary market once the product has compliance-friendly positioning and documentation.
**Growth path:** Side project → onboarding packet, per-client link, step-by-step flow with e-sign, and advisor dashboard live, first 5-8 advisor clients through XY Planning Network. Replaces-part-time-income → 15-20 advisors, add the annual review preparation module, add integration with Redtail CRM (the most common CRM among independent advisors). Replaces-full-time-salary → 40+ advisors, add Wealthbox CRM integration, add a client portal where clients can see all their documents and update their profile at any time, expand to estate planning attorneys with an identical multi-document collection problem.

### 4. Equipment Maintenance Scheduling for Food Trucks and Mobile Vendors
**Problem:** A food truck operator running 1-3 trucks has multiple pieces of equipment that require regular maintenance: the truck engine (oil changes, tire rotation, DOT inspection), the generator (oil changes every 100 hours, air filter, load testing), the commercial refrigeration units (coil cleaning, temperature calibration, door gasket inspection), the hood and fire suppression system (semi-annual cleaning and inspection per NFPA 96), and the propane system (regulator check, hose inspection). Missing any of these creates real risk: an engine that blows a head gasket on a Saturday market day costs $4,000-8,000 in repairs and a day of lost revenue; a failed health inspection because the hood system wasn't cleaned on schedule costs the operator their permit until the work is done. Food truck operators track this across 4-6 different calendars and reminder apps, usually missing the ones they forgot to set up, and they have no single view of what's due across all their equipment.
**What to build:** A maintenance scheduling tool for food trucks and mobile food vendors — the operator registers each piece of equipment (make, model, current hours or mileage where applicable) and configures its maintenance schedule from a pre-built template library (engine maintenance, generator service, refrigeration service, hood cleaning, propane system inspection). The tool calculates next-due dates for both calendar-based (every 6 months) and usage-based (every 100 generator hours) intervals. A weekly email digest on Mondays: every maintenance item due in the next 30 days across all equipment, sorted by urgency. Usage-based tracking: the operator logs generator hours weekly via a 30-second SMS check-in. A service log: when maintenance is completed, the operator logs the date, service provider, and cost in 2 minutes. A maintenance cost dashboard: total spend on each piece of equipment over the trailing 12 months — so the operator knows when a piece of equipment's maintenance cost is approaching replacement economics.
**Skill fit:** The schedule calculation is calendar-based and usage-based interval arithmetic. The SMS usage check-in is the same Twilio reply-to-SMS flow as the fleet maintenance tool. The weekly digest is a date-prioritized aggregation across all equipment records. The service log is a simple entry form. The maintenance cost dashboard is a sum-by-equipment aggregation over service log entries. The pre-built maintenance template library (standard intervals for common equipment types) is the product's primary moat — building and maintaining accurate service intervals for 20-30 common food truck equipment types is the work that makes the tool immediately useful without manual setup.
**MVP scope:** 1-2 weekends — equipment registration with pre-built maintenance templates, calendar-based due date calculation, weekly digest email, and service log. A food truck operator who catches an overdue hood cleaning before a health inspection — avoiding a permit suspension that would cost 3-5 days of revenue — has justified the tool before paying the first invoice.
**Time to first $:** 14-30 days — food truck operators are reachable through the National Food Truck Association (NFTA), local food truck association chapters (most cities with an active food truck scene have one), and food truck Facebook groups (Food Truck Nation, Mobile Food Entrepreneur — 50,000+ members combined). A post showing the tool's weekly digest — "this is what I see every Monday about my truck's upcoming maintenance" — resonates immediately in those communities because every operator recognizes the scattered-reminders problem.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $29-79/month per operator depending on truck and equipment count. This is a volume game. Average operator at $49/month. 200 operators × $49/month = **$9,800/month**. Low price point means low churn — an operator paying $49/month who just avoided a failed health inspection isn't canceling. Upsell: a health inspection readiness checklist ($19/month add-on) — a pre-inspection checklist the operator runs through 2 weeks before a scheduled inspection, covering all equipment, temperature logs, and permit documents, with a pass/fail status for each item.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Maintenance intervals recur continuously, generating new due-date alerts every week without any input from the operator. The service log accumulates cost data over time, making the replacement economics calculation more accurate with each passing year. An operator who has 2 years of maintenance records per piece of equipment — and can see that their 2019 generator has cost $1,800 in maintenance over the last year while a new one costs $3,500 — has data that informs a major capital decision.
**Biggest risk:** Low price point limits revenue ceiling without volume. Mitigation: the distribution strategy leans on food truck association chapter sponsorships and national Facebook group presence — a $10/month referral bounty per active customer creates a viral loop within food truck communities where operators are tightly networked; and the low price point means zero sales friction, converting on a free trial without a sales call.
**Growth path:** Side project → equipment registration, pre-built maintenance templates, weekly digest, and service log live, first 20-30 operator clients through food truck Facebook groups. Replaces-part-time-income → 80-100 operators, add generator hours SMS check-in, add health inspection readiness checklist. Replaces-full-time-salary → 200+ operators, add a mobile vendor marketplace feature (operators can see which service providers other operators in their city use for hood cleaning, refrigeration service, etc.), expand to mobile pet grooming vans and mobile boutique retail vehicles with identical multi-equipment maintenance dynamics.

### 5. Vendor Payment Terms Optimizer for Small Manufacturers
**Problem:** A small manufacturer paying 40-80 vendors has payment terms that were set at the start of the relationship and have never been renegotiated. Some vendors offer 2/10 net 30 (a 2% early payment discount if paid within 10 days) — and the manufacturer pays on day 28 without taking the discount, leaving a 2% savings on the table because nobody flagged it. Other vendors are being paid in 15 days when the contract allows 45, tying up working capital unnecessarily. And some vendors who would offer extended terms (60 or 90 days) to a consistently good-paying customer have never been asked, because nobody has analyzed which vendors are strong enough relationships to negotiate with. For a manufacturer spending $1.2M/year on materials and components, a systematic payment terms optimization — taking every available early-pay discount, extending terms where possible — could improve working capital by $60,000-120,000 and capture $20,000-40,000/year in early payment discounts.
**What to build:** A vendor payment terms optimization tool — the manufacturer connects their QBO or uploads a vendor payment history export. The tool analyzes each vendor relationship: current payment terms, actual average days-to-pay, whether the vendor offers an early payment discount (entered manually or inferred from invoice terms), and trailing 12-month spend with that vendor. Three outputs: an early payment discount opportunity list (vendors offering 2/10 net 30 or similar where the manufacturer is currently paying on day 25-30, missing the discount window — ranked by annual discount value), a working capital release analysis (vendors the manufacturer is paying in 15-20 days when their terms allow 30-45 — ranked by the cash released if they paid at the full terms), and a terms renegotiation candidate list (high-spend, long-tenure vendors where the relationship justifies asking for 60 or 90-day terms — ranked by annual spend). A simulation tool: the manufacturer selects which optimizations to implement and the tool shows the projected annual discount capture and working capital improvement. A vendor terms tracker: after renegotiating, the new terms are recorded per vendor and the tool monitors whether payment timing aligns with the new terms.
**Skill fit:** QBO API for vendor payment history (invoice date, payment date, amount, vendor). The early payment discount analysis is a comparison of the manufacturer's actual pay date vs. the discount window on each invoice. The working capital release calculation is (current average days-to-pay ÷ 365) × annual vendor spend. The renegotiation candidate ranking is annual spend × tenure weighting. The simulation is a what-if aggregation. This is applied financial analysis — a natural fit for the builder's data analytics background, and highly defensible because the inputs (payment timing data from QBO) are hard to replicate in a spreadsheet without significant manual work.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — QBO API integration for vendor payment history, early payment discount opportunity analysis, working capital release analysis, and the renegotiation candidate list. A manufacturer who captures $18,000/year in early payment discounts they were missing — by paying 8 vendors 12 days earlier — has a 3,600% ROI on a $499/month subscription in year 1.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — small manufacturers are reachable through the Manufacturing Extension Partnership (MEP) network, NFIB member community, and manufacturing-focused fractional CFOs (who serve multiple small manufacturer clients and are a high-leverage referral channel for a tool that directly improves their clients' working capital). A post showing the working capital math — "your manufacturer client is probably paying vendors 3x too early and missing $20K/year in early pay discounts" — addressed to fractional CFOs generates immediate referral interest.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249-599/month per manufacturer depending on vendor count and spend volume. Average manufacturer at $399/month. 30 manufacturers × $399/month = **$11,970/month**. The pricing is justified by the ROI — a manufacturer capturing $20,000/year in early payment discounts is paying $4,788/year for the tool and pocketing $15,000+ net.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** New vendor relationships are added continuously as the manufacturer's supplier base evolves. The payment timing analysis runs monthly against updated QBO data, surfacing new early payment discount opportunities as invoice terms shift. A manufacturer with 2 years of vendor payment data and a fully optimized payment calendar has a treasury management capability they didn't have before — and won't rebuild from scratch.
**Biggest risk:** Early payment discount capture requires cash availability — if the manufacturer is cash-constrained, they can't pay early even when the discount is attractive. Mitigation: the simulation tool models the working capital impact of each optimization simultaneously, showing the manufacturer whether implementing all optimizations simultaneously creates a cash gap or whether they need to sequence the changes; and the working capital release analysis (paying some vendors later) often frees up enough cash to fund the early payment discounts.
**Growth path:** Side project → QBO integration, early payment discount analysis, working capital release analysis, and renegotiation candidate list live, first 5-8 manufacturer clients through MEP network outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-18 manufacturers, add Xero integration, add the vendor terms tracker for monitoring post-renegotiation compliance. Replaces-full-time-salary → 30+ manufacturers, add a cash flow integration with the contractor cash flow forecasting tool (Session 107, Idea 4) for manufacturers with project-based revenue, expand to wholesale distributors and food service operators with identical multi-vendor payment optimization dynamics.
