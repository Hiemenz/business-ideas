## Who this is for
You're 29, you write software and know your way around data — pipelines, scrapers, structured reference data, rules engines. You want to replace a full-time salary ($8-15K+/mo) within 12-24 months with something you build solo in evenings and weekends, no outside funding, no co-founder, and you want the first paying customer inside 90 days. The wedge in this series is always the same: small operators in a regulated industry who have non-optional government or accreditation-body obligations — licenses, permits, certifications, filings, inspections — and no compliance department to track them. You sell a monthly-retainer SaaS that turns their compliance binder into a living calendar with alerts and hard gates. The regulator (or accrediting body) creates the deadline; you sell the peace of mind of never missing it.

## Ideas

### 1. Maritime Harbor & Bar Pilot Association State Commission, Apprenticeship & Vessel-Endorsement Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** State-commissioned harbor and bar pilots — the specialized mariners who board inbound and outbound commercial vessels to navigate a specific harbor, river bar, or narrow channel no visiting ship's captain knows well enough to run alone — hold their authority from a state Board of Commissioners of Pilots (New York/New Jersey, San Francisco Bar Pilots, Columbia River Bar Pilots, and similar boards in most major port states) or, on the Great Lakes and boundary waters, from a federal pilotage license issued under 46 U.S.C. Chapter 87. A working pilot's commission is tied to a biennial physical examination meeting the board's specific medical standard (vision, hearing, cardiovascular fitness for a physically demanding boarding-by-ladder job), a recurring full-mission ship-handling simulator recertification, and a set of vessel-class and draft endorsements — a pilot licensed for vessels up to a certain draft or a certain vessel type cannot legally pilot a larger tanker or a deeper-draft container ship until the additional endorsement is earned and documented through a logged number of supervised transits. New pilots additionally work through a multi-year apprenticeship tracked in logged observed and supervised transits before the board will commission them independently, and a pilot association's executive office has to confirm every working member's physical, simulator recertification, and endorsement status is current before that member can be dispatched to board a ship — a lapse discovered only after a pilot boards a vessel outside their current endorsement is a serious Coast Guard and state-board finding that can suspend the individual pilot's commission and exposes the association to real liability on a job where the pilot's authority to be aboard that specific ship is the entire product being sold. A small pilot association running a dozen to several dozen working pilots tracks biennial-physical due dates, simulator-recertification cycles, and per-pilot vessel/draft endorsements on a shared roster spreadsheet maintained by a dispatcher, with no systematic gate stopping a dispatch to a pilot whose endorsement or physical has lapsed.

**What to build:** A per-pilot commission dashboard tracking biennial physical exam due dates and simulator recertification cycles with an alert well ahead of expiration; a vessel-class/draft endorsement tracker recording each pilot's current endorsements and the supervised-transit logs required to earn the next one; a dispatch-eligibility gate cross-checking a proposed assignment's vessel class and draft against the assigned pilot's current endorsements before dispatch is confirmed; and an apprentice-pilot transit-hour log tracking progress toward independent commissioning.

**Skill fit:** State pilot-commission board regulations and each board's endorsement/recertification cycle are public, structured regulatory data, and the dispatch-eligibility gate — cross-checking a specific job's vessel requirements against a specific pilot's current credential set before confirming an assignment — is the same deterministic "block the action until the record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a licensed-maritime-professional category this repo has never touched.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the per-pilot physical/simulator recertification tracker, the vessel-class/draft endorsement tracker, and the dispatch-eligibility gate for one or two pilot association clients. First 5-8 pilot association clients through direct outreach, since there are a limited but well-organized number of state pilot associations nationally.

**Time to first $:** 30-60 days — the American Pilots' Association (APA), the national trade association representing state and federally licensed pilots, maintains a directory of member pilot associations at every major U.S. port, giving direct access to the dispatch offices that would actually buy this.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $500-1,200/mo per pilot association depending on member-pilot count, since the product serves the whole association's roster rather than billing per individual pilot. 12-18 associations × $750/mo avg = $9,000-13,500/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every working pilot's physical and simulator-recertification clock runs on its own independent cycle for as long as they hold a commission, and every apprentice pilot generates a multi-year sequence of logged transit hours — a continuous, roster-driven compliance stream that never stops as long as the association keeps working pilots on staff.

**Biggest risk:** The buyer population is a small, tight-knit professional community (there are only a few dozen state and federally recognized pilot associations nationally), which caps how large this can get and means early credibility with the APA network matters enormously. Mitigate by treating the first 2-3 association clients as reference accounts and building the product visibly enough at APA's own conference that word travels through the tight professional network.

**Growth path:** Side project → 5-6 associations ($3-4.5K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 10-12 associations ($7.5-9K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 14-18 associations plus an individual-pilot mobile app tier for logging simulator hours and transits directly.

### 2. Registered Dietitian/Nutritionist Private Practice State Licensure, CDR CPE-Cycle & Medicare MNT Referral-Documentation Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent registered dietitians and nutritionists running a private practice or telehealth nutrition-counseling business hold a state dietetics licensure board credential (required in most states) renewed on a fixed cycle, and separately maintain their Commission on Dietetic Registration (CDR) credential — the RD/RDN designation — through a 5-year Professional Development Portfolio cycle requiring a minimum number of Continuing Professional Education (CPE) units logged and categorized against the practitioner's own self-assessed learning-need areas, a structure that runs on an entirely different clock and documentation format than the state license's CE requirement. Billing Medicare for Medical Nutrition Therapy (MNT) adds a third, sharply specific compliance layer: MNT is only reimbursable for patients with diabetes or non-dialysis kidney disease, and every claim requires a physician or qualified practitioner's referral on file documenting the specific diagnosis and the date of referral before the RD can bill — a claim submitted without a complete, current referral on file is a straightforward, commonly-triggered Medicare audit finding, and a lapsed state license or an incomplete CDR CPE cycle discovered at recertification can end the practitioner's ability to bill any payer at all. A solo or small-group RD practice running telehealth or in-person nutrition counseling across a caseload of MNT-eligible Medicare patients alongside private-pay and other-insurance clients tracks state license renewal, CDR CPE-unit accumulation by category, and per-patient MNT referral documentation on a personal spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance function, and a referral that expired or was never obtained before a claim was billed is exactly the gap a Medicare Administrative Contractor audit is built to catch.

**What to build:** A state license renewal tracker with CE-hour requirements specific to the practitioner's state; a CDR Professional Development Portfolio tracker logging CPE units by category against the 5-year cycle's minimum requirement, with an alert before the recertification deadline; a per-patient MNT referral tracker confirming a current physician referral documenting the qualifying diagnosis is on file before a claim can be marked billable; and a referral-expiration/renewal-request calendar for patients on an ongoing MNT care plan.

**Skill fit:** State dietetics board CE rules, CDR's published Professional Development Portfolio requirements, and CMS's MNT referral-documentation rule are all public, structured regulatory and credentialing-body data, and the per-patient referral-currency gate before a claim is billable is the same deterministic "block the action until the record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a licensed clinical-nutrition practice category this repo has never addressed despite covering many adjacent therapy and clinical-practice niches.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the state license and CDR CPE-cycle tracker plus the per-patient MNT referral tracker with the billing gate. First 5-8 practice clients through direct outreach to solo and small-group RD telehealth practices.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics (AND), the RD profession's national association, runs an active Nutrition Entrepreneurs practice-group community and state-affiliate member directories reaching exactly the solo-practice and small-group RD owners who currently track this by hand.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $99-249/mo per practice depending on caseload size and whether the practice bills Medicare MNT at volume. 55-70 practices × $170/mo avg = $9,350-11,900/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** State license and CDR CPE cycles recur for every practicing RD without exception, and every new Medicare MNT patient opens a fresh referral-currency clock that has to be checked before every claim — continuous, caseload-driven compliance activity tied directly to the practice's ability to bill at all.

**Biggest risk:** A cash-pay-only nutrition coach without any Medicare billing has a much weaker pain point. Mitigate by qualifying leads for active Medicare MNT billing or state-license/CDR-CPE tracking specifically, and by positioning the state-license/CDR tracker as a lower-tier standalone offer that captures practices before they take on Medicare billing.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 practices ($2.55K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 35 practices ($6K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 55-70 practices plus a diabetes self-management education (DSMT) program accreditation-tracking add-on for practices that also run CMS-recognized DSMT programs.

### 3. Genetic Counselor Independent & Telehealth Private Practice State Licensure & ABGC Board Certification Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent genetic counselors — increasingly running solo or small-group telehealth practices serving patients directly for cancer-risk, prenatal, and pediatric genetic counseling rather than working inside a hospital genetics department — operate under a rapidly expanding state licensure requirement: more than 30 states now require a genetic counselor license distinct from any physician or nurse licensure, each with its own renewal cycle and continuing-education-hour requirement, and a genetic counselor seeing telehealth patients across state lines has to hold and track a separate license in every state where a patient is physically located at the time of the session — a multi-state licensure portfolio problem this repo has addressed for other telehealth-capable professions but never for genetic counseling specifically. Layered on top, board certification through the American Board of Genetic Counseling (ABGC) requires a structured continuing-education and recertification cycle (a point system spanning CE credits, professional activities, and case-log documentation) renewed on a multi-year timeline entirely separate from any state license's CE requirement, and a lapsed ABGC certification can disqualify a counselor from insurance panels that specifically credential against active board certification. A solo genetic counselor running a multi-state telehealth practice tracks a growing roster of state licenses with different renewal dates and CE-hour requirements, plus an independent ABGC recertification point count, on a personal spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance function — and a session conducted with a patient physically located in a state where the counselor's license had already lapsed, or a claim billed under an expired ABGC certification an insurance panel required, is exactly the gap a state licensing board complaint or payer credentialing audit is built to catch.

**What to build:** A multi-state license portfolio tracker with each state's renewal date and CE-hour requirement, gated against a "cleared to see patients in this state today" check keyed to the patient's location at time of session; an ABGC recertification point-accumulation tracker logging CE credits, professional activities, and case-log entries against the multi-year cycle's requirement; and a payer-credentialing-status tracker flagging which insurance panels require active ABGC certification specifically, so a lapse is caught before it silently drops the counselor from a panel.

**Skill fit:** State genetic-counselor licensure statutes (a rapidly growing list a developer can encode and maintain incrementally) and ABGC's published recertification point system are structured, public regulatory and credentialing-body data, and the per-session "cleared to practice in this state today" gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly for multi-state telehealth-capable licensed professionals, applied here to a licensed-counseling category distinct from the genetic-testing-laboratory idea already in this repo, which tracks a lab's own CLIA/CAP compliance rather than an independent counselor's personal multi-state licensure and board certification.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the multi-state license tracker with the per-session location gate and the ABGC recertification point tracker. First 5-8 practice clients through direct outreach to independent telehealth genetic-counseling practices.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the National Society of Genetic Counselors (NSGC) runs an active Private Practice/independent-practice special interest group and member directory, a small but fast-growing community of counselors who left hospital genetics departments to build direct-to-patient telehealth practices and are actively discussing multi-state licensure burden.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $79-199/mo per counselor depending on number of states licensed and caseload volume. 60-80 counselors × $135/mo avg = $8,100-10,800/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new state a telehealth counselor gets licensed in adds its own renewal cycle to track indefinitely, and the growing list of states adopting genetic-counselor licensure statutes for the first time means the product's state-coverage matrix keeps expanding on its own — a structural reason the compliance surface never becomes "finished."

**Biggest risk:** A counselor practicing in only one or two states has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads for active multi-state telehealth practice specifically, where the license-portfolio problem is acute, and by positioning single-state ABGC-only tracking as a lower-tier entry offer.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 counselors ($2K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 40 counselors ($5.4K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 60-80 counselors plus expansion into genetic-counseling assistant (GCA) credential tracking as a second, adjacent user tier within the same practice.

### 4. USFWS-Licensed Exotic & Non-Domestic Wildlife Dealer Import/Export, CITES & Lacey Act Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Commercial dealers who buy, sell, import, and export non-domestic reptiles, exotic birds, and other non-domestic animals for the pet trade and zoological collectors hold a U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service (USFWS) import/export license under 50 CFR Part 14, and every single shipment crossing a U.S. port of entry — inbound or outbound — requires a Form 3-177 Declaration for Importation or Exportation of Fish or Wildlife filed with accurate species identification, quantity, and country of origin before the shipment clears; a declaration filed late, with a misidentified species, or missing entirely is a strict-liability violation USFWS actively enforces at the handful of designated wildlife ports. Any species listed under CITES (the Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species) — a large share of the exotic reptile and parrot trade — additionally requires a CITES export permit from the country of origin and, for Appendix I species, additional documentation before a US import permit will even be considered, layered with Lacey Act declaration requirements that make knowingly trading in wildlife taken or possessed in violation of any underlying state, federal, or foreign law a federal offense independent of the wildlife-specific rules. A small exotic-animal dealer moving dozens of shipments a year across multiple species, multiple countries of origin, and a mix of CITES-listed and non-listed animals tracks USFWS license renewal, per-shipment Form 3-177 filings, and per-species CITES permit status on paper customs paperwork and email threads with brokers, and a shipment that clears without a complete Form 3-177 or a missing CITES permit on a listed species is exactly the finding a USFWS wildlife inspector at the port of entry is positioned to catch — with consequences ranging from shipment seizure to license revocation and criminal referral.

**What to build:** A USFWS import/export license renewal tracker; a per-shipment Form 3-177 declaration log with a completeness check (species, quantity, country of origin, port of entry) before a shipment is marked ready to ship; a per-species CITES-status reference flagging which species in a given shipment require an export permit from the origin country and a corresponding US import permit, with a release gate blocking shipment until both are on file; and a Lacey Act sourcing-documentation log tying each animal back to its underlying legal-acquisition paperwork.

**Skill fit:** USFWS's Form 3-177 requirements, the designated wildlife-port list, and the CITES species-appendix listings are public, structured federal regulatory and treaty data — building a species-lookup engine that flags CITES status and required permits per shipment is a genuine reference-data and rules-engine problem exactly suited to this series' pattern, applied to a wildlife-trade category distinct from the Taxidermist and Wildlife Rehabilitator ideas already in this repo, neither of which involves commercial live-animal import/export.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the license tracker, the per-shipment Form 3-177 log with completeness checks, and the CITES-status lookup with the shipment-release gate for the most commonly traded reptile and bird families. First 5-8 dealer clients through direct outreach.

**Time to first $:** 30-60 days — USARK (United States Association of Reptile Keepers) and PIJAC (Pet Industry Joint Advisory Council) both run active member directories and advocacy communities of commercial exotic-animal dealers and importers who navigate USFWS declarations and CITES permitting as a routine, high-stakes part of every shipment.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-449/mo per dealer depending on shipment volume and share of CITES-listed species handled. 35-45 dealers × $299/mo avg = $10,465-13,455/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every inbound or outbound shipment generates a fresh Form 3-177 filing event and, for CITES-listed species, a fresh permit-verification event — continuous, shipment-volume-driven compliance activity tied directly to the dealer's ability to legally move product across a border.

**Biggest risk:** A shipment seized or a license revoked for a paperwork gap is a severe, business-ending event that could unfairly reflect on the tracking tool if a dealer treats an alert as optional — mitigate by making the CITES-permit and Form 3-177 completeness checks hard release gates rather than advisory flags, with a clear disclaimer that final broker/customs sign-off remains the dealer's responsibility.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10-12 dealers ($3-3.6K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 25-28 dealers ($7.5-8.4K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 35-45 dealers plus a USFWS-designated-wildlife-port shipment-scheduling add-on for dealers coordinating live-animal clearance timing.

### 5. Concentrated Animal Feeding Operation (CAFO) NPDES Permit & Comprehensive Nutrient Management Plan Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Dairy, hog, and poultry operations that exceed EPA's size thresholds for a Concentrated Animal Feeding Operation (CAFO) under the Clean Water Act must hold a National Pollutant Discharge Elimination System (NPDES) permit — issued either directly by EPA or by an authorized state agency — and operate under a state-approved Comprehensive Nutrient Management Plan (CNMP) that specifies exactly how much manure the operation's storage structures may hold, precise land-application rates and setback distances from waterways and wells for each field the operation spreads manure on, and a required schedule of manure and soil nutrient testing to confirm actual application rates match the plan's limits. The permit and CNMP are not a one-time filing: manure storage-structure capacity has to be tracked against actual accumulation to avoid an overflow discharge, every land-application event has to be logged against that specific field's current nutrient-testing results and permitted setback requirements, and periodic manure/soil sample results have to be submitted to the state on the permit's reporting schedule — a discharge event or a land-application record that doesn't match the CNMP's own limits is exactly the finding an EPA or state environmental agency inspection is built to catch, and a documented Clean Water Act discharge violation carries some of the most severe per-day civil penalties in all of environmental law. A small-to-mid-size family-owned CAFO running a handful of manure storage structures and land-applying across a dozen or more fields tracks storage capacity, per-field application history against soil-test results, and the recurring manure/soil sampling schedule on paper records and a filing cabinet, with no dedicated environmental-compliance staff — exactly the gap that turns into a six- or seven-figure discharge-violation penalty when it's missed.

**What to build:** A manure storage-structure capacity tracker comparing current accumulation against each structure's permitted capacity with an alert before the structure approaches its limit; a per-field land-application log capturing application date, volume, and nutrient content, checked against that field's current soil-test results and the CNMP's permitted application rate and waterway/well setback distance before the application is logged as compliant; a recurring manure and soil nutrient-testing calendar computing each test's next due date from the permit's required schedule; and a state/EPA annual or periodic report generator assembling the storage, application, and testing records into the format the permitting agency requires.

**Skill fit:** EPA's CAFO regulations (40 CFR Part 122 Subpart B) and state-specific CNMP requirements are published federal and state regulatory text with concrete numeric thresholds (storage capacity, application rates, setback distances), and the per-field application-versus-limit check plus the storage-capacity threshold alert are exactly the deterministic "compute the limit, log the event, flag the threshold breach" pattern this series builds repeatedly — applied here to a livestock-operation category distinct from every other agricultural idea already in this repo, none of which track a CAFO's own water-discharge permit or land-application compliance.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the manure storage-capacity tracker, the per-field land-application log with the CNMP compliance check, and the nutrient-testing calendar for one or two states with well-documented CNMP frameworks (Wisconsin, Iowa, or North Carolina are all active dairy/hog CAFO states with detailed public CNMP guidance). First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent family-owned CAFOs.

**Time to first $:** 30-60 days — state Farm Bureau chapters and the National Milk Producers Federation both reach independent dairy CAFO operators directly, and state-level pork and poultry producer associations (many state pork producers councils run active member outreach) reach the hog and poultry side of this same buyer population.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per operation depending on the number of manure storage structures and land-application fields being tracked. 25-30 operations × $399/mo avg = $9,975-11,970/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every manure storage structure runs a continuous accumulation clock against its permitted capacity, every land-application event across every field generates a fresh compliance check against that field's current soil-test data, and the recurring nutrient-testing schedule never stops for as long as the operation holds its NPDES permit — a permanent, operation-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the CAFO's legal right to keep operating.

**Biggest risk:** A discharge violation is one of the most severe possible outcomes in this entire repo's compliance categories, and a tool that gives false confidence could make the consequence worse rather than better — mitigate by scoping the product explicitly around documentation and threshold-alerting (not engineering or agronomic judgment), with a clear disclaimer that a certified nutrient-management planner, not the software, makes the underlying CNMP's technical determinations.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10-12 operations ($3.6-4.4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 20-22 operations ($7.2-7.9K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 25-30 operations plus expansion into the smaller Animal Feeding Operation (AFO) tier that voluntarily adopts CNMP-style recordkeeping ahead of crossing the CAFO threshold.

## This Session's Pick: #4 (USFWS Exotic Wildlife Dealer) for the sharpest per-shipment compliance mechanics; #5 (CAFO) for the highest-stakes enforcement backdrop

USFWS exotic wildlife dealer compliance stands out because the compliance event repeats with every single shipment rather than on an annual cycle — a fresh Form 3-177 filing and, for CITES-listed species, a fresh permit-verification check every time product crosses a border — which gives the product genuinely continuous, volume-linked usage from day one. USARK and PIJAC give a direct, well-organized channel into a dealer community that already treats customs paperwork as an existential, not administrative, part of the business.

CAFO compliance is the highest-stakes bet in this session: a Clean Water Act discharge violation carries some of the largest per-day civil penalties in all of environmental law, which gives the pitch real teeth with a buyer who has genuinely seen a neighboring operation get fined. State Farm Bureau chapters and the National Milk Producers Federation give direct access to exactly the small, family-owned dairy and hog operations that have outgrown a paper CNMP binder but can't justify hiring a dedicated environmental compliance manager.
