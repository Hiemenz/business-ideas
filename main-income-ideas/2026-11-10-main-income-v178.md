# Main Income Ideas - 2026-11-10 (Session 178)

## Who this is for
A 29-year-old software engineer with data analytics skills who wants to replace a full-time salary ($8-15K+/mo) within 12-24 months by building solo, in evenings and weekends, with no outside funding. Every idea below must have a realistic path to first revenue inside 90 days and a named, reachable distribution channel.

## Lens for this session: Alex Hormozi
Built from the $100M Offers / $100M Leads frameworks:
- **Value equation:** Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood of Success) / (Time Delay x Effort & Sacrifice).
- **Grand Slam Offer:** performance-priced or guarantee-backed, so the buyer carries no risk.
- **Lead levers:** speed, follow-up and reactivation of people who already raised a hand.

Per standing feedback, none of these are compliance or regulatory tools. Each solves a revenue-leakage or growth problem.

## Ideas

### 1. Missed-Call Recovery & After-Hours Booking for Emergency Plumbers and Locksmiths

**Problem:** An emergency tradesperson on a job cannot answer the phone, and a caller with a burst pipe or a locked-out car dials the next number within minutes. A solo plumber can miss 20-40% of calls, and each emergency call is worth $300-900.

**What to build:** Forward missed calls to a number that texts back within 30 seconds ("We saw your call. What's going on, and what's your address?"), collects the problem and photo, offers a booking window and alerts the owner with a one-line summary. Show a missed-call-to-booked-job report.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Every booked job from a missed call is yours, and we only take $25 per job we book." No risk for the owner and no setup effort.

**Skill fit:** Twilio call-forwarding and SMS flows, simple intent capture and a reporting dashboard.

**MVP scope:** 2 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 21-45 days through plumber and locksmith Facebook groups and Google Maps cold outreach.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $79/mo + $25 per job booked. An owner booking 12 jobs/mo pays $379/mo. 35 owners x $379 = **$13,265/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** The tool pays for itself in the first booked job, so cancellation is rare.

**Biggest risk:** Call forwarding setup varies by carrier. Provide a short setup video and a done-for-you setup call.

**Growth path:** Plumbers and locksmiths, then garage-door, towing, water-damage restoration and appliance repair.

### 2. Inquiry-to-Booking Follow-Up Engine for Wedding & Event Vendors

**Problem:** Photographers, florists and DJs answer an inquiry once and rely on the couple to reply. Couples contact 5-8 vendors and book the one that stays in touch. A vendor who loses 3 extra bookings per year at $3K each is leaving $9K on the table.

**What to build:** Capture inquiries from the vendor's website form and email. Send a prompt reply with availability, a pricing guide and a tour of past work, then a timed follow-up sequence over 3 weeks. A pipeline board shows inquiry, reply, proposal sent and booked, plus a booking rate by source.

**Grand Slam angle:** "A 60-day guarantee: if you do not book at least one extra client from inquiries you were going to lose, you pay nothing."

**Skill fit:** Email/SMS automation, simple CRM and funnel analytics.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-60 days through vendor Facebook groups and local wedding networking groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $129/mo. 90 vendors = **$11,610/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Seasonal peaks bring a wave of new inquiries each year, and booking data shows vendors exactly which lead source pays.

**Biggest risk:** A seasonal market with lumpy churn. Offer an annual plan at 2 months free.

**Growth path:** Photographers, then videographers, DJs, florists, venues and planners.

### 3. Menu-and-Price Optimizer for Independent Detailing and Car-Wash Businesses

**Problem:** A detailer prices by feel, undercuts the market and sells a flat list of services. Hormozi's value equation says that bundling, naming and tiering raise average ticket without lowering demand, but owners rarely test it.

**What to build:** Import past jobs from Square, Stripe or a spreadsheet. Show average ticket, attach rate and margin by service. Generate a three-tier "good, better, best" menu with a decoy, and a booking page that tests the new menu against the old one. Report the average ticket lift.

**Grand Slam angle:** "We raise your average ticket in 60 days or we refund the setup fee."

**Skill fit:** Pricing analytics, a booking page and A/B tests.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-60 days.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $499 setup + $99/mo. 12 new setups per month in months 1-3 gives $5,988 setup revenue per month. 40 retained clients bring $3,960/mo. Month 6: about **$9,900/mo** recurring plus new setups.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Menu changes lift income the same week, and the case studies sell the next client.

**Biggest risk:** A small market per city. Sell nationally via detailing communities and trade forums.

**Growth path:** Detailers, then window tinting, pressure washing, mobile mechanics and junk removal.

### 4. Upgrade Radar for Small Managed IT Service Providers

**Problem:** A 3-10 person IT shop supports 40-100 small-business clients, and its own ticket data shows which clients are ready for upsells: aging laptops, repeated outages, no backup, no security training. The owner is too busy fixing tickets to notice, so growth only comes from new clients.

**What to build:** Ingest ticket exports from the shop's PSA (Autotask, HaloPSA, Syncro). Detect upsell signals per client and generate a ready-to-send, one-page proposal with a price. Track proposals sent and accepted and the revenue per client.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Free for 30 days, then 10% of the first-year value of every upgrade the radar finds."

**Skill fit:** Ticket-pattern analytics and proposal generation.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends with CSV import first.

**Time to first $:** 45-75 days through MSP owner communities (Reddit r/msp, Discord and local MSP peer groups).

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** An MSP closes $15K of upgrades per quarter from the radar, so 10% is $1,500 per quarter, about $500/mo. $99/mo platform. 22 MSPs x about $600/mo = **$13,200/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Upgrades become recurring revenue for the MSP, and the shop's own data makes the product feel personal.

**Biggest risk:** PSA integrations. Start with CSV and one API.

**Growth path:** MSPs, then web agencies and small accounting firms.

### 5. Idle-Asset Pricing & Utilization Engine for Small Equipment Rental Shops

**Problem:** A rental shop with tools, party equipment or camera gear has assets that sit idle, and the owner prices every day the same. Utilization on many items is 20-30%, so a few price moves and bundles could add thousands per month.

**What to build:** Import rental history from the shop's software or a spreadsheet. Compute utilization and revenue per asset, flag chronic idle items, and suggest weekday discounts, weekend surcharges and bundles ("the whole tile-cutting kit"). Run suggested prices and track the lift.

**Grand Slam angle:** "We take 15% of any revenue lift we can show against your last 90 days."

**Skill fit:** Time-series utilization analytics and simple pricing rules.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 45-75 days.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** A shop earning $400/mo of lift pays $60/mo, plus a $79/mo platform fee, about $139/mo. That is too low alone, so target shops with a fleet of 100+ assets, where lift averages $2,000/mo = $300 share + $79. 35 shops x about $380/mo = **$13,300/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** The utilization data gives the shop a buy-versus-sell decision on each asset.

**Biggest risk:** Rental shops use old software. Offer a spreadsheet import and onboarding help.

**Growth path:** Tool and party rentals, then camera gear, bike and boat rentals, and equipment dealers.
