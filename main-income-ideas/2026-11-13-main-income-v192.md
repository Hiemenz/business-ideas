# Main Income Ideas - 2026-11-13 (Session 192)

## Who this is for
A 29-year-old software engineer with data analytics skills who wants to replace a full-time salary ($8-15K+/mo) within 12-24 months by building solo, in evenings and weekends, with no outside funding. Every idea below must have a realistic path to first revenue inside 90 days and a named, reachable distribution channel.

## Lens for this session: Alex Hormozi
Built from the $100M Offers / $100M Leads frameworks:
- **Value equation:** Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood of Success) / (Time Delay x Effort & Sacrifice).
- **Grand Slam Offer:** performance-priced or guarantee-backed, paid from savings or revenue the client can see.
- **Own the customer relationship:** Hormozi's lead frameworks stress owning your audience. This session moves customers off rented platforms and into the owner's own list and channels.

Per standing feedback, none of these are compliance or regulatory tools. Each solves a revenue-leakage or growth problem.

## Ideas

### 1. Delivery-App-to-Direct-Ordering Migration Engine for Independent Restaurants

**Problem:** Third-party delivery apps take 20-30% of each order and keep the customer's contact details. A restaurant with $15K/mo of app orders pays $3-4.5K in fees and has no way to market to those diners again.

**What to build:** A direct-ordering page and a small insert for delivery bags (QR code with a "next order direct, free dessert" offer). Capture the customer's phone and email at first direct order, send reorder nudges and a loyalty reward. Show the owner fees saved and the share of orders that moved from apps to direct.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Move 20% of your app orders to direct in 90 days, or you do not pay." The fee is 3% of direct-order revenue, well below the app fees saved.

**Skill fit:** Ordering page, QR and SMS flows, POS integrations and a savings dashboard.

**MVP scope:** 3-4 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-60 days through restaurant-owner groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** A restaurant moving $4,000/mo to direct pays 3% = $120/mo, plus a $79/mo platform fee, about $200/mo. 60 restaurants x $200 = **$12,000/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** The restaurant owns its customer list, and the savings are visible monthly.

**Biggest risk:** Competition from direct-ordering vendors. Differentiate on a done-for-you migration campaign and a flat low fee.

**Growth path:** Independent restaurants, then ghost kitchens, bakeries, pizzerias and coffee shops.

### 2. Food-Cost Tracker and Menu Re-Pricer for Small Restaurants

**Problem:** Ingredient prices change weekly, but menu prices change once a year. Owners rarely know the food cost of each dish, so a popular item may lose margin without anyone noticing.

**What to build:** Capture supplier invoices (photo or email), update ingredient prices and compute the live cost of each recipe. Rank dishes by margin and popularity from POS sales, flag dishes whose margin fell below target and suggest a price or portion change. Report margin recovered.

**Grand Slam angle:** "We find $1,500 a month in menu margin or the first quarter is free."

**Skill fit:** Invoice OCR, recipe costing and POS data analysis.

**MVP scope:** 4 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 45-75 days through restaurant-owner groups and chef communities.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $129/mo. 90 restaurants = **$11,610/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Invoice history and recipe cost make it part of weekly ordering, and the margin savings are large relative to the fee.

**Biggest risk:** Invoice capture effort. Use photo upload and learn each supplier's format.

**Growth path:** Restaurants, then bakeries, cafes, catering companies and food trucks.

### 3. Online Booking and Add-On Engine for Small Outfitters and Rental Operators

**Problem:** A kayak, bike, paddleboard or ski rental shop takes bookings by phone and walk-in, misses online searchers at night and rarely sells add-ons (guided tour, shuttle, photo package, lunch).

**What to build:** An online booking page showing live availability, with add-ons offered at checkout and a pre-trip text with a weather note and an upsell. Show revenue per booking, add-on attach rate and capacity filled by day.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Free to set up. We take 4% of online bookings and 15% of add-on revenue."

**Skill fit:** Booking calendar, inventory limits, checkout and messaging.

**MVP scope:** 4 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 45-75 days, timed ahead of the season.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** An operator with $20,000/mo of online bookings in season pays $800 + add-ons $300 = $1,100 in-season, about $450/mo averaged across the year, plus $49/mo. 25 operators x about $500 = **$12,500/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Operators grow online bookings every year, and add-on revenue is a clear gain.

**Biggest risk:** Strong seasonality. Prefer year-round or long-season markets and sell annual plans.

**Growth path:** Rental outfitters, then tour operators, boat charters, climbing gyms and activity parks.

### 4. Local Sponsorship Sales Toolkit for Youth Sports Clubs and Booster Groups

**Problem:** Youth clubs depend on local sponsors, but the volunteers who sell sponsorships have no pitch deck, no list of businesses to ask and no tracking. The same business gets asked by three teams, and renewals are forgotten.

**What to build:** A toolkit with a sponsorship package builder (tiers, benefits, pricing), a local business list by category, an email and call script and a pipeline board with renewal reminders. Provide sponsor recognition pages and a year-end results report for sponsors.

**Grand Slam angle:** "We help you raise your sponsorship goal, or the second season is free."

**Skill fit:** Package templates, lead lists, a simple CRM and recognition pages.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-60 days through league and booster-club networks.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $29/mo per club. 380 clubs = **$11,020/mo**. A 5% success fee on new sponsorship revenue would raise it.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Clubs renew each season, and sponsors seen across many clubs create a local-business database.

**Biggest risk:** Low budgets and volunteer turnover. Keep pricing low and sell through leagues for bulk adoption.

**Growth path:** Youth sports, then school booster clubs, bands, theater groups and community events.

### 5. Event and School Photographer Gallery Sales Engine

**Problem:** A photographer who shoots schools, sports leagues and events earns from print and digital sales, but only 10-20% of families ever buy. After the first email, there is no follow-up, and the gallery expires unnoticed.

**What to build:** A gallery with timed reminder sequences to parents ("Maya's best photos, 20% off until Friday"), bundles (digital + prints), face-grouping so each family sees their child first and a final-day deadline offer. Report conversion and revenue per gallery.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Raise your gallery conversion by 30% or you pay nothing." The fee is 10% of the added revenue.

**Skill fit:** Gallery delivery, reminder flows and bundle pricing.

**MVP scope:** 3-4 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-60 days through event and school photographer groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** A photographer with $6,000/mo of gallery sales adds 30% = $1,800, so 10% = $180/mo, plus $49/mo = about $230/mo. 55 photographers x $230 = **$12,650/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Each shoot produces a new gallery, and the added revenue repeats.

**Biggest risk:** Competition from established gallery platforms. Differentiate on aggressive, family-targeted follow-up.

**Growth path:** School and league photographers, then wedding, portrait and dance-recital photographers.
