# Main Income Ideas - 2026-08-24 (Session 133)

## Who this is for
You're 29, you write software and know your way around data — pipelines, scrapers, structured reference data, rules engines. You want to replace a full-time salary ($8-15K+/mo) within 12-24 months with something you build solo in evenings and weekends, no outside funding, no co-founder, and you want the first paying customer inside 90 days. You sell a monthly-retainer SaaS that turns their compliance binder into a living calendar with alerts and hard gates. The regulator (or accrediting body) creates the deadline; you sell the peace of mind of never missing it.

### 1. Biodiesel & Renewable Fuel Producer EPA RFS RIN Generation & Quarterly Excise-Credit Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Small biodiesel producers converting waste vegetable oil, animal fats, or virgin feedstock into biodiesel operate under EPA's Renewable Fuel Standard (RFS, 40 CFR Part 80 Subpart M), which requires every registered producer to generate a Renewable Identification Number (RIN) for each qualifying batch, submit quarterly production and RIN-generation reports through EPA's Moderated Transaction System, and maintain the underlying feedstock and production records an EPA audit can request at any time — a batch produced without a properly generated and reported RIN is fuel the producer can't monetize through the RFS credit market, and a reporting gap discovered during an EPA compliance audit can suspend the facility's registration entirely. Producers claiming the federal biodiesel mixture excise tax credit under IRC §6426 must separately file Form 720 on a strict quarterly schedule, documenting the exact blend percentage and gallon count for every qualifying blend, and every batch sold as finished biodiesel must meet ASTM D6751's fuel-quality specification — cetane number, water and sediment content, acid number, and oxidative stability — with test results on file before the fuel ships, since an off-spec batch discovered after delivery is a liability and a contract dispute with the fuel distributor who bought it. A small producer running a continuous or batch-reactor process across a growing feedstock-supplier list manages RIN generation and quarterly EPA reporting, the Form 720 excise-credit filing cycle, and ASTM D6751 test-result documentation on a mix of a plant operator's spreadsheet and a filing cabinet of lab reports, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific batch's RIN was actually generated and its quality testing cleared before the fuel is invoiced to a buyer.

**What to build:** A per-batch RIN generation and EPA quarterly-report tracker computing each report's due date and flagging any batch missing its RIN before it can be marked sellable; a Form 720 excise-tax-credit filing tracker computing the quarterly deadline and the blend-percentage documentation required; and an ASTM D6751 test-result log gating a batch's "cleared to ship" status until cetane, water/sediment, and acid-number results are on file.

**Skill fit:** EPA's RFS reporting cycle and ASTM D6751's fuel-quality parameters are public, structured federal regulatory and standards-body data with concrete quarterly deadlines, and the per-batch "cleared to ship" gate plus the RIN-report tracker are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a renewable-fuel-manufacturing niche this repo has never addressed.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the RIN/EPA-report tracker, the Form 720 excise-credit tracker, and the ASTM D6751 test-result gate. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent small-volume biodiesel producers.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — Clean Fuels Alliance America (formerly the National Biodiesel Board) runs an active member directory and annual conference reaching exactly the small independent producers currently reconciling quarterly RIN reports and excise-credit filings against a batch log with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per producer depending on batch volume and feedstock-supplier count. 22-28 producers × $429/mo avg = $9,438-12,012/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every batch produced generates its own RIN-and-reporting obligation regardless of production volume, the Form 720 excise-credit filing recurs every quarter for as long as the producer claims the credit, and every new feedstock supplier or buyer contract the producer signs adds a fresh reconciliation record — a continuous, batch-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the producer's output.

**Biggest risk:** A very small producer running an occasional batch for personal or cooperative use with no RFS registration has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads for registered producers selling into the RIN credit market on a regular production schedule, where the quarterly reporting-and-testing calendar genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10 producers ($4.3K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 18 producers ($7.7K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 22-28 producers plus a fuel-buyer-facing quality-certificate export producers can hand a distributor at delivery.

### 2. Cotton Gin Operator OSHA Cotton Dust Medical Surveillance & State Air Permit Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Cotton gin operators separating fiber from seed at compressed harvest-season volume operate under OSHA's Cotton Dust Standard (29 CFR 1910.1043), which requires periodic airborne cotton-dust exposure monitoring for every employee working in the gin's dust-generating areas, a written compliance program triggered the moment monitoring results exceed the permissible exposure limit, and mandatory baseline-plus-periodic pulmonary function (spirometry) testing for every exposed employee — a gin that dispatches a crew into the ginning stand without current exposure monitoring or a lapsed spirometry enrollment is exactly the finding an OSHA inspection during the compressed fall ginning season is built to catch, and byssinosis (brown lung disease) surveillance gaps are the specific hazard the standard exists to prevent. Every gin's dust-collection and gin-trash-burning equipment also operates under a state air-quality permit with its own emissions-testing and periodic reporting cycle, and a permit exceedance during peak-season operation can force a costly shutdown at the exact moment growers are delivering their harvest and expecting same-day ginning turnaround. Every bale ginned is also sent to a USDA Agricultural Marketing Service classing office for an HVI (High Volume Instrument) grade, and the gin must reconcile the returned classing data against its own bale-tagging records before growers can be paid accurately for the graded quality of their cotton. A small independent gin running several employees across a two-to-three-month compressed harvest season manages per-employee dust-monitoring and spirometry-enrollment currency, the state air permit's emissions-testing cycle, and per-bale classing-data reconciliation on a mix of a gin manager's paper log and a spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific employee is actually cleared to work the ginning stand before the season's compressed schedule starts.

**What to build:** A per-employee Cotton Dust exposure-monitoring and spirometry-enrollment tracker with a "cleared to work the stand" gate; a state air permit emissions-testing and reporting-deadline tracker; and a per-bale HVI classing-data reconciliation log matching USDA classing results against the gin's own bale-tagging records for grower payment.

**Skill fit:** OSHA's Cotton Dust Standard's monitoring and medical-surveillance triggers, and USDA's HVI classing-data structure, are public, structured federal regulatory and agency data with a concrete per-employee and per-bale record structure, and the cleared-to-work gate plus the classing-reconciliation log are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a raw-cotton-processing niche entirely distinct from any textile, apparel, or leather-manufacturing idea already in this repo, none of which processes seed cotton or triggers OSHA's byssinosis-surveillance standard at all.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the exposure/spirometry tracker with the work-eligibility gate, the state air permit tracker, and the HVI classing-reconciliation log. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent cotton gins.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the National Cotton Ginners Association and its state-chapter affiliates run an active member directory and annual conference reaching exactly the small independent gins currently tracking dust-monitoring and classing reconciliation on paper during the compressed harvest season.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249-449/mo per gin depending on employee headcount and bale volume. 26-34 gins × $339/mo avg = $8,814-11,526/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every employee's spirometry enrollment recurs on its own periodic cycle for as long as they work the stand, every ginning season generates a fresh state air-permit testing obligation, and every bale ginned adds another classing-reconciliation record — a continuous, season-and-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the gin's harvest throughput.

**Biggest risk:** A very small, family-run gin with a handful of seasonal employees and low bale volume has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads for gins running several dust-exposed employees and a multi-thousand-bale season, where the compressed-season monitoring calendar genuinely outgrows a paper log.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 gins ($4.1K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 22 gins ($7.5K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 26-34 gins plus a grower-facing classing-reconciliation export gins can share at payout time.

### 3. Fireworks Manufacturer ATF Explosives Manufacturing License & DOT/PHMSA Hazard-Classification Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Fireworks manufacturers producing consumer and display pyrotechnic devices operate under an ATF explosives manufacturing license issued under 27 CFR Part 555 — a manufacturing-privileged license entirely distinct from a display operator's storage-only Federal Explosives License — subject to unannounced ATF compliance inspections of the manufacturing facility's magazine storage, employee-possessor background clearances, and daily explosives-materials inventory reconciliation. Before any new device design can be shipped in interstate commerce at all, it must clear DOT/PHMSA's UN/DOT hazard classification approval process (49 CFR §173.56), a test-and-approval sequence assigning the device its shipping classification (commonly UN0335 or UN0336 for consumer fireworks) that every subsequent shipment's paperwork depends on — a device shipped under an expired or never-obtained classification approval is exactly the finding a PHMSA hazmat roadside inspection or a carrier's own compliance review is built to catch, and can halt an entire product line's distribution mid-season. Every consumer-grade device must also meet CPSC's construction and labeling standards (16 CFR §§1500.14 and 1507) — fuse burn-time limits, maximum pyrotechnic composition weight by device category, and required warning labeling — with test documentation on file before the design can be sold at retail, and a state fire marshal's manufacturing-facility permit carries its own separate inspection and renewal cycle from the ATF federal license. A small manufacturer running several product lines through a compressed pre-Independence-Day and pre-New-Year's production season manages per-device DOT classification-approval status, CPSC construction/labeling test documentation, ATF magazine-inventory reconciliation, and the state fire marshal permit cycle on a mix of a plant manager's binder and a spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific product line is actually cleared to ship before the season's compressed distribution window opens.

**What to build:** A per-device-design DOT/PHMSA hazard-classification tracker with a "cleared to ship" gate blocking any product line from being marked shippable until its UN/DOT classification approval is current; a CPSC construction and labeling test-documentation log per device category; an ATF magazine-inventory reconciliation tracker; and a state fire marshal manufacturing-facility permit renewal tracker.

**Skill fit:** DOT/PHMSA's hazard-classification approval process and CPSC's device-category construction standards are public, structured federal regulatory data with a concrete per-design approval requirement, and the per-device "cleared to ship" gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a pyrotechnics-manufacturing niche explicitly distinct from the Fireworks Display Operator & Retailer idea already in this repo, whose storage-and-display-use license framework never touches a manufacturer's own DOT classification-approval or CPSC device-construction compliance surface at all.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the DOT classification tracker with the ship-eligibility gate, the CPSC test-documentation log, and the ATF/state permit trackers. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent fireworks manufacturers.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the American Pyrotechnics Association (APA) runs an active member directory and annual conference reaching exactly the small independent manufacturers currently tracking per-device DOT classification approvals and CPSC test documentation across a compressed seasonal production window without dedicated compliance software.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $349-699/mo per manufacturer depending on product-line count and production volume. 20-26 manufacturers × $499/mo avg = $9,980-12,974/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new device design opens its own independent DOT classification-approval and CPSC test-documentation cycle, every compressed seasonal production run generates a fresh magazine-inventory reconciliation obligation, and every new product line the manufacturer develops adds a fresh classification-and-testing record — a continuous, product-line-driven compliance stream tied directly to the manufacturer's ability to keep shipping legally into the next season.

**Biggest risk:** A very small manufacturer producing only a handful of long-established device designs with no new-product development has a comparatively lower-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads for manufacturers actively developing new device designs across multiple product categories, where the per-design classification-and-testing calendar genuinely outgrows a plant manager's binder.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10 manufacturers ($5K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 16 manufacturers ($8K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 20-26 manufacturers plus a distributor-facing classification-status export manufacturers can hand a carrier ahead of a seasonal shipping surge.

### 4. Film & Television Production Armorer & Weapons Master Chain-of-Custody & State Production-Safety Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Film and television production armorers who supply, maintain, and issue firearms modified to fire blank ammunition operate under an ATF Federal Firearms License with a Special Occupational Taxpayer (SOT) endorsement, a licensing structure entirely distinct from a retail dealer/gunsmith's civilian-transfer FFL, since an armorer's weapons never transfer to a civilian buyer at all — they're issued to a performer for a scene and returned to the armorer's custody the same day. Since the 2021 "Rust" set shooting, a growing number of states have enacted specific on-set firearms-safety statutes requiring a qualified armorer be physically present for any scene involving a weapon, a documented pre-scene safety meeting with the performer and camera crew before the first take, and — the mechanism that actually prevents the exact failure mode that led to that incident — an unbroken per-weapon, per-round chain-of-custody log tracking every firearm and every blank round issued each shooting day against what comes back at wrap, since a round unaccounted for at the end of the day is exactly the gap that state statute's reporting requirement is built to force into the open before it becomes tomorrow's tragedy. Every inter-location move of blank ammunition — a genuine low-order explosive under DOT's hazard classification — must also travel under 49 CFR Part 173's Class 1.4S placarding and shipping-paper requirements, a hazmat compliance obligation most production armorers manage informally between location moves. A working armorer servicing several productions across a shooting season manages per-weapon and per-round chain-of-custody logs, the state-specific pre-scene safety-meeting documentation requirement, and DOT shipping-paper compliance for blank-ammunition transport on a mix of a paper weapons log and a production coordinator's memory, with no systematic cross-check confirming every round issued that day is actually accounted for before the set wraps.

**What to build:** A per-production, per-weapon chain-of-custody log recording every firearm and blank round issued and returned each shooting day, with an end-of-day reconciliation flag for any unaccounted round; a state-specific pre-scene safety-meeting documentation tracker tied to the production's filming-location statute; and a DOT Class 1.4S shipping-paper generator for inter-location blank-ammunition transport.

**Skill fit:** State on-set firearms-safety statutes and DOT's Class 1.4S hazmat shipping requirements are public, structured legal and federal regulatory data with a concrete per-day reconciliation requirement, and the end-of-day chain-of-custody reconciliation is the same deterministic "verify the record before the day closes" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a production-safety niche explicitly distinct from every Federal Firearms Licensee retail-dealer idea already in this repo, whose Form 4473/NICS civilian-transfer framework a blank-firing weapon issued and returned to the same armorer's custody every day never triggers.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the per-weapon/per-round chain-of-custody log with the reconciliation flag, the state safety-meeting tracker, and the DOT shipping-paper generator. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent working armorers and prop-house weapons departments.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — IATSE Local 44 (the Affiliated Property Craftspersons local, whose membership includes production armorers) and its regional counterparts reach exactly the working armorers currently tracking per-round chain-of-custody on a paper weapons log across a shooting season with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per armorer depending on concurrent-production count and weapons-inventory size. 22-28 armorers × $429/mo avg = $9,438-12,012/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every shooting day generates its own fresh chain-of-custody reconciliation event regardless of how routine the scene, every new production the armorer books opens a fresh state-safety-statute compliance record, and every inter-location move triggers its own DOT shipping-paper obligation — a continuous, production-and-shooting-day-driven compliance stream tied directly to the armorer's ability to keep working in an industry now scrutinizing this exact risk closely.

**Biggest risk:** A very small operation working only local, low-budget productions with a handful of weapons has a comparatively lower-stakes tracking need a paper log can still handle. Mitigate by qualifying leads for armorers running multiple concurrent productions or a larger weapons inventory, where the per-day, per-round reconciliation volume genuinely outgrows manual tracking.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10 armorers ($4.3K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 18 armorers ($7.7K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 22-28 armorers plus a production-insurer-facing safety-compliance export armorers can hand a completion-bond company during underwriting.

### 5. Juice & Cider Processor FDA 21 CFR 120 Juice HACCP & Pathogen-Reduction Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Small juice processors and apple cider mills producing fresh-pressed and cold-pressed products for retail and wholesale sale operate under FDA's Juice HACCP rule (21 CFR Part 120), which requires every processor to identify and control the specific food-safety hazards associated with fruit and vegetable juice — pathogens like E. coli O157:H7, Salmonella, and Cryptosporidium that can survive on unwashed or bruised produce — through a written HACCP plan with documented critical control points. A processor achieving the rule's required 5-log pathogen reduction, commonly through pasteurization or UV treatment, must validate and document that process is actually hitting the target reduction on an ongoing basis, while a small fresh-pressed operation that skips pathogen-reduction treatment entirely must instead affix the FDA-mandated warning label (21 CFR §101.17(g)) to every container sold at retail — a processor that ships product with neither a validated 5-log reduction process nor the required warning label is exactly the finding an FDA or state agriculture-department inspection is built to catch, and an unlabeled batch tied to a foodborne-illness outbreak is a liability exposure that can shut a small operation down entirely. Every processor must also maintain FDA Food Facility Registration, and cider mills selling into multiple states navigate a patchwork of state-specific cottage-food and juice-bar licensing thresholds that determine whether a given production volume even requires the full HACCP plan or falls under a lighter-touch state exemption. A small mill or juice bar running a seasonal fall cider-pressing rush or a year-round cold-press wholesale account load manages HACCP critical-control-point documentation, pasteurization/UV-validation records, and warning-label compliance for unpasteurized batches on a mix of a production log and a folder of lab validation reports, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific batch is actually cleared to ship — either through validated pathogen reduction or the correct warning label — before it leaves the plant.

**What to build:** A per-batch HACCP critical-control-point log with a pasteurization/UV pathogen-reduction validation tracker computing whether the 5-log target was actually met; a warning-label compliance gate for any unpasteurized batch, blocking it from being marked shippable until the correct 21 CFR §101.17(g) label is confirmed applied; and an FDA Food Facility Registration and state cottage-food/juice-license threshold tracker for processors selling across multiple states.

**Skill fit:** FDA's Juice HACCP rule and its pathogen-reduction-or-warning-label branching structure are public, structured federal regulatory data with a concrete per-batch compliance path, and the per-batch "cleared to ship" gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a juice-and-cider-processing niche explicitly distinct from the bottled water plant idea already in this repo, whose 21 CFR Part 129 non-caloric-beverage framework carries no fruit-juice-specific pathogen risk or warning-label branching requirement at all.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the HACCP/pathogen-reduction-validation tracker, the warning-label compliance gate, and the FDA registration/state-threshold tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent cider mills and cold-pressed juice processors.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the American Cider Association's annual CiderCon and member directory reach exactly the small independent mills and juice processors currently reconciling batch-level pathogen-reduction validation against a production log with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249-449/mo per processor depending on batch volume and multi-state sales footprint. 26-34 processors × $339/mo avg = $8,814-11,526/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every batch produced generates its own pathogen-reduction-validation-or-warning-label compliance event regardless of volume, every new wholesale account or multi-state sale reopens the state-threshold determination, and a mill's seasonal production rush only concentrates the batch-level compliance volume into a shorter window — a continuous, production-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the processor's ability to keep shipping product.

**Biggest risk:** A very small operation selling only at a single farm stand within one state's cottage-food exemption has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads for mills and processors selling wholesale into retail accounts or across state lines, where the full HACCP-plan-and-labeling calendar genuinely outgrows a production log.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 processors ($4.1K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 22 processors ($7.5K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 26-34 processors plus a retailer-facing batch-compliance export processors can hand a grocery buyer during a wholesale-account onboarding review.
