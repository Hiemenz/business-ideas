# Main Income Ideas - 2026-08-26 (Session 144)

## Who this is for
You're 29, you write software and know your way around data — pipelines, scrapers, structured reference data, rules engines. You want to replace a full-time salary ($8-15K+/mo) within 12-24 months with something you build solo in evenings and weekends, no outside funding, no co-founder, and you want the first paying customer inside 90 days. The wedge in this series is always the same: small operators in a regulated industry who have non-optional government or accrediting-body obligations — licenses, permits, certifications, filings, inspections — and no compliance department to track them. You sell a monthly-retainer SaaS that turns their compliance binder into a living calendar with alerts and hard gates. The regulator (or accrediting body) creates the deadline; you sell the peace of mind of never missing it.

### 1. Assistance/Service Dog Training Organization ADI Accreditation, Public Access Test & Per-Dog Training-Milestone Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent assistance and service dog training organizations that raise and train dogs to mitigate a client's disability under the ADA operate under Assistance Dogs International (ADI) or International Guide Dog Federation (IGDF) accreditation — a rigorous, on-site program review renewed on a multi-year cycle covering training methodology, dog health and welfare standards, and instructor qualification — and an organization whose accreditation lapses loses the credential that placement referral sources, funders, and grant programs require before they'll send a client its way. Before a dog-and-handler team can be certified "public access ready," it must pass a standardized Public Access Test assessing real-world behavior in stores, restaurants, and transit, and a team placed into public settings without that documented pass is exactly the finding an ADA-access dispute or an accreditation site-review is built to catch, since a poorly behaved "service dog" incident reflects on every graduate team the organization has ever placed. Every breeding dog in the program's pipeline must also clear OFA hip and elbow screening before being bred, and every puppy-in-training moves through a long, multi-stage development pipeline — foundational obedience, task-specific training tied to the client's disability, then public-access proofing — typically spanning 18 to 24 months, with a dog that skips a stage or is placed before its task training is actually complete a liability the organization can't undo once the dog is in a client's home. An organization running several dogs simultaneously at different pipeline stages, supported by a volunteer puppy-raiser network, currently tracks breeding clearances, per-dog training milestones, and Public Access Test results on a training director's spreadsheet and a filing cabinet of paper evaluation forms, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific dog's milestones and its handler's Public Access Test are both actually complete before that placement is finalized.

**What to build:** A per-dog training-milestone tracker moving each dog through foundational obedience, task-specific training, and public-access proofing with a "cleared to place" gate blocking any placement until every stage is documented complete; a Public Access Test results log tied to each handler-and-dog team with a re-test reminder for any team that doesn't pass on the first attempt; and an ADI/IGDF accreditation renewal tracker with a breeding-dog OFA health-clearance log feeding the program's next site review.

**Skill fit:** ADI's accreditation standards and the Public Access Test's standardized evaluation criteria are public, structured accrediting-body data with a concrete per-dog and per-team record requirement, and the multi-stage "cleared to place" gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a service-dog-training niche entirely distinct from the commercial breeder and Class C traveling exhibitor ideas already in this repo, neither of which tracks a disability-task-training pipeline or a public-access certification at all.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the per-dog training-milestone tracker with the placement gate, the Public Access Test log, and the ADI accreditation/OFA clearance tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent, non-hospital-affiliated assistance dog training organizations.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — Assistance Dogs International's own member-program directory and its regional conference reach exactly the small training organizations currently tracking training milestones and Public Access Test results on a training director's spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per organization depending on active-dog-pipeline size and puppy-raiser network scale. 22-28 organizations × $429/mo avg = $9,438-12,012/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every dog entering the pipeline opens its own 18-24 month training-milestone tracking obligation, every graduating team generates its own Public Access Test event, and the ADI/IGDF accreditation recurs on its own multi-year cycle for as long as the organization operates — a continuous, pipeline-and-placement-driven compliance stream tied directly to the organization's breeding and training volume.

**Biggest risk:** A very small organization placing only one or two dogs a year with a single trainer has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward organizations running a multi-dog pipeline and a volunteer puppy-raiser network, where the staggered training-and-testing calendar genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 organizations ($5.1K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 20 organizations ($8.6K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 22-28 organizations plus an ADI-site-review-ready compliance export organizations can produce ahead of their periodic accreditation renewal.

### 2. Vending Machine Operator FDA ACA §4205 Calorie-Labeling & Per-Location Food-Vending Permit Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent vending machine operators running a fleet of snack, beverage, and food machines across office buildings, schools, and public facilities must post calorie information for every food item once their nationwide machine count crosses 20 units, under Section 4205 of the Affordable Care Act (21 CFR 101.8) — a threshold-triggered federal labeling obligation that most small operators discover only after an FDA inspection flags a newly installed machine that pushed the fleet over the line, since the requirement applies to the operator's entire fleet the moment the 20-machine threshold is crossed, not machine by machine. Every machine dispensing a "potentially hazardous food" — a pre-made sandwich, a dairy cup, cut fruit — is also a retail food establishment under the local health department's adopted FDA Food Code, requiring its own per-location vending permit and a periodic health inspection, and a machine placed at a new location without first securing that location's permit is exactly the finding a routine health-department sweep is built to catch, with several states additionally requiring the machine's coin mechanism to be registered through the state weights-and-measures office. An operator running dozens to hundreds of machines across a scattered service territory manages calorie-label compliance status, per-location permit renewal, and perishable-product date rotation on a route driver's clipboard and a spreadsheet the office manager updates when they remember to, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific machine's calorie signage, its location's permit, and its perishable stock's dates are all actually current before that route stop is serviced.

**What to build:** A fleet-wide calorie-labeling compliance tracker flagging the operator's proximity to (or crossing of) the 20-machine ACA threshold and confirming every food item's calorie signage is posted; a per-location health-department food-vending permit renewal tracker; and a perishable-product date-rotation log for machines dispensing potentially hazardous food, with a restock alert before any item nears its sell-by date.

**Skill fit:** The FDA's ACA §4205 calorie-labeling threshold and local health departments' Food Code vending-permit requirements are public, structured federal and municipal regulatory data with a concrete fleet-size and per-location trigger, and the threshold-crossing calculator is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a vending-machine-operator niche this repo has never addressed and that shares no license type or per-unit record model with any restaurant, retail, or food-service idea already in this repo.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the fleet-wide calorie-labeling threshold tracker, the per-location permit renewal tracker, and the perishable-product date-rotation log. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent regional vending machine operators.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the National Automatic Merchandising Association's (NAMA) state and regional affiliate meetings reach exactly the small operators currently tracking calorie-label thresholds and per-location permits on a route driver's clipboard with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249-499/mo per operator depending on fleet size and number of jurisdictions served. 24-30 operators × $359/mo avg = $8,616-10,770/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new machine placed reopens the fleet-wide threshold calculation and that location's own permit question, every perishable restock generates its own date-tracking event, and an operator's own fleet growth is the single thing that eventually triggers the calorie-labeling obligation in the first place — a continuous, fleet-and-location-growth-driven compliance stream tied directly to the operator's route expansion.

**Biggest risk:** A very small operator running under 20 machines with no perishable-food units has no ACA labeling obligation at all. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward operators actively expanding their machine count or adding fresh-food micro-markets, where the threshold-and-permit calendar genuinely earns its keep.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 operators ($4.3K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 20 operators ($7.2K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 24-30 operators plus an FDA/health-department-inspection-ready compliance export operators can hand an inspector during a routine facility check.

### 3. Captive Cervid (Deer & Elk) Farm State Game-Farm License, USDA APHIS CWD Herd Certification & Fence-Integrity Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Captive cervid farms and ranches raising deer and elk for breeding stock, velvet antler, or shooting-preserve access hold a state agriculture or fish-and-wildlife game-farm/game-breeder license requiring double perimeter fencing to a specific height and escape-prevention design, and a fence found out of spec during a state inspection is exactly the finding that can suspend the farm's license regardless of the herd's own health. Every escape must be reported within a state-mandated window (commonly 24-72 hours), triggering a documented fence-repair record, and a farm that discovers a breach but delays reporting past the window is a separate, independently citable violation on top of the escape itself. Farms wanting to sell breeding stock or move animals across state lines must enroll in USDA APHIS's Voluntary CWD Herd Certification Program (HCP) — a minimum five-consecutive-year enrollment requiring mandatory chronic wasting disease testing of every mortality over 12 months old — and a single missed mortality test, or an undocumented escape, resets the herd's certification clock back to year one, destroying years of accumulated certified status a farm depends on to sell into other states at all. A farm running a herd across several fenced pastures manages fence-inspection records, per-mortality CWD test submission, and the herd's running HCP enrollment-year count on a rancher's paper log and a filing cabinet of lab results, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific mortality's test was actually submitted and the herd's certification year is actually intact before the next interstate sale is booked.

**What to build:** A per-mortality CWD test-submission tracker tied to the herd's running HCP enrollment-year count, flagging any missed test that would reset certification; a fence-inspection log with an escape-reporting countdown timer against the state's mandated notification window; and an interstate-movement eligibility checker cross-referencing the herd's current certification year against the destination state's minimum-year requirement before a sale is confirmed.

**Skill fit:** USDA APHIS's HCP enrollment-year rules and state game-farm fencing/escape-reporting requirements are public, structured federal and state regulatory data with a concrete per-mortality and per-incident trigger, and the certification-year tracker is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a live-animal captive-cervid-farming niche explicitly distinct from the post-mortem Deer & Big-Game Processor idea and the touring-itinerary Class C exhibitor idea already in this repo, neither of which holds a live, fixed-facility herd under a multi-year CWD certification clock.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the per-mortality CWD test tracker with the certification-year calculator, the fence-inspection/escape-reporting log, and the interstate-movement eligibility checker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent captive cervid farms and ranches.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the North American Deer Farmers Association (NADeFA) and state deer/elk farmer association directories reach exactly the small farm owners currently tracking mortality testing and HCP enrollment years on a paper log with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249-499/mo per farm depending on herd size and number of destination states served. 24-30 farms × $359/mo avg = $8,616-10,770/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every mortality over 12 months old generates its own CWD test-submission event for as long as the herd is enrolled, every fence inspection recurs on its own periodic cycle, and every interstate sale reopens the certification-year eligibility question — a continuous, herd-and-sales-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the farm's breeding-stock business.

**Biggest risk:** A farm that's already had an undocumented escape or a missed mortality test has an underlying certification-clock reset no tracking tool can retroactively undo. Mitigate by positioning the product as a forward-looking certification-protection aid and by qualifying leads toward farms actively building toward interstate sales, where the tracking genuinely earns its keep.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 farms ($4.3K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 20 farms ($7.2K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 24-30 farms plus a state-inspection-ready compliance export farms can hand an inspector during a routine game-farm license review.

### 4. Industrialized/Modular Building Manufacturer State Modular-Program Registration, In-Plant Inspection & Insignia-Issuance Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent modular building manufacturers producing portable classrooms, multi-family housing modules, and construction-site offices build to the same state-adopted IBC or IRC code as a site-built structure, but off-site, under a state's own modular-building program — and because each destination state runs its own separate program with its own approved third-party inspection-agency roster, a manufacturer selling into several states must register the plant separately in each one, a registration that lapses independently per state regardless of how current the others are. Every module must pass in-plant inspection from the state-approved third-party inspection agency at defined construction stages — framing, MEP rough-in, insulation, final — before a state insignia or data plate is affixed certifying that specific module's code compliance, and a module shipped without its insignia is legally unsellable and unpermittable at the destination site the moment it arrives, a discovery that strands a finished module and its buyer's project schedule simultaneously. Because different states' programs recognize different (and sometimes non-overlapping) rosters of approved inspection agencies, a manufacturer has to track which specific agency is authorized to inspect modules destined for which specific state, and a module inspected by an agency not approved for its destination state produces an insignia the receiving state's building department will reject outright. A manufacturer running several production lines shipping into multiple states manages per-state program registration, per-module inspection-stage scheduling, and insignia issuance on a production manager's spreadsheet and a filing cabinet of state approval letters, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific module's inspecting agency is actually approved for its destination state before that module ships.

**What to build:** A per-state modular-program registration renewal tracker with a state-by-state approval-status dashboard; a per-module in-plant inspection-stage scheduler tied to the assigned third-party agency, flagging any module proceeding to the next construction stage before its current stage's inspection is signed off; and an insignia-issuance log cross-referencing each module's inspecting agency against its destination state's approved-agency roster before shipment is cleared.

**Skill fit:** State modular-building program registration rules and third-party inspection-agency approval rosters are public, structured state regulatory data with a concrete per-state and per-module record requirement, and the destination-state/agency cross-check is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an off-site-construction niche explicitly distinct from the federal HUD-Code manufactured/mobile home installer idea already in this repo, which answers to a single nationwide federal label rather than a state-by-state program-and-agency structure.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the per-state program registration tracker, the per-module inspection-stage scheduler, and the insignia-issuance/destination-state cross-check. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent modular building manufacturers.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Modular Building Institute's (MBI) manufacturer-member directory and its annual World of Modular conference reach exactly the small manufacturers currently reconciling per-state program registration and inspection-agency approval on a production manager's spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance system.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new state a manufacturer expands into reopens its own separate program-registration-and-agency-approval question from scratch, every module produced generates its own multi-stage inspection-and-insignia event regardless of how routine the design, and every production-line expansion multiplies the number of simultaneously tracked modules — a continuous, state-expansion-and-production-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the manufacturer's growth.

**Biggest risk:** A manufacturer selling only within its home state under a single program has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward manufacturers actively expanding into multiple states' modular programs, where the per-state-and-per-module tracking load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10 manufacturers ($5.5K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 16 manufacturers ($8.8K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 18-24 manufacturers plus a destination-state-building-department-ready compliance export manufacturers can attach directly to a shipping manifest.

### 5. Independent Crop Insurance Loss Adjuster USDA RMA Certification, Crop-Program Currency & Appraisal-Deadline Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent crop insurance loss adjusters working claims for Approved Insurance Providers (AIPs) under USDA's Risk Management Agency (RMA) and the Federal Crop Insurance Program must complete RMA's basic certification training and pass program-specific modules — Actual Production History/yield-based coverage, Revenue Protection, named-peril policies — before they're "cleared to adjust" claims for that specific crop program in a given crop year, with mandatory annual continuing-education recertification required just to keep adjusting at all; an adjuster who lets a crop-program module lapse and adjusts a claim anyway produces a determination the AIP's own quality-review process can invalidate, potentially unwinding a payment already made to the farmer. Every claim also runs against strict, crop-stage-specific procedural deadlines with no analog in a property claim — a pre-harvest appraisal that must be scheduled and completed within a narrow window tied to the specific crop's growth stage (an appraisal done too early misses evidence of the loss, one done too late after harvest destroys the evidence entirely), and a Good Farming Practices determination that must be documented before any yield-shortfall payment can be approved — and a missed appraisal window is exactly the finding an RMA compliance review or a farmer's own appeal is built to catch. Actual Production History records must also reconcile against the farm's own reported acreage before a claim can close, and a discrepancy discovered only at final reconciliation is a delayed payment during exactly the season a farmer is counting on cash flow. An adjuster running a full harvest-season caseload across multiple crop programs and AIPs manages per-crop-program certification currency, per-claim appraisal-deadline scheduling, and APH reconciliation on a paper claim file and a spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific claim's crop-stage deadline and the adjuster's own program certification are both actually current before that appraisal is scheduled.

**What to build:** A per-crop-program RMA certification and annual CE-currency tracker with a "cleared to adjust" gate blocking any claim assignment outside the adjuster's current certification; a per-claim appraisal-deadline calculator computing the pre-harvest inspection window from the specific crop's growth stage and flagging any claim approaching its window; and an Actual Production History reconciliation tracker flagging any claim whose reported acreage doesn't match the farm's APH record before final submission.

**Skill fit:** RMA's crop-program certification requirements and the growth-stage-driven appraisal-deadline structure are public, numerically precise federal regulatory data with concrete per-adjuster and per-claim triggers, and the "cleared to adjust" gate plus the appraisal-deadline calculator are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a federal-crop-insurance niche entirely distinct from every state-DOI property/catastrophe/public adjuster idea already in this repo, none of which registers with USDA's Risk Management Agency or tracks a crop-growth-stage appraisal deadline at all.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the crop-program certification gate, the appraisal-deadline calculator, and the APH reconciliation tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent crop insurance loss adjusters working a multi-state harvest-season caseload.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — National Crop Insurance Services' (NCIS) adjuster-training network and state Farm Bureau crop-insurance-adjuster affiliate meetings reach exactly the small independent adjusters currently tracking certification currency and appraisal deadlines on a paper claim file with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per adjuster depending on caseload volume and number of crop programs certified. 22-28 adjusters × $429/mo avg = $9,438-12,012/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every crop program's certification renews on its own independent annual CE cycle for as long as the adjuster works it, every claim opens its own crop-stage-driven appraisal-deadline event regardless of how routine the farm, and every harvest season generates a fresh caseload multiplying the number of simultaneously tracked deadlines — a continuous, caseload-and-certification-driven compliance stream tied directly to the adjuster's crop-year workload.

**Biggest risk:** An adjuster working only one crop program in a single state with a small, stable caseload has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward adjusters certified across multiple crop programs and working several AIPs during a compressed harvest-season surge, where the staggered deadline-and-certification calendar genuinely outgrows a paper file.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 adjusters ($5.1K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 20 adjusters ($8.6K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 22-28 adjusters plus an RMA-audit-ready compliance export adjusters can produce during a routine quality-control review.
