### 1. Film & Television Animal Wrangler/Trainer AHA "No Animals Were Harmed" Notification, USDA APHIS Licensing & On-Set Safety Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent animal wranglers and trainers who supply and handle animals for film and television productions operate under a dual welfare-and-licensing framework most small operations track from memory. Every production using an animal in filmed action must be advance-notified to the American Humane Association under its "Guidelines for the Safe Use of Animals in Filmed Media," which classifies each scripted scene against a defined risk scale and, for higher-risk action (fire, water, gunfire, stunt work near animals), requires a certified AHA Safety Representative physically present on set to monitor compliance and file a post-production report — a production that runs a qualifying scene without that documented notification and monitoring is exactly the finding that costs the production its "No Animals Were Harmed" end-credit disclaimer, a reputational and contractual failure the wrangler is blamed for regardless of fault. Separately, most commercial animal use on a production counts as public exhibition under the Animal Welfare Act, requiring the wrangler to hold a current USDA APHIS Class C Exhibitor license with its own facility-inspection cycle and veterinary-care program, and any animal transported to an out-of-state shoot location must carry a Certificate of Veterinary Inspection current within that destination state's specific window — a wrangler who arrives on location with an animal whose CVI has lapsed, or whose APHIS license is under suspension, faces an immediate production shutdown of every scene involving that animal. Individual species carry their own state exotic-animal-possession permit requirements that vary sharply by state, and a wrangler booking work across several states must confirm a specific species is even legal to transport into that state before accepting the job. A small wrangling operation running a roster of a dozen or more trained animals across several concurrent productions currently tracks AHA per-production notification status, per-animal APHIS license and CVI currency, and state-by-state exotic-permit eligibility on a production coordinator's spreadsheet and a folder of vet certificates carried in the truck, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific animal is cleared to film in today's scene and today's state before the cameras roll.

**What to build:** A per-production AHA notification and scene-risk classification tracker with a "cleared to film this scene" gate blocking a qualifying scene from being scheduled until Safety Representative assignment is confirmed; a per-animal USDA APHIS Class C exhibitor license and per-state CVI countdown tracker; and a state-by-state exotic-animal-possession permit matrix flagging which states a specific species can legally be transported into and used for filming.

**Skill fit:** AHA's published Guidelines classify scenes against a defined, structured risk scale, and APHIS's exhibitor-licensing and CVI-currency rules are public regulatory data with a concrete per-production and per-animal trigger — the same deterministic "block the action until the record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a production-animal-welfare niche this repo has never addressed.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the AHA notification/scene-classification tracker, the per-animal APHIS/CVI tracker, and the state exotic-permit matrix. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent working animal wranglers and trainers.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — regional animal-actor and exotic-animal-trainer trade networks and AHA's own registered production-animal-safety-representative roster reach exactly the working wranglers currently tracking AHA notification and APHIS/CVI currency on a production coordinator's spreadsheet with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249/mo base + $20/mo per animal in active roster. Average client (12 animals) = $249 + $240 = $489/mo. 20-25 wranglers × $489/mo = **$9,780-12,225/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new production booking reopens the AHA notification and scene-risk classification, every animal added to a growing roster reopens the APHIS/CVI tracking, and every new shoot state reopens the exotic-permit eligibility check — a continuous, booking-and-roster-driven compliance stream.

**Biggest risk:** The total number of working production-animal wranglers nationally is a small, concentrated market. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward wranglers running multiple concurrent bookings and larger multi-species rosters supplying several productions at once, where the cross-check load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10 wranglers ($4.9K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 20-25 wranglers ($9.8-12.2K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding a production-insurer-facing safety-compliance export a wrangler can hand a completion-bond company during underwriting.

### 2. On-Farm Anaerobic Digester & Biogas Facility Operator Nutrient Management, RNG Pipeline-Quality & EPA RFS RIN-Generation Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Dairy and livestock farms running anaerobic digesters to convert manure and co-digestion feedstock into biogas operate under a layered environmental and energy-market compliance framework most small operators track from memory. The digestate byproduct spread on farm fields must follow a Nutrient Management Plan governing per-field application rates, timing restrictions (no spreading on frozen or saturated ground), and buffer zones near waterways — a field spread outside its plan's window or rate is exactly the finding a state environmental agency inspection is built to catch, and repeated violations threaten the farm's own water-discharge permit. Facilities that upgrade raw biogas to pipeline-quality renewable natural gas (RNG) for injection must meet the receiving pipeline operator's own gas-quality tariff specification — methane content, moisture, siloxanes, and hydrogen sulfide thresholds verified through periodic interconnection testing — and gas delivered out of spec can be rejected at the interconnection point, stranding a full day's production. Facilities registered under EPA's Renewable Fuel Standard program to generate D3 or D5 RIN credits must additionally file a per-quarter RIN-generation report reconciling actual metered gas production against RINs generated and sold; over-generating RINs relative to verified production is a federal violation carrying significant per-RIN penalties, not a mere paperwork lapse. A small digester operator — a single farm-based system or an independent developer managing several farm digesters — currently tracks per-field nutrient management windows, RNG interconnection test results, and RIN-generation reconciliation on a farm manager's spreadsheet, with no systematic alert before a specific field's application window closes or a quarterly RIN report deadline arrives.

**What to build:** A digestate land-application tracker per field computing the Nutrient Management Plan's allowed application windows and rates, blocking a spread event scheduled outside the compliant window; an RNG interconnection quality-test log tracking gas composition against the pipeline's tariff specification with an out-of-spec alert; and an EPA RFS RIN-generation reconciliation tracker computing verified gas production against RINs generated and reported each quarter with a hard block on over-generation.

**Skill fit:** Nutrient management plan rules, pipeline gas-quality tariffs, and EPA's RFS RIN-generation reporting structure are public, structured regulatory and contractual data with a concrete per-field and per-quarter trigger, and the reconciliation-and-gate pattern is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a farm-based biogas niche this repo has never addressed.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the digestate land-application tracker, the RNG interconnection quality-test log, and the RIN-generation reconciliation tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent digester developers and dairy cooperatives running shared digesters.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — the American Biogas Council member directory and EPA AgSTAR's project list reach exactly the small digester operators currently reconciling nutrient management and RIN generation on a farm manager's spreadsheet with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $349/mo base + $30/mo per field tracked for digestate land application. Average client (10 fields) = $349 + $300 = $649/mo. 18-22 operators × $649/mo = **$11,682-14,278/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every application season reopens the digestate land-application tracking from scratch, every quarter reopens the RIN-generation reconciliation, and RNG interconnection testing recurs on its own contractual cycle independent of production volume — a continuous, field-and-quarter-driven compliance stream.

**Biggest risk:** The total number of on-farm digesters is still a small, though rapidly growing, market as state RNG incentive programs expand. Mitigate by targeting multi-farm digester developers and operators who manage several sites' compliance centrally, where the cross-site tracking load is highest and most valuable.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10 operators ($6.5K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 18-22 operators ($11.7-14.3K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding a pipeline-operator-facing interconnection compliance export a facility can produce the moment a quality dispute arises.

### 3. Precast/Prestressed Concrete Manufacturer PCI Plant Certification & State DOT Approved-Fabricator Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Precast and prestressed concrete manufacturers producing structural components — bridge girders, box culverts, parking-structure panels, architectural wall panels — for public infrastructure and commercial construction must maintain Precast/Prestressed Concrete Institute (PCI) plant certification, a program-specific credential (Group A bridge products, Group C architectural, and others) requiring unannounced semi-annual plant audits covering mix-design documentation, prestressing-strand tensioning records, curing-cycle logs, and ACI-certified QC personnel currency — a plant whose certification lapses, even briefly, is immediately ineligible to supply product on any state DOT project requiring PCI-certified fabricators, which is most public bridge work, cutting off its highest-margin revenue line overnight. Separately, each state DOT maintains its own approved-fabricator list requiring periodic resubmission of qualification documentation (current PCI certificate, QC manual, personnel roster), and an out-of-date submission silently drops the plant from a state's bidder list mid-project without the plant necessarily noticing until a bid is rejected. Every individual precast unit poured also carries its own concrete-cylinder-break strength test and prestressing-strand release-strength verification that must clear before that specific unit can ship — a unit shipped without documented release-strength meeting the design minimum is a structural liability the manufacturer bears if a failure occurs after installation, exactly the finding a post-incident forensic review targets first. A small precast plant running continuous daily pours across several product lines currently tracks PCI audit-cycle currency, state DOT resubmission deadlines, and per-unit cylinder-break/release-strength documentation on a QC manager's spreadsheet and a filing cabinet of test reports, with no systematic gate confirming a specific unit's strength documentation is complete before it's marked ready to ship.

**What to build:** A PCI certification audit-cycle tracker (per plant, per program group) with a "cleared to supply DOT work" gate; a state DOT approved-fabricator-list resubmission tracker across every state the plant bids into; and a per-unit concrete-cylinder-break and prestressing-strand release-strength test log blocking a unit from being marked shippable until its required strength documentation is on file.

**Skill fit:** PCI's certification-audit cycle and state DOT approved-fabricator resubmission requirements are public, structured standards-body and regulatory data with a concrete per-plant and per-unit trigger, and the shippable-unit gate is the same deterministic "block the action until the record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a structural-precast manufacturing niche entirely distinct from the ready-mix concrete producer and structural steel fabricator ideas already in this repo, neither of which involves a PCI plant-certification audit cycle, a prestressing-strand release-strength record, or a per-unit shippability gate tied to a cast structural component.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the PCI audit-cycle tracker, the state DOT resubmission tracker, and the per-unit strength-documentation log. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent precast/prestressed plants.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — PCI's own certified-plant directory and the National Precast Concrete Association (NPCA) member network reach exactly the small plants currently tracking audit cycles and per-unit strength documentation on a QC spreadsheet with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $349/mo base + $8/mo per unit poured per month tracked. Average client (40 units/mo) = $349 + $320 = $669/mo. 16-20 plants × $669/mo = **$10,704-13,380/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every pour reopens the per-unit strength-documentation record, every semi-annual audit cycle reopens PCI certification currency, and every new state bid reopens the DOT approved-fabricator resubmission requirement — a continuous, production-volume-driven compliance stream.

**Biggest risk:** A very small plant producing only non-structural architectural precast with no DOT work has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward plants actively bidding bridge and structural DOT work, where PCI certification is a hard bid-eligibility requirement and a lapse is an existential threat to the plant's public-work revenue.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10 plants ($6.7K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 16-20 plants ($10.7-13.4K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding a bid-package-ready qualification export a plant can attach to every DOT bid submission.

### 4. Motorsports Racetrack & Drag Strip Operator NHRA/IHRA Sanctioning, SFI Safety-Equipment & Tech-Inspection Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent drag strips and short-track motorsports facilities running sanctioned events under NHRA or IHRA membership must maintain the sanctioning body's own facility-safety certification — barrier and wall specifications, timing-system certification, and an emergency-response plan reviewed on the sanctioning body's own renewal cycle — and a facility whose sanctioning lapses can't legally sanction points-paying events, an immediate loss of the racer turnout that drives gate revenue. Every competing vehicle above a given elapsed-time or speed threshold must pass a per-event technical ("tech") inspection confirming its SFI Foundation-rated safety equipment — roll cage, racing harness, fuel cell, driver suit — is current and correctly rated for that vehicle's performance class; SFI-rated items carry their own item-specific expiration dates (commonly 2, 3, or 5 years depending on the component), and a car waved to the line with an expired SFI-rated harness or roll cage is exactly the finding a post-incident sanctioning-body investigation is built to catch, one that can suspend the track's own sanctioning regardless of who's at fault. Every driver must also hold a current sanctioning-body competition license matched to their vehicle's performance class, with a graduated-licensing requirement — a documented number of supervised passes — before a driver may run a faster class. A small independent track running weekly test-and-tune nights alongside monthly sanctioned points events currently tracks facility sanctioning-certification renewal, per-racer SFI-equipment expiration dates, and per-driver competition-license class eligibility on a tech inspector's clipboard and a paper log at the tower, with no systematic gate confirming a specific car's SFI-rated equipment is current and its driver is licensed for that class before it's waved to the line.

**What to build:** A facility sanctioning-certification renewal tracker (barrier spec, timing-system cert, emergency-response plan) with a "cleared to sanction events" gate; a per-racer SFI-rated safety-equipment expiration tracker (roll cage, harness, fuel cell, suit) with a "cleared to stage" gate blocking a car from the line until every required item is current; and a per-driver competition-license class-eligibility tracker enforcing graduated-licensing pass requirements before a faster class is authorized.

**Skill fit:** NHRA/IHRA's published sanctioning requirements and SFI's per-item certification-expiration schedule are public, structured standards-body data with a concrete per-facility, per-racer, and per-item trigger, and the stage-eligibility gate is the same deterministic "block the action until the record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a competitive-motorsports niche entirely distinct from the Go-Kart Track & Family Entertainment Center idea already in this repo (Session 90), whose NAARSO/ASTM F2007 mechanical-ride inspection governs a permanently installed powered amusement ride for the general public, not a sanctioned competition facility's own barrier certification, SFI equipment-expiration tracking, or graduated racer-licensing structure.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the facility sanctioning tracker, the per-racer SFI-equipment tracker, and the per-driver license class-eligibility tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent NHRA/IHRA member tracks.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — NHRA's Member Track program directory and IHRA's affiliated-track network reach exactly the small independent strips currently tracking SFI equipment expiration and sanctioning renewal on a tech inspector's clipboard with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299/mo base + $2/mo per active competitor license tracked. Average client (200 competitors) = $299 + $400 = $699/mo. 15-19 tracks × $699/mo = **$10,485-13,281/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every event weekend reopens the per-racer SFI-equipment and license-eligibility check, every driver's SFI-rated gear expires on its own independent item-specific clock, and the facility's own sanctioning certification renews on its own cycle — a continuous, event-and-roster-driven compliance stream.

**Biggest risk:** A very small bracket-racing-only track running low-speed classes with no SFI-equipment threshold has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward tracks running a full sanctioned points series with a real competitor roster crossing SFI-mandatory performance thresholds.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10 tracks ($7K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 15-19 tracks ($10.5-13.3K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding a sanctioning-body-facing post-incident compliance export a track can produce the moment an investigation is opened.

### 5. Outdoor Sporting Clays, Trap & Skeet Range Operator EPA Lead-Management BMP & NSCA/NSSA Range-Certification Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Outdoor shotgun-sports facilities — trap, skeet, and sporting clays courses — disperse tons of lead shot across range grounds every season, placing them under EPA's Best Management Practices for lead management at outdoor shooting ranges: a written Environmental Stewardship Plan, periodic soil-lead testing at defined intervals, and documented lead-reclamation events (berm reclamation, shot recovery) intended to keep lead from migrating into groundwater or adjacent waterways before it triggers a Clean Water Act or CERCLA enforcement action — a range with no documented reclamation history is exactly the finding an EPA regional office or state environmental agency site visit targets, and a contamination finding can force a range closure that ends the business outright. Separately, ranges hosting National Sporting Clays Association (NSCA) or National Skeet Shooting Association (NSSA)-sanctioned tournaments must maintain the sanctioning body's own facility-safety certification — shot-fall zone and buffer distances, trap-house and tower spacing — reviewed on that body's own renewal cycle, and every certified range instructor running a squad must hold a current NSCA or NSSA instructor certification renewed on its own continuing-education cycle; an instructor running a squad on a lapsed certification is exactly the gap a sanctioning-body compliance review is built to catch. **What to build:** An Environmental Stewardship Plan tracker computing each testing zone's soil-lead sampling interval with an overdue alert; a lead-reclamation event log documenting berm and shot-fall-zone reclamation history, exportable for an EPA or state environmental agency site visit; and a per-instructor NSCA/NSSA certification renewal tracker with a "cleared to run a squad" gate.

**Skill fit:** EPA's published Best Management Practices for lead at outdoor shooting ranges and NSCA/NSSA's certification-renewal structure are public, structured regulatory and standards-body data with a concrete per-zone and per-instructor trigger, and the reclamation-documentation-and-instructor-gate pattern is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an outdoor shotgun-sports niche entirely distinct from the Indoor Shooting Range idea already in this repo (Session 54), whose OSHA occupational lead-exposure standard governs employee blood-lead medical surveillance and indoor ventilation-system certification for a range with no soil, groundwater, or EPA environmental-lead-management component at all, since an indoor range's lead hazard is airborne particulate captured by ventilation, not lead shot dispersed across open range grounds.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the Environmental Stewardship Plan tracker, the lead-reclamation event log, and the per-instructor certification tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent sporting clays and trap/skeet facilities.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the NSCA and NSSA facility-member directories reach exactly the small independent ranges currently tracking soil-lead testing and reclamation events on a paper binder with no dedicated system.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every season reopens the soil-lead testing interval and reclamation-documentation cycle, every sanctioned tournament reopens the facility-certification and per-instructor eligibility check, and a growing range's own instructor roster compounds the number of simultaneously tracked NSCA/NSSA credentials.

**Biggest risk:** A very small range with only casual walk-in shooting and no sanctioned tournaments has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward ranges running a full NSCA/NSSA-sanctioned tournament calendar with multiple certified instructors, where the environmental-stewardship and instructor-certification tracking genuinely outgrows a paper binder.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 ranges ($5.5K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 28-35 ranges ($10.3-12.9K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding an EPA-site-visit-ready reclamation-history export a range can hand an inspector on request.
