### 1. Cooling Tower / Evaporative Water System Legionella Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Cooling towers, evaporative condensers, and decorative fountains in hotels, hospitals, office towers, and industrial plants are the leading source of Legionnaires' disease outbreaks. ASHRAE Standard 188 (2018) is the nationally adopted baseline requiring a written Water Management Program (WMP), and CMS enforces it indirectly by requiring every Medicare/Medicaid-certified healthcare facility to have a WMP or face survey citation (CMS QSO-17-30). At the city level, NYC Local Law 77/78 requires every cooling tower to be registered with the Department of Health, tested for Legionella via culture sample every 90 days, inspected twice a year by a Certified Cooling Tower Inspector, and disinfected/documented if any sample exceeds 1,000 CFU/mL (mandatory remediation and city notification above 10,000 CFU/mL). New Jersey, Illinois, and several other states/counties have adopted similar registration-and-testing ordinances. Small independent water treatment companies that service the mechanical systems of dozens to hundreds of buildings have no dedicated system for tracking which properties are registered, when the next culture sample is due, whether a result crossed an action threshold requiring notification, and whether the WMP document has had its required annual review.
**What to build:** A property/tower portfolio tracker (address, tower ID, city registration number) with a 90-day culture-sample calendar, a CFU/mL result log with automatic threshold flagging (amber at 1,000 CFU/mL, red at 10,000 CFU/mL triggering a remediation-and-notification workflow), a WMP document repository with an annual-review reminder, inspection-report uploads tied to the biannual Certified Cooling Tower Inspector visit, and a client-facing dashboard the building's facility manager can view.
**Skill fit:** This is a rules-and-thresholds monitoring system — exactly the kind of numeric-trigger workflow a data-oriented developer builds cleanly — layered on public, scrapable ordinance text (ASHRAE 188, NYC Local Law 77/78) that needs to stay current across jurisdictions.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends: portfolio CRUD, 90-day sample calendar, CFU threshold alerting, WMP document store, PDF compliance report generator per building. Sell to independent water treatment companies (not the majors) rather than building owners directly, since one water-treatment-company account covers their entire building portfolio at once. First 5-8 clients via the Association of Water Technologies (AWT) member directory and its annual convention.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — direct outreach to independent regional water treatment company owners listed in the AWT member directory.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $70-79/property/mo. A mid-size independent water treatment company with 100-200 buildings in its service portfolio buys a portfolio license: 150 properties × $75/mo = $11,250/mo from a single anchor account. Two such accounts realistically land inside 12-18 months = $18-22K/mo aggregate gross; even one solid account plus 3-4 smaller independents at $99/mo flat (<20 properties each) comfortably clears $12-14K/mo.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** The 90-day culture-testing cycle plus annual registration renewal and threshold-triggered remediation notifications create continuous, mandatory, non-optional usage tied directly to a public-health liability the client cannot ignore.
**Biggest risk:** A missed >10,000 CFU/mL notification deadline is a real outbreak-liability event for the client — mitigated by redundant email+SMS alerting and an exportable audit log the water treatment company can hand to regulators or its insurer as proof of due diligence.
**Growth path:** Side project → first 2 independent water-treatment-company accounts → replaces-part-time-income → 3-4 mid-size accounts nationwide → replaces-full-time-salary.

### 2. Weather Modification / Cloud Seeding Operator Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Weather modification — cloud seeding for drought relief, hail suppression, or snowpack augmentation — is licensed at the state level in roughly a dozen states with active programs, most notably Texas (Texas Weather Modification Act, administered by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation under 16 TAC Chapter 78) and North Dakota (Atmospheric Resource Board under NDCC Chapter 61-04), with additional statutes in Colorado, Idaho, Utah, Wyoming, California, and Nevada. Federally, the Weather Modification Reporting Act (15 U.S.C. §330) requires every operator to file a pre-activity and post-activity report with NOAA for each individual seeding operation. Small operators (typically 2-10 people, flying aircraft or running ground-based silver-iodide generators for agricultural cooperatives, ski resorts, and water districts) must simultaneously track per-state license renewal, per-project state permits (often annual, per target area), the NOAA activity report for every single seeding event during a multi-month season, silver iodide usage totals for environmental reporting, and insurance/bonding certificates — with no dedicated software existing for this narrow niche.
**What to build:** A license/permit portfolio tracker across every state of operation, a per-project permit renewal calendar, an event-logging module where each seeding flight or generator run auto-populates a NOAA pre/post-activity report draft with a filing-deadline countdown, a silver iodide usage ledger for annual environmental reporting, an insurance/bond expiration tracker, and a client-facing project dashboard for the funding water district or agricultural cooperative.
**Skill fit:** The NOAA per-event reporting requirement is genuinely obscure and involves exactly the kind of structured, repeatable data-entry-to-filing pipeline a software engineer builds well; there is no existing competitor because the addressable market is too small for a venture-funded company to bother with, but not too small for a solo operator.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends: state license/permit tracker, per-event logging form with auto-drafted NOAA report, deadline calendar. Given there are only ~30-40 active commercial weather modification operators nationally, target the first 3-5 clients directly.
**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — direct outreach via the Weather Modification Association (WMA) member roster and its annual conference, where a founder can personally reach nearly every operator in the country.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $250-400/mo per operator base tier, plus a $100-150/mo environmental-reporting add-on (compiling annual silver iodide totals for state agencies). Capturing 20 of the ~30-40 total US operators at an average $475/mo (base + add-on) = $9,500/mo.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every seeding event during a multi-month season (often dozens per operator) requires a fresh NOAA filing, and both state licenses and per-project permits renew annually — the compliance workload is continuous and directly gates whether the operator can legally fly that day.
**Biggest risk:** The total addressable market (~30-40 US operators) genuinely caps this near the low end of the target range — mitigated by expanding to international programs with similar reporting regimes (parts of Australia and the UAE) or bundling adjacent agricultural-aviation compliance once the core product is proven.
**Growth path:** Side project → first 5 WMA-member clients → replaces-part-time-income → 20 operators + environmental add-on → approaches-full-time-income, with international expansion needed to fully clear $15K/mo.

### 3. Flood Zone Determination Company Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Under the Flood Disaster Protection Act and its implementing regulations at 12 CFR 339 (federal banking agencies) and 12 CFR 1024 (RESPA), every federally regulated mortgage lender must obtain a Standard Flood Hazard Determination (FEMA Form 086-0-32) before closing a loan, and must continue monitoring that determination for the life of the loan — because a FEMA map revision (a LOMA or LOMR) can newly place a property inside a Special Flood Hazard Area and trigger a mandatory flood-insurance-purchase notice to the borrower. Small, independent flood zone determination companies — B2B vendors distinct from insurance agencies, selling determination certificates and life-of-loan monitoring subscriptions directly to community banks and credit unions that can't justify building their own FEMA map-pull infrastructure — must track loan-level determination-certificate delivery deadlines (required before every closing), monitor FEMA National Flood Hazard Layer (NFHL) panel updates across every county they cover, and manage life-of-loan monitoring renewals per lender contract.
**What to build:** A lender/loan-portfolio tracker with loan-level SFHD status, an ingestion pipeline for FEMA NFHL map-panel effective-date changes that auto-flags any monitored loan in a revised panel, a life-of-loan monitoring subscription renewal tracker per lender contract, a determination-certificate delivery SLA countdown for loans in the active closing pipeline, and a LOMA/appeal workflow tracker.
**Skill fit:** This is fundamentally a data-pipeline business — ingesting public FEMA GIS map data and matching it against a client's address portfolio is a data-engineering problem, and re-checking flood status automatically on every map revision is exactly the kind of recurring-check system a software engineer builds cleanly.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends: CSV loan-portfolio import, FEMA NFHL panel-change ingestion, per-loan revision flagging, SFHD certificate PDF generator. First 5-8 clients from small community banks and credit unions, most of which already outsource this function and are price-sensitive, reached via Independent Community Bankers of America (ICBA) state-association directories and local chapter meetings.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — ICBA state association member lists name compliance officers directly; a cold email offering cheaper determination-plus-monitoring than the client's current vendor converts quickly because the service is already a recognized, budgeted line item.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $10-15 per new-loan determination plus $2-3/mo per loan for life-of-loan monitoring. A community bank with 3,000 active mortgages × $2.50/mo monitoring = $7,500/mo, plus ~50 new loans/mo × $12 = $600/mo — roughly $8,100/mo from one mid-size bank client. Three to four smaller bank clients (500-1,500 loan portfolios each) realistically combine to $9-12K/mo within the first year.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Federal law requires this determination on every single mortgage closing nationally, and life-of-loan monitoring runs for the full 15-30 year term of each loan — one of the few truly non-optional, per-transaction, decades-long recurring compliance events in all of consumer banking.
**Biggest risk:** An incorrect flood-zone certification exposes the bank to regulatory fines or uninsured flood losses — mitigated by sourcing exclusively from FEMA's authoritative NFHL data with a documented, auditable methodology and E&O insurance from day one.
**Growth path:** Side project → first 3-4 community bank/credit union clients → replaces-part-time-income → 8-10 clients with compounding life-of-loan monitoring revenue → replaces-full-time-salary.

### 4. Risk Retention Group (RRG) Management Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Risk Retention Groups — member-owned liability insurance companies formed under the federal Liability Risk Retention Act of 1986 (15 U.S.C. §3901 et seq.) that let groups of similar businesses (trucking fleets, physician groups, contractors) self-insure and sell liability coverage across state lines under a single "domiciliary state" license — number roughly 200-250 active entities nationally, most of them thinly staffed. Each RRG must maintain full insurer licensure in its domicile state (exams, actuarial opinion filings) while separately filing notice-and-registration paperwork in every "non-domiciliary" state where it writes business — commonly 20-40 states per RRG — each with its own renewal date, required-document list (current Annual Statement copy, Certificate of Good Standing, biographical affidavits), premium tax filing, and registered-agent requirement. Missing a single state's annual renewal or premium tax filing can trigger a cease-and-desist order barring the RRG from writing new business in that state.
**What to build:** A multi-state registration matrix (one row per non-domiciliary state) with renewal date, document checklist, premium tax deadline and amount-due calculator, domicile-state exam/actuarial-opinion calendar, registered-agent expiration tracker, and a board-ready compliance dashboard.
**Skill fit:** Normalizing ~45 different state renewal cadences and document requirements into one portfolio view is a data-normalization problem well suited to a developer, built on public state insurance department registration data and forms that need periodic re-scraping to stay current.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends: state registration matrix covering the ~15 most common non-domiciliary states, renewal calendar, document checklist, premium tax calculator. Sell first to RRG "captive managers" — third-party firms that administer 5-8 RRGs each — rather than individual RRGs, since one captive-manager account covers their whole book at once.
**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — the National Risk Retention Association (NRRA) annual conference and member directory list nearly every active RRG and captive manager in the country, letting a founder personally reach the whole addressable market.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $300-500/mo per RRG, plus a $100/mo premium-tax-calculation add-on. Capturing 20-25 RRGs — whether directly or via 3-4 captive-manager accounts — at an average $475/mo = $9,500-12,000/mo.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every RRG's non-domiciliary registrations run in parallel across dozens of jurisdictions on independent annual cycles, and a captive manager administering multiple RRGs sees this compliance workload multiply — the product becomes more valuable, not less, as a client scales.
**Biggest risk:** The total market (~200-250 RRGs) caps organic growth — mitigated by selling first to captive managers (multiplying reach per sales conversation) and later extending the same state-registration-matrix engine to Purchasing Groups, a related entity type created under the same 1986 Act.
**Growth path:** Side project → first 2 captive-manager accounts (10-15 RRGs) → replaces-part-time-income → 4-5 captive-manager accounts plus direct RRG sales → replaces-full-time-salary.

### 5. Health Care Sharing Ministry (HCSM) Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Health care sharing ministries — faith-based cost-sharing organizations exempt from ACA-era insurance regulation under IRC §5000A(d)(2)(B) — have grown from roughly 200,000 to over 1.7 million members nationally over the past decade, and a growing list of states (Colorado, Texas, Louisiana, Missouri, Oklahoma, Virginia, and others) now require HCSMs to register with the state insurance department and include mandated disclosure language on every application ("this is not insurance," an itemized statement of what is and isn't shared, member-complaint contact information), with several states additionally requiring annual reporting of membership counts and dollar volume shared. Most of the 50+ HCSMs now operating — beyond the three large incumbents with in-house compliance staff — have no dedicated system for tracking per-state registration status as they expand into new states, disclosure-language version control across state-specific application forms, annual reporting deadlines, or the IRS §5000A eligibility criteria the organization itself must continuously satisfy to keep its members' tax-exempt status intact.
**What to build:** A state-by-state registration tracker flagging which states an HCSM's members reside in versus which states have active registration requirements, a versioned disclosure-language template library that alerts when a state amends its required text, an annual-report data-compilation module that ingests membership/sharing-volume data via CSV with a deadline countdown, and a §5000A eligibility documentation checklist.
**Skill fit:** HCSM regulation is a genuinely new, fast-changing state-by-state patchwork (several statutes passed only in the last 3-5 years); building a system that ingests legislative changes methodically fills a real gap, since no incumbent has built dedicated tooling for this niche yet.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends: state registration matrix covering the ~10 states with active HCSM statutes, disclosure-template library, annual-report deadline tracker with CSV import. First 5-8 clients from the newer/regional HCSMs (the three large incumbents likely already have in-house compliance staff).
**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — the Alliance of Health Care Sharing Ministries (AHCSM) member directory and annual meeting is a direct channel to nearly every organization in the category.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $400-600/mo per HCSM. Capturing 15-20 of the ~50-60 nationally addressable organizations at an average $500/mo = $7,500-10,000/mo, with more of the range reachable as membership growth continues to push HCSMs into newly-regulating states.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every state an HCSM expands into is a new registration-and-disclosure obligation, every year brings an annual reporting cycle, and every new state law requires immediate disclosure-language updates across all affected applications — a growing regulatory surface rather than a static one.
**Biggest risk:** Some incumbents may already handle this informally through outside counsel rather than dedicated software — mitigated by positioning the tool as materially cheaper than counsel for routine tracking and renewal work, reserving legal review for genuine interpretation questions.
**Growth path:** Side project → first 3-5 regional HCSM clients → replaces-part-time-income → 12-15 clients as more states regulate and the category grows → approaches-full-time-income.
