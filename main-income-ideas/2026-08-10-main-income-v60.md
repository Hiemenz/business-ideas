### 1. Credit Repair Organization CROA Compliance & State Bonding/Fee-Timing Tracker
**Problem:** Credit repair organizations (CROs) — firms retained by consumers to dispute inaccurate or unverifiable items on their credit reports with Experian, Equifax, and TransUnion — operate under the federal Credit Repair Organizations Act (CROA, 15 U.S.C. §1679 et seq.), which bans collecting any fee until the promised services have actually been fully performed, requires a written contract disclosing the consumer's rights and the specific services to be performed, and mandates a 3-business-day right-to-cancel period during which no fee may be collected at all. Most states layer their own credit services organization (CSO) statute on top, requiring state registration and a surety bond (commonly $10,000-$100,000) before a CRO can legally solicit clients in that state, with several states (Texas among them) imposing even stricter advance-fee restrictions than the federal baseline. The FTC and CFPB have both run repeated enforcement sweeps against credit repair companies specifically for collecting fees before completing work or misrepresenting likely dispute outcomes — among the most heavily litigated consumer-protection categories in financial services — and a CRO caught charging a client during the 3-day cancellation window, or before a dispute cycle actually resolved, is exactly the fact pattern these sweeps target. Separately, every dispute a CRO files with a bureau on a client's behalf triggers the bureau's own FCRA-mandated 30-day response clock, and a CRO managing hundreds of active client files across three bureaus needs to track each dispute's status to know when to escalate or refile. A small CRO running a handful of staff and hundreds of active client files manages fee-timing compliance, per-dispute 30-day cycle tracking across three bureaus, and multi-state bond/registration renewal with no dedicated compliance function — and a premature fee charge discovered during a state CSO audit or a client complaint is exactly the violation category that has shut down credit repair firms outright.
**What to build:** A fee-collection compliance gate blocking any charge to a client's account until the CROA-mandated 3-day cancellation window has elapsed and the required contract disclosure was confirmed delivered; a per-client, per-bureau dispute cycle tracker (dispute sent date, bureau, the FCRA 30-day response deadline, response received, item outcome — removed, verified, or updated) surfacing which disputes are approaching their response deadline without an update; and a multi-state CSO registration and surety bond renewal tracker for every state where the CRO has active clients.
**Skill fit:** CROA's advance-fee ban and state CSO bonding/registration statutes are public federal and state regulatory text, and the fee-collection gate plus the per-bureau 30-day dispute-cycle tracker are the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance condition is met / track the deadline from the trigger event" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a consumer-facing financial service most small CROs currently run on a generic CRM with no built-in fee-timing safeguard.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the fee-collection gate, the per-bureau dispute cycle tracker, and the multi-state bond/registration tracker; land the first 5-8 CROs through National Association of Credit Services Organizations (NACSO) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — National Association of Credit Services Organizations (NACSO) member directory outreach, whose member firms already navigate CROA's advance-fee ban and multi-state CSO bonding on a case-by-case, unsystematic basis.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $99/mo base + $2/mo per active client file tracked. Average client (a CRO managing 150 active client files) = $99 + $300 = $399/mo. 28-34 CROs × $399/mo = **$11,172-13,566/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new client enrollment opens a fresh fee-timing compliance event, every dispute filed against a bureau starts its own 30-day response clock, and state bond/registration renewals recur on their own cycle as the CRO expands into new states — a continuous, per-client compliance stream tied directly to caseload growth.
**Biggest risk:** Collecting a fee before the 3-day cancellation window elapses, or before a promised dispute cycle actually resolved, is precisely the fact pattern behind the FTC's and CFPB's most aggressive enforcement sweeps against credit repair companies — the fee-collection gate is the core protection.
**Growth path:** Side project → fee-gate, dispute tracker, and bond/registration tracker deployed, first 5-8 CRO retainers through NACSO outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 14-18 CRO retainers. Replaces-full-time-salary → 28-34 CRO retainers, pushing past $11.17-13.57K/mo.

### 2. Skydiving Dropzone USPA Instructor Currency, Reserve-Repack & Jump-Eligibility Compliance
**Problem:** USPA (United States Parachute Association) Group Member dropzones must maintain their Group Membership (annual renewal, minimum liability insurance) and ensure every AFF instructor, tandem instructor, and coach conducting student or tandem jumps holds a current USPA instructor rating — a rating that requires periodic instructional activity to remain "current" under USPA's Basic Safety Requirements, and an instructor whose currency lapses cannot legally supervise a student or tandem jump. Tandem instructors must separately hold a current manufacturer-specific tandem rating (UPT Sigma, Strong, and similar systems each issue their own certification, distinct from and in addition to the USPA rating) tied to the specific tandem system used. Every reserve parachute at the dropzone — on student rigs, tandem rigs, and any licensed jumper's rig serviced on-site — must be inspected, packed, and re-certified by an FAA-certificated Master or Senior Parachute Rigger within a hard 180-day cycle under 14 CFR §105.43; a reserve packed past that window is not legally airworthy, and jumping it is a direct FAA violation carrying severe liability exposure if a malfunction occurs during that jump. Every student and tandem passenger must sign a liability waiver before the jump — a document whose absence has repeatedly been the deciding factor in wrongful-death litigation against dropzones. A small dropzone running 8-20 instructors and riggers and dozens of tandem/student jumps a weekend manages staggered instructor rating currency, a rolling 180-day reserve-repack cycle across dozens of individual rigs, and per-jumper waiver documentation with no dedicated compliance function — and a tandem jump conducted by an instructor whose rating lapsed, or on a reserve packed past its 180-day window, is exactly the failure state behind the sport's most severe FAA findings and wrongful-death verdicts.
**What to build:** A per-instructor/rigger USPA rating and manufacturer-specific tandem certification tracker with a "cleared to jump" gate blocking a tandem or student-jump assignment the moment a rating lapses; a per-rig reserve-repack tracker computing each reserve's 180-day due date from its last pack date, with a hard block preventing a rig from being marked "available for use" once that window elapses; a per-jumper waiver log confirming a signed liability waiver is on file before a manifest entry can be finalized; and a USPA Group Membership and aircraft airworthiness renewal tracker.
**Skill fit:** USPA's Basic Safety Requirements, FAA Part 105, and the 180-day reserve-repack rule under 14 CFR §105.43 are public regulatory and association sources, and the reserve-repack due-date computation plus the jump-eligibility gate are the same deterministic "compute the deadline from the trigger event, block the action until it clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a sport-aviation niche with an unusually severe liability tail most dropzones currently track on a whiteboard.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the instructor/rigger currency tracker with the jump-eligibility gate, the reserve-repack tracker, and the waiver log; land the first 5-8 dropzones through USPA's public Group Member directory outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — USPA's public Group Member dropzone directory, a ready-made national outreach list of exactly the operators required to maintain this compliance record.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $12/mo per instructor/rigger tracked. Average client (a dropzone with 10 instructors/riggers) = $199 + $120 = $319/mo. 35-42 dropzones × $319/mo = **$11,165-13,398/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every reserve on-site recurs against its own independent 180-day repack clock, instructor ratings renew on staggered currency cycles tied to actual jump activity, and every tandem/student jump generates a fresh waiver event — a high-frequency, per-rig and per-instructor compliance stream tied directly to the dropzone's weekend jump volume.
**Biggest risk:** A tandem jump made by an instructor whose rating lapsed, or on a reserve packed past its 180-day window, is the exact fact pattern behind the sport's most severe FAA enforcement findings and wrongful-death litigation — the jump-eligibility gate and the reserve-repack block are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → currency tracker, reserve-repack tracker, and waiver log deployed, first 5-8 dropzone retainers through USPA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 16-20 dropzones. Replaces-full-time-salary → 35-42 dropzones, pushing past $11.17-13.4K/mo.

### 3. Residential/Commercial Solar Installer State License, NABCEP Certification & Utility PTO Sequencing Compliance
**Problem:** Residential and commercial solar installers must hold a state contractor or electrical specialty license (the specific classification and exam requirements vary by state), renewed on a 1-2 year cycle with continuing education. NABCEP (North American Board of Certified Energy Practitioners) PV Installation Professional certification, while voluntary in most states, is required as an eligibility condition by a growing number of state rebate/incentive programs and utility interconnection programs, and NABCEP certification runs on its own recertification cycle (continuing education hours every 3 years) entirely independent of the state contractor license's own renewal timeline. Every installed system must clear a defined permit-to-interconnection sequence before it can legally be turned on: a completed interconnection application filed with the utility, a witness test in some utility territories, and a Permission-to-Operate (PTO) authorization issued by the utility — a system energized before PTO clears is both a code violation and, in many utility territories, grounds for the utility denying net-metering credit retroactively, a direct financial loss to the homeowner that lands squarely on the installer's reputation. A small installer running several concurrent residential and commercial projects manages state license/NABCEP renewal and a permit-to-PTO sequencing tracker across multiple utility territories with no dedicated compliance function — and a system flipped on before PTO clears, or a NABCEP certification lapse discovered when a state rebate program audits its list of eligible installers, is exactly the gap that costs the installer both the client's rebate eligibility and the referral relationship.
**What to build:** A state contractor license and NABCEP certification tracker per technician with tiered renewal alerts; a per-project permit-to-PTO sequencing tracker (permit issued, inspection passed, interconnection application filed, utility witness test where required, PTO received) with a hard block preventing a project from being marked "cleared to energize" until PTO is confirmed on file; and a utility-territory reference tracking each served utility's specific interconnection requirements and typical PTO turnaround time.
**Skill fit:** State contractor licensing rules, NABCEP's certification/recertification structure, and utility interconnection processes are public regulatory and program sources, and the permit-to-PTO sequencing gate is the same deterministic "block the action until the required approval is confirmed" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a fast-growing trade most small installers currently track on a spreadsheet with no systematic PTO-confirmation step.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the license/NABCEP tracker, the permit-to-PTO sequencing tracker with the energize-eligibility gate, and the utility-territory reference; land the first 5-8 installers through Solar Energy Industries Association (SEIA) state chapter and NABCEP outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — NABCEP's public certificant directory and SEIA state chapter meetings, where small installer owners already discuss PTO-timing headaches and rebate-eligibility documentation gaps.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $175/mo base + $6/mo per active project tracked monthly. Average client (25 active projects/mo) = $175 + $150 = $325/mo. 34-40 installers × $325/mo = **$11,050-13,000/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new project opens its own permit-to-PTO sequence, NABCEP certification and state license renewals recur on independent cycles per technician, and a growing installer footprint across utility territories adds new interconnection requirements continuously — a per-project compliance stream tied directly to installation volume.
**Biggest risk:** Energizing a system before PTO clears voids net-metering eligibility retroactively in many utility territories — a direct financial loss to the homeowner the installer bears reputational responsibility for — the energize-eligibility gate is the core protection.
**Growth path:** Side project → license/NABCEP tracker and PTO sequencing gate deployed, first 5-8 installer retainers through SEIA/NABCEP outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 16-20 installers. Replaces-full-time-salary → 34-40 installers, pushing past $11.05-13K/mo.

### 4. Commercial Fishing Vessel State Permit, NOAA Catch Quota/VMS & USCG CFVS Decal Compliance
**Problem:** Commercial fishing vessel owners must hold a state commercial fishing license or permit (species- and gear-specific in most coastal states) and, for federally managed fisheries, a NOAA Fisheries permit tied to the vessel, renewed annually, with catch reported through mandatory logbooks or, in an increasing number of fisheries, a continuously transmitting Vessel Monitoring System (VMS) — a vessel fishing without current VMS transmission in a VMS-mandated fishery is itself a violation independent of any catch issue. Federal Individual Fishing Quota (IFQ) programs covering several major fisheries require the vessel to report landings against its specific allocated quota, and landing catch beyond the vessel's remaining allocation is a strict-liability violation triggering quota forfeiture and civil penalties regardless of intent. Separately, every commercial fishing vessel must pass a USCG Commercial Fishing Vessel Safety (CFVS) dockside exam under 46 CFR Subchapter C — covering survival craft, EPIRB, fire extinguishers, flares, and, for vessels operating beyond a size/distance threshold, a stability letter — earning a compliance decal valid for a set period, with additional safety-equipment requirements layered on for vessels operating further offshore or carrying more crew. A small commercial fishing operation running 1-5 vessels manages state/federal permit renewal, per-trip logbook/VMS compliance, quota-balance tracking against IFQ allocation, and CFVS decal renewal with no dedicated compliance function — and a vessel that lands past its remaining quota, or is boarded with a lapsed CFVS decal, is exactly the gap state and federal fisheries enforcement boardings are built to catch.
**What to build:** A per-vessel state/federal permit and CFVS decal renewal tracker with tiered alerts; a per-trip logbook and VMS-transmission compliance log; an IFQ quota-balance tracker computing each vessel's remaining allocation per species from reported landings, with a hard block flagging any trip that would land the vessel over its remaining quota; and a multi-vessel permit portfolio view for operators fishing several species/gear types across overlapping seasons.
**Skill fit:** State commercial fishing license rules, NOAA Fisheries permit/VMS requirements, and USCG CFVS dockside exam standards are public federal and state regulatory sources, and the quota-balance gate plus decal renewal tracker are the same deterministic "compute the constraint from reported activity, block the action once it's exceeded" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a maritime trade distinct from every passenger-carrying vessel idea already in this repo.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the permit/decal renewal tracker, the per-trip logbook/VMS log, and the IFQ quota-balance tracker with the landing-eligibility gate; land the first 5-8 vessel operators through regional commercial fishermen's association outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — state and regional commercial fishermen's associations (groundfish, shellfish, and lobster associations organized by coastal region) and NOAA Fisheries permit-holder outreach, where small vessel owners already track quota balances and CFVS decal renewal on paper logbooks.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149/mo base + $25/mo per vessel tracked. Average client (a 2-vessel operation) = $149 + $50 = $199/mo. 55-65 operators × $199/mo = **$10,945-12,935/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every trip generates a fresh logbook/VMS compliance event and recomputes the vessel's remaining quota balance, and state/federal permit and CFVS decal renewals recur on their own cycles per vessel — a continuous, per-trip compliance stream tied directly to the operation's fishing activity, not an annual filing.
**Biggest risk:** Landing catch beyond a vessel's remaining IFQ quota allocation is a strict-liability violation triggering quota forfeiture and civil penalties regardless of intent — the landing-eligibility gate blocking a trip that would exceed remaining quota is the core protection.
**Growth path:** Side project → permit/decal tracker, logbook/VMS log, and quota-balance gate deployed, first 5-8 operator retainers through fishermen's association outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 26-32 operators. Replaces-full-time-salary → 55-65 operators, pushing past $10.9-12.9K/mo.

### 5. CDL Driving School Entry-Level Driver Training (ELDT) & FMCSA Training Provider Registry Compliance
**Problem:** Since February 2022, FMCSA's Entry-Level Driver Training (ELDT) rule (49 CFR Part 380 Subpart F) requires anyone seeking a new commercial driver's license or upgrading to certain endorsements to complete training from a provider registered on FMCSA's Training Provider Registry (TPR) — a school that isn't registered, or whose registration lapses, cannot legally issue the certification a student needs to even schedule their CDL skills test with the state DMV. Registered providers must deliver both a theory (knowledge) curriculum and behind-the-wheel training meeting FMCSA's required topic list, and every instructor delivering behind-the-wheel training must meet FMCSA's instructor qualification requirements — a CDL held in the relevant vehicle class, a clean driving record, and a specific experience threshold. The core, hard-edged compliance event is the electronic submission itself: within a required window after a student completes training, the provider must submit that student's certification of training completion directly to the TPR's electronic system — a student whose completion was never submitted cannot schedule their state skills test through no fault of their own, and a training provider found to have issued a completion certificate without meeting the curriculum's required topics faces removal from the TPR, which immediately halts the school's ability to operate since state DMVs check TPR status before scheduling any skills test. A small CDL school running several instructors and dozens of students a month manages instructor qualification currency, per-student curriculum-topic completion documentation, and the TPR submission deadline for every graduating student with no dedicated compliance function — and a missed TPR submission, discovered only when a frustrated student can't schedule their state test, is exactly the operational failure that damages a school's word-of-mouth reputation fastest.
**What to build:** A per-instructor FMCSA qualification tracker (CDL class held, driving record currency, experience threshold — with a "cleared to instruct" gate); a per-student curriculum-topic completion checklist mapped to FMCSA's required theory and behind-the-wheel topics, blocking a completion certificate from being generated until every required topic is confirmed covered; and a TPR submission tracker computing each student's required submission deadline from their completion date, with a submission-confirmation flag distinguishing "submitted" from merely "due."
**Skill fit:** FMCSA's ELDT curriculum requirements and TPR submission rules are public federal regulatory text with a defined, checkable structure, and the curriculum-topic gate plus TPR submission-deadline tracker are the same deterministic "block the certificate / compute the deadline from the trigger event" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a training niche most small CDL schools currently run on a paper sign-in sheet with no systematic TPR-submission cross-check.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the instructor qualification tracker with the instruct-eligibility gate, the curriculum-topic completion checklist, and the TPR submission-deadline tracker; land the first 5-8 schools through Commercial Vehicle Training Association (CVTA) outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — Commercial Vehicle Training Association (CVTA) member directory and state trucking association driver-training committees, where small CDL school owners already discuss TPR submission gaps and instructor-qualification documentation burden.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $179/mo base + $3/mo per student processed monthly. Average client (35 students/mo) = $179 + $105 = $284/mo. 40-48 schools × $284/mo = **$11,360-13,632/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every graduating student generates a fresh TPR submission deadline, instructor qualifications require ongoing driving-record currency checks, and a growing school adds new cohorts continuously through the year — a per-student, enrollment-volume-driven compliance stream, not a single annual filing.
**Biggest risk:** A missed TPR submission — a bookkeeping failure rather than a training failure — blocks a graduated student from scheduling their state CDL skills test and is exactly the operational gap that damages a school's referral-driven reputation fastest; the submission-deadline tracker with a confirmation flag is the core protection.
**Growth path:** Side project → instructor tracker, curriculum checklist, and TPR submission tracker deployed, first 5-8 school retainers through CVTA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 20-24 schools. Replaces-full-time-salary → 40-48 schools, pushing past $11.36-13.63K/mo.
