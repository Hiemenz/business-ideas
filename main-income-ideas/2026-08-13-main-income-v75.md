### 1. Asbestos Abatement Contractor EPA NESHAP, OSHA Construction Standard & Accreditation Compliance
**Problem:** Asbestos abatement contractors operate under EPA's asbestos NESHAP (40 CFR Part 61, Subpart M), which requires written notification to EPA or the delegated state agency before any renovation or demolition disturbing a threshold amount of regulated asbestos-containing material (RACM) — commonly a 10-working-day advance notice — along with mandatory wetting, containment, and disposal-manifest documentation at an approved landfill; a job started without the notification on file, or RACM disposed of without a matching waste-shipment record, is one of the most common findings an EPA or state air-quality inspection targets, and can trigger per-day penalties that scale with the duration of the violation. OSHA's Construction Asbestos Standard (29 CFR 1926.1101) classifies work into four exposure classes with escalating engineering-control, respiratory-protection, and medical-surveillance requirements, and every worker and supervisor must hold current EPA Model Accreditation Plan (40 CFR Part 763, Appendix C) accreditation from an EPA-approved training provider, renewed through an annual refresher course — a worker sent onto a jobsite with a lapsed accreditation, or assigned to a work class above their medical-surveillance clearance, is exactly the finding an OSHA compliance inspection following any incident is built to catch. A small abatement contractor running several crews across dozens of residential and commercial jobs a year manages per-worker accreditation currency, per-job EPA notification-and-disposal-manifest documentation, and OSHA work-class medical-surveillance tracking with no dedicated compliance function.
**What to build:** A per-worker/supervisor EPA accreditation tracker with a "cleared to work" gate blocking a crew assignment once annual refresher training lapses; a per-job EPA notification and waste-disposal manifest tracker computing each job's required notification lead time and confirming a matching manifest is on file before the job is marked closed; and a per-worker OSHA work-class and medical-surveillance currency tracker.
**Skill fit:** EPA's NESHAP notification requirements and accreditation-refresher cycle are public, structured federal regulatory data with a well-defined per-job and per-worker record requirement, and the work-eligibility gate plus the notification-deadline tracker are the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance record clears / compute the deadline from the trigger event" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a hazardous-materials trade most small contractors currently track on a shared spreadsheet with no systematic cross-check between a worker's accreditation status and the job they're dispatched to.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the accreditation tracker with the work-eligibility gate, the notification/manifest tracker, and the medical-surveillance tracker; land the first 5-8 companies through Environmental Information Association (EIA) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — Environmental Information Association (EIA) member directory and state asbestos abatement contractor association meetings, where small contractors already navigate per-job EPA notification deadlines and annual worker-accreditation refreshers with no dedicated compliance staff.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $229/mo base + $18/mo per accredited worker/supervisor tracked. Average client (6 workers) = $229 + $108 = $337/mo. 28-35 companies × $337/mo = **$9,436-11,795/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every worker's EPA accreditation renews on its own independent annual clock, every job opens its own notification-and-manifest documentation event, and OSHA medical-surveillance requirements recur on a fixed schedule per exposure class — a continuous, per-worker and per-job compliance stream tied directly to the contractor's crew size and job volume.
**Biggest risk:** A job started without the required EPA notification on file, or RACM disposed of without a matching waste manifest, is exactly the finding an EPA or state air-quality inspection is built to catch — the notification/manifest tracker and the work-eligibility gate are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → accreditation tracker, notification/manifest tracker, and medical-surveillance tracker deployed, first 5-8 company retainers through EIA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 14-18 companies. Replaces-full-time-salary → 28-35 companies, pushing past $9.44-11.80K/mo.

### 2. EPA Lead-Based Paint Renovation Contractor RRP Rule & Certified Renovator Compliance
**Problem:** Contractors performing renovation, repair, or painting work for compensation in pre-1978 housing and child-occupied facilities operate under EPA's Renovation, Repair, and Painting Rule (RRP Rule, 40 CFR Part 745, Subpart E), which requires the firm itself to hold current EPA (or state-delegated) "Lead-Safe Certified Firm" status, renewed on a 5-year cycle, and every individual performing lead-disturbing work to hold a Certified Renovator credential from an accredited training provider, also renewed on a fixed cycle through a refresher course — a firm whose certification has lapsed, or a renovator working past their credential's expiration, has performed the job outside EPA's regulatory authorization entirely, exposing the firm to per-violation civil penalties that scale with the number of affected jobs. Every RRP-covered job must follow specific lead-safe work practices — plastic-sheeting containment, HEPA vacuum cleanup, and in many states a dust-wipe clearance test before the area is released back to occupants — with supporting records retained for three years per 40 CFR 745.86; a renovator who cannot produce the containment and cleanup documentation for a specific job during an EPA or state audit is exactly the gap that turns a routine records request into a penalty action. A small remodeling or painting company running several crews across dozens of pre-1978 home jobs a year manages firm-certification renewal, per-renovator credential currency, and per-job work-practice/clearance-test documentation with no dedicated compliance function.
**What to build:** A firm-level Lead-Safe Certified renewal tracker; a per-renovator credential currency tracker with a "cleared to work" gate blocking assignment to an RRP-covered job once a credential lapses; and a per-job work-practice and clearance-test documentation log retaining the containment/cleanup record for the required three-year window.
**Skill fit:** The RRP Rule's firm-certification and renovator-credential cycles are public, structured EPA regulatory data with a well-defined per-firm and per-worker record requirement, and the work-eligibility gate plus the three-year document-retention log are the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance record clears / retain the record for the required window" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a remodeling-adjacent trade most small companies currently track on a shared spreadsheet with no systematic cross-check between a renovator's credential status and the job they're assigned to.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the firm-certification tracker, the renovator credential tracker with the work-eligibility gate, and the work-practice/clearance-test documentation log; land the first 5-8 companies through National Center for Healthy Housing (NCHH) and local remodeling-contractor association outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — National Center for Healthy Housing (NCHH) contractor network and state lead poisoning prevention program contractor lists, where small remodeling and painting companies already navigate firm-certification renewal and per-renovator credential tracking with no dedicated compliance staff.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $15/mo per certified renovator tracked. Average client (5 renovators) = $199 + $75 = $274/mo. 33-42 companies × $274/mo = **$9,042-11,508/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every renovator's credential renews on its own independent cycle, every RRP-covered job opens its own work-practice-and-clearance documentation event, and firm-certification renewal recurs on its own five-year clock — a continuous, per-worker and per-job compliance stream tied directly to the company's pre-1978-housing job volume.
**Biggest risk:** A job performed by a firm whose Lead-Safe certification lapsed, or a renovator working past their credential expiration, has operated outside EPA's authorization entirely — the firm-certification tracker and the renovator work-eligibility gate are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → firm-certification tracker, renovator credential tracker, and work-practice documentation log deployed, first 5-8 company retainers through NCHH outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 16-20 companies. Replaces-full-time-salary → 33-42 companies, pushing past $9.04-11.51K/mo.

### 3. Radon Mitigation Contractor NRPP/NRSB Certification & ANSI/AARST Standard Compliance
**Problem:** Radon mitigation contractors must hold current certification through one of the two nationally recognized certifying bodies — the National Radon Proficiency Program (NRPP) or the National Radon Safety Board (NRSB) — or a state-specific equivalent, before installing a mitigation system for compensation in most states, with certification renewed on a fixed multi-year cycle requiring continuing education; a technician installing a system on a lapsed certification has performed work the state radon program doesn't recognize, a gap a state licensing audit or a real-estate closing dispute over an unlicensed installation is built to catch. Every mitigation system must be designed and installed to ANSI/AARST consensus standards governing soil-gas extraction point placement, fan sizing, and pipe routing, and every completed job requires a post-mitigation clearance test confirming the radon level has been reduced below EPA's 4 pCi/L action level — a system installed without a documented post-mitigation test result is exactly the gap a state program audit or a homeowner's own follow-up test targets, and in a real-estate transaction context an undocumented clearance test can unwind a closing that was contingent on radon remediation. A small mitigation company running several installers across dozens of jobs a month — many originating from real-estate closing contingencies with hard closing-date deadlines — manages per-installer certification currency, per-job ANSI/AARST design-standard compliance, and post-mitigation clearance-test documentation with no dedicated compliance function.
**What to build:** A per-installer NRPP/NRSB certification tracker with a "cleared to install" gate; a per-job system-design checklist confirming the installation matches the applicable ANSI/AARST standard for the structure type; and a post-mitigation clearance-test tracker flagging any completed job missing its documented below-action-level result.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every installer's certification renews on its own independent cycle, every job opens its own design-standard and clearance-test documentation event, and real-estate-driven job volume keeps the compliance stream tied directly to closing-date deadlines the company can't control.

### 4. Funeral Home & Crematory FTC Funeral Rule, State Licensure & Chain-of-Custody Compliance
**Problem:** Funeral homes operate under the FTC Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453), which requires a funeral director to provide an itemized General Price List (GPL) before any in-person discussion of arrangements and prohibits conditioning a direct cremation on the purchase of a casket — a home that skips the GPL disclosure or bundles cremation with an unwanted casket sale is exactly the fact pattern behind an FTC mystery-shopper enforcement sweep, which the agency runs periodically against funeral homes nationwide. Every funeral establishment holds a state funeral board license, and every practicing funeral director and embalmer holds an individual license renewed with continuing-education hours on the state's own cycle — a director working past a lapsed license, or an establishment operating without current facility licensure, is one of the most common findings a state funeral board complaint investigation targets. Crematories separately hold a state crematory authority license governing the cremation-authorization form (which in most states must be signed by the legally authorized next of kin before cremation proceeds) and a chain-of-custody protocol tracking remains from receipt through cremation and return to the family — a cremation performed without a properly executed authorization form on file, or a chain-of-custody gap between two sets of remains processed the same day, is exactly the catastrophic-error scenario a state crematory board investigation is built to catch. A small funeral home and crematory operation running several directors and embalmers across dozens of cases a month manages GPL-disclosure documentation, per-director license and continuing-education currency, and per-case cremation-authorization/chain-of-custody tracking with no dedicated compliance function.
**What to build:** A per-case GPL-disclosure and itemized-statement tracker confirming the FTC-required price disclosure was provided before arrangements were finalized; a per-director/embalmer license and continuing-education currency tracker with a "cleared to practice" gate; and a per-cremation authorization-form and chain-of-custody tracker matching each set of remains to its signed authorization from intake through final disposition.
**Skill fit:** The FTC Funeral Rule's disclosure requirement and state funeral board license-renewal cycles are public, structured regulatory sources with a well-defined per-case and per-director record requirement, and the practice-eligibility gate plus the chain-of-custody tracker are the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance record clears / reconcile the record against the physical asset" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a death-care trade most small funeral homes currently track on a paper case file with no systematic cross-check between a director's license status and a cremation's authorization paperwork.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the GPL-disclosure tracker, the director/embalmer license tracker with the practice-eligibility gate, and the cremation authorization/chain-of-custody tracker; land the first 5-8 funeral homes through National Funeral Directors Association (NFDA) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — National Funeral Directors Association (NFDA) member directory and state funeral director association meetings, where small independent funeral home owners already navigate GPL-disclosure documentation and per-cremation chain-of-custody tracking with no dedicated compliance staff.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299/mo base + $25/mo per licensed funeral director/embalmer tracked. Average client (3 directors) = $299 + $75 = $374/mo. 24-30 homes × $374/mo = **$8,976-11,220/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every case opens its own GPL-disclosure documentation event, every director's license renews on its own continuing-education cycle, and every cremation generates its own authorization-form and chain-of-custody record — a continuous, per-case compliance stream tied directly to the home's call volume.
**Biggest risk:** A cremation performed without a properly executed authorization form, or a chain-of-custody gap between two cases processed the same day, is exactly the catastrophic error a state crematory board investigation is built to catch — the authorization/chain-of-custody tracker and the practice-eligibility gate are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → GPL-disclosure tracker, director license tracker, and chain-of-custody tracker deployed, first 5-8 home retainers through NFDA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-15 homes. Replaces-full-time-salary → 24-30 homes, pushing past $8.98-11.22K/mo.

### 5. Scrap Metal & Recycling Dealer State Licensure, Seller Verification & Metal-Theft Reporting Compliance
**Problem:** Scrap metal and recycling dealers hold a state scrap/secondhand-metal-dealer license, and every purchase transaction must be documented under the state's seller-verification statute — commonly requiring a photographed government ID, recorded vehicle license plate, and an itemized description of the material purchased — with the completed record electronically reported to a law-enforcement metal-theft database (systems like LeadsOnline or a state-run scrap theft alert network) within a statutory window, often 24 hours; a dealer who buys material without capturing the required seller documentation, or fails to report the transaction within the window, is one of the most common findings a state licensing audit or a police department's metal-theft sting operation targets. Most states impose a mandatory holding period — commonly 3 to 15 days, and often longer for specifically regulated items like catalytic converters or copper wire and pipe — before purchased material can be melted, altered, or resold, giving law enforcement a window to recover stolen material matched against a theft report; a dealer who processes regulated material before its holding period expires is exactly the violation a police recovery investigation is built to catch, and repeat violations are grounds for license suspension. A small scrap yard running several buying stations and employees across a high transaction volume manages per-transaction seller-ID verification, database-reporting-window compliance, and per-material holding-period tracking with no dedicated compliance function — and a transaction processed without the required documentation, or regulated material melted before its holding period cleared, is exactly the gap a state licensing audit or a police sting is built to catch.
**What to build:** A per-transaction seller-verification log capturing photo ID, plate number, and material description with a completeness gate before the purchase can be logged as closed; a database-reporting-window tracker flagging any transaction approaching its statutory reporting deadline; and a per-material holding-period tracker blocking a batch from being marked "cleared to process" until its statutory hold expires.
**Skill fit:** State scrap dealer licensing statutes' seller-verification and holding-period requirements are public, structured regulatory data with a well-defined per-transaction record requirement, and the reporting-window tracker plus the holding-period gate are the same deterministic "compute the deadline from the trigger event / block the action until the compliance record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a high-transaction-volume trade most small scrap yards currently track on a paper ledger with no systematic per-transaction deadline cross-check.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the seller-verification log, the database-reporting-window tracker, and the holding-period gate; land the first 5-8 dealers through Institute of Scrap Recycling Industries (ISRI) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — Institute of Scrap Recycling Industries (ISRI) member directory and state scrap recyclers association meetings, where small independent dealers already navigate per-transaction seller documentation and holding-period tracking across a high daily transaction volume with no dedicated compliance staff.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249/mo base + $15/mo per transaction-processing employee tracked. Average client (8 employees) = $249 + $120 = $369/mo. 24-30 dealers × $369/mo = **$8,856-11,070/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every purchase transaction opens its own seller-verification and database-reporting event, every regulated-material batch carries its own independent holding-period clock, and a growing employee count expands the number of buying stations needing tracked compliance — a high-frequency, per-transaction compliance stream tied directly to the yard's daily buying volume.
**Biggest risk:** A transaction processed without the required seller documentation, or regulated material melted before its statutory holding period expired, is exactly the finding a state licensing audit or a police metal-theft sting is built to catch — the seller-verification log and the holding-period gate are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → seller-verification log, reporting-window tracker, and holding-period gate deployed, first 5-8 dealer retainers through ISRI outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-15 dealers. Replaces-full-time-salary → 24-30 dealers, pushing past $8.86-11.07K/mo.
