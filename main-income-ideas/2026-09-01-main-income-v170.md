### 1. Safe & Vault Technician Bank Protection Act, UL Rating & Dual-Control Service-Record Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Independent safe and vault service companies that install, repair, and service UL-rated commercial safes and financial-institution vaults for banks, credit unions, and cash-intensive businesses (jewelers, casinos, cannabis dispensaries) operate at the intersection of two obligations most small shops track from memory. First, every combination change or reset on a bank vault or safe must follow the institution's own dual-control policy — two authorized bank employees present and witnessing, with the technician's service record standing as the documentation a bank's own physical-security officer produces during an FDIC or NCUA examination under the Bank Protection Act of 1968 (12 CFR 216 and its state-chartered equivalents), which sets minimum physical-security standards for insured institutions. Second, a bank's blanket bond and fidelity insurance carrier prices coverage directly against the vault or safe's UL 687 burglary-resistance rating (and UL 608 for smaller units), so a technician who services or modifies a unit without producing current UL-rating documentation exposes the client bank to a coverage gap that surfaces only when a claim is filed. A small service company running a route across dozens of bank branches, jewelers, and cash businesses currently tracks per-unit UL rating, dual-control combination-change witness records, and each client's insurance-documentation requests on a paper service ticket and a filing cabinet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific vault's service record and rating documentation are actually on file before a bank's next examiner visit.
**What to build:** A per-client vault/safe inventory tracker (unit model, UL rating, install date) generating an insurance-ready rating-documentation packet on request; a dual-control combination-change log capturing both witnessing employees' names and the date, satisfying the bank's own Bank Protection Act physical-security recordkeeping; and a technician-side SAVTA certification, bonding, and background-check currency tracker with a "cleared to enter vault room" gate many banks require before granting access.
**Skill fit:** Bank Protection Act physical-security standards and UL 687/608 rating structures are public, well-documented regulatory and standards-body frameworks with a concrete per-unit and per-service-event trigger, and the dual-control witness log plus the ratings-documentation generator are the same deterministic recordkeeping pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a legal-metrology-adjacent trade most small vault service companies currently track on a paper ticket book.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the vault/safe inventory tracker, the dual-control combination-change log, and the technician certification/bonding tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent safe and vault service companies.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Safe & Vault Technicians Association (SAVTA) member directory and its annual convention reach exactly the small service companies currently tracking dual-control service records and UL documentation on a paper ticket book with no dedicated system.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249/mo base + $15/mo per vault/safe account tracked. Average client (30 accounts) = $249 + $450 = $699/mo. 15-18 service companies × $699/mo = **$10,485-12,582/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every combination change reopens the dual-control witness-log requirement, every new bank branch or cash-business client adds a fresh unit to the UL-rating documentation set, and a growing service company's own technician roster compounds the number of simultaneously tracked bonding and background-check renewals.
**Biggest risk:** A very small one-technician shop servicing a handful of local units has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward service companies running routes across 15+ bank branches or cash-intensive businesses, where a missed dual-control record ahead of an examiner visit genuinely threatens the client relationship.
**Growth path:** Side project → 8 service companies ($5.6K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 15-18 companies ($10.5-12.6K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding a bank-facing examiner-ready compliance export a service company can hand a branch manager the moment an FDIC visit is scheduled.

### 2. Unpatented Mining Claim & Mineral Exploration BLM Claim-Maintenance & Notice-of-Intent Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Every unpatented lode or placer mining claim staked on federal land under the General Mining Law of 1872 must be kept current with the Bureau of Land Management through one of two paths: paying BLM's annual per-claim maintenance fee by the hard September 1 deadline, or — for a small holder of 10 or fewer claims who has filed a Small Miner Waiver Certification — completing and documenting at least $100 of annual assessment work per claim instead, filed as an Affidavit of Annual Assessment Work with both BLM and the county recorder. A claim that misses this single annual date doesn't get fined — it lapses entirely and becomes open to relocation by anyone else, a total forfeiture of exploration work and any discovered mineral value that traces directly back to a missed calendar date rather than any technical violation. Layered on top, any exploration activity causing surface disturbance above a regulatory threshold (commonly 5 acres cumulative, or any use of mechanized earth-moving equipment) requires either a Notice of Intent to Operate filed with the BLM field office or U.S. Forest Service district (36 CFR 228 Subpart A on Forest Service land), or a full Plan of Operations for larger disturbance, each with its own reclamation-bond posting requirement before ground can be broken. A junior exploration company or claim-management firm holding a portfolio of dozens to hundreds of claims across several states and BLM field offices currently tracks per-claim maintenance-fee-versus-assessment-work election, the September 1 deadline, and Notice-of-Intent/Plan-of-Operations surface-disturbance thresholds on a geologist's spreadsheet, with no systematic alert before a specific claim's maintenance deadline passes unfiled.
**What to build:** A per-claim BLM maintenance-fee-or-assessment-work election tracker computing each claim's September 1 deadline from its location date, with an escalating alert well ahead of the cutoff; a cumulative surface-disturbance tracker per project area flagging when a Notice of Intent or Plan of Operations threshold is approached; and a reclamation-bond expiration tracker per BLM field office or Forest Service district.
**Skill fit:** BLM's LR2000 claim-recordation system and the General Mining Law's maintenance-fee and assessment-work rules are public, structured federal land-records data with a concrete per-claim, per-year trigger, and the deadline-countdown tracker is the same deterministic "compute the hard date and alert before it's missed" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a federal land-tenure niche this repo has never addressed.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the maintenance-fee/assessment-work deadline tracker, the surface-disturbance threshold tracker, and the reclamation-bond tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to junior exploration companies and claim-management firms.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the American Exploration & Mining Association (AEMA) member directory and its annual convention, plus state Geological Survey prospector-registry contacts, reach exactly the small claim holders and junior exploration companies currently tracking the September 1 deadline on a spreadsheet with no dedicated system.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $3/mo per claim tracked. Average client (150 claims) = $199 + $450 = $649/mo. 18-22 companies × $649/mo = **$11,682-14,278/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every claim carries its own independent annual maintenance deadline regardless of exploration activity, every new claim staked as a portfolio grows adds a fresh deadline to track, and any expanding exploration project reopens the surface-disturbance threshold calculation — a continuous, portfolio-size-driven compliance stream.
**Biggest risk:** A prospector holding only 2-3 claims has a low-stakes tracking need barely worth a subscription. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward junior mining companies and claim-management firms running portfolios of 50+ claims across multiple states, where a single missed deadline is a real forfeiture risk.
**Growth path:** Side project → 8 companies ($5.2K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 18-22 companies ($11.7-14.3K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding a BLM-field-office-ready filing export a claim manager can produce the moment an audit or ownership-transfer diligence request arrives.

### 3. Grain Bin & Silo Erection Contractor Engineering-Certification & Erection-Safety Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Contractors who erect grain bins and silos for farms, co-ops, and elevators build each structure to a manufacturer-stamped engineering plan (GSI, Brock, Sukup, and similar manufacturers each certify a specific bin design against a target bushel capacity and the wind/snow load requirements of the county it's erected in) — a bin erected without confirming the certified plan actually matches that site's load requirements and intended fill capacity is a structural failure risk the contractor, not the manufacturer, is liable for once the crew leaves. During erection itself, crews work at height on curved sheet-steel walls and roof structures, triggering OSHA fall-protection documentation, and the post-erection commissioning cleanout — clearing construction debris from inside a newly built bin before it's certified ready to receive grain — is itself a permit-required confined space entry under OSHA general industry rules the moment residual dust or an engulfment hazard is present. A small erection crew running several bin-raising projects a season across scattered farm and co-op sites currently tracks per-project engineering-certification matching, crew fall-protection plans, and commissioning confined-space entry permits on a foreman's paper job folder, with no systematic gate confirming a specific bin's engineering certification actually matches the site before the crew starts welding, or that the commissioning cleanout's confined-space permit is on file before the bin is cleared to receive its first load.
**What to build:** A per-project engineering-certification tracker matching each bin's manufacturer-stamped plan to the site's county wind/snow-load requirement and target bushel capacity, with a "certified to commission" gate blocking a bin from being marked ready until the match is confirmed; a per-crew OSHA fall-protection plan and confined-space entry-permit tracker for erection and post-erection cleanout; and a building-permit and final-inspection tracker per site.
**Skill fit:** Manufacturer engineering-certification structures and OSHA's confined-space and fall-protection rules are public, structured technical and regulatory data with a concrete per-project trigger, and the certification-matching gate is the same deterministic "block the action until the record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an agricultural-construction niche entirely distinct from the Grain Elevator & Grain Warehouse Operator and Official Grain Inspection & Weighing Agency ideas already in this repo, neither of which involves erecting the physical storage structure at all.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the engineering-certification tracker, the fall-protection/confined-space permit tracker, and the building-permit tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent bin-erection contractors and authorized manufacturer dealer-installers.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the National Grain and Feed Association (NGFA) member network and the authorized-dealer directories of GSI, Brock, and Sukup reach exactly the small erection contractors currently tracking engineering certifications and confined-space permits on a foreman's paper job folder with no dedicated system.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per contractor depending on annual project volume. 25-32 contractors × $420/mo avg = **$10,500-13,440/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every bin raised reopens the engineering-certification match and the fall-protection plan from scratch, every commissioning cleanout reopens the confined-space permit requirement, and the recurring fall harvest-season rush compounds a contractor's simultaneously tracked projects every year.
**Biggest risk:** A very small crew running only a handful of projects a season has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward contractors running a full spring/fall erection season across multiple co-op and farm accounts, where the per-project certification-matching load genuinely outgrows a paper job folder.
**Growth path:** Side project → 12 contractors ($5K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 25-32 contractors ($10.5-13.4K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding a manufacturer-facing warranty-registration export a contractor can generate the moment a bin is commissioned.

### 4. City-Licensed Walking Tour Guide Company Badge-Renewal & Landmark-Permit Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Walking tour companies operating in historic cities with municipal tour-guide licensing regimes — New Orleans (City Code Sec. 30-1301 et seq.), Savannah, and Charleston among them — must ensure every individual guide leading a tour holds a current city-issued badge, earned through a written exam and a background check and renewed on the city's own cycle (commonly one to two years), entirely independent of any vehicle or driver's license since the licensing obligation attaches to the person narrating the tour on foot. A company that schedules a guide whose badge has lapsed, discovered when a city code-enforcement officer or the tourism commission spot-checks a tour in progress, faces a per-incident citation and, on repeat findings, risk to the company's own standing with the city — a failure mode distinct from any vehicle-based transportation idea since no vehicle registration or motor-carrier authority is involved at all. Layered on top, tours that stop at specific landmarks — a national or state park unit, a cemetery, a private historic property — commonly require their own separate authorization: the National Park Service's Commercial Use Authorization program for guided tours on NPS land, or a city parks department's group-size and route permit for public squares and monuments, each renewing on its own cycle and specifying a maximum group size the guide must not exceed. A small tour company running several guides across a rotating schedule of route offerings currently tracks per-guide badge renewal, exam-and-background-check currency, and per-landmark authorization status on an office manager's spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific guide is currently badged and every stop on today's route is currently authorized before the tour departs.
**What to build:** A per-guide city badge and exam/background-check renewal tracker with a "cleared to lead tours" gate blocking a guide from being scheduled once their badge lapses; a per-landmark authorization tracker (NPS Commercial Use Authorization, city parks group-size permit) with renewal alerts and a maximum-group-size flag; and a daily route-assignment board cross-referencing guide badge status against every stop's current authorization before a tour is confirmed to depart.
**Skill fit:** Municipal tour-guide licensing ordinances and NPS Commercial Use Authorization terms are public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-guide and per-landmark trigger, and the "cleared to lead" gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a municipal-professional-licensing niche entirely distinct from every vehicle-based transportation idea already in this repo, none of which involve an individual guide's own city-issued badge at all.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the guide badge tracker, the per-landmark authorization tracker, and the route-assignment board. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent walking tour companies in badge-required cities.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the New Orleans Tour Guide Association, the Savannah and Charleston tour-guide professional associations, and the Guild of Professional Tour Guides (a national member network) reach exactly the small companies currently tracking guide badge renewals on an office spreadsheet with no dedicated system.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every guide's badge renews on its own independent city cycle, every landmark stop a growing company adds to its route offerings reopens the authorization-tracking requirement, and every new hire compounds the number of simultaneously tracked exam-and-background-check renewals.
**Biggest risk:** A very small company running 2-3 guides has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward companies running 8+ guides across multiple route offerings in badge-required cities, where a lapsed badge discovered mid-tour is a real, repeatable business risk.

### 5. Independent Marine Cargo Surveyor NCB Grain-Stability Approval & Survey-Report Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Independent marine cargo surveyors — hired by P&I clubs, cargo underwriters, and charterers to certify the condition, quantity, or loading stability of goods aboard a vessel, distinct from a surveyor who inspects the vessel itself — carry a federally delegated authority in one specific niche: for any vessel loading bulk grain from a US port, U.S. Coast Guard regulation under 46 CFR Subchapter D and the International Grain Code requires an approved authority to calculate and certify the vessel's grain-loading stability before it can legally sail, and the USCG has delegated this Document of Authorization function exclusively to National Cargo Bureau (NCB)-approved surveyors — a surveyor who issues that document without current NCB-approved status, or whose stability calculation doesn't match the vessel's actual stowage plan, exposes both the surveyor and the vessel to a Coast Guard hold at the dock. Outside grain-stability work, cargo surveyors conduct loading, discharge, and damage surveys documenting cargo condition and quantity for insurance claims and charter-party disputes — work product a P&I club or underwriter relies on as the primary evidence in a cargo-damage claim, meaning a survey report missing required photographic documentation or a clear chain of custody from loading to discharge can cost a client its claim. A small independent surveyor or two-person survey firm running jobs across several ports currently tracks NCB-approved-surveyor status renewal, per-vessel grain-stability documentation, and survey-report completeness on a laptop folder of PDFs with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific vessel call's authorization documentation and survey report are both complete before invoicing the client.
**What to build:** An NCB-approved-surveyor status and continuing-training renewal tracker with a "cleared to issue Documents of Authorization" gate for grain-stability work; a per-vessel-call survey job tracker (loading, discharge, or damage survey) with a report-completeness checklist requiring photographic documentation and chain-of-custody notes before a report is marked deliverable; and a per-client (P&I club, underwriter, charterer) invoicing and job-history log.
**Skill fit:** NCB's published approved-surveyor requirements and the USCG grain-stability documentation structure are public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-vessel-call trigger, and the report-completeness gate is the same deterministic "block delivery until the record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a maritime-cargo niche entirely distinct from the Marine Surveyor (Vessel Condition & Valuation) idea already in this repo, whose SAMS/NAMS-accredited pre-purchase inspection of a vessel's own condition never touches cargo stowage, grain-loading stability, or a Coast Guard-delegated authorization function at all.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the NCB-status tracker, the per-vessel-call survey job tracker with report-completeness checklist, and the client invoicing log. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent cargo surveyors at major grain-export and general-cargo ports.
**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — the National Cargo Bureau's own approved-surveyor network and the American Institute of Marine Underwriters' member directory reach exactly the small independent surveyors currently tracking NCB status and survey-report completeness on a laptop folder with no dedicated system.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249-449/mo per surveyor or small firm depending on monthly survey volume. 28-35 surveyors × $340/mo avg = **$9,520-11,900/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every vessel call reopens the survey-report completeness check from scratch, every grain-loading vessel reopens the Document of Authorization requirement, and NCB-approved status itself renews on its own continuing-training cycle — a continuous, vessel-call-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to port traffic.
**Biggest risk:** A surveyor working only general damage surveys with no grain-stability work has no NCB-authorization-tracking need at all. Mitigate by leading with the survey-report completeness gate as the baseline value proposition for every surveyor, and treating the NCB grain-stability module as a premium tier for surveyors who do hold that approval.
**Growth path:** Side project → 10 surveyors ($3.4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 20 surveyors ($6.8K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 28-35 surveyors plus a P&I-club-facing claim-documentation export a surveyor can hand an underwriter the moment a cargo-damage claim is filed.
