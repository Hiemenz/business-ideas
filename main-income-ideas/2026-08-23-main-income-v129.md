### 1. Sports Agent & NIL Agent Multi-State UAAA Registration & Per-Contract Filing-Deadline Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Sports agents and NIL agents representing student-athletes and professional prospects across multiple states operate under the Uniform Athlete Agents Act (UAAA) or its 2015 revision (RUAAA), adopted in some form by most states, which requires the agent to register in every state where they solicit or sign an athlete — commonly through that state's Secretary of State, with a criminal-background check and a surety bond (commonly $50,000-$100,000 depending on the state) — and, once a contract is signed, to file a copy with the athlete's school athletic director within a state-specified window, commonly 72 hours. A contract filed late, or an agent who solicited an athlete in a state where they hadn't yet registered, can render the agency contract voidable under UAAA's own terms, exposes the agent to a private right of action from the school for any resulting scholarship or eligibility loss the athlete suffers, and triggers a criminal misdemeanor charge for unregistered solicitation in several states. Since the 2021 NIL rule changes, a growing number of states have layered a second, NIL-specific agent-registration statute on top of the traditional UAAA framework, requiring separate registration and compensation-term disclosure to the athlete's university NIL compliance office before an NIL deal can be finalized. An agency representing a roster of a dozen or more athletes recruited from and playing in different states manages staggered state-by-state registration renewal, bond currency, and a 72-hour (or state-specific) contract-filing clock for every single signed deal — usually on a spreadsheet an associate updates between recruiting trips, and a contract filed a day late, or a state where the agent never got around to registering before making first contact, is exactly the fact pattern a UAAA enforcement action or a rival agent's complaint to a state athletics regulator is built to catch.

**What to build:** A per-state UAAA/NIL-agent registration and bond-renewal tracker computing each state's specific renewal date; a per-contract filing-deadline tracker computing the exact due date from the contract's execution timestamp and the specific state's filing window, with the athlete's school athletic director or NIL compliance office contact attached; and a pre-solicitation eligibility check confirming the agent is currently registered in the athlete's state before a new recruiting conversation is logged as a live prospect.

**Skill fit:** UAAA's state-by-state registration and per-contract filing-deadline requirements are public, structured state statutory data with a concrete numeric deadline, and the pre-solicitation eligibility gate plus the per-contract filing-deadline computation are the same deterministic "verify the rule before the action proceeds / compute the deadline from the trigger event" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an athlete-representation compliance surface this repo has never addressed.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the multi-state registration/bond tracker, the per-contract filing-deadline tracker, and the pre-solicitation eligibility gate. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent sports and NIL agencies.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Sports Lawyers Association (SLA)'s annual conference and member directory reach exactly the small independent agents and boutique agencies juggling multi-state UAAA registration and per-contract filing deadlines without dedicated compliance staff.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-399/mo per agent/agency depending on athlete-roster size and state footprint. 35-45 agencies × $299/mo avg = $10,465-13,455/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every state an agency recruits in carries its own independent registration and bond-renewal clock, every signed contract opens a fresh filing-deadline event regardless of how many the agency has already filed, and NIL's continued growth keeps expanding the number of states layering a second registration requirement on top of the traditional UAAA framework — a continuous, roster-and-footprint-driven compliance stream tied directly to the agency's ability to keep signing athletes legally.

**Biggest risk:** A solo agent representing athletes in only one or two home-state markets has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads for agencies recruiting nationally across eight or more states, where the staggered registration-and-filing calendar genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 agencies ($4.5K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 30 agencies ($9K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 35-45 agencies plus a school-compliance-office delivery-confirmation add-on for agencies wanting a defensible record if a filing is ever disputed.

### 2. NAID AAA-Certified Data Destruction & Secure Shredding Company Chain-of-Custody & Certificate of Destruction Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Companies that shred paper records and destroy hard drives, SSDs, and other data-bearing media on behalf of clients hold i-SIGMA's (formerly NAID) AAA Certification, which subjects the operation to unannounced on-site audits reviewing employee background-screening currency and security-training records for every destruction technician, and requires a Certificate of Destruction be issued for every completed job — documenting the specific asset list destroyed, the destruction method and particle size or sanitization standard used (DIN 66399 for shredded paper, NIST 800-88 for digital media), and the date and location of destruction. Clients retain a NAID AAA-certified destruction company specifically because that Certificate becomes their own legal evidence of compliance with HIPAA's disposal safeguards (45 CFR §164.530(c)), the FACTA Disposal Rule (16 CFR §682.3), and the GLBA Safeguards Rule — meaning a shredding company's own documentation gap doesn't just risk its own certification, it silently invalidates the compliance defense of every client relying on that Certificate. A mobile shredding operation running several trucks across a daily multi-stop route must maintain an unbroken chain-of-custody record from pickup at each client site through destruction completion, since a gap — a truck that shredded material off-manifest, or a Certificate issued for a job where the chain-of-custody log has a hole in it — is exactly the finding an i-SIGMA unannounced audit or a client's own vendor-compliance review is built to catch. A small shredding company running a handful of trucks and a growing roster of recurring commercial accounts tracks per-job chain-of-custody, per-technician background-screening renewal, and Certificate-of-Destruction issuance on a mix of a route dispatcher's paper manifest and a filing cabinet of completed certificates, with no systematic cross-check confirming a Certificate was actually generated for every job on the day's route before the truck returns to the yard.

**What to build:** A per-job chain-of-custody log capturing pickup time/location, route/truck assignment, and destruction-completion timestamp, generating a Certificate of Destruction automatically once the job is marked complete; a per-technician background-screening and security-training renewal tracker feeding i-SIGMA's audit-readiness documentation; and a destruction-method/particle-size compliance log confirming the correct standard was applied per media type on every job.

**Skill fit:** i-SIGMA's AAA Certification requirements and the DIN 66399/NIST 800-88 destruction standards are published, structured accrediting-body and technical-standards data, and the per-job chain-of-custody-to-certificate-generation pipeline is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a data-destruction niche distinct from the electronics recycling and medical/biohazard waste ideas already in this repo.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the chain-of-custody/certificate-generation tracker, the per-technician background-screening tracker, and the destruction-method compliance log. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent shredding and data-destruction companies.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — i-SIGMA's (International Secure Information Governance & Management Association) member directory and regional chapter meetings reach exactly the small independent shredding companies currently assembling Certificates of Destruction and audit-readiness documentation by hand ahead of an unannounced NAID AAA audit.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249-449/mo per company depending on truck/route count and monthly job volume. 30-40 companies × $339/mo avg = $10,170-13,560/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every completed job generates its own chain-of-custody-to-certificate event regardless of volume, every technician's background screening renews on its own cycle, and every recurring commercial account the company signs adds a fresh, permanent stream of jobs needing a defensible certificate — a continuous, job-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the company's standing with the clients whose own regulatory programs depend on its certificates.

**Biggest risk:** A very small operation running a single truck for a handful of local clients has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need a paper manifest can still handle. Mitigate by qualifying leads for companies running multiple trucks and a growing recurring-commercial-account base, where the daily multi-stop chain-of-custody volume genuinely outgrows manual tracking.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 companies ($4.1K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 26 companies ($8.8K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 30-40 companies plus a client-facing certificate-delivery portal companies can offer their largest healthcare and financial-services accounts as a premium add-on.

### 3. EV & Industrial Lithium-Ion Battery Recycler Universal Waste Clock, DOT Shipping-Classification & State Battery-Stewardship Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Companies that collect, consolidate, and recycle spent lithium-ion batteries from EVs, power tools, and industrial equipment operate under EPA's Universal Waste Rule (40 CFR §273.2), which allows streamlined battery handling only if every storage container is properly labeled with an accumulation-start date and never held past the rule's strict one-year accumulation limit — a container that quietly ages past that limit converts the facility's entire handling exemption into a full hazardous-waste violation. Every outbound shipment of batteries must also clear DOT/PHMSA's lithium-battery shipping regulation (49 CFR §173.185), which imposes its own packaging, marking, and shipping-paper requirements, and applies an even stricter special-provision packaging regime to any damaged, defective, or recalled (DDR) cell or battery — a mis-classified DDR shipment is exactly the fact pattern behind documented lithium-battery fire incidents in transit, and a shipment moved under the wrong classification is a DOT hazmat citation waiting to happen. A fast-growing wave of state-specific battery-stewardship statutes — California's SB 615 EV battery producer-responsibility program among the first movers — layer a separate registration and reporting cycle on top of the federal framework, distinct from a state's general electronics EPR law. A small battery recycler running dozens of active storage lots and a steady outbound shipping schedule tracks each container's accumulation-start date against the one-year Universal Waste clock, each outbound shipment's classification (standard vs. DDR) and packaging compliance, and state battery-stewardship registration and reporting deadlines on a mix of a warehouse spreadsheet and a shipping clerk's paper log, and a lot that quietly crosses the one-year accumulation mark, or a DDR shipment packaged under the standard classification, is exactly the finding an EPA or PHMSA inspection is built to catch.

**What to build:** A per-lot Universal Waste accumulation-start-date tracker with a hard flag at the one-year mark; a per-shipment DOT classification checklist distinguishing standard from damaged/defective/recalled battery shipments, gating packaging and shipping-paper requirements accordingly; and a state battery-stewardship program registration and reporting-deadline tracker for every state the recycler operates in.

**Skill fit:** EPA's Universal Waste Rule accumulation limit and DOT's lithium-battery shipping classification are published, structured federal regulatory data with concrete numeric thresholds, and the per-lot accumulation clock plus the per-shipment classification gate are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a battery-specific recycling niche distinct from the general electronics/e-waste recycler idea already in this repo.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the Universal Waste accumulation tracker, the DOT shipment-classification checklist, and the state battery-stewardship registration tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent battery collection and recycling companies.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Institute of Scrap Recycling Industries (ISRI)'s battery recycling committee and member directory reach exactly the small independent recyclers navigating the Universal Waste accumulation clock and DOT's lithium-battery shipping classification without dedicated compliance staff.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249-499/mo per recycler depending on active-lot count and shipping volume. 28-36 recyclers × $369/mo avg = $10,332-13,284/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every storage lot carries its own independent one-year accumulation clock regardless of how quickly it turns over, every outbound shipment triggers a fresh classification-and-packaging determination, and state battery-stewardship programs continue to expand as more states adopt EV-specific producer-responsibility statutes — a continuous, lot-and-shipment-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the recycler's ability to keep operating under its Universal Waste exemption.

**Biggest risk:** A very small recycler with low battery volume and a single state's registration has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads for recyclers running multiple concurrent storage lots and shipping across state lines, where the accumulation-clock-and-classification volume genuinely outgrows a warehouse spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 recyclers ($4.4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 24 recyclers ($8.9K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 28-36 recyclers plus a multi-state stewardship-registration concierge tier for recyclers expanding into newly adopted state programs.

### 4. Industrial & Flame-Resistant (FR) Uniform/Garment Rental & Laundering Service NFPA 70E Care-Compliance & ANSI/ISEA 107 Retirement-Clock Tracker

**Problem:** Industrial laundries that rent and launder flame-resistant coveralls and high-visibility garments for utility, oil-and-gas, and electrical-contractor clients operate under NFPA 70E's requirement that FR garments be laundered exactly per the specific manufacturer's care instructions to preserve their arc rating — an incorrect detergent, a fabric softener, or bleach can silently strip a garment's flame-resistant treatment with no visible sign of damage, meaning a worker can wear a garment that looks fine but no longer protects against an arc flash. Every high-visibility garment serviced additionally falls under ANSI/ISEA 107's retroreflective-performance standard, which sets a minimum reflectivity threshold that degrades with each wash cycle and requires the garment be retired once it can no longer meet that minimum after a documented number of laundering cycles. Because OSHA's PPE standard (29 CFR §1910.132) places the ultimate responsibility for maintaining protective equipment on the employer — the industrial laundry's own client — a laundry that can't produce documented proof a specific garment batch was laundered per the manufacturer's process, or that a hi-vis garment hasn't quietly aged past its wash-cycle retirement point, exposes its client to an arc-flash injury and an OSHA citation traceable directly back to the laundry's own process. A small industrial laundry running thousands of garments through the plant weekly across dozens of client accounts tracks per-garment wash-cycle counts, manufacturer-specific detergent/process compliance by garment brand, and hi-vis retirement flags on a mix of the plant's production-line software (built for throughput, not compliance) and a paper log, and a garment that ships back to a client past its retirement point, or laundered with the wrong process for its specific manufacturer, is exactly the finding a client's own safety audit is built to catch.

**What to build:** A per-garment-batch wash-cycle counter tied to an RFID or serial tag with a manufacturer-specific detergent/process compliance checklist enforced at the wash-line level; a retroreflectivity/FR-integrity retirement-flag tracker pulling a garment from service automatically once it crosses its documented wash-cycle threshold; and a client-facing wash-certification export confirming every serviced garment's compliance status ahead of a client's own safety audit.

**Skill fit:** NFPA 70E's manufacturer-specific FR-care requirements and ANSI/ISEA 107's wash-cycle-to-retirement threshold are published, structured standards-body data, and the per-garment wash-cycle counter with the automatic retirement gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a protective-garment-preservation niche distinct from the healthcare/infection-control laundry idea already in this repo.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the per-garment wash-cycle tracker with the compliance checklist, the retirement-flag gate, and the client-facing certification export. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent industrial uniform rental laundries.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — TRSA (Textile Rental Services Association), the national trade association for the linen and uniform rental industry, runs an active member directory and annual conference reaching exactly the small independent industrial laundries servicing FR and hi-vis garment fleets without dedicated compliance software.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per laundry facility depending on garment fleet size and client-account count. 25-32 facilities × $429/mo avg = $10,725-13,728/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every garment in the fleet accumulates its own independent wash-cycle count for the life of its service, every new client account the laundry signs adds a fresh batch of garments needing tracking, and the retirement clock recurs continuously regardless of how the client roster fluctuates — a continuous, garment-fleet-driven compliance stream tied directly to the laundry's insurability and its clients' willingness to trust it with life-safety PPE.

**Biggest risk:** A general uniform-rental laundry servicing only ordinary non-FR workwear has a comparatively lower-stakes tracking need for this specific offer. Mitigate by qualifying leads for laundries with an established FR/hi-vis garment book serving utility and oil-and-gas clients, where the wash-cycle-and-retirement volume genuinely outgrows a paper log.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10 facilities ($4.3K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 20 facilities ($8.6K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 25-32 facilities plus a client-facing fleet-compliance dashboard for utility and oil-and-gas accounts managing their own OSHA audit readiness.

### 5. Heir Search & Probate Genealogy Firm Contingency-Fee Compliance & Probate Case-Deadline Tracker

**Problem:** Heir search and probate genealogy firms perform contingency-fee genealogical research to locate unknown or missing heirs entitled to a share of a decedent's probate estate or to state-held unclaimed property, operating under state "heir finder" fee-cap statutes — California Probate Code §215 among them — that cap the contingency-fee percentage a firm may charge, require the fee agreement itself be filed with and approved by the probate court before it becomes legally enforceable, and in several states require the firm to disclose any identifying or location information about the heir that was already a matter of public record, so the heir isn't paying a contingency fee for information they could have found themselves for free. A fee agreement signed above the statutory cap, or never filed for court approval before the firm starts collecting, is exactly the finding a probate court's own review or a disgruntled heir's objection is built to catch, and can void the firm's entire fee on that case. Every case also races against the underlying probate estate's own statutory closing deadline — an heir found and confirmed after the estate has already closed can find their share has permanently escheated to the state, extinguishing both the heir's inheritance and the firm's fee in one stroke — and a firm pursuing claims through a state's unclaimed-property program separately manages that program's own claim-documentation and filing-window requirements. A small heir search firm running dozens of concurrent cases across multiple states' probate courts and unclaimed-property programs manages per-case fee-agreement compliance against each state's specific cap, the probate court's own closing-deadline clock, and multi-state unclaimed-property claim documentation on a spreadsheet and a case-file folder per state, with no systematic cross-check flagging which cases are approaching a court's closing date before the firm's fee is at risk.

**What to build:** A per-case contingency-fee compliance checker validating a proposed fee agreement against the specific state's statutory cap and required public-record disclosures before it's presented to a client; a probate case-closing deadline tracker computing each court's specific closing date and flagging any case approaching it without a confirmed, court-approved fee agreement on file; and a multi-state unclaimed-property claim-documentation checklist tracking each claim's required filing elements and window.

**Skill fit:** State heir-finder fee-cap statutes and unclaimed-property claim-filing requirements are public, structured state regulatory data with concrete numeric caps and deadlines, and the per-case fee-compliance check plus the closing-deadline tracker are the same deterministic "verify the rule before the agreement is signed / compute the deadline from the case's own clock" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a probate-genealogy niche distinct from the ongoing court-supervised fiduciary idea already in this repo.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the contingency-fee compliance checker, the probate closing-deadline tracker, and the unclaimed-property claim-documentation checklist. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent heir search and probate genealogy firms.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Unclaimed Property Professionals Organization (UPPO)'s member directory and annual conference reach exactly the small independent heir search firms navigating state fee caps and probate closing deadlines across a growing multi-state caseload without dedicated compliance staff.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-399/mo per firm depending on active-case volume and multi-state footprint. 35-45 firms × $289/mo avg = $10,115-13,005/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new case opens its own independent probate-closing clock and fee-compliance check, every state the firm operates in carries its own statutory cap and disclosure rules to verify against, and a firm's caseload only grows as it takes on new probate and unclaimed-property leads — a continuous, case-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the firm's ability to keep its contingency fees legally enforceable.

**Biggest risk:** A very small firm working only a handful of cases in its home state has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need a spreadsheet can still handle. Mitigate by qualifying leads for firms running a multi-state caseload of a dozen or more active searches, where the staggered fee-cap-and-closing-deadline calendar genuinely outgrows manual tracking.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 firms ($4.3K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 30 firms ($8.7K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 35-45 firms plus a court-filing-package export for firms wanting a defensible, pre-formatted fee-agreement submission ready for probate-court review.
