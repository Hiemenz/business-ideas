### 1. Community Bank/Credit Union BSA/AML SAR/CTR Filing, FinCEN Beneficial Ownership & CRA Documentation
**Problem:** Community banks (assets under $10B) and credit unions must maintain comprehensive Bank Secrecy Act and Anti-Money Laundering compliance programs that generate continuous filing obligations, documentation requirements, and regulatory examination exposure — managed by BSA officers who are often shared with other compliance duties. SAR filing compliance: when a bank or credit union detects a suspicious transaction, it must file a Suspicious Activity Report with FinCEN within 30 calendar days of the date the suspicious activity is initially detected (or 60 calendar days if no suspect can be identified) — and may not inform the customer that a SAR has been filed. SAR filing timeliness and completeness are primary examination findings by federal bank examiners (OCC, FDIC, NCUA, Federal Reserve) — a pattern of late or incomplete SAR filings generates BSA/AML program deficiency findings that can escalate to enforcement actions and civil money penalties. CTR filing compliance: cash transactions above $10,000 must trigger a Currency Transaction Report filed with FinCEN within 15 calendar days of the transaction — and multiple transactions by the same person on the same day that aggregate above $10,000 must be combined into a single CTR. Exemptions for eligible businesses (frequent cash businesses like restaurants and retailers) must be reviewed and renewed annually. FinCEN Beneficial Ownership reporting: FinCEN's Corporate Transparency Act beneficial ownership reporting rules (effective 2024) require most legal entities formed or registered in the US to file Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) reports with FinCEN — and financial institutions must implement CDD (Customer Due Diligence) rules collecting and verifying beneficial ownership information from legal entity customers at account opening and upon change. CRA documentation: community banks subject to CRA (Community Reinvestment Act) examinations must maintain loan, investment, and service activity documentation demonstrating community development lending and investment in their assessment areas — CRA examination ratings directly affect merger and acquisition approval prospects for community banks. Small community banks ($100M-$2B assets) and credit unions with 1-2 person compliance departments managing all of these simultaneously are the target.
**What to build:** A community bank and credit union BSA/AML compliance management service — a SAR case management system (for each suspicious activity case: case open date, detecting employee, transaction date(s), transaction amount(s), account holder information, the 30-day SAR filing deadline calculated from case open date, SAR filing status and FinCEN confirmation number, and any continuing activity follow-up — with a daily alert list showing all open SAR cases with deadlines within 5 business days and any cases that have exceeded the 30-day window without a filed SAR or a documented extension justification), a CTR filing tracker (for each CTR-triggering transaction: transaction date, account holder name, cash amount, branch and teller, CTR filing deadline — 15 calendar days from the transaction date — CTR filing status and BSA-E batch confirmation, and for exempted businesses the exemption renewal due date — with a daily CTR pending queue and a weekly exemption renewal calendar), a FinCEN BOI/CDD customer due diligence log (for each legal entity customer: entity name, entity type, date of account opening, beneficial ownership certification on file with the identity of all beneficial owners holding 25%+ equity, the certification review date, and any change-in-ownership event that triggers a new CDD review — with alerts for annual CDD refresh cycles for high-risk customers), a BSA/AML independent testing calendar (tracking the bank's scheduled annual independent BSA/AML audit — required by FFIEC BSA/AML Examination Manual — the auditor, the audit scope, the most recent audit findings and management responses, and the remediation status for each finding), and a CRA activity documentation log (for banks subject to CRA: community development loans, investments, and services with amount, date, geography — inside or outside assessment area — and CRA eligibility category, producing a running CRA performance tally by assessment area that previews the bank's CRA examination position ahead of the next scheduled exam).
**Skill fit:** SAR and CTR filing requirements are documented in FinCEN's SAR Instructions and CTR instructions (FinCEN Form 112). FFIEC BSA/AML Examination Manual procedures are publicly available and document all BSA compliance program requirements for bank examiners. FinCEN BOI reporting requirements are documented in FinCEN's BOI Small Compliance Guide. CRA documentation requirements are in the OCC/FDIC/Federal Reserve CRA interagency Q&A. Building the SAR case management system, CTR tracker, CDD log, independent testing calendar, and CRA activity log are data management and workflow problems the builder's engineering and analytics skills address directly. ICBA (Independent Community Bankers of America) and CUNA (Credit Union National Association) member networks provide defined buyer communities.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a SAR case management system with 30-day deadline tracking and FinCEN confirmation logging, a CTR filing tracker with 15-day deadline management and exemption renewal calendar, a FinCEN BOI/CDD customer log with annual refresh alerts for high-risk customers, a BSA independent testing calendar, and a CRA activity documentation log; first 5-8 community bank or credit union clients through ICBA member outreach while the SAR case management system and CTR tracker are validated against real institution filing workflows.

### 2. Behavioral Health / SUD Treatment Center State License, CARF Accreditation & Medicaid MCO Credentialing
**Problem:** Outpatient behavioral health clinics, residential substance use disorder treatment facilities, and mental health residential programs — entities providing behavioral health and SUD treatment services to Medicaid and commercially insured patients — face a multi-layer license and accreditation compliance framework that has grown significantly as states have expanded Medicaid behavioral health benefits under 1115 waivers and CCBHC (Certified Community Behavioral Health Clinic) models. State behavioral health facility licensure: treatment centers must hold state-issued behavioral health facility licenses (issued by the state department of health, department of behavioral health, or substance abuse authority) with annual renewal, annual state licensure survey compliance, and in many states separate licenses for distinct service types — outpatient mental health, outpatient SUD, residential SUD, medication-assisted treatment (MAT), and crisis stabilization. CARF accreditation: CARF (Commission on Accreditation of Rehabilitation Facilities) behavioral health accreditation — issued on a 1-year, 2-year, or 3-year cycle depending on the survey outcome — is increasingly required by Medicaid MCOs as a condition of behavioral health network participation. CARF accreditation requires comprehensive quality management, human rights protections, outcome measurement, and program evaluation documentation. Medicaid MCO credentialing: in states with Medicaid managed care (44 states as of 2025), behavioral health and SUD treatment centers must credential with each Medicaid MCO operating in the state — typically 2-5 MCOs per state — to bill Medicaid for services. Each MCO conducts initial credentialing (often taking 90-180 days) and re-credentialing every 2-3 years, requiring current state facility licenses, CARF or Joint Commission accreditation certificates, NPI numbers, malpractice insurance certificates, and key staff credential verifications. Centers that miss an MCO re-credentialing application are removed from the network and cannot bill Medicaid for new patients — a direct revenue disruption. Mid-size behavioral health and SUD centers (50-300 patient capacity, 10-50 clinical staff) managing state license renewals, CARF accreditation cycles, and 3-5 MCO credentialing timelines across multiple service lines operate without a systematic compliance tracking system.
**What to build:** A behavioral health and SUD treatment center state license, accreditation, and MCO credentialing compliance management service — a state facility license portfolio (for each licensed service line: license type, license number, annual renewal deadline, most recent state survey date and result, outstanding deficiency findings with required plan of correction and remediation deadline, and any state notification obligations — such as change in medical director or change in licensed bed count — with tiered renewal alerts and a survey readiness gap checklist), a CARF accreditation management system (for the facility's CARF accreditation: accreditation cycle length — 1, 2, or 3 years — expiration date, the 6-month-before-expiration re-accreditation application deadline, any conditional findings from the most recent survey with required corrective action plans and submission deadlines, and annual report submission requirements for 3-year accredited programs — with tiered alerts beginning 9 months before accreditation expiration to initiate the re-accreditation process), an MCO credentialing and re-credentialing tracker (for each Medicaid MCO the center participates with: MCO name, credentialing effective date, re-credentialing cycle length, anticipated re-credentialing application deadline, application submission status, and panel participation confirmation — with alerts 90 days before re-credentialing deadlines and a credential expiration calendar showing every expiring document — malpractice insurance, facility license, accreditation certificate — that must be updated in the MCO credentialing packet before submission), a CAQH facility profile tracker (many MCOs use CAQH for facility-level credentialing data — tracking the facility's CAQH organization ID, attestation due date, and the attestation completion status for each attestation cycle), and a clinical staff credential verification log (for each licensed clinical staff member: licensure type and state, license expiration date, malpractice insurance certificate expiration, and any supervisory agreements for pre-licensed clinicians — maintained for MCO credentialing packet assembly and state licensure survey staff credential verification).
**Skill fit:** State behavioral health facility licensure requirements vary by state and are documented by each state's behavioral health authority. CARF accreditation standards for behavioral health are published in CARF's Behavioral Health Standards Manual. Medicaid MCO credentialing requirements are documented in each MCO's provider manual. Building the state facility license portfolio, CARF accreditation management system, MCO credentialing tracker, CAQH profile tracker, and clinical staff credential log are data management problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. ### 3. Licensed Clinical Laboratory CLIA Certification, State Lab Permit & Proficiency Testing Compliance
**Problem:** Clinical laboratories — physician office labs (POLs), independent reference labs, hospital outreach labs, and specialty testing labs — that perform testing on human specimens for diagnosis, prevention, or treatment of disease must comply with a multi-agency regulatory framework under CLIA (Clinical Laboratory Improvement Amendments of 1988) administered by CMS and, in 9 states (New York, Washington, California, Florida, Georgia, Hawaii, Illinois, Montana, and Pennsylvania), under separate state laboratory licensing programs that are more stringent than CLIA. CLIA certificate and fee compliance: every clinical lab must hold a CLIA certificate appropriate to the complexity of testing it performs — Certificate of Waiver (for waived tests only), Certificate of Provider-Performed Microscopy, Certificate of Registration (pre-accreditation), Certificate of Compliance (CMS-inspected), or Certificate of Accreditation (accreditation body-inspected). Certificates are renewed biennially (every 2 years) and renewal fees are assessed based on the number and type of specialties tested. A lapsed CLIA certificate — because the biennial renewal payment wasn't made — results in immediate loss of authority to perform patient testing and federal civil money penalties. Proficiency testing (PT) program compliance: labs holding CLIA Certificates of Compliance or Accreditation must enroll in and successfully participate in CMS-approved PT programs for each regulated analyte they test. PT challenges are typically sent three times per year (events), and labs must test the PT samples using the same methods and same personnel they use for patient testing, report results within the required timeframe, and achieve a minimum passing score. Repeated PT failures — two unsuccessful events in three consecutive testing events — result in mandatory CLIA sanctions including suspension or revocation of the CLIA certificate. State laboratory permit compliance: in the 9 states with independent state lab programs, labs must hold state laboratory permits (in addition to CLIA certificates), pass state inspections, and in some states meet state-specific PT requirements that go beyond federal CLIA PT minimums. Laboratory accreditation renewal: labs accredited by CAP (College of American Pathologists), The Joint Commission, or AAAHC must undergo biennial accreditation surveys — inspection scheduling must begin 6 months before accreditation expiration to ensure continuous accreditation coverage.
**What to build:** A clinical laboratory CLIA, state permit, and proficiency testing compliance management service — a CLIA certificate management system (for each laboratory site: CLIA certificate number, certificate type, certificate expiration date — biennially from the date of issuance — biennial renewal fee payment status and CMS payment confirmation, and any CLIA specialties and subspecialties listed on the certificate — with tiered renewal alerts beginning 6 months before certificate expiration and a specialty change tracker confirming the certificate reflects the lab's current testing menu), a proficiency testing enrollment and result tracker (for each PT program the lab is enrolled in: PT program name — CAP, COLA, API, AAAB — the regulated analytes or specialty for which PT is required, the annual testing event schedule — typically 3 events per year — the most recent PT shipment receipt date, the result submission deadline for each event, the score achieved, and the cumulative pass/fail status across the most recent 3 events — with a PT calendar showing all upcoming event shipment dates and result submission deadlines across all enrolled programs), a state laboratory permit compliance tracker (for labs in the 9 state-regulated states: state permit number, state permit renewal deadline, state inspection date and result, any outstanding state inspection deficiencies with required correction timeframes, and state-specific PT requirements beyond federal CLIA minimums), a laboratory accreditation renewal scheduler (for CAP- or JC-accredited labs: accreditation organization, certificate expiration date, the 6-month-before-expiration inspection scheduling trigger, inspection completion status, and any accreditation deficiency findings with required corrective action plan submission deadlines), and a CLIA personnel qualification tracker (CLIA requires labs to verify that laboratory directors, technical supervisors, clinical consultants, general supervisors, and testing personnel meet CLIA education and experience qualifications for the certificate type — tracking each personnel role, the individual's qualifications documentation, and any changes to personnel that require CLIA notification or updated qualification verification).
**Skill fit:** CLIA certificate requirements, PT program requirements, and laboratory personnel qualification standards are codified in 42 CFR Part 493. State laboratory permit requirements are available from each of the 9 states' clinical laboratory licensing programs. CAP accreditation requirements are documented in CAP's Laboratory Accreditation Program checklist. Building the CLIA certificate management system, PT enrollment and result tracker, state permit tracker, accreditation renewal scheduler, and personnel qualification tracker are data management and analytics problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. ASCLS (American Society for Clinical Laboratory Science) and AACC (Association for Diagnostics & Laboratory Medicine) member networks provide defined buyer communities.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a CLIA certificate management system with biennial renewal cycle tracking for all certificate types, a PT enrollment and result tracker with event scheduling for the top 5 PT programs (CAP, COLA, API, AAAB, Bio-Rad), a state laboratory permit compliance tracker for the 9 state-licensed states, a laboratory accreditation renewal scheduler for CAP and JC accreditation cycles, and a CLIA personnel qualification tracker; first 5-8 laboratory clients through ASCLS member outreach while the PT tracker and CLIA certificate system are validated against real lab compliance situations.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through ASCLS (American Society for Clinical Laboratory Science) member network and AACC member outreach (ASCLS's annual meeting and AACC's ADLM annual scientific meeting are the primary industry events for laboratory professionals and lab directors), CAP and COLA accreditation program referral networks (accreditation bodies whose surveyors conduct biennial lab inspections see PT compliance gaps and CLIA certificate management failures at every survey and refer compliance management tools to lab directors in their accreditation client portfolios), and laboratory billing software vendor referral networks (laboratory information systems vendors whose customers include thousands of clinical labs see CLIA certificate and PT compliance failures in their customer support interactions and refer compliance management tools through partner marketplace programs).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per laboratory: $250-600/mo (depending on CLIA certificate type, PT program count, and whether the lab is in a state-licensed state — a physician office lab with a Certificate of Compliance performing 8 regulated analytes enrolled in 2 PT programs: $250/mo; a multi-site independent reference lab with CAP accreditation, 25 regulated specialties across 4 PT programs, and permits in 2 state-licensed states: $600/mo). Managing 30-50 laboratory retainers at $375/mo average = **$11,250-18,750/mo**. ### 4. Skilled Nursing Facility MDS Submission Compliance, Five-Star Quality Rating & State Survey Readiness
**Problem:** Medicare and Medicaid-certified skilled nursing facilities (SNFs) — nursing homes providing post-acute skilled nursing care and long-term residential care — face a high-stakes CMS compliance framework centered on the Minimum Data Set (MDS), the CMS Five-Star Quality Rating System, and annual state health department recertification surveys. MDS submission compliance: for every resident admitted to a Medicare- or Medicaid-certified SNF, the facility must complete and submit MDS assessments through CMS's QIES ASAP system at mandated assessment reference dates: admission assessment (within 14 days of admission), quarterly assessments (within 92 days of the prior assessment reference date), annual comprehensive assessments (within 366 days of the prior comprehensive assessment), and discharge assessments (within 14 days of discharge). Late or missing MDS submissions — when the assessment's final submission date falls outside the allowable submission window — generate QIES ASAP error reports that reduce the accuracy of CMS's Five-Star Quality Rating for the facility. Five-Star Quality Rating impact: the CMS Five-Star Quality Rating System rates SNFs on three domains — health inspections (state survey results), staffing (RN, total nurse, and physical therapist hours from payroll-based journal data), and quality measures (calculated from MDS data). The quality measure domain score is calculated directly from MDS submission data — facilities with late, missing, or inaccurate MDS submissions receive degraded quality measure scores that suppress their Five-Star rating. A lower Five-Star rating directly reduces hospital referral volume (hospital discharge planners actively route post-acute referrals to 4-5 star facilities) and affects the facility's performance on publicly visible tools like Medicare's Care Compare website. State health department annual survey: CMS requires state health department surveyors to conduct an annual standard health inspection survey of every certified SNF, assessing compliance with the Requirements of Participation (RoPs) across all regulatory domains. Deficiency citations from the survey generate a Statement of Deficiencies requiring a Plan of Correction submitted within 10 days. Immediate jeopardy (IJ) citations — the most severe deficiency level — require immediate corrective action and can result in temporary manager appointment or Medicaid termination if not immediately remedied.
**What to build:** A skilled nursing facility MDS submission, Five-Star management, and state survey readiness service — an MDS assessment schedule and submission tracker (for each resident: admission date, most recent MDS assessment reference date and assessment type, the next required assessment reference date calculated by assessment type — 92 days for quarterly, 366 days for annual, 14 days from discharge for discharge — the MDS submission deadline (7-day window after the assessment reference date for standard assessments), the QIES ASAP submission confirmation, and any RFI (Request for Information) error responses required from QIES ASAP — with a daily MDS due queue showing all assessments whose reference dates fall within the next 14 days and all assessments whose submission windows are closing within 3 days), a Five-Star quality measure tracking dashboard (for each quality measure in the CMS Five-Star Quality Measure domain: the measure definition, the facility's most recent published score on CMS Care Compare, the MDS data elements that feed the measure, the facility's current-quarter performance on those MDS data elements — enabling the MDS coordinator to see which measures are being positively or negatively affected by current MDS data before CMS publishes the next quarter's rating update), a payroll-based journal (PBJ) submission calendar (CMS requires SNFs to submit actual staffing data through the Payroll-Based Journal every quarter — tracking the PBJ submission deadline — 45 days after each calendar quarter ends — and submission confirmation from CMS, since PBJ staffing data feeds the staffing domain of the Five-Star rating), a state survey deficiency and plan of correction management system (for each citation from the most recent state survey: F-tag number and citation description, severity and scope, the 10-day plan of correction submission deadline, the plan of correction submission date and acceptance confirmation, the implementation timeline, and corrective action documentation status — tracking that each cited deficiency's corrective action is documented and implementable before the state survey revisit), and a survey readiness audit checklist (a pre-survey self-assessment tool organized by CMS Requirements of Participation domain — care planning, infection control, resident rights, quality of care, environment — enabling the Director of Nursing to identify and close documentation gaps before a state surveyor arrives at the facility).
**Skill fit:** MDS assessment schedule requirements and QIES ASAP submission rules are documented in CMS's RAI User's Manual (Resident Assessment Instrument User's Manual). Five-Star Quality Rating calculation methodology is documented in CMS's Five-Star Quality Rating System Technical Users' Guide. PBJ submission requirements are documented in CMS's PBJ Policy Manual. State survey Requirements of Participation are codified in 42 CFR Part 483. Building the MDS schedule and submission tracker, Five-Star quality measure dashboard, PBJ submission calendar, survey deficiency management system, and survey readiness checklist are data management and analytics problems the builder's engineering and data analytics skills address directly. ### 5. Registered Apprenticeship Program Sponsor DOL Registration, OJT Hour Documentation & RTI Completion Tracking
**Problem:** Employers that sponsor DOL-registered or state apprenticeship agency-registered apprenticeship programs — construction companies, manufacturers, utilities, healthcare systems, and tech employers increasingly using apprenticeship for IT and cybersecurity roles — must maintain program registration compliance, document each apprentice's on-the-job training (OJT) progress, track related technical instruction (RTI) completion, and manage apprentice wage progression through the program's defined wage schedule. DOL/SAA program registration: registered apprenticeship programs must be registered with DOL's Office of Apprenticeship (OA) or the applicable State Apprenticeship Agency (SAA) in the 27 states with independent SAA programs, with program standards defining the OJT hour standard (typically 2,000 hours per year), the RTI hour standard (typically 144 hours per year), apprentice-to-journeyman ratio requirements, wage progression schedule, and apprentice competency areas. Programs must notify the OA or SAA of any changes to the program standards and must submit annual reports documenting the number of apprentices enrolled, completions, and demographic information. OJT hour documentation: for each apprentice, the sponsor must document OJT hours worked in each competency area or work process listed in the program standards — some programs use a competency-based approach (apprentice demonstrates competency in each task) while others use a time-based approach (hours accumulated per competency area). OJT records must be maintained and available for OA or SAA review. RTI completion tracking: each apprentice must complete the required RTI hours through a community college, apprenticeship training trust fund, or online training program — RTI completion records (course name, provider, hours, grade) must be maintained for each apprentice. Wage progression compliance: most registered apprenticeship programs specify a wage progression schedule tied to OJT hour completion milestones — sponsors must advance apprentice wages to the next wage bracket when the OJT hour threshold is met, and failing to advance wages on schedule creates a wage underpayment liability under the program standards.
**What to build:** A registered apprenticeship program compliance management service — a program registration compliance calendar (tracking the program's DOL OA or SAA registration number, the program sponsor's designated program administrator, annual report due date and submission confirmation, any pending program standards amendments, and the apprentice-to-journeyman ratio compliance status — with tiered alerts for annual report deadlines and ratio compliance monitoring), an apprentice OJT hour tracker (for each enrolled apprentice: apprentice name, registration date, program start date, OJT hour standard per year and total program hour standard, OJT hours completed to date by competency area, projected completion date at current OJT accumulation rate, and whether the apprentice is on pace to meet the annual OJT hour requirement — with alerts when an apprentice's OJT pace falls below the required accumulation rate and when the annual OJT hour milestone is approaching for wage progression), an RTI completion tracker (for each apprentice: RTI provider and course name, RTI hours completed per term, cumulative RTI hours completed against the program's annual 144-hour standard, RTI enrollment status for the current term — with alerts when an apprentice's RTI enrollment is not confirmed for a term and when the annual RTI completion is projected to fall short), a wage progression management system (for each apprentice: current wage rate, OJT hour threshold for next wage bracket, hours remaining until next wage progression trigger, and the wage progression effective date — generating a wage progression alert when an apprentice's OJT hours reach the threshold, ensuring the wage advancement is implemented on time and documented), and a program completion and certificate management system (for each apprentice approaching program completion: completion eligibility confirmation — OJT hours met, RTI hours met, all competency areas signed off — the DOL/SAA completion certificate application submission, and the journeyman certificate issuance date and document on file).
**Skill fit:** DOL registered apprenticeship program registration requirements and annual report requirements are documented in 29 CFR Part 29 (Standards of Apprenticeship) and 29 CFR Part 30 (Equal Employment Opportunity in Apprenticeship). SAA program requirements vary by state and are documented by each state's apprenticeship agency. Building the program registration calendar, OJT hour tracker, RTI completion tracker, wage progression management system, and program completion system are data management and analytics problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. NCCASAS (National Council for Career and Technical Education) and Urban Institute Workforce Development networks, plus employer-facing apprenticeship intermediaries like ApprenticeshipNC and JFF, provide defined buyer communities.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a program registration compliance calendar for the 27 SAA states plus DOL OA programs, an apprentice OJT hour tracker with competency area tracking and on-pace projection, an RTI completion tracker with annual 144-hour standard management, a wage progression management system with threshold-based progression alerts, and a program completion and certificate management system; first 5-8 employer apprenticeship program sponsor clients through DOL OA-registered employer outreach while the OJT hour tracker and RTI completion tracker are validated against real apprentice cohort documentation situations.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through apprenticeship intermediary referral networks (organizations like JFF — Jobs for the Future — and the Urban Institute's workforce development programs actively work with employer apprenticeship sponsors and refer compliance management tools to program sponsors who struggle with OJT and RTI documentation), state SAA program coordinator referral networks (SAA coordinators who conduct annual compliance reviews for registered programs see OJT documentation gaps and wage progression failures at every review and refer systematic documentation tools to program sponsors in their registered program portfolio), and workforce development industry group referral networks (Associated Builders and Contractors — ABC — and the National Electrical Contractors Association — NECA — both operate large registered apprenticeship training programs and advise member companies on program registration requirements, creating referral opportunities for compliance management tools among member companies launching their own registered programs).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per employer program sponsor: $300-750/mo (depending on active apprentice count, trade complexity, and whether SAA or DOL OA registration is involved — a 15-apprentice construction company sponsor with a DOL OA-registered carpentry program: $300/mo; a 60-apprentice manufacturing company sponsor with a multi-occupation registered program across 3 SAA states requiring separate OJT standards per state: $750/mo). Managing 20-40 sponsor retainers at $475/mo average = **$9,500-19,000/mo**. Program standards amendment packages (one-time $300-500 for sponsors who need to modify their registered program standards — adding a new occupation, changing the OJT hour standard, or updating the wage schedule — and submit the amendment to the OA or SAA for approval) generate project-driven supplemental revenue. Reaching 20-40 retainers requires engagement with DOL OA-registered employer communities through intermediary referral networks and construction/manufacturing trade association outreach.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** OJT hours are documented for every apprentice throughout the year — a 40-apprentice program generates continuous OJT hour recording across all apprentices and all competency areas. RTI enrollment and completion is tracked every semester for every apprentice. Wage progressions trigger throughout the year as different apprentices reach different OJT hour thresholds — in a well-run 40-apprentice program, 2-4 wage progressions occur every month.
