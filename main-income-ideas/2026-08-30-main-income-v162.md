### 1. Commercial Drone/UAS Operator Service Company FAA Part 107 Pilot Certification & Per-Mission Airspace Authorization Tracker

**Problem:** Small commercial drone service companies — providing aerial real-estate photography, construction-progress mapping, agricultural crop-scouting, roof and cell-tower inspection, and insurance-claim documentation — operate every flight under FAA Part 107, which requires every pilot flying commercially to hold a current Remote Pilot Certificate, renewed through a free online recurrent-training course every 24 calendar months (a hard date tied to the individual's original test or last recurrent-training completion, not a calendar-year cycle, making it easy for an operator running several pilots to lose track of which pilot's clock is actually closest to expiring). Every mission flown in controlled airspace near an airport requires either real-time authorization through the FAA's LAANC system (instant for most altitude/location combinations, but some require a manual application with a 90-day lead time) or a standing airspace waiver, and any mission that departs from a standard Part 107 operating rule — flying at night, over people, or beyond visual line of sight — requires its own specific waiver or exemption on file before the drone leaves the ground. A company that dispatches a pilot on a lapsed Remote Pilot Certificate, or that flies a mission in controlled airspace without confirmed LAANC authorization, has committed an FAA violation that can trigger certificate suspension for the pilot and real liability exposure for the company the moment a client's insurance carrier or the FAA itself asks for the flight's authorization record. A company running 4-10 pilots across dozens of monthly missions currently tracks each pilot's 24-month recurrent-training deadline and each mission's airspace-authorization status on a scheduling spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific pilot assigned to a specific mission today is both currently certified and cleared for that mission's exact airspace.

**What to build:** A per-pilot Remote Pilot Certificate 24-month recurrent-training tracker with an automatic reminder well ahead of each individual's specific renewal date; a per-mission airspace-authorization log capturing LAANC confirmation or waiver/exemption number before a mission is marked dispatch-ready; and a "cleared to fly" gate cross-referencing the assigned pilot's certification currency against the mission's airspace-authorization status before the flight is confirmed on the schedule.

**Skill fit:** The FAA's published Part 107 recurrent-training interval and the LAANC system's own authorization-status API are structured, publicly documented data with a concrete per-pilot and per-mission trigger, and the "cleared to fly" gate is the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an FAA-regulated aviation-services niche entirely distinct from the Parasailing Operator (Session 161) and ATV/OHV & Personal Watercraft Rental Operator ideas already in this repo, neither of which involves FAA airspace authorization, a Remote Pilot Certificate, or a LAANC/waiver compliance record of any kind.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the recurrent-training tracker, the per-mission airspace-authorization log, and the dispatch-eligibility gate. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small commercial drone service companies.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Association for Uncrewed Vehicle Systems International (AUVSI) member directory and regional drone-services Facebook and LinkedIn groups reach exactly the small operators currently reconciling pilot recurrent-training dates and mission airspace authorization on a spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149-299/mo per drone service company depending on pilot headcount and monthly mission volume. 40-55 companies × $220/mo avg = $8,800-12,100/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every pilot's 24-month recurrent-training clock runs on its own individual schedule, every mission booked reopens the airspace-authorization question from scratch, and every new pilot a growing company hires compounds the number of simultaneously tracked certification records — a continuous, mission-volume-and-headcount-driven compliance stream tied directly to the company's own growth.

**Biggest risk:** A solo operator flying occasional, uncontrolled-airspace missions has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward companies running 4+ pilots or regularly flying missions near controlled airspace (most metro areas), where the multi-pilot-and-per-mission-authorization load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 companies ($3.3K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 32 companies ($7K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 40-55 companies plus an FAA-inspection-ready authorization export a company can produce the moment an insurance carrier or the FAA asks for a specific mission's flight record.

### 2. State-Licensed Cannabis Dispensary & Cultivation Operator Seed-to-Sale Track-and-Trace & License Renewal Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** State-licensed cannabis cultivators, manufacturers, and retail dispensaries operate under a dual compliance obligation unlike almost any other industry: a state cannabis-control-agency license (cultivation, manufacturing, or retail, each its own license class with its own renewal cycle, and commonly a separate local-jurisdiction operating agreement or conditional-use permit layered on top that renews on its own independent clock), and a legal requirement to report every plant and product movement — propagation, harvest, transfer between licensees, retail sale, and destruction — into the state's mandated seed-to-sale track-and-trace system (Metrc in most states, BioTrackTHC or a state-built platform in others) within the state's required reporting window, commonly 24-48 hours. Regulators treat a missed or late tag-transfer entry as a potential diversion event — product that may have left the legal supply chain — and a pattern of late entries is one of the most common findings that triggers a full compliance audit and can lead to license suspension, since the track-and-trace system exists specifically to prove every gram is accounted for from seed to sale. A small cultivation or dispensary operator running a facility with dozens of active batches and hundreds of weekly transactions currently reconciles what should have been entered into the state's track-and-trace system against what actually was on a compliance manager's spreadsheet built after the fact, with no systematic real-time check confirming today's harvest, transfer, or sale was actually logged before the state's reporting window closes.

**What to build:** A per-license renewal tracker spanning the state cannabis license and any local operating agreement, each with its own renewal clock; a track-and-trace reconciliation dashboard that ingests a facility's actual plant/product movement log and flags any event approaching or past the state's reporting deadline without a confirmed system entry; and a pre-audit readiness report rolling up every license and reporting-window status a state compliance inspector would check first.

**Skill fit:** Most state track-and-trace platforms (Metrc especially) publish a documented API, making the reconciliation dashboard a direct data-integration and alerting problem well suited to a developer with API-integration experience, layered on top of a standard multi-license renewal calendar — no cannabis-industry credential of your own is required to build it.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the license-renewal tracker and the track-and-trace reconciliation dashboard for a single state's Metrc integration. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small licensed cultivators and dispensaries in that state.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — state cannabis-industry trade associations (which nearly every legal-cannabis state now has, commonly named the state's "Cannabis Industry Association" or "Cannabis Business Association") and their member directories reach exactly the small licensees currently reconciling track-and-trace entries by hand with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per licensed facility given the direct license-suspension stakes involved. 25-35 facilities × $420/mo avg = $10,500-14,700/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every single plant movement and every retail transaction reopens its own track-and-trace reporting obligation, and both the state license and any local operating agreement recur on their own independent renewal clocks — a continuous, transaction-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the facility's own throughput.

**Biggest risk:** The regulatory and track-and-trace platform landscape varies enormously state by state, so the product has to be built as a state-specific integration layer rather than one hardcoded system; mitigate by launching first in a single large-market state (California, Michigan, or Illinois) before expanding the integration state by state.

**Growth path:** Side project serving 5-6 facilities in one state → state cannabis association channel presence → 25-35 facilities across a few states replaces-full-time-salary.

### 3. Hearing Aid Dispenser & Audiology Practice State License, FDA Documentation-Branch & OTC/Prescription Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Hearing aid dispensers and audiology practices operate under a state hearing-instrument-dispenser license (a distinct license track, separate from but sometimes co-held with a clinical audiology license, each renewed on its own state-board cycle with its own CEU requirement) and must comply with FDA rules that now branch by device category since the FDA's 2022 over-the-counter hearing aid rule took effect: a prescription-level or severe-loss-appropriate hearing aid still requires a documented pre-sale medical evaluation by a licensed physician, or a signed waiver of that evaluation from an adult patient, on file under 21 CFR 801.421 before the sale, while an OTC-eligible mild-to-moderate-loss device can be sold without any medical evaluation or waiver at all — meaning a dispensing practice has to correctly classify every device sold into the right documentation branch and produce the matching paperwork, since selling a prescription-tier device without the evaluation-or-waiver record on file is exactly the finding a state licensing-board complaint investigation or an FDA inspection is built to catch. A small independent audiology or hearing-aid-dispensing practice fitting dozens of patients a month currently tracks dispenser license renewal, CEU hours, and per-patient evaluation-or-waiver documentation split across OTC and prescription device categories on a front-office binder, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific patient's device category and paperwork actually match before the sale is finalized.

**What to build:** A state hearing-instrument-dispenser and audiology-license renewal tracker with CEU-hour accumulation; a per-patient fitting record that classifies the device as OTC-eligible or prescription-tier and gates the sale on the correct medical-evaluation or signed-waiver document being on file for prescription-tier devices; and a practice-wide compliance-readiness report a licensing-board investigator or FDA inspector could review on request.

**Skill fit:** State hearing-instrument-dispenser licensing rules and the FDA's OTC/prescription device-classification rule are public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-practitioner and per-patient trigger, and the documentation-branch gate is the same deterministic "block the sale until the record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a hearing-healthcare niche entirely distinct from every other clinical-practice-licensure idea already in this repo (CRNA, AA, perfusionist, OMS, respiratory therapist, dental hygienist), none of which involves a state hearing-instrument-dispenser license or an FDA device-category documentation branch of any kind.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the license/CEU tracker and the per-patient device-category documentation gate. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent hearing-aid-dispensing and audiology practices.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the International Hearing Society's (IHS) member directory and state hearing-aid-dispenser association chapters reach exactly the small independent practices currently reconciling license renewal and OTC/prescription documentation on a front-office binder with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149-299/mo per practice depending on practitioner headcount and monthly patient-fitting volume. 45-60 practices × $210/mo avg = $9,450-12,600/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every patient fitting reopens the device-category documentation question, the dispenser license and CEU cycle recur on their own independent clock, and the OTC/prescription classification rule applies to every single device sold — a continuous, patient-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the practice's own growth.

**Biggest risk:** A practice selling exclusively OTC devices with no prescription-tier fittings has a comparatively low-stakes documentation burden. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward practices fitting a mix of OTC and prescription-tier devices, where the dual-branch documentation load genuinely outgrows a binder.

**Growth path:** Side project → 18 practices ($3.8K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 35 practices ($7.4K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 45-60 practices plus a licensing-board-ready documentation export a practice can produce the moment a patient complaint or FDA inspection arrives.

### 4. Orthotics & Prosthetics (O&P) Practice Practitioner Certification & Facility Accreditation Dual-Gate Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Orthotics and prosthetics practices — fitting custom braces, artificial limbs, and other externally applied devices — operate under a dual compliance gate that has no counterpart in any other allied-health practice: every practitioner holds board certification through the American Board for Certification in Orthotics, Prosthetics and Pedorthics (ABC) or the Board of Certification/Accreditation (BOC), renewed on a multi-year cycle requiring a specific split of continuing-education credits between orthotic-scope and prosthetic-scope categories, while the practice's own physical fabrication facility must separately hold ABC or BOC facility accreditation — inspected against specific standards for the fabrication lab, fitting rooms, and clinical documentation process — on its own independent renewal cycle. Critically, Medicare's DMEPOS billing rules require both gates to be simultaneously current before a claim for a custom-fabricated orthosis or prosthesis is payable: a practice with an accredited facility but a practitioner whose individual certification has lapsed, or a certified practitioner working out of a facility whose accreditation expired, generates a claim Medicare and most private payers will deny on documentation grounds alone, regardless of whether the device itself was properly fitted. A small independent O&P practice running 3-6 certified practitioners out of one or two fabrication facilities currently tracks each practitioner's ABC/BOC certification cycle, CEU split, and the facility's own accreditation renewal on a practice manager's spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific claim being billed today has both a currently certified practitioner and a currently accredited facility behind it.

**What to build:** A per-practitioner ABC/BOC certification-cycle tracker with orthotic-versus-prosthetic CEU-category breakdown; a per-facility accreditation renewal tracker; and a claim-readiness gate cross-referencing the billing practitioner's certification status and the fabricating facility's accreditation status before a custom-fabricated-device claim is marked ready to submit.

**Skill fit:** ABC's and BOC's published certification-and-accreditation renewal cycles and CEU-category rules are public, structured certifying-body data with a concrete per-practitioner and per-facility trigger, and the dual practitioner-plus-facility gate is the same deterministic "block the claim until both records clear" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an O&P niche entirely distinct from every DME/DMEPOS supplier idea already in this repo, which tracks a supplier's equipment-category accreditation but not a practitioner's separate clinical certification layered against a fabrication facility's own accreditation.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the practitioner certification tracker, the facility accreditation tracker, and the claim-readiness gate. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent O&P practices.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the American Orthotic & Prosthetic Association's (AOPA) member directory reaches exactly the small independent practices currently reconciling practitioner certification and facility accreditation on a spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249-499/mo per practice depending on practitioner headcount and number of accredited facilities. 30-40 practices × $340/mo avg = $10,200-13,600/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every practitioner's ABC/BOC cycle recurs on its own independent clock, every facility's accreditation reopens its own renewal question, and every claim submitted depends on both gates being simultaneously current — a continuous, claim-volume-and-headcount-driven compliance stream tied directly to the practice's own billing activity.

**Biggest risk:** A single-practitioner practice with one facility has a comparatively low-stakes dual-gate tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward practices running 3+ practitioners across one or more facilities, where the dual-gate reconciliation load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 14 practices ($4.8K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 26 practices ($8.8K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 30-40 practices plus a payer-audit-ready certification-and-accreditation export a practice can produce the moment a Medicare claim is questioned.

### 5. Small-Dollar Consumer Lender (Payday/Title/Installment) State License, Rate-Cap & Statewide Database Query Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Small-dollar consumer lenders — payday, title-loan, and short-term installment lenders — operate under a per-state, often per-location licensing regime (a deferred-deposit, payday, or title-lending license commonly requiring its own surety bond and a separate license for every physical location, rather than one company-wide license) layered against a state-specific rate cap and rollover-restriction structure that varies dramatically from state to state. Several states — Florida, Illinois, Oklahoma, and others — require every lender to query a centralized state loan-database system in real time before issuing a loan, confirming the borrower doesn't already hold an outstanding loan that would violate the state's one-loan-at-a-time rule or mandatory cooling-off period between loans, and a lender that issues a loan without completing that database check, or that issues a loan the check should have blocked, is committing a state-lending-law violation that regulators use civil penalties and license revocation to enforce. A small multi-location lender operating 5-15 storefronts across one or more states currently tracks per-location license renewal, per-state rate-cap and rollover rules, and per-transaction database-query completion on a compliance manager's spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific loan issued today actually cleared the required state-database query before the cash went out the door.

**What to build:** A per-location state lending-license renewal and bond tracker; a state-specific rate-cap and rollover-rule reference engine flagging any loan structured outside that state's limits; and a pre-loan database-query completion gate blocking a transaction from being marked funded until the required state loan-database check is confirmed clear.

**Skill fit:** State small-dollar-lending statutes and rate-cap formulas are public, structured regulatory text, and several state loan-verification databases publish an integration API, making the pre-loan query gate a direct data-integration and rules-engine problem — the same deterministic "block the transaction until the compliance record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a small-dollar-lending niche entirely distinct from the Earned Wage Access Provider idea (Session 158) and every other financial-services idea already in this repo, none of which involves a per-location state lending license or a mandatory pre-loan state-database query of this kind.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the license-renewal tracker, the rate-cap/rollover reference engine, and the pre-loan database-query gate for a single state's database integration. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small multi-location lenders in that state.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — the Community Financial Services Association of America's (CFSA) and state small-dollar-lender association member directories reach exactly the small multi-location operators currently reconciling per-location licensing and database-query compliance on a spreadsheet with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per lender depending on the number of licensed locations. 25-35 lenders × $420/mo avg = $10,500-14,700/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every loan issued reopens its own database-query and rate-cap compliance check, and every physical location's own license recurs on its own independent renewal clock — a continuous, transaction-and-location-driven compliance stream tied directly to the lender's own loan volume.

**Biggest risk:** The regulatory landscape here draws real political and enforcement scrutiny, and rules shift by state legislative session; mitigate by building the rate-cap/rollover engine as an easily updated rules library rather than hardcoded logic, and by launching first in a single state with a well-documented database-query API before expanding state by state.

**Growth path:** Side project serving 5-6 lenders in one state → state small-dollar-lender association channel presence → 25-35 lenders across a few states replaces-full-time-salary.
