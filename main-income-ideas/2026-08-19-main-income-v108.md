### 1. Employee Turnover Analytics for Small Manufacturers
**Problem:** A plant manager running 40-100 production employees knows turnover is high — they're hiring 3-4 new people every month to fill the same roles — but they don't know where the problem actually lives. Is it 1st shift or 3rd shift? Is it one supervisor whose team keeps quitting? Is it a specific job classification with a 90-day dropout pattern? The HR data to answer these questions exists in ADP, Gusto, or Paycom — hire date, termination date, department, shift, job code, supervisor — but nobody has connected it to a turnover analysis. The plant manager gets a monthly headcount report from HR and an annual turnover percentage from their payroll provider, neither of which tells them anything actionable. Meanwhile, each production employee turnover costs the plant $3,000-7,000 in recruiting, onboarding, and productivity loss — a plant losing 40 employees per year at $5,000 average cost is burning $200,000 annually on a problem that's concentrated in one or two places they can't see.
**What to build:** An employee turnover analytics tool that integrates with ADP Workforce Now and Gusto via their APIs (or accepts a CSV export for payroll systems without public APIs), and produces three core outputs: a turnover rate breakdown by shift, department, supervisor, and job classification — so the plant manager sees immediately that 3rd shift under one supervisor has a 68% annualized turnover rate vs. 22% for every other supervisor; a 90-day new hire survival curve showing what percentage of new hires make it to 30, 60, and 90 days by department and shift (the dropout pattern is almost always front-loaded, and knowing when it spikes tells the manager where to intervene); and a monthly cost-of-turnover estimate using the plant's configured cost-per-hire and lost-productivity assumptions, so the problem has a dollar figure attached to it in every conversation with ownership.
**Skill fit:** ADP and Gusto both have developer APIs for HR and payroll data; the CSV export path covers the remainder. The turnover rate calculation is a straightforward cohort analysis: terminated employees in the period ÷ average headcount, bucketed by the desired dimension. The survival curve is a standard Kaplan-Meier-style analysis applied to employee tenure data — well within the builder's data analytics background and achievable without ML. The cost-of-turnover model is a configurable multiplication of headcount × rate × cost-per-hire.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — ADP API or CSV import, turnover rate dashboard by shift/department/supervisor, 90-day survival curve, and the monthly cost estimate. A plant manager who sees that one supervisor's team turns over at 3x the plant average — and can walk into a conversation with the HR director with a dollar figure — has gotten career-defining value from the tool in the first week.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — plant managers and HR directors at small manufacturers are reachable through the National Federation of Independent Business (NFIB) member community, the Society for Human Resource Management (SHRM) manufacturing special interest group, manufacturing LinkedIn groups, and ADP's partner marketplace. A workforce consultant who works with small manufacturers is a high-leverage referral channel — they're already having the turnover conversation and need a tool to give their analysis structure.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249-499/month per plant depending on headcount. Average client at $350/month. 30 plants × $350/month = **$10,500/month**. Upsell: a retention intervention module ($99/month add-on) — a 60-day new hire check-in survey (2 questions, sent via SMS) that flags at-risk new hires before they quit, giving the supervisor a chance to intervene while there's still time.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** The turnover data accumulates continuously as employees are hired and terminated, making the survival curves and rate trends more statistically meaningful with each month of history. A plant manager who has 12 months of turnover data by supervisor and shift won't rebuild that from scratch in Excel — and the cost-of-turnover figure has become part of how they justify headcount and HR investments to ownership.
**Biggest risk:** Payroll system fragmentation — small manufacturers use dozens of different HR and payroll platforms. Mitigation: the CSV export path (a standard export from any payroll system) is the universal fallback and is sufficient for the MVP. ADP and Gusto integrations are added based on which platforms the first 10 customers use.
**Growth path:** Side project → CSV import, turnover rate dashboard, 90-day survival curve, and cost estimate live, first 5-8 plant clients. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-16 plants, add ADP Workforce Now API integration, add the 60-day new hire check-in survey module, add exit interview tracking (a 3-question SMS survey sent on the last day). Replaces-full-time-salary → 30+ plants, add Gusto and Paycom integrations, expand to distribution centers and warehouses (identical turnover dynamics, even higher frequency), add a retention benchmarking report (your turnover rate by shift vs. regional manufacturing peers using anonymized aggregate data).

### 2. SMS Marketing for Single-Location Local Businesses
**Problem:** A salon, barbershop, gym, chiropractor, or local restaurant has a customer phone list — collected through booking software, loyalty programs, or simple sign-up sheets — and is doing essentially nothing with it. Email marketing open rates average 20-25% for local businesses; SMS open rates are consistently 95-98%, with most messages read within 3 minutes of receipt. Full-featured SMS marketing platforms like Podium ($400+/month), Attentive ($500+/month), and Klaviyo ($150+/month with SMS add-ons) are built for multi-location operations or e-commerce brands and priced accordingly. The single-location business owner who just wants to send a promotional text once a month to their 400-person customer list — "Book by Friday, get 20% off your next cut" — has no product built for them at a price they'll pay without flinching.
**What to build:** A stripped-down SMS marketing tool for single-location local businesses — dead simple: the owner imports their customer list (CSV or direct integration with Square, Acuity Scheduling, or Vagaro for salons and barbershops), writes a text message in a single text box, picks a send time, and sends. A contact opt-out list maintained automatically (any customer who replies STOP is removed permanently and never messaged again, fully TCPA compliant). A delivery and response report after each send: messages sent, delivered, opted out, and any replies. Monthly send limits to keep the tool in the "occasional promotional message" use case rather than high-frequency spam territory. That's the entire product — no automations, no segments, no drip sequences. The value is access to the channel, not sophistication.
**Skill fit:** Twilio for SMS delivery (well-documented API, per-message pricing that keeps costs low at the volume a single-location business sends). CSV import and Square/Acuity API integrations for contact list sync. TCPA-compliant opt-out handling is a solved problem — Twilio handles STOP keyword responses automatically. The UI is a single-page form: list selector, message composer with character count, scheduled send time, send button. The entire application is buildable in a weekend.
**MVP scope:** 1 weekend — CSV upload, Twilio SMS delivery, opt-out handling, and a post-send delivery report. The product is intentionally minimal. A barbershop owner who sends a "Book your summer cut this week, mention this text for $10 off" message to 380 customers and gets 22 bookings in 48 hours has a conversion rate 5x better than anything they've seen from email. That's the product pitch.
**Time to first $:** 7-14 days — the distribution strategy is direct and local: walk into local salons, barbershops, gyms, and chiropractors in your city, demonstrate the product on a laptop, and offer a 30-day free trial with a setup assist. The first 5-10 customers are within walking distance. At scale, the Square and Vagaro partner marketplaces (Vagaro has 100,000+ beauty and fitness businesses) are the organic distribution channels.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $49-99/month per business. This is a volume game. 200 businesses at $69/month = **$13,800/month**. The acquisition strategy is local saturation first (one city, 50-100 businesses), then the Vagaro and Square partner marketplaces for national scale. Low price point means low churn — a business owner paying $69/month who saw 20 bookings from last month's text message doesn't cancel.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** The customer list grows over time as the business continues collecting phone numbers at checkout and booking. A business owner who has 400 customers in the tool today has 550 in 6 months — the value of the tool grows without any product changes, and the switching cost is the contact list they've built and the opt-out history they'd lose.
**Biggest risk:** TCPA compliance exposure — SMS marketing to consumers has strict opt-in requirements (explicit written consent before sending marketing messages). Mitigation: the onboarding flow requires the business owner to confirm they have explicit opt-in consent for every contact before sending; the tool provides an opt-in consent template they can use at checkout or booking; and STOP handling is automatic via Twilio. The compliance responsibility is on the business owner, with the tool making compliance easy rather than an afterthought.
**Growth path:** Side project → CSV upload, Twilio delivery, opt-out handling, and delivery report live, first 5-10 local business clients through direct local outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 40-60 businesses, list in Vagaro and Square partner marketplaces, add Acuity Scheduling integration for salons and spas, add a simple reply management view (showing any customer responses to a campaign). Replaces-full-time-salary → 200+ businesses, add a scheduling feature for recurring monthly sends (so the owner can set up their promotional calendar once per quarter), expand to restaurants and food service with OpenTable or Toast contact integrations.

### 3. Pricing Digest for Airbnb Hosts with 1-5 Properties
**Problem:** An individual Airbnb host with 1-5 properties sets a nightly rate when they first list and adjusts it occasionally based on gut feel. Dynamic pricing tools like PriceLabs ($20-40/property/month) and Beyond Pricing ($19.99/property/month) exist but require learning a dashboard, configuring base prices and seasonal adjustments, and actively managing a system that assumes the host wants granular control over every night. Most individual hosts don't — they want to know if they're leaving money on the table and what to change, without logging into another tool. The result: hosts with 2-3 properties are underpricing peak weekends by 30-40% and overpricing slow midweek periods, leaving $4,000-12,000 in annual revenue behind, because no one has told them what comparable listings nearby are charging and they don't have time to research it themselves.
**What to build:** A weekly email digest for Airbnb hosts — no dashboard, no app, just a useful email every Monday morning. The email contains: the host's upcoming 4-week calendar with their current listed prices, what comparable listings within 1 mile and similar bedroom count are charging for the same nights (pulled from AirDNA's API or a similar short-term rental data provider), a simple flag for each week — "You're priced 28% below comps this weekend, consider raising to $189" or "Midweek looks soft in your area, consider a 15% discount to fill it" — and a one-click link to Airbnb's calendar to make the adjustment. That's the entire product. The host reads the email in 3 minutes, makes 2-3 price changes, and closes the email. No dashboard, no login, no configuration beyond the initial setup (list your properties, set your email preferences).
**Skill fit:** AirDNA has a commercial API for comparable listing data (pricing per night, occupancy rates, competitor listings by location and bedroom count). The comparable selection logic is a configurable radius and bedroom-count filter. The email digest is a formatted HTML email via SendGrid — built with a template that renders cleanly on mobile. The price comparison and flag logic is a simple percentage calculation: host price vs. median comp price for each night, flagged if the gap exceeds a configurable threshold (e.g., 20%). No ML, no complex optimization — the value is in delivering the insight to the host's inbox rather than requiring them to seek it out.
**MVP scope:** 1-2 weekends — AirDNA API integration for comp data, a manual property onboarding form (host enters their Airbnb listing URL and email), the weekly digest email generation and delivery, and a simple flag logic for underpriced and overpriced nights. A host who raises their Saturday night rate from $142 to $189 based on the first digest email and gets booked the same day has validated the product in 72 hours.
**Time to first $:** 14-30 days — individual Airbnb hosts are reachable through Reddit communities (r/airbnb has 200,000+ members, r/airbnbhosts has 80,000+), BiggerPockets short-term rental forums (the most active STR investor community), Airbnb host Facebook groups (Airbnb Hosts Community — 100,000+ members), and a Product Hunt launch. The weekly digest format is inherently shareable — a host who likes the email forwards it to their co-host or their friend who also has a listing.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $29-49/month per property. Average host with 2 properties at $39/property/month = $78/month per host. 175 hosts × $78/month = **$13,650/month**. Low price point, high volume, zero support overhead (it's just an email). The AirDNA API cost is the primary variable expense — budget $5-8/property/month, leaving a healthy margin at $39/property/month.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** The host never has to think about the tool — the email arrives every Monday regardless. The value compounds as the tool builds a history of the host's pricing decisions and their resulting occupancy (pulling occupancy data from the host's Airbnb calendar), eventually allowing the digest to show "last time you priced this weekend at $189, you booked 2 nights earlier than average." Low churn because cancellation requires active effort that the $39/month price point never motivates.
**Biggest risk:** AirDNA API cost structure — if AirDNA's per-query pricing is high relative to the subscription revenue per property, margins compress. Mitigation: evaluate Transparent (another STR data provider) as an alternative; at the scale needed to validate the model (50-100 properties), negotiate a flat monthly data access rate rather than per-query pricing.
**Growth path:** Side project → AirDNA integration, weekly digest email, price comparison flags live, first 20-30 host properties through Reddit and BiggerPockets. Replaces-part-time-income → 75-100 host properties, add occupancy data from the Airbnb host API (Airbnb has a host-facing API available through their official partner program) to track whether price changes correlate with booking speed. Replaces-full-time-salary → 175+ host properties (88+ hosts), add VRBO property support using comparable data, add a quarterly seasonal pricing recommendation (what to charge for Thanksgiving week, holiday weekends, and local events based on prior-year comp data).

### 4. Milestone Billing Tracker for Custom Manufacturers
**Problem:** A custom cabinet shop, furniture maker, metal fabricator, or sign shop takes a 40-50% deposit when an order is placed and collects the remainder at delivery or in scheduled milestone installments. With 20-50 active orders at any given time, each at a different payment stage, tracking which orders have received deposits, which are awaiting a mid-production milestone payment, and which are past due on their final balance is done on a whiteboard, a spreadsheet, or inside QuickBooks with inconsistent job naming that makes cross-order visibility impossible. The result: the shop owner discovers a final balance is 45 days past due while the customer has already received delivery — the leverage to collect is gone. A custom shop doing $1.2M in annual revenue with a 30-day average collection lag on final balances is carrying $100,000 in outstanding receivables at any moment, with no systematic view of which ones are aging into bad debt.
**What to build:** A milestone billing tracker for custom manufacturers — a tool where each order is entered with its production milestones and corresponding payment amounts (deposit due at order, 30% at material confirmation, 30% at delivery). The tool connects to QBO via API to check which payments have been received against each order, and surfaces a live order billing dashboard: every active order, its current stage, the next payment due, the amount, and how many days until it's expected. A "past due" flag for any milestone payment that has gone past its expected collection date without a matching payment in QBO. Automated payment reminder sequences: when a milestone payment is due, the tool sends the customer a payment link (Stripe-generated invoice) via email and SMS; if unpaid after 7 days, a second reminder; after 14 days, a flag to the owner for manual follow-up. A monthly collections report: total billed in the period, total collected, average days to payment by milestone type (deposits vs. finals), and a list of the top 10 outstanding balances by age.
**Skill fit:** QBO API for payment matching against order records. Stripe for generating payment links per invoice. The milestone stage logic is a configurable order template (the shop defines their standard milestone structure once, applies it to every new order with amount overrides). The past-due detection is a date comparison: expected payment date vs. today, cross-referenced against QBO receipts. SMS via Twilio, email via SendGrid. The data model is clean: orders, milestones, payments, customers.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — manual order entry with milestone configuration, QBO API payment matching, the billing dashboard with past-due flags, and the automated payment reminder sequence with Stripe payment links. A shop owner who discovers 3 final balances that have been sitting unpaid for 60+ days — and recovers them in the first week through the automated reminder sequence — has covered the annual subscription cost in the first billing cycle.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — custom shop owners are reachable through the Woodworking Business Network, the Cabinet Makers Association, metal fabrication communities on LinkedIn and Instagram, and sign industry associations (ISA — International Sign Association has 2,600+ member companies). A fractional CFO or bookkeeper who serves custom manufacturers is a high-leverage referral channel — they're already dealing with the collections lag problem on behalf of their clients.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149-299/month per shop depending on order volume. Average client at $199/month. 45 shops × $199/month = **$8,955/month**. Upsell: a production milestone tracking module ($79/month add-on) — where the shop also tracks the production stage of each order (materials ordered, materials received, in production, quality check, ready for delivery), so the billing milestone and the production milestone are linked and the owner can see at a glance which orders are at risk of missing their delivery-and-collect date.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new order adds new milestones to track. The payment history per customer builds over time — the tool can flag customers who consistently pay late on final balances, so the shop can require a larger deposit upfront on their next order. The QBO payment matching runs continuously, making the dashboard self-updating without any manual data entry after the initial order setup.
**Biggest risk:** Order entry friction — custom shops have highly variable order structures, and requiring manual milestone configuration for every order adds upfront work. Mitigation: the tool supports order templates (a "standard cabinet job" template pre-configures the deposit/mid/final split), and new orders clone from the template with amount overrides — reducing per-order setup to 2-3 minutes.
**Growth path:** Side project → manual order entry, QBO payment matching, billing dashboard, and automated reminder sequence live, first 5-8 shop clients through woodworking and fabrication communities. Replaces-part-time-income → 15-20 shops, add the production milestone tracking module, add WooCommerce integration for shops selling custom products through their own e-commerce store. Replaces-full-time-salary → 45+ shops, add a customer portal where clients can see their order's production status and make milestone payments directly, expand to event and wedding vendors (photographers, caterers, florists) with identical deposit-and-milestone billing dynamics.

### 5. Onboarding Completion Tracker for Small B2B SaaS Companies
**Problem:** A B2B SaaS company at $150K-$1M ARR has 50-300 customers and no dedicated customer success team. New customers get a welcome email with a getting-started guide and then fall into a black hole — the founder or a part-time support person has no systematic view of which customers have completed onboarding vs. which are stuck and silently heading toward churn. The problem is predictable: a customer who never connects their first integration, never invites a teammate, and never reaches the product's activation event (whatever action correlates with long-term retention) churns within 90 days at 3-5x the rate of customers who do. But without instrumentation, the founder only knows which customers churned, not which ones are at risk right now. By the time the customer cancels, the intervention window is closed.
**What to build:** An onboarding completion tracker for small B2B SaaS companies — a tool that integrates with the company's product via a lightweight event tracking API (the SaaS company sends events like "user_invited_teammate," "integration_connected," "first_report_generated" via a simple REST endpoint or a Segment integration), and produces three outputs: a per-customer onboarding checklist view showing which activation milestones each customer has and hasn't completed, a risk flag for customers who are past their expected activation timeline without completing key milestones (configurable: "flag any customer who hasn't connected an integration within 14 days of signup"), and an automated nudge email triggered by the gap — personalized to the specific missing milestone ("You haven't connected your first integration yet — here's a 5-minute walkthrough"). A weekly digest for the founder: new signups this week, customers who completed activation, customers newly flagged as at-risk, and customers who churned (with their onboarding completion status at the time of churn, so the founder can see the pattern).
**Skill fit:** A REST API endpoint that accepts event payloads (customer ID, event name, timestamp) — a few hours of work. Segment integration as the premium connection option for SaaS companies already using Segment. The milestone completion logic is a per-customer checklist state machine: each configured milestone is either complete (event received) or pending (no event yet). The risk flag is a date calculation: days since signup vs. expected activation window, cross-referenced against milestone completion. Nudge emails via SendGrid with per-milestone templates the SaaS company configures. The weekly digest is a standard aggregation.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — REST event API, configurable milestone checklist, at-risk customer flag, automated nudge email per missing milestone, and the weekly founder digest. A SaaS founder who sees 12 customers flagged as at-risk in the first week — and sends targeted nudges that convert 4 of them into activated users — has directly saved $X in MRR from churn that would otherwise have been invisible until it happened.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — B2B SaaS founders are the most reachable technical buyer on the internet: Indie Hackers (100,000+ SaaS builders), MicroConf community, SaaStr community, Lenny's Newsletter readership, and Product Hunt. A "we reduced early churn by 34% in 60 days" case study shared on Indie Hackers generates 50-100 upvotes and immediate inbound from founders with the exact same problem. This is a product its own target customer would use — the builder is the ideal early customer.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-499/month per SaaS company depending on customer count. Average client at $320/month. 30 companies × $320/month = **$9,600/month**. Upsell: a customer health score module ($99/month add-on) — a continuous health score per customer (not just during onboarding) built from product usage events, combining login frequency, feature adoption breadth, and support ticket volume into a single 0-100 score, giving the founder an at-a-glance view of which customers are thriving and which are quietly disengaging months after their initial activation.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new customer the SaaS company signs sends new events, automatically appearing in the onboarding tracker without any manual entry. The historical activation data — which milestones correlate with long-term retention in that specific product — becomes a proprietary dataset that improves the risk flag accuracy over time. A SaaS company with 6 months of activation data and a validated "customers who don't connect an integration within 14 days churn at 4x the rate" finding won't abandon that insight by switching tools.
**Biggest risk:** Event instrumentation burden — requiring the SaaS company to add event tracking calls to their product code creates an integration step that can slow onboarding. Mitigation: provide a copy-paste JavaScript snippet and a Segment integration that covers the most common tech stacks with minimal engineering effort; the REST API approach means any language can send events in under an hour of work; and provide a "quick start" template with pre-configured milestone definitions for the 5 most common SaaS onboarding patterns (connect integration, invite teammate, create first project, set up billing, generate first report).
**Growth path:** Side project → REST event API, configurable milestone checklist, at-risk flag, nudge email, and weekly digest live, first 5-8 SaaS company clients through Indie Hackers and MicroConf. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-18 companies, add Segment integration, add the customer health score module, add a Slack notification option (at-risk alerts delivered to the founder's Slack instead of or in addition to email). Replaces-full-time-salary → 30+ companies, add cohort analysis (how does onboarding completion rate and time-to-activate compare across signup month cohorts?), expand to B2B SaaS companies up to $3M ARR where a part-time CS person exists but lacks tooling.
