### 1. Amusement Ride & Inflatable/Bounce House Operator NAARSO Certification & ASTM F2374 Inspection Compliance
**Problem:** Operators running portable amusement rides, inflatable bounce houses, and carnival-style attractions across a rotating schedule of fairs, corporate events, and private parties must have each device inspected and tagged by a state amusement ride safety bureau (typically housed in a state Department of Agriculture, Labor, or a dedicated ride-safety office) before it can legally operate at a public event, with the exact inspection cycle and tag validity period varying by state and device class — a ride or inflatable set up at an event without a current, matching state inspection tag is one of the most common findings behind a state shutdown order at a fair or festival, an outcome that costs the operator the booking fee and often the venue relationship. Every inflatable device must be manufactured, set up, and operated according to the ASTM F2374 standard, and operators must maintain NAARSO (National Association of Amusement Ride Safety Officials) operator or inspector certification for staff running the rides — a certification most state ride-safety bureaus require as a precondition for the device's own operating permit — while a per-event setup checklist (anchoring, stake pattern, wind-speed monitoring threshold, weight-limit signage) must be completed and documented before the first rider or child steps on, since an incident traced back to a missed anchoring step or an ignored wind-speed threshold is exactly the liability exposure a state investigation and the operator's insurer will scrutinize first. A small operator running 10-25 units across dozens of weekend bookings a season manages per-device inspection-tag currency, per-staff NAARSO certification renewal, and per-event setup-checklist documentation with no dedicated compliance function — and a device deployed on an expired inspection tag, or an event set up without a completed anchoring/wind-threshold checklist on file, is exactly the gap a state ride-safety inspector or a post-incident investigation is built to catch.
**What to build:** A per-device inspection-tag tracker computing each unit's next-due state inspection date with tiered alerts and a "cleared to deploy" gate blocking a unit from being assigned to a booking once its tag lapses; a per-staff NAARSO certification currency tracker; and a per-event setup checklist (anchoring pattern, wind-speed monitoring log, weight/age signage confirmation) with a hard block preventing an event from being marked "ready for riders" until the checklist is complete.
**Skill fit:** State amusement ride safety bureau inspection-tag data and NAARSO's certification structure are public, checkable regulatory sources with a well-defined per-device and per-staff record requirement, and the deploy-eligibility gate plus the per-event setup checklist are the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a portable-attraction trade most small operators currently track on a paper unit list with no systematic tag-currency cross-check against booking assignments.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the inspection-tag tracker with the deploy-eligibility gate, the NAARSO certification tracker, and the per-event setup checklist; land the first 5-8 operators through IAAPA (International Association of Amusement Parks and Attractions) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — IAAPA member directory and state inflatable/amusement operator association meetings, where small operators already navigate per-device tag currency and per-event setup documentation across a growing rotating booking calendar.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149/mo base + $15/mo per device tracked. Average client (10 devices) = $149 + $150 = $299/mo. 30-40 operators × $299/mo = **$8,970-11,960/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every booking opens its own setup-checklist event, device inspection tags recur on their own state-set cycle independent of booking volume, and every new unit purchased expands the tracked device count — a continuous, per-event compliance stream tied directly to the operator's booking calendar and fleet growth.
**Biggest risk:** A device deployed at an event on an expired state inspection tag, or an event set up without a completed anchoring and wind-speed-threshold checklist on file, is exactly the gap a state ride-safety inspector or post-incident liability investigation is built to catch — the deploy-eligibility gate and the setup checklist are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → inspection-tag tracker, NAARSO certification tracker, and setup checklist deployed, first 5-8 operator retainers through IAAPA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 16-20 operators. Replaces-full-time-salary → 30-40 operators, pushing past $8.97-11.96K/mo.

### 2. Firing Range Operator NRA RSO Certification & OSHA Lead Exposure Compliance
**Problem:** Indoor and outdoor firing range operators employ Range Safety Officers who must hold current NRA RSO (or an equivalent state-recognized) certification before independently supervising a firing line, renewed on a multi-year cycle with recertification training — a range caught operating a firing line without a currently certified RSO present is one of the most common findings behind an insurer's coverage denial after an incident, since RSO-on-duty status is a standard policy condition. Every range, especially indoor ranges, operates under OSHA's lead exposure standard (29 CFR 1910.1025), which requires periodic airborne lead monitoring at the firing line and in employee breathing zones, and — critically — a blood-lead-level medical surveillance program for any employee whose monitored exposure exceeds the action level, with surveillance tests due on a recurring schedule (every six months once triggered, more frequently if a result comes back elevated) that most small range operators track informally at best; a range that cannot produce current air-monitoring results and up-to-date employee blood-lead surveillance records during an OSHA inspection faces citations that scale directly with the number of affected employees and the duration of the lapse. Range facility ventilation systems themselves typically require periodic performance verification against NRA or range-manufacturer airflow specifications to keep airborne lead within safe limits, and a range that skips this verification is compounding its OSHA exposure with a genuine health hazard to staff and patrons. A small range running several RSOs and range-safety staff across daily public and private-lane hours manages staggered RSO certification renewal, the OSHA-mandated air-monitoring and blood-lead surveillance calendar, and ventilation-verification scheduling with no dedicated compliance function — and an employee whose blood-lead surveillance lapsed past its due date, or a firing line staffed without a currently certified RSO, is exactly the gap an OSHA inspection or an insurer's post-incident audit is built to catch.
**What to build:** A per-staff RSO certification currency tracker with a "cleared to supervise the line" gate; an OSHA lead-exposure compliance calendar computing each employee's next-due blood-lead surveillance test from their triggering exposure result, with tiered alerts before the deadline; and a ventilation-system performance-verification tracker confirming airflow testing stays current against the range's required schedule.
**Skill fit:** OSHA's lead exposure standard and its surveillance-trigger logic, plus NRA's RSO certification structure, are public, well-documented regulatory sources with a clear per-employee record requirement, and the surveillance-deadline calculator plus the supervise-eligibility gate are the same deterministic "compute the deadline from the trigger event / block the action until the compliance record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a facility-operation category distinct from the firearms dealer's transfer-and-recordkeeping compliance already covered in this repo.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the RSO certification tracker, the blood-lead surveillance deadline calculator, and the ventilation-verification tracker; land the first 5-8 ranges through National Association of Shooting Ranges (NASR) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — National Association of Shooting Ranges (NASR) member directory and state range-owner association meetings, where small range operators already navigate staggered blood-lead surveillance deadlines and RSO scheduling by hand.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299/mo base + $20/mo per RSO-certified employee tracked. Average client (4 RSOs) = $299 + $80 = $379/mo. 24-32 ranges × $379/mo = **$9,096-12,128/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every employee's exposure monitoring result can retrigger its own surveillance cycle, RSO certifications renew on staggered independent schedules per staff member, and ventilation verification recurs on its own fixed interval — a continuous, health-and-safety-driven compliance stream tied directly to staffing and facility hours.
**Biggest risk:** An employee whose blood-lead surveillance test lapsed past its OSHA-mandated due date, or a firing line staffed without a currently certified RSO, is exactly the finding an OSHA inspection or insurer post-incident audit is built to catch — the surveillance-deadline calculator and the RSO supervise-eligibility gate are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → RSO tracker, blood-lead surveillance calendar, and ventilation-verification tracker deployed, first 5-8 range retainers through NASR outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-16 ranges. Replaces-full-time-salary → 24-32 ranges, pushing past $9.10-12.13K/mo.

### 3. Auto Body & Collision Repair Shop EPA NESHAP 6H, RCRA Hazardous Waste & I-CAR Certification Compliance
**Problem:** Auto body and collision repair shops operating a paint booth are regulated under EPA's National Emission Standards for Hazardous Air Pollutants Subpart HHHHHH ("6H," Paint Stripping and Miscellaneous Surface Coating Operations), which requires spray technicians to complete EPA-recognized training in reduced-emission spray techniques and the shop to use compliant spray equipment and filtration — a shop that cannot produce technician training records during an EPA or state air-quality inspection is committing an independently citable 6H violation regardless of how well the actual paint job turned out. Every shop also generates regulated waste streams — used oil, spent solvents, paint waste, and often refrigerant recovered during A/C service — that trigger RCRA hazardous-waste-generator recordkeeping obligations, including proper waste characterization, manifest documentation for off-site disposal, and generator-status recalculation as waste volume grows, with a shop that lets its generator status drift upward without adjusting its recordkeeping practice facing exposure during an EPA or state environmental agency audit. Increasingly, insurer direct-repair program (DRP) network eligibility — the referral pipeline that drives the majority of a collision shop's volume — requires the shop to maintain current I-CAR or OEM-specific certification per repair procedure (structural welding, ADAS calibration, aluminum repair), certifications that expire on staggered cycles per technician and per procedure, and a shop that loses DRP eligibility because a key certification lapsed loses its highest-volume referral channel overnight. A small shop running 5-15 technicians across structural, cosmetic, and calibration work manages EPA 6H spray-technician training currency, RCRA waste-generator documentation, and per-technician, per-procedure I-CAR/OEM certification tracking with no dedicated compliance function — and a spray technician without current 6H training on file, or a certification lapse that silently drops the shop from an insurer's DRP network, is exactly the gap an EPA inspection or a lost-referral audit is built to catch.
**What to build:** An EPA 6H spray-technician training currency tracker with tiered renewal alerts; a RCRA hazardous-waste-generator log tracking waste stream volumes, characterization, and disposal manifests with a generator-status recalculation flag as volume grows; and a per-technician, per-procedure I-CAR/OEM certification tracker with a "DRP-eligible" dashboard flagging any certification approaching expiration before it silently drops the shop from an insurer network.
**Skill fit:** EPA 6H's training requirement, RCRA's generator-status thresholds, and I-CAR's certification structure are public, checkable regulatory and certifying-body sources with a well-defined per-technician and per-waste-stream record requirement, and the training-currency tracker plus the DRP-eligibility dashboard are the same deterministic "compute the deadline / block the action until the compliance record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a repair-facility category distinct from every route-based automotive-service idea already covered in this repo.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the 6H training tracker, the RCRA waste-generator log, and the I-CAR/OEM certification dashboard; land the first 5-8 shops through Society of Collision Repair Specialists (SCRS) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — Society of Collision Repair Specialists (SCRS) member directory and state collision repair association meetings, where small shop owners already navigate staggered I-CAR certification renewal and RCRA waste documentation with no dedicated compliance staff.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $12/mo per technician tracked. Average client (10 technicians) = $199 + $120 = $319/mo. 28-38 shops × $319/mo = **$8,932-12,122/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every waste pickup generates its own manifest-reconciliation event, I-CAR/OEM certifications renew on staggered independent cycles per technician and per procedure, and EPA 6H training currency must be maintained continuously — a compliance stream that grows directly with the shop's technician count and repair-procedure mix.
**Biggest risk:** A spray technician without current EPA 6H training on file, or a lapsed I-CAR certification that silently drops the shop from an insurer's direct-repair program network, is exactly the gap an EPA inspection or a lost-referral-volume audit is built to catch — the training-currency tracker and the DRP-eligibility dashboard are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → 6H training tracker, RCRA waste log, and I-CAR/OEM dashboard deployed, first 5-8 shop retainers through SCRS outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 16-20 shops. Replaces-full-time-salary → 28-38 shops, pushing past $8.93-12.12K/mo.

### 4. Commercial Swimming Pool & Spa Service Company CPO/AFO Certification, MAHC Pool Code & VGBA Compliance
**Problem:** Commercial pool and spa service companies servicing hotels, apartment complexes, gyms, and HOA pools must staff technicians holding Certified Pool Operator (CPO) or Aquatic Facility Operator (AFO) certification, renewed on a multi-year cycle, before those technicians can legally perform water-chemistry management and equipment servicing at a public pool in most jurisdictions — a technician servicing a commercial pool without current certification on file is one of the most common findings a state or local health department pool inspection targets. Every serviced pool must maintain documented water-chemistry logs (chlorine/bromine level, pH, alkalinity) at the testing frequency the local health code requires under the Model Aquatic Health Code (MAHC) framework most jurisdictions have adopted, and a pool with a gap in its chemistry-log record during a routine health department inspection faces an immediate compliance citation regardless of whether the water was actually safe at the time. Critically, every public pool and spa must have a compliant anti-entrapment drain cover under the federal Virginia Graeme Baker Pool and Spa Safety Act (VGBA) — a drain cover that has exceeded its manufacturer-rated service life or was never verified compliant is a mandatory pool-closure finding the moment a health inspector or the service company itself checks the cover's date-stamp, since an entrapment incident is one of the most severe liability events a commercial pool operator can face. A small pool service company running 4-10 technicians across dozens of commercial pool accounts manages per-technician CPO/AFO certification currency, per-pool chemistry-log completeness against local code frequency, and per-pool VGBA drain-cover compliance-date tracking with no dedicated compliance function — and a pool serviced by an uncertified technician, or a drain cover past its compliance date that nobody caught, is exactly the gap a health department inspection is built to catch.
**What to build:** A per-technician CPO/AFO certification tracker with a "cleared to service" gate; a per-pool water-chemistry logging tool enforcing the local code's required testing frequency with a gap-flag for any missed reading; and a per-pool VGBA drain-cover compliance tracker computing each cover's service-life expiration and blocking the pool from being marked "compliant" once it lapses.
**Skill fit:** MAHC's chemistry-logging frequency standards and VGBA's drain-cover compliance requirement are public, structured regulatory sources with a well-defined per-pool, per-reading record requirement, and the service-eligibility gate plus the drain-cover compliance tracker are the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance record clears / compute the deadline from the trigger event" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an aquatic-facility-service category with no prior coverage in this repo.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the CPO/AFO certification tracker, the water-chemistry logging tool with gap detection, and the VGBA drain-cover tracker; land the first 5-8 companies through Pool & Hot Tub Alliance (PHTA) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — Pool & Hot Tub Alliance (PHTA) member directory and state commercial pool service association meetings, where small service company owners already navigate chemistry-log completeness and drain-cover compliance-date tracking across a growing commercial account list.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $179/mo base + $18/mo per technician tracked. Average client (5 technicians) = $179 + $90 = $269/mo. 34-45 companies × $269/mo = **$9,146-12,105/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every service visit generates its own chemistry-log entry, drain-cover compliance dates recur on independent per-pool cycles as covers age, and technician certification renews on its own multi-year schedule — a high-frequency, per-visit compliance stream tied directly to the company's serviced-pool count.
**Biggest risk:** A pool serviced by an uncertified technician, or a VGBA-noncompliant drain cover that lapsed past its service life without being flagged, is exactly the finding a health department inspection is built to catch — the certification gate and the drain-cover tracker are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → CPO/AFO tracker, chemistry-logging tool, and drain-cover tracker deployed, first 5-8 company retainers through PHTA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 18-22 companies. Replaces-full-time-salary → 34-45 companies, pushing past $9.15-12.11K/mo.

### 5. Vehicle Towing & Repossession Company State Licensure, Notification & Storage Lien Compliance
**Problem:** Towing and vehicle repossession companies hold state towing/recovery-agent licensure — frequently a distinct repossession-agent credential layered on top of a general tow-operator license, each renewed on its own cycle — and every nonconsensual private-property tow is governed by a state notification statute requiring the tow company to notify local police within a defined window (commonly 30-60 minutes) and photo-document the vehicle's condition and violation before towing, since a tow performed without the required police notification or documentation is one of the most common findings behind a state consumer-complaint investigation and can trigger fines or license suspension. Every repossession performed on a lender's behalf must comply with UCC Article 9's notice-of-sale requirements — a specific written notice sent to the debtor within a defined timeframe before the vehicle can be resold at auction, with the exact required disclosures varying by state — and a repossessed vehicle sold without a compliant notice-of-sale on file exposes both the repossession company and the lender to a deficiency-judgment challenge in court. Every vehicle held in the company's lot, whether towed or repossessed, accrues under a state storage-lien statute capping the daily storage rate the company may charge and the notice it must send the vehicle's owner before the vehicle can be sold to satisfy the lien — a vehicle held past its statutory notice deadline, or storage fees charged above the statutory cap, is exactly the fact pattern behind the consumer complaints state regulators pursue most aggressively against tow operators. A small company running 3-8 trucks across nonconsensual tows and lender repossessions manages per-tow police-notification timing, per-repossession UCC notice-of-sale deadlines, and per-vehicle storage-lien notice/rate compliance with no dedicated compliance function — and a tow performed without documented police notification, or a vehicle sold past its lien-notice deadline, is exactly the gap a state licensing board audit or a consumer complaint investigation is built to catch.
**What to build:** A per-tow police-notification timer starting at hookup and flagging any tow approaching or past the statutory notification window before it becomes a violation; a per-repossession UCC Article 9 notice-of-sale tracker computing each vehicle's required notice deadline and disclosure content from the state's specific statute; and a per-vehicle storage-lien tracker computing the current storage fee against the state's daily rate cap and the lien-notice deadline before a sale can proceed.
**Skill fit:** State towing notification statutes, UCC Article 9's notice-of-sale requirements, and state storage-lien caps are public, structured regulatory data with well-defined per-transaction deadlines, and the notification timer plus the notice-of-sale deadline tracker are the same deterministic "compute the deadline from the trigger event / block the action until the compliance record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a licensed vehicle-recovery trade most small companies currently track on a paper tow log with no systematic notification or notice-of-sale deadline cross-check.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the police-notification timer, the UCC notice-of-sale tracker, and the storage-lien fee/notice tracker; land the first 5-8 companies through Towing and Recovery Association of America (TRAA) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — Towing and Recovery Association of America (TRAA) member directory and state towing and repossession association meetings, where small company owners already navigate notification-window timing and UCC notice-of-sale deadlines by hand across dozens of monthly tows and repossessions.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $20/mo per truck tracked. Average client (4 trucks) = $199 + $80 = $279/mo. 32-43 companies × $279/mo = **$8,928-11,997/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every tow opens its own police-notification countdown, every repossession opens its own UCC notice-of-sale deadline, and every held vehicle accrues its own storage-lien notice clock — a high-frequency, per-transaction compliance stream tied directly to the company's daily tow and repossession volume.
**Biggest risk:** A tow performed without documented police notification within the statutory window, or a vehicle sold past its storage-lien notice deadline or above the state's rate cap, is exactly the finding a state licensing board audit or consumer complaint investigation is built to catch — the notification timer and the storage-lien tracker are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → notification timer, UCC notice-of-sale tracker, and storage-lien tracker deployed, first 5-8 company retainers through TRAA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 18-22 companies. Replaces-full-time-salary → 32-43 companies, pushing past $8.93-12.0K/mo.

Idea #5 (towing & repossession) has the cleanest, most frequent statutory-deadline failure mode of the five — a police-notification window that starts counting down at every single hookup — but per-company economics depend more on truck count than the compliance mechanism itself, making TRAA's national directory more important than usual to reach density quickly.
