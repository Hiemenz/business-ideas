# Main Income Ideas - 2026-11-12 (Session 187)

## Who this is for
A 29-year-old software engineer with data analytics skills who wants to replace a full-time salary ($8-15K+/mo) within 12-24 months by building solo, in evenings and weekends, with no outside funding. Every idea below must have a realistic path to first revenue inside 90 days and a named, reachable distribution channel.

## Lens for this session: Alex Hormozi
Built from the $100M Offers / $100M Leads frameworks:
- **Value equation:** Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood of Success) / (Time Delay x Effort & Sacrifice). This session attacks time delay: jobs sold but waiting, reorders forgotten, hires that take months.
- **Grand Slam Offer:** performance-priced or guarantee-backed.
- **Core Four lead sources:** warm outreach to existing customers and former employees, and content and ads aimed at the people a business needs next.

Per standing feedback, none of these are compliance or regulatory tools. Each solves a revenue-leakage or growth problem.

## Ideas

### 1. Consumables Reorder Engine for Water-Delivery, Filter and Supply Businesses

**Problem:** Businesses that sell consumables (bottled water, HVAC filters, coffee supplies, janitorial products, propane) depend on customers remembering to reorder. Customers run out, buy elsewhere and the business never knows why they left.

**What to build:** Estimate each customer's usage cycle from order history and send a reorder reminder a few days before they run out, with a one-tap reorder link. Offer a subscription at a small discount. Show the owner customers overdue for a reorder, and the revenue from recovered reorders.

**Grand Slam angle:** "We bring back overdue reorders, and you pay 10% of the revenue we recover."

**Skill fit:** Usage-cycle estimation, messaging and one-tap order pages.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days through small distributor and delivery-business owner groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** A business with 600 customers recovers about $2,500/mo of reorders. 10% = $250/mo, plus a $59/mo platform fee, about $310/mo. 40 businesses x $310 = **$12,400/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Subscriptions convert one-off buyers into recurring revenue, and the usage data improves forecasting.

**Biggest risk:** Usage cycles vary widely by customer. Start with businesses that have regular, predictable consumption.

**Growth path:** Water and filters, then coffee service, uniform and linen supply, pet food and restaurant supplies.

### 2. Recruiting & Crew-Rehire Funnel for Seasonal Trades and Landscaping Companies

**Problem:** A landscaper, roofer or pool company loses 30-50% of its crew each season and starts hiring from scratch in March. Job-board posts bring unqualified applicants, and last year's good workers are never contacted. Every unfilled position is lost jobs.

**What to build:** An automated rehire campaign to last season's crew (text and email with a return bonus), then a recruiting page and ad template with a quick SMS pre-screen (license, availability, experience) that books interviews. Track applicants, interviews, hires and cost per hire.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Fill your open positions by April, or you do not pay." The fee is $250 per hire that lasts 30 days.

**Skill fit:** SMS flows, landing pages and applicant tracking.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-60 days, best timed for late winter.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** A company hiring 8 workers a season pays $2,000, spread as about $170/mo plus a $49/mo platform fee = $219/mo. 55 companies x $219 = **$12,045/mo**. Fees lump in spring, so keep a monthly platform fee to smooth revenue.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Seasonal hiring recurs every year, and the rehire list builds an asset the owner will pay to keep.

**Biggest risk:** Seasonal revenue. Add a year-round track for trades that hire continuously (HVAC, plumbing, electrical).

**Growth path:** Seasonal trades, then year-round trades, restaurants and cleaning companies.

### 3. Front-Desk Phone-Prompt Tool for Small Clinics, Salons and Service Shops

**Problem:** The person answering the phone decides whether a caller books, books the bigger service or never calls back. Most front-desk staff have no script and no prompt for add-ons or new-customer offers, and callers who ask "how much?" are lost.

**What to build:** A small on-screen prompt pad that appears during a call: how to answer common questions ("how much is...?"), which add-on to offer for the service requested and how to capture the caller's details. Log call outcomes with one tap (booked, quote only, no), and show booking rate by staff member.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Lift your call-to-booking rate by 10% in 45 days, or the first two months are free."

**Skill fit:** Lightweight web app, script library and call-outcome analytics.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days through local-business owner groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $79/mo. 140 shops = **$11,060/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** It changes behavior at the point where revenue is won or lost, and the call-outcome data is valuable to the owner.

**Biggest risk:** Staff adoption. Keep it to one screen and run a 15-minute onboarding.

**Growth path:** Small clinics, salons and shops, then home-service dispatch, auto shops and dental practices.

### 4. Sold-But-Unscheduled Job Backlog Tracker for Contractors

**Problem:** A contractor sells $200K of work in a good month, and some of it sits unscheduled for weeks because the office is busy. The customer grows impatient, cancels or reviews badly, and the cash is delayed. Few owners can see how many sold jobs have no date.

**What to build:** Connect to the contractor's job system and quote history. Show every sold job with its sold date, days waiting, deposit status and customer contact. Alert when a sold job passes a set wait time without a date, send the customer a "we have you in the queue" update and show revenue stuck in backlog.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Get every sold job on the calendar within 7 days, or the first quarter is free."

**Skill fit:** Job-system integrations, alerts and customer messaging.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-60 days through contractor owner groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $129/mo. 90 contractors = **$11,610/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** It speeds cash collection and reduces cancellations, which an owner can see in the numbers.

**Biggest risk:** Integrations differ. Start with a CSV import and one job system.

**Growth path:** Roofers and remodelers, then solar, HVAC installers, fence and deck builders.

### 5. Multi-Location Benchmark Dashboard for Owners of 3-10 Small Business Locations

**Problem:** An owner of five salons, cafes or gyms sees five sets of numbers in five places and cannot tell which location is underperforming or why. They find out when a manager quits or the profit drops.

**What to build:** Pull sales, labor, and customer counts from each location's POS or booking system into one view. Rank locations on a few key ratios (revenue per labor hour, average ticket, repeat rate) and flag any location that falls below its own trend or the group average. Send a weekly summary with the single biggest gap.

**Grand Slam angle:** "We point out one fixable gap per month worth at least $1,000, or you do not pay."

**Skill fit:** Multi-source ETL, ratio analytics and weekly reports.

**MVP scope:** 4 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 45-75 days through multi-unit owner groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $49 per location per month, minimum 3. A 5-location owner pays $245/mo. 48 owners x $245 = **$11,760/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** The more locations an owner adds, the more the dashboard is worth, and benchmarks across customers become a data asset.

**Biggest risk:** Locations on different systems. Start with owners whose locations share one POS.

**Growth path:** Small chains, then franchisees, regional restaurant groups and multi-clinic practices.
