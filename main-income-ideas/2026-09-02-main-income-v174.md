### 1. Industrial Hemp Farmer & Processor USDA 7 CFR Part 990 Production-Plan, THC Pre-Harvest Testing & DEA-Lab Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Under the 2018 Farm Bill's implementing rule at 7 CFR Part 990, every licensed hemp grower operates under a state, tribal, or USDA hemp production plan requiring every planted lot's GPS location to be reported to the hemp program before planting, and every lot must be sampled and tested for total THC (delta-9 THC plus its potential THCA conversion, against the 0.3% dry-weight threshold) by a DEA-registered laboratory within a fixed 30-day pre-harvest window — a grower who harvests before a compliant test result is filed, or whose sample was drawn or tested outside that window, has harvested a federally noncompliant crop regardless of its actual THC content, and a lot testing above the 1% "negligent violation" threshold starts a rolling five-year violation count that ends, on a third violation, in a five-year federal disqualification from growing hemp at all. Processors buying from a network of small growers must additionally confirm each contracted grower's own state licensure and per-lot pre-harvest test result before accepting delivery, since processing an out-of-compliance lot exposes the processor's own finished product to seizure. A small hemp farm or fiber/grain processor running a dozen to several dozen lots across a growing season currently tracks lot GPS locations, sampling-window deadlines, and DEA-lab test results on a farm manager's spreadsheet, with no systematic alert before a specific lot's 30-day pre-harvest testing window opens or closes.

**What to build:** A per-lot GPS/GIS planting-report tracker feeding the state hemp program's required pre-plant report; a 30-day pre-harvest sampling-and-testing-window countdown per lot with a DEA-registered-lab result upload and a "cleared to harvest" gate; and a rolling five-year negligent-violation history tracker computing a grower's violation count against the federal disqualification threshold.

**Skill fit:** USDA's 7 CFR Part 990 sampling-window and THC-threshold rules and each state's hemp production plan are public, structured federal and state regulatory data with a concrete per-lot, per-season trigger, and the harvest-readiness gate is the same deterministic "block the action until the record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a hemp-cultivation niche this repo has never addressed.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the per-lot planting-report tracker, the pre-harvest testing-window countdown with lab-result upload, and the violation-history tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent hemp farms and small processors.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the National Hemp Association and the U.S. Hemp Growers Association member directories reach exactly the small growers and processors currently tracking pre-harvest testing windows on a spreadsheet with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $15/mo per lot tracked. Average client (25 lots) = $199 + $375 = $574/mo. 20-24 farms/processors × $574/mo = **$11,480-13,776/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new planting season reopens the per-lot report-and-testing cycle from scratch, every lot added mid-season reopens its own 30-day window countdown, and a processor's growing roster of contracted growers compounds the number of simultaneously tracked pre-harvest test results.

**Biggest risk:** The licensed hemp-grower market contracted sharply after the CBD-market crash. Mitigate by targeting processors and multi-grower cooperatives that aggregate compliance tracking across many contracted growers, where the per-lot cross-check load is highest and a single missed testing window threatens an entire processing run.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10 farms ($5.7K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 20-24 farms/processors ($11.5-13.8K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding a buyer-facing compliant-lot certificate a processor can hand a downstream fiber, grain, or extract purchaser at the point of sale.

### 2. Mosquito Abatement & Public Health Vector Control Contractor District-Contract, ULV Spray-Notification & Arbovirus Surveillance-Reporting Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Private contractors running mosquito-control programs under contract to municipal mosquito abatement districts, HOAs, golf courses, and event venues operate a public-health pesticide-application category distinct from general structural pest control: applying an ultra-low-volume (ULV) adulticide by truck-mounted or aerial sprayer requires a state "public health pest control" or "mosquito control" applicator category separate from the general/ornamental categories a typical pest control license covers, and a district contract typically requires the contractor to post a public spray-notification (date, treatment zone, product) ahead of every application under the district's own notification ordinance — a contractor who sprays a zone without the required advance notification is exactly the finding a resident complaint or a state pesticide-agency inspection is built to catch. Larval-source (larvicide) treatments in standing water carry their own separate per-site treatment log, and many district contracts obligate the contractor to submit weekly mosquito trap-count data and, where the district participates in state arbovirus surveillance, pooled-mosquito West Nile Virus or Eastern Equine Encephalitis test results to the state health department on a fixed reporting cycle — a contractor who misses a surveillance-reporting deadline jeopardizes the district's own state public-health funding and its own contract renewal. A small vector-control contractor running several district and HOA contracts across mosquito season currently tracks applicator-category certification, per-zone spray notifications, and weekly surveillance-data submission on a technician's paper log and a shared spreadsheet, with no systematic alert before a specific zone's required notification or a district's surveillance-reporting deadline is missed.

**What to build:** A public-health/mosquito applicator-category certification and CE tracker with a "cleared to apply" gate; a per-zone spray-notification and larvicide-treatment log matched to each district contract's own notification ordinance; and a weekly trap-count and arbovirus surveillance-data submission tracker computing each district's reporting deadline with an overdue alert.

**Skill fit:** State public-health pesticide-applicator category rules and district spray-notification ordinances are public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-zone and per-week trigger, and the notification-and-surveillance-reporting gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a public-health vector-control niche entirely distinct from the Commercial Pesticide Applicator Multi-State License idea already in this repo (Session 45) and the Aquatic Herbicide Applicator & Lake/Pond Management Company idea (Session 173), as detailed in the Ground Rules section above.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the applicator-certification tracker, the per-zone spray-notification/larvicide log, and the surveillance-reporting tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent vector-control contractors.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the American Mosquito Control Association (AMCA) member directory and its annual meeting reach exactly the small contractors currently tracking spray notifications and surveillance-reporting deadlines on a shared spreadsheet with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249/mo base + $10/mo per treatment zone tracked. Average client (20 zones) = $249 + $200 = $449/mo. 24-30 contractors × $449/mo = **$10,776-13,470/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every spray event reopens the zone-notification requirement, every week of mosquito season reopens the surveillance-data submission cycle, and every new district or HOA contract a growing contractor wins compounds the number of simultaneously tracked notification ordinances.

**Biggest risk:** The business is heavily seasonal, concentrated in spring-through-fall mosquito season. Mitigate with annual billing tied to the pre-season certification-renewal cycle, and by expanding into year-round HOA and commercial-property contracts in warm-climate states where mosquito season runs nearly all year.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 contractors ($5.4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 24-30 contractors ($10.8-13.5K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding a district-facing surveillance-report export a contractor can submit directly to the state health department the moment a reporting deadline arrives.

### 3. Fur Trapper & Raw Fur/Hide Dealer State Trapping License, Fur Dealer Permit & CITES Pelt-Tagging Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Licensed fur trappers who sell raw pelts to buyers, and the licensed fur dealers who buy and resell them, operate under a dual state-licensing regime plus a federal wildlife-treaty tagging obligation most small operations track from memory: every trapper must hold a current state trapping license (renewed annually, often gated behind a mandatory trapper-education certificate for first-time license holders) and report harvest by species and county on the state's own fur-harvest survey, while anyone buying pelts for resale must separately hold a bonded state fur dealer or fur buyer license with its own per-transaction recordkeeping requirement — seller name, license number, species, and county of harvest for every pelt purchased — and a dealer who buys from an unlicensed trapper or fails that transaction-record requirement is exactly the finding a state game warden's routine dealer inspection is built to catch. Because several furbearer species (bobcat, river otter, lynx, and others) resemble CITES-listed species closely enough to require export control, every such pelt sold across a state line or exported must carry a CITES tag issued through the state wildlife agency's own tagging program before the sale — a dealer who ships a bobcat or otter pelt without its CITES tag has committed a federal Endangered Species Act/Lacey Act violation regardless of the legality of the underlying harvest. A small fur dealer running a buying season with dozens of trapper-suppliers across several counties currently tracks trapper license verification, per-transaction purchase records, and CITES tag applications and issuance on a paper ledger and a shoebox of state tag stubs, with no systematic gate confirming a specific trapper's license is current and a specific bobcat or otter pelt's CITES tag is on file before that pelt is resold or shipped.

**What to build:** A per-trapper-supplier state license and trapper-education-certificate verification tracker with a "cleared to buy from" gate; a per-transaction purchase-record log (seller, species, county, price) feeding the dealer's own state fur-harvest reporting; and a per-pelt CITES tag application-and-issuance tracker for bobcat, otter, and lynx blocking a resale or shipment from being marked ready until the tag is confirmed on file.

**Skill fit:** State trapping/fur-dealer licensing rules and the CITES furbearer tagging program are public, structured state and federal wildlife-regulatory data with a concrete per-trapper and per-pelt trigger, and the CITES tag-gate is the same deterministic "block the action until the record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a fur-trade niche this repo has never addressed.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the trapper-license verification tracker, the per-transaction purchase-record log, and the CITES tag tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent licensed fur dealers.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — the National Trappers Association and Fur Takers of America member and dealer directories reach exactly the small fur dealers currently tracking CITES tags and trapper-license verification on a paper ledger with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $4/mo per active trapper-supplier tracked. Average client (50 suppliers) = $199 + $200 = $399/mo. 25-30 dealers × $399/mo = **$9,975-11,970/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every buying season reopens the trapper-license verification and per-transaction recordkeeping requirement from scratch, and every bobcat or otter pelt bought reopens the CITES tag-and-shipment gate — a continuous, harvest-season-and-species-driven compliance stream.

**Biggest risk:** A very small dealer buying from a handful of local trappers has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward dealers running a full regional buying season across dozens of trapper-suppliers and regularly handling CITES-tagged species, where a single untagged shipment is a real federal-violation risk.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 dealers ($4.8K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 25-30 dealers ($10-12K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding a buyer-facing CITES-tag verification export a dealer can hand an international garment manufacturer at the point of export sale.

### 4. Sleep Study & Home Sleep Apnea Testing Center AASM Accreditation, BRPT Technologist Credential & CMS Medical-Necessity Documentation Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent sleep diagnostic centers running in-lab polysomnography (PSG) and home sleep apnea testing (HSAT) programs must maintain American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AASM) facility accreditation — a five-year cycle requiring a documented policies-and-procedures manual, a board-certified sleep medicine physician medical director, and a facility inspection — while every polysomnographic technologist performing or scoring a study must hold a current Board of Registered Polysomnographic Technologists (BRPT) RPSGT credential or the equivalent state license, renewed on its own CE cycle; a study scored by a technologist whose credential has lapsed, or a facility operating past its AASM re-accreditation deadline, is exactly the finding a CMS or private-payer audit is built to catch, and can force a clawback of every claim billed during the lapse. Every study also carries its own CMS/insurer medical-necessity documentation trail — a physician order specifying the clinical indication, a signed Attestation of Medical Necessity for certain HSAT devices, and, for a CPAP therapy to be reimbursed following a positive diagnosis, a documented 90-day compliance-data download (average nightly usage hours) the DME supplier needs before the CPAP claim can even be submitted — a center that can't produce the specific order-to-result-to-compliance-data chain for a given patient loses that claim on audit regardless of the diagnosis's clinical accuracy. A small independent sleep center running dozens of studies a month across in-lab and home-testing modalities currently tracks technologist credential renewal, AASM re-accreditation deadlines, and per-patient medical-necessity documentation on a scheduling coordinator's spreadsheet and a stack of paper orders, with no systematic gate confirming a specific technologist is credentialed and a specific patient's documentation chain is complete before a study is billed.

**What to build:** A per-technologist BRPT/state-license credential and CE renewal tracker with a "cleared to score studies" gate; an AASM facility-accreditation and physician medical-director currency tracker with a re-accreditation countdown; and a per-patient medical-necessity documentation-chain tracker (order, study result, attestation, CPAP compliance-data window) gating a claim from being marked billable until every required record is on file.

**Skill fit:** AASM's published accreditation standards and BRPT's certification and CE structure are public, structured credentialing data with a concrete per-technologist and per-patient trigger, and the claim-readiness gate is the same deterministic "block the action until the record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a sleep-diagnostics niche entirely distinct from the Hyperbaric Oxygen Therapy Center idea and the Durable Medical Equipment (DME) Supplier idea already in this repo, as detailed in the Ground Rules section above.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every study scored reopens the technologist-credential and documentation-chain check, AASM re-accreditation recurs on its own five-year cycle independent of study volume, and every new technologist a growing center hires compounds the number of simultaneously tracked BRPT credentials.

**Biggest risk:** A very small center running only a handful of studies a month for one referring physician has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. ### 5. Cathodic Protection Technician & Pipeline Corrosion-Control Contractor NACE/AMPP Certification & PHMSA Rectifier-Survey Compliance Tracker

A small CP contracting crew running routes across dozens of rectifiers and hundreds of miles of client pipeline currently tracks technician certification levels, the rolling two-month rectifier-inspection cycle, and annual pipe-to-soil survey due dates on a field technician's paper route sheet, with no systematic alert before a specific rectifier's inspection interval or a specific segment's annual survey lapses.

**What to build:** A per-technician NACE/AMPP certification-level tracker with a "cleared to perform this scope" gate; a per-rectifier bi-monthly inspection-interval tracker computing each unit's due date from its last reading; and a per-pipeline-segment annual pipe-to-soil potential survey tracker feeding a PHMSA-inspection-ready compliance export.

**Skill fit:** PHMSA's 49 CFR 192/195 CP-survey-interval rules and NACE/AMPP's published certification-level structure are public, structured federal regulatory and standards-body data with a concrete per-rectifier and per-segment trigger, and the interval-countdown-plus-certification-gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a pipeline-corrosion-control niche this repo has never addressed.

**Biggest risk:** A very small crew servicing a handful of rectifiers for one municipal utility has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need.
