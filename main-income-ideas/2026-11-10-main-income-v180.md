# Main Income Ideas - 2026-11-10 (Session 180)

## Who this is for
A 29-year-old software engineer with data analytics skills who wants to replace a full-time salary ($8-15K+/mo) within 12-24 months by building solo, in evenings and weekends, with no outside funding. Every idea below must have a realistic path to first revenue inside 90 days and a named, reachable distribution channel.

## Lens for this session: Alex Hormozi
Built from the $100M Offers / $100M Leads frameworks:
- **Value equation:** Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood of Success) / (Time Delay x Effort & Sacrifice).
- **Grand Slam Offer:** performance-priced or guarantee-backed.
- **Leads levers:** warm outreach, customer referrals and partner (affiliate-style) lead flow, the cheapest lead sources a small business has.

Per standing feedback, none of these are compliance or regulatory tools. Each solves a revenue-leakage or growth problem.

## Ideas

### 1. Job-Site Neighbor Marketing for Roofers, Pest Control and Home-Service Companies

**Problem:** A roofer finishes a $14K job and a street of homeowners watched the truck for three days, but the company does nothing with that attention. Neighbors are the warmest cold audience a contractor has: the same roof age, the same storm, social proof visible from the driveway.

**What to build:** When a job is marked complete, the tool pulls the 40-60 nearest addresses, generates a personalized postcard ("We just finished your neighbor's roof at 123 Oak St") and mails it through a print API. It tracks a unique phone number or QR code per job, scans in responses and reports cost per lead and per booked job by neighborhood.

**Grand Slam angle:** "We send it, we pay for the postage, you pay $60 per appointment it generates."

**Skill fit:** Address lookup, print and mail APIs, call tracking and attribution analytics.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days through roofing and pest-control owner groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** A company completing 15 jobs per month produces 600 postcards, about 8 appointments at $60 = $480/mo, with a $99/mo platform fee, about $580/mo. 22 companies x $580 = **$12,760/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Each completed job becomes a marketing asset, so volume grows with the client's business, and cost per lead data shows the owner a reliable channel.

**Biggest risk:** Postage and print costs eat margin. Price per appointment high enough to cover them and run batch mailings.

**Growth path:** Roofing, then pest control, solar, window replacement, landscaping and painting.

### 2. Trial-Lesson-to-Student Conversion Engine for Independent Music and Dance Schools

**Problem:** A music or dance studio runs trial lessons and wins fewer than half of them. The follow-up is one email from the front desk, and the studio never learns why families decline. Each student who stays is worth $1,500-3,000 over their time at the studio.

**What to build:** Capture trial bookings, send reminders, then a post-trial sequence: teacher's note, a "your child's first-month plan," a limited-time enrollment bonus. Track trial-to-enrollment rate by teacher, instrument and lead source and flag the families that need a call.

**Grand Slam angle:** "We lift your trial conversion within 90 days, or you pay nothing." A $40 fee per enrolled student.

**Skill fit:** Messaging flows, calendar integration and funnel analytics.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-60 days through studio-owner Facebook groups and music-education forums.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** A studio running 25 trials a month converts 5 more families = $200/mo plus a $79/mo platform fee, about $280/mo. 45 studios x $280 = **$12,600/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Conversion data by teacher helps the owner coach staff, and each enrolled student is recurring revenue for the studio.

**Biggest risk:** The studio's schedule software may not export trials. Start with a simple intake form that feeds the tool.

**Growth path:** Music and dance studios, then martial-arts schools, tutoring centers, swim schools and art studios.

### 3. Proposal View-Tracking & Follow-Up Engine for Small Agencies and Consultants

**Problem:** A small agency sends a $10K proposal PDF and waits. It cannot see whether the prospect opened it, who forwarded it or which page they read, and it follows up too early or too late.

**What to build:** A proposal link that tracks views, time per page and forwards to new viewers. When a prospect re-opens the pricing page, the owner gets an alert with a suggested message. Add a pipeline board and win-rate by proposal type.

**Grand Slam angle:** "A 45-day free trial. If you do not win at least one proposal you would have lost, you do not pay."

**Skill fit:** Web tracking, notifications and dashboards.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days through agency-owner Skool groups and consultant communities.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $59/mo per user, with 3 users per typical agency = $177/mo. 65 agencies x $177 = **$11,505/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Proposal win-rate data per proposal type shows the agency how to price, and it integrates into every sales process.

**Biggest risk:** Crowded space (DocSend, PandaDoc). Differentiate with price and a simple "next message" suggestion for tiny teams.

**Growth path:** Agencies and consultants, then freelancers, contractors and B2B sales reps.

### 4. First-30-Days Onboarding Engine for Local Subscription Businesses

**Problem:** Local subscription businesses (meal prep, flower subscriptions, farm boxes, coffee subscriptions) lose most cancellations in the first 30-60 days. The customer is unsure how to use the product and gets only a receipt.

**What to build:** A timed onboarding sequence after the first delivery: how-to message, recipe or usage idea, a check-in text, a customization offer and a referral invitation at the happy moment. A dashboard shows where in the first 30 days customers pause or cancel.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Cut early cancellations by a third in 90 days, or pay nothing." The fee is 15% of subscription revenue retained above baseline.

**Skill fit:** Lifecycle messaging, cohort analytics and subscription-platform integrations (Stripe, Shopify, Cratejoy).

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days through food-entrepreneur and subscription-box communities.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** A business with 400 subscribers at $60/mo saves 12 extra subscribers per month = about $720/mo of added MRR in the first month, compounding. A 15% share of the lift is about $108/mo in month one and grows as saved subscribers stay. Combine with a $99/mo platform fee. 40 businesses at about $300/mo average = **$12,000/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Saved subscribers accumulate, so the tool's fee grows without new clients.

**Biggest risk:** Measuring the baseline. Capture 3 months of history before launch.

**Growth path:** Food subscriptions, then flowers, wine clubs, pet boxes and local service memberships.

### 5. Referral-Partner Program Manager for Painters, Stagers and Movers Working with Realtors

**Problem:** Painters, home stagers, cleaners and movers get much of their work from real-estate agents, but they manage those relationships from memory. A contractor does not know which agent sent the most jobs, when an agent last sent one or who is drifting to a competitor.

**What to build:** A partner CRM: log each agent, the jobs they sent and the revenue. Surface partners who have gone quiet for 45+ days with a suggested touch (a thank-you gift, a closing-gift offer, a coffee). Offer a co-branded "listing-day checklist" the contractor can send agents. Show revenue by partner.

**Grand Slam angle:** A "one new active referral partner in 60 days or you do not pay."

**Skill fit:** CRM design, outreach templates and revenue attribution.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-60 days through painter, stager and mover groups, and local real-estate investor meetups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $99/mo. 110 contractors = **$10,890/mo**. A $29/mo agent-facing add-on for the checklist could add more.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** The partner list is the contractor's most valuable asset and the data inside the tool is hard to leave behind.

**Biggest risk:** Low willingness to pay among very small crews. Qualify for firms with 3+ crews doing over $300K a year.

**Growth path:** Painters, stagers and movers, then cleaners, handymen, photographers and mortgage-adjacent vendors.
