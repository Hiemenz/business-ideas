### 1. Dental Practice Patient Reactivation Engine
**Problem:** The average dental practice has 1,500-4,000 patients of record. At any given time, 25-40% of them — 400 to 1,600 people — haven't been in for 18+ months and are effectively lapsed. The practice's revenue hinges almost entirely on recall: getting active patients to come back every 6 months. But lapsed patients are invisible until someone digs through the practice management software (Dentrix, Eaglesoft, Curve) and exports a spreadsheet that immediately goes stale. The standard response is a form letter mailed to everyone, which gets a 1-3% response rate and costs $800-1,500 in printing and postage for a mid-size practice. The real problem is that "lapsed patients" aren't one segment — they're a mix of patients who moved, patients who had a bad experience, patients who lost insurance, and patients who just got busy and forgot. Treating them identically produces identical mediocre results. One reactivated patient who completes a hygiene visit and a crown is $800-2,500 in revenue. A practice with 600 lapsed patients that reactivates even 10% annually recovers $500,000-$1.5M in lifetime patient value.
**What to build:** A patient reactivation engine that integrates with the practice's PMS via API (Dentrix, Curve Dental, and Eaglesoft all have integration frameworks or data export capabilities) and automatically identifies, segments, and communicates with lapsed patients. Core functionality: a lapsed patient dashboard that updates daily (last visit date, last treatment type, insurance status if available, contact information currency), automatic segmentation by lapse duration and last procedure type (6-month lapse with only a cleaning vs. 2-year lapse with a treatment-planned crown = different messages), and a multi-channel outreach sequence: a personalized SMS on day 1 ("Hi [first name], we miss you at [practice name] — it's been a while and we wanted to check in. Ready to get back on the schedule?"), a follow-up email at day 7 with a one-click booking link, a second SMS at day 14 with a different message framing, and a final email at day 21. A booking link that opens directly into the practice's online scheduler (Zocdoc, NexHealth, or the PMS's own patient portal). A reactivation tracking dashboard: how many sequences are running, how many opened/clicked, how many booked, revenue attributed to reactivated patients in the last 90 days.
**Skill fit:** The hardest part is the PMS integration — Dentrix and Eaglesoft use older data models but have established integration partner programs. Curve Dental has a REST API. An alternative entry point: a CSV import workflow (practice exports lapsed patient list from PMS, uploads to the tool) that delivers 90% of the value without API complexity and can be built in a weekend. SMS via Twilio, email via SendGrid, booking via NexHealth API or a generic calendar widget. The data segmentation logic is simple analytics work.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — CSV import, 3-touch SMS + email sequence via Twilio/SendGrid, one-click booking link generation, and a basic response tracking dashboard. No PMS API required in V1. The sequence alone, eliminating the manual follow-up that currently doesn't happen, is worth $300/month to any practice owner.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — dental practice owners are reachable through dental-specific Facebook groups (Dental Nachos, The Dental Practice Heroes — combined 50,000+ members), dental office manager communities, and dental continuing education event sponsors. One case study ("Practice recovered $47,000 in 90 days from lapsed patients") converts the channel into a repeatable sales motion.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/month per practice depending on patient list size and sequence volume. 35 practices at $400/month = **$14,000/month**. Natural upsell: a new patient nurture sequence ($99/month add-on) that follows up with patients who scheduled but didn't show, and with new patients after their first appointment to build loyalty before they lapse.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Patient reactivation is never finished — the lapsed list refreshes every month as patients cross the 18-month threshold. The practice runs sequences continuously. Switching to a different tool means re-importing the list and losing the historical engagement data (who already received which message), which practices won't do once they're 6 months in. Every new practice the owner opens is a new subscription.
**Biggest risk:** HIPAA — patient contact information and treatment history are PHI. Mitigation: a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) with every practice customer (standard for any dental software vendor), PHI never stored beyond what's needed to send the message, and data processing entirely within a HIPAA-eligible AWS environment. The BAA is a 2-page document; it's not a blocker, it's a checkbox that builds trust.
**Growth path:** Side project → CSV import, outreach sequences, booking link, and response tracking live, first 5-8 practice clients. Replaces-part-time-income → 15-20 practices, add Curve Dental API integration and a treatment plan follow-up sequence for patients with unscheduled accepted treatment. Replaces-full-time-salary → 35+ practices, add NexHealth integration for direct chair-side booking, expand to optometry and chiropractic practices with identical dynamics.

### 2. Job Quoting and Capacity Visibility for Custom Manufacturing Shops
**Problem:** A custom machine shop, sheet metal fabricator, or injection mold maker with 10-50 employees quotes jobs by gut feel and spreadsheet. The owner or estimator looks at a drawing, estimates hours on each machine, multiplies by a shop rate, and sends a quote — without knowing what's already scheduled on those machines for the next 8 weeks. The result is systematic under-quoting when the shop is busy (the job gets added to an overloaded schedule, incurring overtime and rushing costs that destroy the quoted margin) and over-quoting when the shop is slow (jobs are lost to competitors because the estimator was padding for capacity that didn't exist). There's a second problem: shops have no historical data on how long jobs actually take vs. how long they were quoted to take. Every job is estimated from scratch with no feedback loop — the same part estimated at 12 hours gets quoted that way forever even if it consistently takes 16. ERP systems for manufacturers (Shoptech E2, Epicor, JobBOSS) solve this comprehensively but cost $25,000-100,000 to implement and require a full operational overhaul. The shop with 15 employees and $3M in revenue doesn't need an ERP — it needs a shop-floor capacity calendar and a quoting tool that learns from completed job history.
**What to build:** A job quoting and capacity management tool built for small custom manufacturers — a machine/work center capacity calendar showing what's scheduled on each machine or work center for the next 12 weeks, updated as new jobs are confirmed. A quoting module where the estimator enters the operations required for a new job (e.g., laser cutting: 3 hours, press brake: 2 hours, welding: 4 hours), the tool shows the earliest available start date for each operation given current machine loading, and calculates a delivery date promise based on realistic capacity rather than a guess. A job actuals tracker: when a job completes, the actual hours per operation are entered, and the system maintains a per-part-number and per-operation historical average that feeds future quotes for the same or similar work. A win/loss tracker on quotes: which jobs were won, which were lost, and at what price — so the owner can see where the shop is systematically over- or under-priced relative to competitors.
**Skill fit:** A calendar/scheduling data model, a simple constraint-based scheduling engine (jobs can't start until a machine is available), historical average calculation, and a clean web UI. No exotic algorithms required — the capacity calendar is the main product and is fundamentally a Gantt chart with utilization math underneath. The builder's analytics background handles the quoting accuracy feedback loop and the win/loss analysis.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — a machine capacity calendar, a job entry form with estimated hours per operation, an available-start-date calculator, and a delivery date promise generator. No actuals tracking in V1. A shop owner who can see that their laser cutter is booked solid for 6 weeks before promising a 3-week delivery is already getting value worth $300/month.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — custom manufacturing owners are reachable through the Practical Machinist forum (the largest online community for machinist and shop owners, 150,000+ members), the NTMA (National Tooling and Machining Association) member network, and LinkedIn outreach to "shop owner" and "operations manager" titles at SIC code 3599 (industrial machinery) companies. Manufacturing-focused podcasts (The Manufacturing Executive) accept guest spots and have audiences of exactly this buyer.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249-549/month per shop depending on machine count and user seats. 30 shops at $375/month = **$11,250/month**. Upsell: a customer pricing analytics module showing which customers are the most profitable (highest margin jobs, fastest payment, fewest revisions) and which are the worst — worth $100/month additional to any owner who wants to fire their worst customer.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** The capacity calendar is updated every time a new job is confirmed — that's multiple times per week for an active shop. The quoting tool is used for every new quote — that's daily for most shops. Switching costs are high because the historical job data (the actuals that improve future quote accuracy) lives in the system and loses value the moment it's abandoned. The owner also can't go back to quoting blind once they've seen the capacity view.
**Biggest risk:** Shops that run on relationship-based quoting ("I've been building these parts for this customer for 15 years, I don't need software to price them") may resist. Mitigation: lead with the capacity calendar, not the quoting module. Showing the owner a visual of which machines are overloaded and which are idle is immediately compelling regardless of how they quote.
**Growth path:** Side project → machine capacity calendar, job entry, available-start-date calculator, and delivery promise generator live, first 5 shop clients. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-15 shops, add job actuals tracking, historical quoting accuracy feedback, and win/loss tracker. Replaces-full-time-salary → 30+ shops, add customer profitability analytics and a basic scheduling optimizer that automatically sequences jobs to minimize machine idle time.

### 3. Shift Reliability Scoring and No-Show Prevention for Staffing Agencies
**Problem:** A light industrial, hospitality, or healthcare staffing agency places 200-1,000 shift workers per week at client facilities. The single most operationally damaging thing that happens to a staffing agency is a no-show: a worker who was confirmed for a 6am shift at a warehouse or hospital just doesn't appear, and the agency has 45 minutes to find a replacement or send the client a short-staffed morning. No-show rates at light industrial staffing agencies typically run 8-15% — meaning on a 300-worker placement week, 24-45 shifts go unfilled or are scrambled to fill at the last minute. The client relationship damage from repeated no-shows is severe — most agency contracts have SLAs with no-show rate penalties, and clients who experience 3+ no-shows in a month start shopping for a replacement agency. The agencies' response is usually ad hoc: a dispatcher who starts calling workers at 5am, a group text to a list of "reliable" workers, and a lot of guesswork. Every agency has workers who are 95% reliable and workers who no-show 1 in 4 shifts — but the assignment system treats them identically because reliability data lives only in the dispatcher's head.
**What to build:** A shift reliability scoring and no-show mitigation system for staffing agencies — a worker reliability score calculated from confirmed placement history: show-up rate, on-time rate, early cancellation rate (cancellations < 12 hours before shift = partial penalty), and quality feedback from client supervisors. A shift assignment engine that surfaces reliability scores when a dispatcher is filling an order — the dispatcher sees that Worker A is 96% reliable and Worker B is 71% reliable before choosing who to send. Automated shift confirmation: 24 hours before a shift, the worker gets an SMS confirmation request ("Reply YES to confirm your 7am shift at [client] on Tuesday. Reply NO if you can't make it.") — no-reply or NO triggers an immediate alert to the dispatcher and auto-opens the replacement queue, sorted by workers with the highest reliability scores who are available for that shift type. A bench management module: for every client account, the agency maintains a pre-qualified bench of backup workers for common shift types — the system automatically contacts the bench (in reliability score order) when a no-show occurs at 5am. A client no-show dashboard: shows each client their historical no-show rate, how it's trending, and how it compares to the agency average — turning a liability into a transparency feature.
**Skill fit:** The core is a worker data model with shift history, a scoring algorithm (weighted average of show-up rate with recency weighting — a no-show last week matters more than one 6 months ago), Twilio for SMS confirmations, and a clean dispatcher UI. The analytics work (reliability scoring, client reporting) is exactly the builder's background. No specialized staffing industry knowledge required beyond understanding the workflow.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — worker profile database, shift assignment logging, reliability score calculation, and Twilio-powered 24-hour SMS confirmation with dispatcher alert on non-reply. The SMS confirmation alone reduces no-show rates by 20-30% (workers who were going to no-show often just cancel when prompted, giving the agency recovery time). That improvement is immediately visible and worth $400/month to any agency dispatcher.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — staffing agency owners are reachable through ASA (American Staffing Association) member network, the Staffing Industry Analysts (SIA) community, and LinkedIn outreach to "staffing agency owner" and "staffing branch manager" titles. Staffing-specific conferences (Staffing World) have vendor expo floors where a demo booth generates 50+ qualified leads in a single afternoon. The no-show rate improvement is a metric every staffing agency owner cares about obsessively — the pitch takes 2 minutes.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $399-899/month per agency branch depending on weekly placement volume. 20 agencies (or branches) at $600/month = **$12,000/month**. Multi-branch agencies — the larger staffing firms that have 3-10 branch offices — pay per branch. An agency with 5 branches at $600/month = $3,000/month from a single customer. Upsell: a worker acquisition funnel that automatically retargets workers who applied but were never placed (often 30-50% of applicant volume) with new job alerts when relevant shifts open — worth $200-300/month additional.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Shift assignments happen every single working day. The SMS confirmation fires automatically for every confirmed shift. The reliability score updates with every shift outcome — it's a living data product that gets more accurate and more valuable with every week of use. Switching means losing the historical reliability data that took months to accumulate, which no dispatcher accepts mid-season when placement volumes are high.
**Biggest risk:** Worker-side adoption — workers need to respond to SMS confirmations, and some workers will ignore them. Mitigation: frame it to workers as a benefit ("We want to make sure you always get your preferred shifts — confirming quickly puts you at the top of the list") and track response rate as a metric in the reliability score. Non-responsive workers get a lower reliability score and get placed less — a natural incentive to engage.
**Growth path:** Side project → worker profile database, shift logging, reliability scoring, and SMS confirmation system live, first 4-5 agency branch clients. Replaces-part-time-income → 8-12 branches, add bench management, client no-show dashboard, and replacement queue automation. Replaces-full-time-salary → 20+ branches, add worker acquisition funnel and a predictive no-show model that flags high-risk shifts 48 hours in advance based on worker behavior signals (last-minute schedule change requests, low recent engagement with the agency).

### 4. Food Cost Variance Analytics for Independent Restaurants
**Problem:** A restaurant with $2-8M in annual revenue has a theoretical food cost — what the food should cost based on recipe costs multiplied by what was sold — and an actual food cost — what they actually spent on food invoices. The gap between them is called variance, and it represents money disappearing through theft, portioning errors, waste, miscounts, and supplier short-ships. Industry benchmarks say variance should be under 1% of food cost; most independent restaurants run 3-8% variance without knowing it, which on $500,000 in annual food spend is $15,000-$40,000 silently leaving the business every year. The owner knows their actual food cost percentage (from the P&L) and their theoretical food cost percentage (from recipe costing they did once in a spreadsheet) and knows those two numbers don't match. What they don't know is where the gap is: which menu items are running over, which prep station is wasting, whether the variance spikes on weekend nights (over-portioning under pressure) or on receiving days (supplier short-ships). Restaurant management platforms (Toast, Square) track sales. Accounting software tracks invoices. No tool automatically reconciles the two and surfaces variance by item, by station, and by time period — unless the restaurant pays $300-600/month for a platform like Restaurant365 or Margin Edge, which are excellent but require the restaurant to process all their invoices through the platform.
**What to build:** A food cost variance analytics tool that connects to the restaurant's POS via API (Toast, Square, and Lightspeed all have reporting APIs) to pull daily sales by menu item, and to the restaurant's invoice data via a supplier invoice email parser or a lightweight invoice upload flow. Core output: a weekly variance report by menu category (proteins, produce, dairy, dry goods) showing theoretical cost vs. actual cost vs. variance amount and percentage. A per-item variance drill-down: which menu items are running highest over their theoretical cost this week. A receiving variance log: for each supplier order, flag invoices where the delivered quantity or price differs from what was ordered. A time-pattern analysis: does variance spike on Friday/Saturday nights (portioning under pressure) or Monday mornings (waste from weekend over-prep)? The tool surfaces these patterns automatically rather than requiring the owner to deduce them from raw numbers.
**Skill fit:** POS API integration (Toast has a well-documented reporting API), email parsing for invoice PDFs (Google Vision API or a PDF parsing library handles 80% of common formats), recipe cost database (entered once by the restaurant, maintained as menu changes), and analytics on top of the resulting dataset. The variance calculation itself is a join between theoretical cost (recipe cost × sales volume) and actual cost (invoice amounts) — pure analytics work.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — Toast API integration for sales data, manual invoice upload with line-item entry (skip PDF parsing for V1), recipe cost entry for the top 20 menu items by revenue, and a weekly variance report. A restaurant whose owner sees for the first time that their ribeye is running $4.20 over theoretical cost per cover — across 400 ribeye plates last month — has just found $1,680 and is immediately worth $300/month.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — restaurant owner communities (Food & Restaurant Management Facebook group, Toast's customer community, the Restaurant Owner Rockstars Facebook group) are large and active. Independent restaurant consultants and fractional CFOs who serve restaurants are a high-leverage referral channel — one consultant recommending the tool to their 10 restaurant clients generates 10 trials. Toast's partner marketplace provides organic distribution once listed.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-499/month per restaurant depending on menu complexity and location count. 40 restaurants at $325/month = **$13,000/month**. Upsell: a supplier price variance tracker ($79/month add-on) that monitors whether suppliers are charging consistent prices week-over-week across the same items, surfacing price creep (a distributor quietly raising the case price of chicken breasts by $0.40 per pound over 3 months adds up to thousands in unbudgeted cost increases).
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Food cost variance is a weekly management task. The variance report becomes the owner's Monday morning ritual — it's the first thing they look at every week. Switching means losing the historical baseline that makes the tool's trend analysis meaningful (is variance getting better or worse over the last 12 weeks?). Every new location the restaurant opens is a new subscription. Price increases from suppliers are a permanent feature of the restaurant business, making the supplier price variance upsell perpetually relevant.
**Biggest risk:** Invoice processing — the diversity of supplier invoice formats makes automated parsing unreliable. Mitigation: start with manual invoice upload in V1 (the owner enters totals per category, not line items) and charge less for this tier; build the automated parser as V2 once you understand which suppliers' invoices are most common across your customer base and can build targeted parsers for Sysco, US Foods, and the top 5 regional distributors.
**Growth path:** Side project → Toast POS integration, manual invoice upload, recipe cost entry, and weekly variance report live, first 5-8 restaurant clients. Replaces-part-time-income → 15-20 restaurants, add automated invoice parsing for top distributors, add per-item variance drill-down and time-pattern analysis. Replaces-full-time-salary → 40+ restaurants, add supplier price variance tracker, expand to Square and Lightspeed POS integrations, and build a group reporting view for restaurant groups managing 3-10 locations.

### 5. Multi-Unit Franchise Performance Benchmarking
**Problem:** A franchise operator who owns 8 Subway locations, 5 Anytime Fitness clubs, or 12 Servpro territories has a spreadsheet problem. Their best-performing unit does 40% more revenue than their worst. They suspect the gap is driven by a combination of manager quality, local marketing, and operational differences — but they can't prove it because they have no analytical tool that connects performance data across all their units and isolates the variables. The franchisor provides system-wide average data (your units vs. all franchisees nationally) but that comparison is useless for identifying what the owner's own best units are doing differently from their worst units. Most multi-unit franchise operators (3-30 units, the majority of multi-unit ownership in the US) run their business from a combination of the franchisor's reporting portal, individual unit P&Ls from their accountant, and mental models built from years of experience. They know their business is being managed on intuition rather than data — and they don't have a tool that brings it together because no tool exists that isn't either a full-blown ERP ($50,000 to implement) or a generic BI tool (requires a data engineer to configure).
**What to build:** A multi-unit franchise performance benchmarking tool — a data aggregation layer that pulls revenue, labor cost, food/product cost, and customer traffic data from the data sources the franchise operator already has: the franchisor's reporting portal (via CSV export or where APIs exist), point-of-sale system, and payroll system. Core views: a unit scorecard ranking all the operator's locations on the metrics that matter for their franchise type (revenue, labor %, cost %, customer count, average transaction), a week-over-week and year-over-year trend view per unit, a "best vs. worst" gap analysis that quantifies the revenue opportunity if the bottom 3 units performed at the average of the top 3, and a manager performance view (same metrics by manager name, not just location, to expose whether performance follows the location or follows the person). A goal-tracking module where the operator sets targets for each unit and tracks actuals against them. Importantly: the tool is franchise-brand-agnostic (built around configurable KPIs, not hard-coded for any one franchise system) but offers pre-built templates for the 20 most common multi-unit franchise brands (Subway, Snap Fitness, Servpro, Neighborly brands, etc.).
**Skill fit:** The core is a data aggregation and normalization layer (pulling from CSVs or APIs from various sources), a configurable KPI framework, and clean analytics visualizations. This is exactly the builder's data engineering and analytics background applied to a problem that is currently solved only by expensive consultants and generic BI tools. The brand templates can be built incrementally starting with whichever brand the first 5 customers represent.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — a CSV upload workflow (the operator uploads their unit revenue and labor reports from whatever source they have), a unit scorecard and ranking view, and the best-vs.-worst gap quantification. No integrations in V1 — CSV upload is enough to demonstrate the value. An operator who sees for the first time that their bottom 2 units are collectively leaving $340,000/year on the table if brought to their top unit's performance level is immediately worth $400/month.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — multi-unit franchise operators are reachable through the Multi-Unit Franchisee magazine community and conference (the single most concentrated gathering of multi-unit operators in the US, with 2,000+ attendees), Franchise Times magazine readership, and LinkedIn outreach to "multi-unit franchisee" and "area developer" titles. Franchise brokers and franchise consultants who work with operators are a high-leverage referral channel.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-799/month per operator depending on unit count (3-10 units: $299/month; 11-30 units: $799/month). 25 operators at $475/month = **$11,875/month**. Upsell: a manager coaching module ($99/month add-on) that generates a weekly performance email for each unit manager showing their unit's key metrics vs. the operator's average, creating accountability without the operator having to personally generate and share the data every week.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Multi-unit operators review their business performance weekly or more frequently — the tool becomes part of the Monday morning routine. Switching means re-importing historical data and losing trend continuity, which no operator accepts once they've been using it for 6 months and their trend views cover a full year of operations. Operators who buy additional franchise units naturally expand their subscription.
**Biggest risk:** Data source fragmentation — each franchise brand uses a different reporting portal, and each operator may have different POS and payroll systems. The CSV approach handles this in V1. The long-term risk is that franchise brands build this capability natively into their franchisee portals. Mitigation: build for the multi-brand operator (someone who owns both Subway and Anytime Fitness locations) — the franchisor can never serve this segment because they only see their own brand's data.
**Growth path:** Side project → CSV upload, unit scorecard, ranking, and gap analysis live, first 5 operator clients. Replaces-part-time-income → 10-15 operators, add direct integrations with the top 5 franchise POS systems (NCR, Toast, Clover) and the top 3 franchise reporting portals. Replaces-full-time-salary → 25+ operators, add manager coaching module and predictive alerts ("Unit 4's labor % has increased 3 weeks in a row — historically this precedes a margin decline"), expand marketing to the Multi-Unit Franchisee conference circuit.
