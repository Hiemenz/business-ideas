### 1. Premium Cigar & Tobacco Product Manufacturer FDA CTP Registration & TTB Manufacturing Permit Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Small premium cigar manufacturers and other non-cigarette tobacco product makers (pipe tobacco blenders, small cigarette-alternative producers) operate under FDA's Center for Tobacco Products (CTP) following the 2016 "deeming rule" (21 CFR Part 1141), which extended FDA's tobacco jurisdiction to cover cigars and requires every manufacturing facility to register annually and every product formulation to be listed with FDA — and, critically, every new blend or any modification to an existing blend's tobacco leaf composition, wrapper, or filler requires either a Substantial Equivalence (SE) report demonstrating the new product is comparable to a predicate product already on the market, or a full Premarket Tobacco Product Application (PMTA), before that blend can legally be sold at all; a manufacturer that ships a reformulated blend before its SE report clears or its PMTA is granted is exactly the finding an FDA CTP compliance inspection or an import-detention review is built to catch. Separate from FDA's premarket framework entirely, a manufacturer must hold a TTB (Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau) tobacco manufacturer's permit under IRC Chapter 52 (26 U.S.C. §5712), post a continuing bond sized to production volume, and file periodic federal excise tax returns on TTB Form 5000.24 computed against per-thousand-unit or weight-based tax tiers that differ by product class — a filing a manufacturer must reconcile against actual production and shipment records every filing period. Manufacturers shipping product across state lines must also track PACT Act registration and reporting distinct from a retailer's own PACT Act obligations. A small boutique manufacturer running several blends through a hand-rolling or small-batch operation manages FDA facility-registration renewal, per-blend SE/PMTA submission status, TTB permit/bond currency, and excise filing deadlines on a mix of a production log and a compliance consultant's periodic email, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific blend is actually cleared to sell before it ships to a distributor.

**What to build:** An FDA facility-registration renewal tracker with a per-blend SE/PMTA submission-status log gating any blend from being marked "cleared to sell" until its premarket pathway clears; a TTB manufacturer's permit and bond renewal tracker; and an excise tax filing-deadline calculator computing each period's Form 5000.24 liability from logged production volume by tax tier.

**Skill fit:** FDA CTP's deeming-rule registration/listing requirements and TTB's permit-and-excise structure are public, structured federal regulatory data with concrete recurring deadlines, and the per-blend "cleared to sell" gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a tobacco-manufacturing niche entirely upstream of the retail/dispensing compliance surface already covered in this repo.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the FDA registration/SE-PMTA tracker, the TTB permit/bond tracker, and the excise filing calculator. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small boutique cigar manufacturers.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Premium Cigar Association's manufacturer-member directory and its annual trade show reach exactly the small manufacturers currently tracking FDA SE/PMTA status and TTB excise deadlines against a production log with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per manufacturer depending on blend count and production volume. 20-26 manufacturers × $449/mo avg = $8,980-11,674/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new blend a manufacturer develops reopens the FDA SE/PMTA premarket cycle from scratch, every filing period generates a fresh TTB excise return regardless of volume, and every additional distribution state adds its own PACT Act reporting record — a continuous, product-development-and-shipment-driven compliance stream tied directly to the manufacturer's product line growth.

**Biggest risk:** A very small manufacturer selling a single long-established, already-cleared blend has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads for manufacturers actively developing new blends or expanding into new distribution states, where the SE/PMTA-and-excise calendar genuinely outgrows a production log.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10 manufacturers ($4.5K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 16 manufacturers ($7.2K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 20-26 manufacturers plus a distributor-facing "cleared to sell" export manufacturers can hand a wholesale buyer before a new blend ships.

### 2. Psilocybin Service Center & Licensed Facilitator State Training, Session-Sequence & Product-Batch Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Psilocybin service centers and the licensed facilitators who work inside them operate under state "natural medicine" programs — Oregon's Measure 109 (administered under OAR 333-333 by the Oregon Health Authority's Psilocybin Services Section) and Colorado's Natural Medicine Health Act (administered by the Department of Revenue's Natural Medicine Division) — that require every facilitator to complete a state-approved training curriculum (120 or more hours in Oregon, covering safety, facilitation technique, and cultural competency) and pass a state facilitator examination before licensure, renewed annually with continuing-education requirements; a center that schedules a facilitator whose license has lapsed is exactly the finding a state licensing-board complaint investigation is built to catch, and a service delivered by an unlicensed facilitator can void the center's own license. Every administration session must also be preceded by a documented preparation session and followed by a documented integration session — a specific three-part client-session sequence the statute requires as a condition of the center's licensure, not an optional clinical best practice — and the psilocybin product used in each session must be sourced from a state-licensed manufacturer with batch-level potency and contaminant testing results on file, since a session run with an untested or improperly sourced product batch is a licensing violation independent of anything about the facilitator's own credential status. **What to build:** A per-facilitator license and training-hour renewal tracker with a "cleared to facilitate" gate blocking any session from being scheduled with a facilitator whose license has lapsed; a per-client session-sequence tracker enforcing the preparation-administration-integration order with a required-documentation checklist at each stage; and a per-session product-batch traceability log tying the psilocybin product used to its source manufacturer's state testing results.

**Skill fit:** Oregon's and Colorado's licensing statutes are public, structured state regulatory data with concrete training-hour and renewal requirements, and the facilitator-eligibility gate plus the session-sequence tracker are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a psychedelic-facilitation niche operating entirely outside the federal DEA/REMS framework that governs every other behavioral-health idea already in this repo.

### 3. Regulation Crowdfunding Funding Portal Per-Issuer Cap, Form C Filing & FINRA Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Regulation Crowdfunding (Reg CF) funding portals — SEC-registered, FINRA-member intermediaries that host small securities offerings under Securities Act §4(a)(6) and 17 CFR Part 227 — must track each concurrently listed issuer's rolling 12-month offering cap (currently $5M, periodically adjusted by SEC rule) against actual investment commitments flowing through the platform, since a portal that allows an issuer's raise to exceed its statutory cap has facilitated a non-compliant offering the SEC can pursue against the portal itself, not just the issuer. Every issuer must also file Form C at the offering's launch, a Form C-AR annual report for as long as the offering remains outstanding, and a Form C-U for any material change mid-offering — a filing calendar the portal itself is expected to monitor as part of its FINRA Funding Portal Rules obligations, since FINRA's examination program specifically checks whether a portal has a reasonable basis for believing each listed issuer is complying with Regulation Crowdfunding's disclosure requirements. Before listing any new issuer, the portal must also complete a bad-actor disqualification check under Rule 503 of Regulation Crowdfunding, confirming none of the issuer's covered persons has a disqualifying event on record — a check that must be documented and current at the moment of listing, not merely performed once. A small funding portal running a rotating slate of a dozen or more concurrent issuer campaigns manages per-issuer cap tracking, staggered Form C/C-AR/C-U filing deadlines, and per-issuer disqualification-check documentation on a mix of a portal-ops spreadsheet and institutional memory, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific issuer's campaign is actually within cap and current on its filings before the next investment commitment is accepted.

**What to build:** A per-issuer rolling-12-month offering-cap tracker computing real-time cap usage against committed investments with an over-cap alert; a Form C/C-AR/C-U filing-deadline tracker flagging any issuer approaching a missed filing; and a bad-actor disqualification-check log gating any new issuer from being listed until a current, documented Rule 503 check clears.

**Skill fit:** SEC Regulation Crowdfunding and FINRA's Funding Portal Rules are public, structured federal regulatory data with concrete numeric caps and filing deadlines, and the per-issuer cap tracker plus the listing-eligibility gate are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a narrow securities-intermediary category explicitly distinct from the Registered Investment Adviser and FINRA broker-dealer ideas already in this repo, neither of which shares a license type, offering-cap mechanism, or Form C filing structure with a Reg CF funding portal.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the offering-cap tracker, the Form C filing-deadline tracker, and the disqualification-check log. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small independent funding portals.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Crowdfunding Professional Association (CfPA) member directory reaches exactly the small independent portals currently tracking per-issuer caps and Form C deadlines across a rotating campaign slate with no dedicated compliance software.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $399-799/mo per portal depending on concurrent-issuer count. 15-20 portals × $549/mo avg = $8,235-10,980/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new issuer campaign the portal lists opens its own independent cap-and-filing tracking cycle, every issuer still outstanding generates a recurring Form C-AR obligation for as long as the offering remains open, and every new listing reopens the disqualification-check requirement — a continuous, campaign-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the portal's deal flow.

**Biggest risk:** A portal running only one or two campaigns a year has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads for active portals running a continuous multi-issuer campaign slate, where the staggered cap-and-filing calendar genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

### 4. Peanut Buying Point & Sheller USDA Grading, Aflatoxin Test-Gate & FSA Settlement Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Peanut buying points and shelling plants purchasing farmer-delivered peanuts at harvest must obtain USDA quality-factor grading certification for each lot — a peanut-specific grading standard entirely separate from the Federal Grain Inspection Service's wheat/corn/soybean standards under the U.S. Grain Standards Act — and, critically, every lot must clear a mandatory aflatoxin (mycotoxin) test before it can be commingled into food-grade inventory: a lot testing above the FDA action level (20 ppb) cannot be sold for human food use at all and must be diverted to a segregated non-food (crushing/oil-stock) channel, a determination that has to happen before the load touches clean storage, since aflatoxin contamination discovered after commingling can force an entire bin's inventory to be re-tested or downgraded. Grower settlement and payment are computed directly from the USDA grade — percent sound mature kernels, percent damage, foreign material — and many growers additionally finance their delivered crop against USDA Farm Service Agency marketing-assistance loan and quota documentation through the Commodity Credit Corporation, a settlement mechanism the buying point has to reconcile against its own scale-house receiving records before a grower gets paid. A buying point or sheller running a compressed fall harvest season across dozens of grower accounts manages per-load aflatoxin test-gate status, USDA grade documentation, and FSA loan/settlement reconciliation on a mix of a scale-house paper ticket and a spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific load's aflatoxin result actually cleared before it's released into food-grade storage.

**What to build:** A per-load aflatoxin test-gate tracker blocking any load from being marked "cleared to commingle" until its test result is on file and below the FDA action level, with an automatic diversion flag for any load that exceeds it; a USDA grade-documentation log tied directly to the grower settlement calculation; and an FSA marketing-quota/loan reconciliation tracker matching each grower's financed bushels against actual delivered and graded volume.

**Skill fit:** USDA's peanut grading standards and FDA's aflatoxin action level are public, structured federal regulatory data with a concrete per-load compliance gate, and the "cleared to commingle" gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a peanut-specific agricultural-commodity niche explicitly distinct from the Grain Elevator, Official Grain Inspection Agency, and Licensed Grain Dealer ideas already in this repo, none of which handles peanuts at all or carries a mandatory per-load food-safety test gate their storage-bonding, FGIS-designation, or farmer-payment-protection compliance surfaces have any field for.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the aflatoxin test-gate tracker, the USDA grade/settlement log, and the FSA reconciliation tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent peanut buying points and shelling plants.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the American Peanut Shellers Association and state peanut grower/buying-point associations across Georgia, Alabama, Texas, and the Virginia-Carolina belt reach exactly the small operations currently reconciling aflatoxin test gates and grower settlement against a scale-house paper ticket during the compressed fall harvest.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249-449/mo per buying point/sheller depending on load volume and grower-account count. 24-30 operations × $349/mo avg = $8,376-10,470/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every load received during the compressed harvest season generates its own aflatoxin-gate event regardless of how routine the delivery, every season resets the entire settlement-and-FSA-reconciliation calendar, and growing grower-account volume only multiplies the number of load-level records running simultaneously — a continuous, harvest-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the buying point's receiving season.

**Biggest risk:** A very small buying point handling only a handful of loads from a few long-standing growers has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads for operations running a multi-thousand-ton compressed season across dozens of grower accounts, where the staggered test-gate-and-settlement calendar genuinely outgrows a paper ticket system.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 buying points ($4.2K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 20 buying points ($7K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 24-30 operations plus a grower-facing settlement-statement export buying points can hand a farmer at payout time.

### 5. Laser Light Show Operator FDA CDRH Variance & ANSI Z136.6 Beam-Hazard Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Operators running Class IIIb/IV laser light shows at concerts, theme parks, festivals, and touring productions must operate under an FDA Center for Devices and Radiological Health (CDRH) variance issued under 21 CFR 1040.10/1040.11 — a product/show variance establishing that the specific laser system meets required output-power limits and safety-interlock design before it can legally be used to entertain an audience — and every touring show must be able to demonstrate the system it's actually running that night is the one covered by its variance, since operating a system outside its variance terms is exactly the finding a CDRH compliance inspection or complaint investigation is built to catch. Separately, operators must follow ANSI Z136.6's outdoor-laser-operations safety standard, which requires a designated laser safety officer and a projected-beam hazard analysis specific to each venue — audience-scan power-density limits, beam-path clearance from any nearby structure or flight path — before the system is powered on, and for any show operating near navigable airspace, a separate FAA notification is required, since a laser beam projected into an aircraft's flight path is a distinct federal safety violation independent of anything about the CDRH variance. A touring laser show operator running systems across a full festival-and-concert season at many different venues manages per-system CDRH variance-compliance status, a fresh ANSI Z136.6 hazard analysis for every new venue, and FAA notification filings for any show near an airport or flight corridor on a mix of a technician's pre-show setup checklist and institutional memory, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific venue's hazard analysis and FAA notification are both actually on file before the system is powered on for that night's audience.

**What to build:** A per-system CDRH variance-compliance tracker confirming the deployed system matches its filed variance; a per-venue ANSI Z136.6 hazard-analysis checklist with a "cleared to project" gate blocking power-on until the analysis and laser-safety-officer sign-off are on file; and an FAA airspace-notification tracker for any show scheduled near a flight path.

**Skill fit:** FDA CDRH's variance framework and ANSI Z136.6's hazard-analysis structure are public federal regulatory and standards-body data with a concrete per-venue compliance requirement, and the "cleared to project" gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a radiation-emitting entertainment-technology niche with no close analog anywhere in this repo — the nearest thematic neighbor, a side-income drone light show flight-log tracker, involves an entirely different technology and federal agency (FAA Part 107 unmanned-aircraft rules) with no laser-variance or beam-hazard-analysis concept at all.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new venue on a touring season reopens the ANSI Z136.6 hazard-analysis requirement from scratch, every show near a flight path generates its own FAA notification event, and every additional laser system the operator adds to its fleet opens its own CDRH variance-compliance record — a continuous, venue-and-fleet-driven compliance stream tied directly to the operator's touring calendar.
