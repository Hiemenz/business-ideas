### 1. Indoor Skydiving (Vertical Wind Tunnel) Operator State Amusement-Device Permit, IBA Instructor & Flight-Safety Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent vertical wind tunnel operators — running a single-tunnel facility distinct from a national chain — fall under most states' amusement-ride or amusement-device safety act, requiring an annual state inspection of the tunnel's mechanical and structural systems and a currently filed liability-insurance certificate before the facility can legally reopen each season or after any major maintenance event; a tunnel that misses its inspection renewal window is exactly the finding a state amusement-device inspector's routine visit is built to catch, and can shut the facility down mid-weekend during its highest-revenue window. Every flight instructor must hold a current International Bodyflight Association (IBA) coaching certification and log continuing coaching hours on its own renewal cycle, and a facility running instructors past their certification's expiration exposes every lesson they teach to a liability claim with no valid credential behind it. Layered on top, every first-time flyer must complete a documented safety briefing and liability waiver before entering the tunnel, and group bookings for school trips, birthday parties, and corporate events require a per-session flyer roster matched against completed waivers — a facility running several tunnels' worth of daily session slots currently tracks state inspection/insurance renewal, per-instructor IBA certification, and per-session waiver completion on a front-desk binder and a filing folder, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific instructor is currently certified and every flyer in today's session has a signed waiver on file before the fans spin up.

**What to build:** A state amusement-device permit and insurance-certificate renewal tracker with a "cleared to operate" gate per tunnel; a per-instructor IBA certification and coaching-hour renewal tracker; and a per-session flyer roster and digital-waiver completion log blocking a session from being marked ready until every flyer's waiver is confirmed on file.

**Skill fit:** State amusement-device safety-act inspection cycles and IBA's published certification structure are public, structured regulatory and credentialing data with a concrete per-facility and per-instructor trigger, and the "cleared to operate" gate plus the per-session waiver-completion log are the same deterministic recordkeeping pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an indoor-adventure niche this repo has never addressed.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the state permit/insurance tracker, the instructor certification tracker, and the per-session waiver-completion log. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent (non-chain) wind tunnel operators.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — the International Bodyflight Association's own member and coach directory, which supports flyers, coaches, and instructors across 85+ tunnels worldwide, reaches exactly the independent facility operators currently tracking instructor certification and state inspection renewal on a front-desk binder with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299/mo base + $20/mo per certified instructor tracked. Average client (8 instructors) = $299 + $160 = $459/mo. 22-28 facilities × $459/mo = **$10,098-12,852/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every booked session reopens the waiver-completion check, every instructor's IBA certification renews on its own independent coaching-hour cycle, and the state amusement-device inspection recurs annually regardless of session volume — a continuous, session-and-instructor-driven compliance stream.

**Biggest risk:** The independent (non-iFLY-chain) wind tunnel market is a genuinely small niche nationally. Mitigate by expanding the waiver-and-roster module to serve adjacent indoor-adventure venues (climbing gyms, trampoline parks) that share the identical per-session waiver-completion problem.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 facilities ($5.5K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 22-28 facilities ($10.1-12.9K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding a state-inspector-ready compliance export a facility can produce the moment an amusement-device inspection is scheduled.

### 2. High-Power Rocketry Launch Site & Club Operator FAA Waiver, ATF LEUP & NAR/Tripoli Certification-Gated Motor Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent high-power rocketry clubs running a regular launch site must maintain a current FAA altitude waiver tied to that specific site's maximum altitude and radius, activated for each individual launch date — a club that launches on a date its waiver doesn't cover, or above the altitude its waiver authorizes, is exactly the finding an FAA ramp check or a nearby-airspace complaint is built to catch, and repeated violations put the site's entire waiver renewal at risk for every member who flies there. Any motor exceeding 62.5 grams of propellant weight requires ATF-compliant magazine storage, and a club officer who purchases or stores motors on behalf of the membership must hold a federal ATF Low Explosives User Permit (LEUP) renewed on its own cycle — an officer flying an unpermitted storage arrangement exposes the club to a federal explosives violation regardless of how carefully the launch itself is run. Every member must also hold a current NAR or Tripoli Rocketry Association certification at the level matching the motor class they intend to fly (Level 1 for H/I-impulse motors, higher levels for larger classes), since commercial motor distributors will not sell to an uncertified flier and a club allowing an uncertified member to fly a motor above their level is a safety and liability failure the certifying body's own rules exist to prevent. A club running dozens of members across a monthly launch schedule currently tracks FAA waiver activation dates, the launch officer's LEUP renewal, and per-member certification level on a website sign-up sheet and a launch-day paper roster, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific member is certified for the motor they're about to fly before the pad goes hot.

**What to build:** A per-site FAA altitude-waiver tracker with a launch-date activation checklist; an ATF LEUP renewal tracker for the club's motor-purchasing officer with a magazine-storage compliance log; and a per-member NAR/Tripoli certification-level tracker gating which motor-impulse classes a specific member is currently certified to fly at that launch.

**Skill fit:** FAA waiver-activation procedures and NAR/Tripoli's published certification-level structure are public, structured regulatory and credentialing data with a concrete per-launch and per-member trigger, and the certification-gated motor-class check is the same deterministic "block the action until the record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a hobbyist-aerospace niche entirely distinct from the crop-dusting, commercial-drone, and air-tour ideas already in this repo, none of which involve ATF explosives jurisdiction or a member-certification-gated motor system at all.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the FAA waiver tracker, the ATF LEUP/magazine-storage log, and the per-member certification-level gate. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent rocketry clubs.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — the Tripoli Rocketry Association and National Association of Rocketry (NAR) section/prefecture directories reach exactly the small clubs currently tracking FAA waiver dates and member certification levels on a website sign-up sheet with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149/mo base + $5/mo per certified member tracked. Average client (40 members) = $149 + $200 = $349/mo. 30-40 clubs × $349/mo = **$10,470-13,960/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every monthly launch date reopens the FAA waiver-activation check, every new member joining reopens the certification-level verification, and the club officer's LEUP renews independently of launch volume — a continuous, membership-and-launch-driven compliance stream.

**Biggest risk:** A very small club running a handful of launches a year at a borrowed site has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward NAR/Tripoli sections running a full monthly launch calendar with 30+ active members, where a mismatched certification-to-motor-class flight is a real safety incident waiting to happen.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 clubs ($4.2K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 30-40 clubs ($10.5-14K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding an FAA-ramp-check-ready waiver export a launch control officer can pull up on a phone the moment a controller calls.

### 3. Kit Car & Replica-Vehicle Low-Volume Motor Vehicle Manufacturer NHTSA Registration, 325-Unit Cap & VIN-Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Small manufacturers building replica vehicles — reproducing the body of an original vehicle at least 25 years old, sold as a rolling chassis or turnkey car — register with NHTSA under the Low Volume Motor Vehicle Manufacturers Act (49 CFR Part 586) to receive an exemption from most Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards, but that exemption is capped at 325 replica vehicles per calendar year nationwide, a shared ceiling across every low-volume manufacturer using a given World Manufacturer Identifier, meaning two commonly-owned manufacturers must jointly track their combined annual output against the same 325-unit limit or risk losing the exemption entirely. Every vehicle produced under the exemption requires NHTSA-compliant VIN encoding with specific manufacturer-identifier characters placed in defined positions, and the manufacturer must file an annual production report to NHTSA — a manufacturer that loses track of its running annual unit count, or that VIN-encodes a vehicle incorrectly, risks an NHTSA compliance inquiry that can suspend the registration a small builder's entire production run depends on. A small kit car or replica manufacturer building 20-80 vehicles a year currently tracks its running annual production count against the 325-unit cap, per-vehicle VIN assignment, and the annual NHTSA production-report filing on a build-sheet spreadsheet, with no systematic alert confirming the company is still under its cap before the next vehicle is assigned a VIN and shipped.

**What to build:** A running annual production counter against the shared 325-unit cap (with multi-entity aggregation for commonly-owned manufacturers) that blocks a new VIN assignment once the cap is reached; a per-vehicle VIN-encoding validator confirming manufacturer-identifier characters land in the correct positions before a vehicle ships; and an annual NHTSA production-report generator compiling every VIN assigned that year.

**Skill fit:** 49 CFR Part 586's registration and VIN-encoding requirements are public, structured federal regulatory data with a concrete per-vehicle and per-calendar-year trigger, and the running-cap counter plus the VIN-format validator are the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a low-volume-manufacturing niche this repo has never addressed.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the annual production-cap counter, the VIN-encoding validator, and the NHTSA annual production-report generator. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to registered low-volume manufacturers.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — the Specialty Equipment Market Association (SEMA), which advocated for the Low Volume Motor Vehicle Manufacturers Act on behalf of its low-volume and replica-vehicle-manufacturer members, reaches exactly the small builders currently tracking their annual unit count against the 325-vehicle cap on a build-sheet spreadsheet with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $349/mo per manufacturer, flat given the single shared cap-tracking core feature. 28-35 manufacturers × $349/mo = **$9,772-12,215/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every vehicle built reopens the running-cap check and VIN validation from scratch, and the annual NHTSA production report recurs on its own federal filing calendar regardless of how many vehicles a manufacturer builds that year — a continuous, production-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the manufacturer's own growth.

**Biggest risk:** The total number of NHTSA-registered low-volume manufacturers nationally is a genuinely small, specialized market. Mitigate by selling to multi-entity manufacturer groups that must jointly track a shared cap across commonly-owned brands, where the aggregation feature is uniquely load-bearing and hardest to replace with a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10 manufacturers ($3.5K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 28-35 manufacturers ($9.8-12.2K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding a dealer-facing NHTSA-registration verification export a manufacturer can hand a state DMV title clerk unfamiliar with the exemption.

### 4. Salvage-Title Rebuilt-Vehicle Reconstruction Shop State Inspection-Readiness & Parts-Receipt Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent rebuilders who buy salvage-titled vehicles and repair them back to roadworthy condition for resale must pass a state DMV rebuilt-vehicle inspection before the vehicle can be re-titled as "rebuilt" or "reconstructed" — New York, New Jersey, and Massachusetts run the strictest programs, while states like Florida, Arizona, and Vermont are comparatively permissive — and every program requires the rebuilder to produce bills of sale and receipts documenting every major component replaced (frame, airbags, engine, transmission) before the inspector will sign off. A rebuilder who can't produce a specific vehicle's parts-receipt trail at inspection time fails on the spot, losing weeks of labor and parts cost on a vehicle that now can't be titled or sold at all. Every vehicle must also clear the federal National Motor Vehicle Title Information System (NMVTIS) before resale, confirming the salvage brand carries forward correctly and the vehicle isn't flagged for an unresolved theft or total-loss record from another state — a rebuilder who sells a vehicle without confirming its NMVTIS status clean is exposed to a consumer-fraud claim from a buyer who discovers the brand history later. A small rebuilder running 8-20 vehicles through the shop at once currently tracks per-vehicle parts receipts, NMVTIS status, and each state's specific inspection-document checklist on a job folder per vehicle and a receipts shoebox, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific vehicle's receipt trail is actually complete before it's scheduled for inspection.

**What to build:** A per-vehicle parts-receipt and bill-of-sale tracker organized against the specific state's required-documentation checklist, with an "inspection-ready" gate blocking a vehicle from being scheduled until every required receipt is on file; an NMVTIS status checker confirming a vehicle's brand history is clean before resale; and a multi-state inspection-requirement reference library covering the strictest and most common rebuilder states.

**Skill fit:** State DMV rebuilt-vehicle inspection checklists and NMVTIS's public brand-history lookup are structured regulatory data with a concrete per-vehicle trigger, and the "inspection-ready" gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a reconstruction-shop niche entirely distinct from the auto salvage yard/vehicle recycler idea already in this repo, whose NMVTIS obtained/crushed reporting cycle for a dismantling business never involves a vehicle actually returning to the road, a parts-receipt inspection trail, or a rebuilt-title brand at all.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the per-vehicle parts-receipt tracker with state-checklist gating, the NMVTIS status checker, and the multi-state requirement library. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent rebuilders.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — state salvage-vehicle-rebuilder licensing rosters (most DMVs that run a rebuilt-inspection program publish or can be requested to share a list of active rebuilder-license holders) and the National Independent Automobile Dealers Association (NIADA) state chapter meetings, where many rebuilders also hold a used-car dealer license, reach exactly the small shops currently tracking parts receipts in a shoebox with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $15/mo per vehicle in the active rebuild queue. Average client (10 vehicles) = $199 + $150 = $349/mo. 30-38 shops × $349/mo = **$10,470-13,262/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every vehicle acquired reopens the parts-receipt-tracking requirement from intake through inspection, every resale reopens the NMVTIS clean-status check, and a growing rebuilder's own throughput compounds the number of simultaneously tracked vehicle files — a continuous, throughput-driven compliance stream.

**Biggest risk:** A very small rebuilder flipping only a handful of vehicles a year has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward rebuilders running a full multi-vehicle queue in strict-inspection states (NY, NJ, MA), where a single failed inspection is a real, repeatable revenue loss.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 shops ($4.2K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 30-38 shops ($10.5-13.3K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding a buyer-facing rebuilt-vehicle disclosure packet a shop can hand a customer at the point of sale to preempt a later brand-history dispute.

### 5. Equine Breeding Farm & Stallion Station Registry Stallion-Report, DNA-Parentage & Shipped-Semen Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Farms standing one or more breeding stallions must file an annual Stallion Breeding Report with the horse's breed registry — the American Quarter Horse Association requires one by November 30 every year listing every mare bred that season and the breeding method used for each (live cover, cooled shipped semen, or frozen semen) — and a farm that misses the filing deadline or misreports a breeding method can hold up every foal from that season's book from being registered at all, a direct hit to the farm's stud-fee revenue and reputation. Every breeding stallion and mare must have a current DNA type and genetic-panel test on file with the registry, and any foal conceived using cooled or transported semen requires documented DNA parentage verification before that foal's registration can be completed — a stallion station managing a book of 20-60 mares across live-cover and shipped-semen contracts currently tracks per-mare breeding-method records, DNA testing status for every stallion and mare in its program, and the looming annual filing deadline on a breeding-season logbook and a wall calendar, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific mare's breeding record and both animals' DNA status are complete before that mare's owner submits a foal registration application months later. Layered on top, interstate shipment of chilled or frozen semen typically requires a current negative health certificate for the donor stallion under the destination state's import requirements, and a shipment sent without that paperwork can be refused at the receiving farm — a wasted collection cycle during the stallion's limited breeding season.

**What to build:** A per-mare breeding record tracker capturing breeding method, date, and stallion, feeding directly into the annual registry Stallion Breeding Report with a due-date countdown; a per-animal DNA-type and genetic-panel status tracker for every stallion and mare in the program with a "cleared to register offspring" flag; and a per-shipment health-certificate and destination-state requirement tracker for cooled/frozen semen sent off the farm.

**Skill fit:** Breed-registry filing deadlines and DNA-parentage-verification rules (the American Quarter Horse Association's published Stallion Breeding Report and DNA-typing requirements chief among them) are public, structured data with a concrete per-mare and per-season trigger, and the annual filing-deadline countdown plus the DNA-status gate are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an equine-genetics niche entirely distinct from the Livestock Artificial Insemination & Embryo-Transfer Center idea already in this repo, whose bovine donor disease-testing panel (BVD, Johne's, trichomoniasis) and NAAB Certified Semen Services health certification for cattle semen collection has no breed-registry filing deadline, DNA-parentage-verification, or foal-registration concept anywhere in its data model.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the per-mare breeding-record tracker feeding the annual report, the DNA-status tracker, and the per-shipment health-certificate tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent stallion stations.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the American Quarter Horse Association's stallion-owner network and regional breed-association meetings (Paint, Appaloosa, and Warmblood registries run comparable annual-report and DNA-verification requirements) reach exactly the small farms currently reconciling breeding records and DNA status on a wall calendar with no dedicated system ahead of the November filing deadline.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249/mo base + $20/mo per stallion standing at the farm. Average client (3 stallions) = $249 + $60 = $309/mo. 35-45 farms × $309/mo = **$10,815-13,905/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every mare bred during the season reopens the breeding-record-and-DNA-status check, the annual registry filing deadline recurs every year regardless of book size, and every new stallion a growing farm adds to its roster compounds the number of simultaneously tracked DNA and health-certificate records.

**Biggest risk:** A very small farm standing a single stallion to a handful of mares has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward stations running a full book of 20+ mares across live-cover and shipped-semen contracts, where a missed filing deadline or an incomplete DNA record genuinely threatens multiple foal registrations at once.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 farms ($4.6K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 35-45 farms ($10.8-13.9K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding a mare-owner-facing breeding-record export a station can send automatically the moment a foal's registration paperwork is due.
