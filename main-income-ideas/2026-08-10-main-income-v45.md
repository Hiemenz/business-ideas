### 1. RIA Form ADV Annual Amendment, Multi-State Notice Filing & IARD/IAPD Compliance
**Problem:** Investment advisers registered with the SEC (typically those managing $100M+ in regulatory assets under management) must maintain regulatory compliance across three overlapping systems: Form ADV annual filing, state notice filing obligations, and their public disclosure record on IARD/IAPD. Form ADV compliance: SEC-registered RIAs must file an annual amendment to Form ADV Parts 1 and 2 within 90 days of fiscal year-end via IARD (Investment Adviser Registration Depository), and must update Form ADV Part 1 promptly (within 30 days) when material changes occur — such as a change in principal office address, addition of a supervised person, or change in the number of client accounts. Form ADV Part 2 (the brochure) must be updated and delivered to all clients within 120 days of fiscal year-end if materially changed. State notice filing obligations: SEC-registered RIAs that advise clients in states where they have more than the de minimis number of clients (in most states, 5 or more clients in the state requires a notice filing) must file state notice filings through IARD for each such state, pay state notice filing fees, and renew those notice filings annually. Missing a state notice filing triggers a deficiency notice from the state securities regulator and can constitute unlicensed investment adviser activity in that state. IAPD disclosure management: the IAPD (Investment Adviser Public Disclosure) record is pulled directly from IARD and is what prospective clients, journalists, and state regulators check when evaluating an RIA — ensuring the public record accurately reflects the firm's current Form ADV information, that no outdated DRPs (Disclosure Reporting Pages) remain unfiled, and that Form ADV Part 2 brochures and brochure supplements (Form ADV Part 2B for each supervised person) are current and accessible from IAPD. Mid-size RIAs ($100M-$2B AUM) with 10-50 employees, advisors registered in multiple states, and growing multi-state client bases manage these overlapping compliance obligations with a combination of their CCO's calendar and external compliance consultants charging $300-500/hour, with no systematic ongoing tracking.
**What to build:** An RIA Form ADV, multi-state notice filing, and IARD/IAPD compliance management service — a Form ADV annual amendment calendar (tracking the RIA's fiscal year-end date, the 90-day Form ADV Part 1 and 2 annual amendment deadline, the 120-day Form ADV Part 2 delivery deadline to existing clients, and any interim material change events that triggered or should have triggered a prompt amendment — with tiered alerts beginning 30 days before the annual amendment deadline), a multi-state notice filing tracker (for each state where the RIA advises clients: the state, the client count in that state against the de minimis threshold for that state, the current notice filing status — filed and current, approaching renewal, or not yet filed — the state notice filing renewal deadline via IARD, and the state notice filing fee payment status — with alerts when the client count in a new state approaches the de minimis threshold and when any existing state notice filing is within 60 days of renewal), a Form ADV Part 2 brochure and brochure supplement tracker (for each supervised person with a Form ADV Part 2B brochure supplement: the supervised person's name, CRD number, the date the Part 2B was last updated, whether the Part 2B is current given any changes to that person's disciplinary history or outside business activities, and whether the Part 2B has been filed with IARD and is accessible on IAPD), a material change event log (maintaining a running log of events that may constitute material changes requiring prompt Form ADV amendment — new office location, new custodial relationship, new investment strategy offered, DRP addition, change in key personnel — with a determination field indicating whether each event was assessed as a material change requiring prompt amendment or non-material, and the assessment date), and an IAPD public record audit tool (a periodic review of the RIA's IAPD public disclosure record confirming that Form ADV Parts 1 and 2 on IAPD match the firm's current actual information, all brochure supplements for current supervised persons are filed, and no DRPs are outstanding).
**Skill fit:** Form ADV filing requirements and state notice filing obligations are documented in SEC Form ADV instructions, IARD Filing Requirements published by FINRA (which operates IARD), and state-by-state de minimis thresholds documented by NASAA (North American Securities Administrators Association). Building the annual amendment calendar, multi-state notice filing tracker, Part 2 brochure supplement tracker, material change event log, and IAPD audit tool are data management problems the builder's engineering and data analytics skills address directly. NAPFA (National Association of Personal Financial Advisors) and IAA (Investment Adviser Association) member directories provide defined buyer communities.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a Form ADV annual amendment calendar with material change event log, a multi-state notice filing tracker for all 50 states and DC with state-specific de minimis thresholds and IARD renewal deadlines, a Form ADV Part 2 and 2B brochure supplement tracker, and an IAPD public record audit checklist; first 5-8 RIA clients through IAA member outreach while the multi-state notice filing tracker and material change log are validated against real firm IARD filing histories.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through IAA (Investment Adviser Association) member network and NAPFA member outreach (the concentrated professional communities for mid-size RIA principals and CCOs — IAA's compliance forum is the primary industry event for RIA compliance professionals), RIA compliance consultant networks (independent compliance consultants who bill RIA clients hourly for Form ADV preparation benefit from a systematic tracking tool they can license to multiple RIA clients — they become the primary referral channel), and RIA custodian compliance resource networks (major custodians whose RIA client firms receive state notice filing deficiency notices refer compliance management tools to their advisor clients as part of their transition services and compliance support programs).

### 2. Commercial Pesticide Applicator Multi-State License, RUP Record-Keeping & EPA FIFRA Compliance
**Problem:** Commercial pest control companies and agricultural pesticide application businesses applying restricted-use pesticides (RUPs) must maintain multi-layer regulatory compliance managed at both the company level and the individual applicator level — across multiple states if they operate across state lines. State commercial pesticide applicator licensing: commercial pesticide application businesses must hold a state-issued commercial pesticide applicator business license (or pest control operator license) in each state where they apply pesticides commercially, with annual renewal. Individual pesticide applicator certifications: every individual who applies restricted-use pesticides must hold a state-issued certified applicator certification in the relevant pest control category (general pest, termite, ornamental, right-of-way, fumigation, wood-preserving, aquatic, etc.) with continuing education renewal requirements — most states require 4-16 category-specific CE hours per certification renewal cycle (typically 3-5 years). Restricted-use pesticide purchase and use records: under EPA FIFRA, certified applicators who purchase RUPs must maintain purchase records (date, amount, brand name, EPA registration number) for 2 years, and most states add use record requirements (application date, location, target pest, rate applied, operator certification number) that must be retained and available for state pesticide regulatory inspection. Multi-state operations: pest control companies operating across state lines (mosquito control districts, right-of-way vegetation management companies, lawn care franchises) must track individual applicator certifications in each state where they apply, which may require separate certification exams and CE requirements in each state. Informal tracking by office managers using spreadsheets fails when a certifier's CE lapses or when a technician who was certified in State A begins working in State B without obtaining the State B certification.
**What to build:** A commercial pesticide applicator multi-state license and compliance management service — a state business license portfolio (for each operating state: state commercial pesticide applicator business license number, renewal deadline, qualifying operator designation and their certification status in that state, and any bond or insurance requirements specific to the state's pesticide licensing — with tiered renewal alerts), a pesticide applicator certification tracker (for each individual applicator: state certifications held and categories certified in each state, certification number and expiration date, CE hours required per renewal cycle by state and category, CE hours completed to date with provider and course name, and projected CE completion status relative to the renewal deadline — with alerts when CE completion is projected to fall short), a restricted-use pesticide purchase record system (for each RUP purchase: date, product name and EPA registration number, quantity purchased, vendor, and the certified applicator making the purchase — maintaining a 2-year rolling RUP purchase record ready for EPA or state inspector review), a pesticide use record log (for each pesticide application: date, customer site address, target pest, product applied with EPA registration number, application rate and total amount applied, application method, weather conditions if required by state, and applicator certification number — with a log completion gate that confirms all required fields are populated before the job is marked complete), and a state deployment eligibility check (before any applicator is assigned to a job in a given state: the system confirms the applicator holds a current valid certification in that state and in the relevant category — blocking the assignment if the certification is expired or if the applicator lacks the required category certification in the state where the job is located).
**Skill fit:** State commercial pesticide applicator licensing requirements and CE requirements are available from each state's department of agriculture pesticide regulatory program. EPA FIFRA RUP purchase record requirements are documented in 40 CFR Part 171. Building the multi-state business license portfolio, applicator certification tracker with CE management, RUP purchase record system, pesticide use record log, and state deployment eligibility check are data management problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. NPMA (National Pest Management Association) and RISE (Responsible Industry for a Sound Environment — the commercial pesticide applicator trade association) member networks provide defined buyer communities.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a state commercial pesticide applicator business license portfolio for the 30 highest-pest-control-density states, a pesticide applicator certification tracker with state-specific category CE requirements, a RUP purchase record system with the required 2-year retention and inspector-ready report, a pesticide use record log with mandatory field completion gate, and a state deployment eligibility check; first 5-8 pest control company clients through NPMA member outreach while the applicator certification tracker and use record log are validated against real company compliance workflows.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through NPMA (National Pest Management Association) member network and state pest control association outreach (the concentrated professional communities for pest control company owners and operations managers — NPMA's PestWorld annual conference is the primary industry event), pesticide distributor referral networks (distributors who sell RUPs to commercial applicators see RUP purchase record compliance gaps at every inspector visit and refer compliance management tools to their commercial applicator customers), and state department of agriculture pesticide compliance training networks (state pesticide compliance officers who conduct CE training courses see certification tracking failures at every CE event and refer systematic compliance tools to company owners in their training cohorts).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per pest control company: $250-650/mo (depending on applicator count, state footprint, and RUP application volume — a 10-technician single-state pest control company: $250/mo; a 35-technician multi-state right-of-way vegetation management company with applicators certified in 5 states and high RUP application volume: $650/mo). Managing 25-40 company retainers at $425/mo average = **$10,625-17,000/mo**. State pesticide inspection response packages (one-time $300-500 for companies facing a state pesticide regulatory inspection who need to compile and organize RUP purchase records and use record logs on short notice) generate event-driven supplemental revenue. Reaching 25-40 company retainers requires concentrated engagement with NPMA state affiliate associations and 3-4 regional pesticide distributor referral relationships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Individual applicator certification renewals are staggered throughout the year — a 30-technician company with 8 CE renewal cycles distributed across the year has continuous certification tracking obligations. RUP purchase records must be maintained for every purchase throughout the year. Pesticide use records must be completed for every application — a 15-technician company completing 25 applications per day generates 125+ weekly record entries. State business license renewals are annual. New technician hires trigger new certification verification and state deployment eligibility events. Multi-state expansion triggers new state business license applications and new individual state certification requirements for all technicians moving into new service territories.
**Biggest risk:** A pest control technician who applies a restricted-use pesticide in a state without a valid certification in the required category — because the multi-state deployment eligibility wasn't checked before dispatch — violates EPA FIFRA and the state pesticide law, exposing the company to EPA civil penalties (up to $5,000 per violation for commercial applicators under FIFRA Section 14) and state department of agriculture enforcement action. Build the state deployment eligibility check as the primary operational protection: before any job is assigned to a technician, the system verifies the technician's current valid certification in the job's state and in the pest control category applicable to the service — an expired or missing certification blocks the assignment with a specific "certification not current in this state/category" alert.
**Growth path:** Side project → state business license portfolio, applicator certification tracker with CE management, RUP record system, use record log, and state deployment eligibility check deployed, first 5-8 company retainers through NPMA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-18 company retainers, inspection response packages generating event-driven supplemental revenue (~$4-7K/mo). ### 3. Medical Imaging Center State Radiation Machine Registration, Physicist QA & MQSA Mammography Certification
**Problem:** Outpatient medical imaging centers and radiation therapy facilities operating X-ray, CT, fluoroscopy, mammography, and nuclear medicine equipment face a multi-agency compliance framework administered by state radiation control programs, the FDA (for mammography under MQSA), and in some states the NRC or Agreement State radiation protection agencies. State radiation machine registration: in 49 states (all except Wyoming) and DC, medical X-ray equipment must be registered with the state radiation control program annually — each X-ray tube, CT scanner, fluoroscope, and mammography unit must be registered with the state, with annual registration renewal fees per machine. Failure to renew registrations or operate an unregistered machine generates state radiation control enforcement action and, in some states, mandatory facility closure until registrations are restored. Medical physicist QA compliance: state radiation control programs and federal requirements (MQSA for mammography) require periodic medical physicist quality assurance surveys of imaging equipment — mammography units must receive an annual medical physicist performance evaluation under MQSA, CT scanners require annual physicist QA under ACR (American College of Radiology) accreditation and most state radiation control programs, and fluoroscopy requires periodic physicist review for facilities performing high-dose fluoroscopic procedures. The scheduling of these inspections must track the equipment's last physicist survey date, the required survey frequency, and sufficient lead time to schedule an available qualified medical physicist before the survey deadline. MQSA mammography facility certification: all mammography facilities must hold an FDA MQSA facility certificate and undergo annual MQSA inspections by state-contracted MQSA inspectors — the facility certificate must be renewed annually, the facility must pass its annual MQSA inspection, and any MQSA Level 1 or Level 2 findings from an inspection must be remediated within the required 30-day or immediate correction period. An outpatient imaging center with 8-15 machines (2 X-ray rooms, 1 CT, 1 mammography, 1 fluoroscope, 1 bone densitometry, 1 ultrasound in states that regulate ultrasound) tracking state machine registrations, physicist survey schedules, and MQSA certification status across all machines without a dedicated compliance coordinator generates recurring compliance gaps.
**What to build:** A medical imaging center state radiation registration, physicist QA, and MQSA compliance management service — a state radiation machine registration portfolio (for each registered machine: state registration number, machine type and model, annual renewal deadline, registration fee payment status, and any state inspection scheduling obligations tied to the machine registration — with tiered renewal alerts for each machine, generating a unified annual renewal calendar across the entire equipment fleet), a medical physicist QA inspection scheduler (for each machine requiring periodic physicist QA: the machine type, the applicable QA standard — MQSA annual physicist survey, ACR CT accreditation annual physicist survey, state fluoroscopy physicist review — the date of the last physicist survey, the next survey due date, the assigned qualified medical physicist contact, and the inspection scheduling status — with alerts when a survey must be scheduled based on required lead time to ensure the physicist can perform the survey before the deadline), an MQSA facility certification tracker (for the facility's mammography unit: the FDA MQSA facility certificate number and expiration date, the date and result of the most recent annual MQSA inspection, any outstanding MQSA findings with required remediation deadline and remediation documentation status, and the accreditation body — ACR, Hologic, or PGMI — certification status and renewal deadline), a radiation equipment service record (for each machine: the maintenance service history — annual preventive maintenance, tube replacements, detector calibrations — with service dates and service engineer documentation, supporting the physicist QA baseline and the state registration record), and a compliance documentation package generator (producing a per-machine compliance summary showing state registration status, most recent physicist survey date and result, MQSA finding status for mammography, and next action due date — formatted for the facility's radiation safety officer and for presentation to state surveyors).
**Skill fit:** State radiation machine registration requirements are available from each state's radiation control program (typically the department of health or department of environmental quality). MQSA requirements are documented in 21 CFR Part 900 and FDA's MQSA inspection procedures. ACR CT accreditation QA requirements are available from ACR's CT accreditation program. Building the state machine registration portfolio, physicist QA scheduler, MQSA certification tracker, and compliance documentation generator are data management and scheduling problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. AHRA (Association for Medical Imaging Management) and AAPM (American Association of Physicists in Medicine) member networks provide defined buyer communities.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a state radiation machine registration portfolio for the 25 highest-imaging-center-density states, a medical physicist QA inspection scheduler with MQSA annual survey and ACR CT annual survey requirements, an MQSA facility certification tracker with MQSA finding remediation management, and a compliance documentation package generator; first 5-8 outpatient imaging center clients through AHRA member outreach while the machine registration portfolio and physicist QA scheduler are validated against real imaging center equipment fleets.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through AHRA (Association for Medical Imaging Management) member network and RBMA (Radiology Business Management Association) member outreach (the concentrated professional communities for imaging center administrators and radiology practice managers — AHRA's annual conference is the primary industry event), qualified medical physicist referral networks (medical physicists who perform annual QA surveys for imaging centers see state registration gaps and MQSA finding management failures at every survey visit and refer compliance management tools to imaging center administrators in their client portfolio), and imaging equipment service company referral networks (service companies who perform annual preventive maintenance on imaging equipment see registration renewal failures at every service visit and refer compliance tools to imaging center operators as part of their service relationship).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per imaging center: $300-750/mo (depending on machine count, MQSA mammography unit presence, and state regulatory complexity — a 4-machine outpatient imaging center with no mammography: $300/mo; a 12-machine multimodality center with mammography, CT, fluoroscopy, and nuclear medicine operating in a high-compliance state: $750/mo). Managing 20-35 center retainers at $500/mo average = **$10,000-17,500/mo**. State radiation machine registration renewal service packages (one-time $200-400 for imaging centers that have allowed machine registrations to lapse and need registration reinstatement coordination with the state radiation control program) generate event-driven supplemental revenue. ### 4. Motor Vehicle Dealer State License, DMV Title Transfer Deadline & Floor Plan Lender Lot Audit Compliance
**Problem:** Licensed motor vehicle dealers — both independent used car dealers and franchise new car dealers — face a recurring compliance framework across three overlapping systems that are each individually manageable but together create a compliance tracking problem that informal systems fail to handle reliably. State dealer license compliance: motor vehicle dealers must hold a state-issued motor vehicle dealer license with annual renewal, a surety bond in a state-specified minimum amount (typically $25,000-$50,000 for independent dealers), a designated dealer principal whose name appears on the license, a physical lot meeting state inspectability requirements, and in many states a dealer management system or business office requirement. DMV title transfer deadline compliance: in most states, a dealer must transfer title to the buyer within 30 days of the sale date (some states require 20 or even 15 days). Failing to transfer title on time generates per-vehicle penalty fees — typically $50-200 per vehicle per violation — assessed by the state DMV when the buyer ultimately registers the vehicle with a gap between the sale date and title transfer date. For a dealer selling 50-100 vehicles per month, even a 5-10% late title transfer rate generates $500-2,000 in monthly DMV penalty fees paid by the buyer that damage the dealer's reputation and, in states where dealers are required to ensure timely title transfer, can generate DMV dealer compliance complaints. Floor plan lender lot audit compliance: independent dealers who finance their inventory through floor plan lending agreements (typically with Nextgear Capital, AFC, or regional banks) are subject to periodic lot audits — floor plan lenders send field auditors to physically verify that every vehicle on the floor plan is present on the dealer's lot or at a defined off-site location. Vehicles that are sold or consigned but still on the floor plan create audit "out of trust" findings, which trigger immediate floor plan repayment demands and, in severe cases, floor plan line termination. Dealers with 50-150 vehicles on floor plan managing audits informally — without a systematic floor plan vs. physical inventory reconciliation — create "out of trust" exposure.
**What to build:** A motor vehicle dealer state license, title transfer, and floor plan compliance management service — a state dealer license compliance calendar (tracking the dealership's state motor vehicle dealer license number, annual renewal deadline, surety bond carrier and bond expiration date, designated dealer principal information, and any state lot inspection scheduling requirements — with tiered renewal alerts for the dealer license and the surety bond), a DMV title transfer deadline tracker (for each vehicle sold: sale date, vehicle VIN, buyer name, state DMV title transfer deadline (calculated from the sale date based on the state's applicable deadline — 15, 20, or 30 days), title transfer submission date, and title transfer confirmation from the DMV — with a daily alert list showing every sold vehicle whose title transfer deadline falls within the next 5 business days and has not yet been confirmed as transferred), a floor plan inventory reconciliation system (for each vehicle on the floor plan: VIN, floor plan advance date, floor plan lender, floor plan balance, physical lot location or off-site documentation — and a daily reconciliation of floor plan inventory against the dealer management system's inventory records to identify any vehicle on the floor plan that has been sold, consigned, or moved without being curtailed from the floor plan), a pre-audit floor plan preparation report (ahead of a scheduled or surprise floor plan lender lot audit: generating a complete floor plan inventory with each vehicle's physical location, floor plan advance date, and days on floor plan — and a reconciliation certification confirming that every floor plan vehicle has been located and no out-of-trust condition exists), and a dealer compliance dashboard (a morning daily summary showing: the state dealer license days until renewal, the bond expiration date, all sold vehicles with title transfer deadlines approaching in the next 5 business days, any sold vehicles with past-due title transfers, and any floor plan vehicles not confirmed located in the last 7 days).
**Skill fit:** State motor vehicle dealer licensing requirements are available from each state's DMV or motor vehicle dealer licensing agency. DMV title transfer deadline laws are codified in each state's vehicle code. Floor plan lending agreement audit requirements are documented in the dealer's floor plan lending agreement with their specific lender. Building the state dealer license compliance calendar, DMV title transfer deadline tracker, floor plan inventory reconciliation system, and daily compliance dashboard are data management problems the builder's engineering and data analytics skills address directly. NIADA (National Independent Automobile Dealers Association) and NADA (National Automobile Dealers Association) member networks provide defined buyer communities.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a state dealer license compliance calendar for the 30 highest-dealer-density states, a DMV title transfer deadline tracker with state-specific deadline rules and daily alert generation, a floor plan inventory reconciliation system with daily reconciliation against DMS inventory exports, and a compliance dashboard with morning summary; first 5-8 independent dealer clients through NIADA member outreach while the title transfer tracker and floor plan reconciliation system are validated against real dealer inventory workflows.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through NIADA (National Independent Automobile Dealers Association) state affiliate associations and dealer 20 Group networks (the concentrated professional communities for independent dealer owners — NIADA's annual convention is the primary industry event, and 20 Groups — peer study groups of 20 non-competing dealers — are the most trusted channel for peer vendor recommendations), dealer floor plan lender field representative referral networks (Nextgear Capital, AFC, and regional floor plan lenders whose field representatives visit hundreds of dealer lots see floor plan reconciliation failures at every audit and refer compliance tools to their dealer customers as part of their dealer relationship management), and dealer state DMV compliance training networks (state DMV dealer compliance offices that provide dealer compliance education see title transfer penalty patterns and refer compliance tracking tools to dealers with documented late title transfer histories).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per dealership: $200-500/mo (depending on monthly sales volume, floor plan size, and state regulatory complexity — a 20-vehicle-per-month independent dealer with a 30-vehicle floor plan: $200/mo; a 75-vehicle-per-month independent dealer with a 100-vehicle floor plan in a state with 15-day title transfer deadlines: $500/mo). Managing 35-60 dealer retainers at $325/mo average = **$11,375-19,500/mo**. Floor plan audit preparation packages (one-time $200-400 for dealers facing an unannounced floor plan lender audit who need a rapid physical inventory reconciliation and floor plan position report) generate event-driven supplemental revenue. Reaching 35-60 dealer retainers requires engagement with 5-8 NIADA state affiliate chapter communities and 2-3 floor plan lender referral relationships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** DMV title transfer deadline tracking is continuous — every vehicle sold generates a new deadline event within 15-30 days. Floor plan inventory reconciliation is daily — any vehicle sold but not curtailed from the floor plan creates an immediate out-of-trust exposure that grows with each day the discrepancy isn't caught. Floor plan lenders conduct lot audits on a rolling schedule — a 150-vehicle dealer may be audited monthly. New hires who are listed on the dealer license as dealer principals or salespeople may require DMV salesperson license verification in states with individual salesperson licensing.
**Biggest risk:** An independent dealer whose floor plan lender discovers an out-of-trust condition during a lot audit — vehicles that were sold but not curtailed, leaving the lender holding a security interest in vehicles the dealer no longer owns — faces immediate floor plan repayment demands for the out-of-trust vehicles and, in repeated violation cases, floor plan line termination, which would require the dealer to return all floored vehicles or pay off the entire floor plan balance immediately. Build the floor plan inventory reconciliation with a same-day out-of-trust alert: when a vehicle is recorded as sold in the dealer management system and the floor plan record for that VIN has not been marked as curtailed within 24 hours, the system generates an immediate out-of-trust alert with the VIN, floor plan balance, and floor plan lender contact — preventing the condition from persisting to the next audit.
**Growth path:** Side project → state dealer license calendar, DMV title transfer tracker with daily deadline alerts, floor plan inventory reconciliation, and compliance dashboard deployed, first 5-8 dealer retainers through NIADA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 15-20 dealer retainers, floor plan audit preparation packages generating event-driven revenue (~$4-7K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 35-60 dealer retainers, floor plan lender and NIADA state affiliate referral pipelines generating consistent new clients, pushing past $11-19K/mo.

### 5. Professional Employer Organization (PEO) Multi-State Registration, Workers' Comp Self-Insurance & State PEO Act Compliance
**Problem:** Professional employer organizations (PEOs) — companies that co-employ client worksite employees, providing HR, payroll, and benefits services while sharing employer liability with client businesses — face a multi-state regulatory framework that has expanded significantly as states have enacted state PEO acts requiring PEO registration or licensure. State PEO registration: 37 states and DC currently have laws or regulations governing PEOs, ranging from mandatory registration with the state department of insurance or labor (with financial net worth requirements, audited financial statements, surety bonds or letters of credit, and designated representative requirements) to notification-only schemes. PEOs operating in states with mandatory registration that fail to register face enforcement action and may be required to return all client employer of record services until registration is obtained — which can trigger immediate liability exposure for client businesses. Workers' compensation coverage compliance: PEOs must maintain workers' compensation coverage for all co-employed worksite employees, which may be provided through a commercial carrier, a PEO self-insurance arrangement, or a captive insurance structure — each with state-specific authorization requirements. In states that require PEO workers' comp to be placed under a PEO-specific policy (not the client's own policy), the PEO must maintain an auditable workers' comp policy with adequate payroll reporting. State PEO financial reporting: many state PEO registration laws require PEOs to file annual financial reports, audited financial statements, or proof of financial assurance (surety bond or letter of credit renewal) with the state labor or insurance department — with specific filing deadlines and financial thresholds that must be maintained. Mid-size PEOs (500-5,000 worksite employees) operating in 10-35 states manage state registration renewals, workers' comp policy renewals, and annual financial reporting obligations across multiple state regulatory agencies without a dedicated multi-state compliance tracking system.
**What to build:** A PEO multi-state registration, workers' comp, and state PEO act compliance management service — a multi-state PEO registration portfolio (for each operating state: the state PEO registration type — mandatory registration, notification, or no requirement — registration number, annual renewal deadline, financial assurance requirement — surety bond amount and carrier, or letter of credit bank and amount — surety bond or letter of credit expiration date, and annual financial statement or report submission deadline — with tiered renewal alerts for each state registration and each financial assurance instrument), a workers' compensation coverage tracker (for each state where co-employed worksite employees are located: workers' comp carrier or self-insurance authorization, policy number and expiration date, state-specific PEO workers' comp requirements — whether the state requires a PEO-specific policy or permits client-level policies under the PEO's arrangement — payroll audit schedule and most recent audit completion date, and any outstanding workers' comp premium audit items), an annual financial reporting calendar (for each state with annual financial reporting requirements: the specific financial statement or report required — audited financials, unaudited balance sheet, ESAC accreditation certification — the filing deadline, and the submission confirmation — with alerts beginning 90 days before the annual filing deadline to ensure the CPA or auditor engagement is initiated in time), a state PEO registration change event log (tracking events that may require prompt state notification or amendment — new client state, adding co-employed employees in a new state, change in the PEO's designated representative, change in surety bond carrier, financial net worth falling below state minimum — with a determination field and response action tracking), and a client state onboarding compliance check (before the PEO begins co-employing workers in a new state for a new client: confirming whether the PEO has a current registration in that state, whether the PEO's workers' comp policy covers worksite employees in that state, and whether any new state registration application must be filed before co-employment begins — preventing unauthorized PEO activity in unregistered states).
**Skill fit:** State PEO registration requirements and workers' compensation compliance requirements for PEOs are documented in NAPEO (National Association of Professional Employer Organizations) state law resources and each state's PEO registration statute or regulation. Building the multi-state PEO registration portfolio, workers' comp coverage tracker, annual financial reporting calendar, and client state onboarding compliance check are data management problems the builder's engineering and data analytics skills address directly. NAPEO member directory provides a defined buyer community of PEO principals and compliance officers.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a multi-state PEO registration portfolio for the 37 states with PEO registration laws plus the 13 states without requirements (tracking the regulatory status in each), a workers' compensation coverage tracker with state-specific PEO workers' comp requirement documentation, an annual financial reporting calendar with CPA engagement lead time alerts, and a client state onboarding compliance check; first 5-8 PEO clients through NAPEO member outreach while the multi-state registration portfolio and workers' comp tracker are validated against real PEO state registration situations.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through NAPEO (National Association of Professional Employer Organizations) member network outreach (NAPEO's annual conference is the primary industry event for PEO principals and compliance officers — there is no other comparable peer community), ESAC (Employer Services Assurance Corporation) accredited PEO referral networks (ESAC accreditation requires demonstrated compliance with state PEO registration laws, and ESAC's accreditation support team actively refers compliance management tools to accredited members who need systematic state registration tracking), and PEO workers' compensation carrier referral networks (specialty workers' comp insurers who write PEO policies — ADP TotalSource, Aon, and specialty PEO program carriers — include state registration compliance in their PEO underwriting review and refer compliance management tools to PEO clients with incomplete state registration portfolios).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per PEO: $600-1,500/mo (depending on worksite employee count, state registration footprint, and financial reporting complexity — a PEO with 800 worksite employees registered in 12 states: $600/mo; a PEO with 3,500 worksite employees registered in 28 states with complex workers' comp captive structure and multi-state annual financial reporting: $1,500/mo). Managing 12-20 PEO retainers at $1,000/mo average = **$12,000-20,000/mo**. State PEO registration new-state onboarding packages (one-time $400-800 for PEOs expanding into a new state requiring a new registration application with financial statement preparation and surety bond procurement coordination) generate project-driven supplemental revenue. Reaching 12-20 PEO retainers requires concentrated engagement with the NAPEO membership community — NAPEO has approximately 900 member PEOs, giving a defined addressable market.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** State PEO registration renewals are annual in each registered state — a PEO registered in 25 states has 25 annual registration events distributed across the year. Surety bonds and letters of credit renew annually and independently of the registration renewal date. Annual financial statement filings are required by most mandatory-registration states. Workers' comp policies renew annually and require payroll audits that generate follow-up premium adjustments. Every new client in a new state triggers a client state onboarding compliance check and potentially a new state registration application. ESAC accreditation renewal is annual. Financial net worth thresholds must be monitored quarterly to ensure continued compliance with state minimums.
**Biggest risk:** A PEO that begins co-employing workers in a state with mandatory PEO registration without first obtaining the required state registration — because the client state onboarding compliance check wasn't performed — operates as an unregistered PEO in that state, potentially voiding the co-employment arrangement under state law and exposing both the PEO and the client business to liability for employment obligations (workers' comp claims, wage payment obligations) that should have been covered by the registered co-employment structure. Build the client state onboarding compliance check as a non-bypassable gate: before the PEO's system generates a co-employment agreement for a new client in any state, the check confirms the PEO's current registration status in that state — if no current registration exists, the gate generates a registration application initiation prompt with the state's application requirements and estimated processing time, preventing co-employment from beginning before registration is secured.
**Growth path:** Side project → multi-state PEO registration portfolio, workers' comp coverage tracker, annual financial reporting calendar, and client state onboarding compliance check deployed, first 5-8 PEO retainers through NAPEO outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 6-8 PEO retainers, new-state registration packages generating project-driven revenue (~$5-8K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 12-20 PEO retainers, ESAC and workers' comp carrier referral pipelines generating consistent new clients, pushing past $12-20K/mo.
