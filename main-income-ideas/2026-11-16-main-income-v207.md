# Main Income Ideas - 2026-11-16 (Session 207)

## Who this is for
A 29-year-old software engineer with data analytics skills who wants to replace a full-time salary ($8-15K+/mo) within 12-24 months by building solo, in evenings and weekends, with no outside funding. Every idea below must have a realistic path to first revenue inside 90 days and a named, reachable distribution channel.

## Lens for this session: Alex Hormozi
Built from the $100M Offers / $100M Leads frameworks:
- **Value equation:** Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood of Success) / (Time Delay x Effort & Sacrifice). This session leans on community and status: people pay more for a place where they belong and a result they can show.
- **Grand Slam Offer:** performance-priced or guarantee-backed.
- **Get customers to bring customers:** Hormozi's referral and "customer-get-customer" lead methods. Each idea turns existing participants into the marketing channel.

Per standing feedback, none of these are compliance or regulatory tools. Each solves a revenue-leakage or growth problem.

## Ideas

### 1. Registration, Sponsor and Repeat-Runner Engine for Local Race Directors

**Problem:** A race director running 5Ks, trail runs and charity walks handles registration with generic forms, chases sponsors by email and loses runners after the finish line. Early registration lags, and the same participants are never invited back.

**What to build:** A race page with tiered pricing that rises by date, team and refer-a-friend discounts, a sponsor package builder with a results report, a runner list for next season and a series pass. Show registrations by day versus last year, referrals and sponsor revenue.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Add 20% more registrations or the first season is free." Fee: $99/mo plus $1 per registration.

**Skill fit:** Registration pages, pricing tiers and sponsor reports.

**MVP scope:** 3-4 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 45-75 days through race-director groups, ahead of spring racing.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** A director with 3,000 registrations a year pays about $1,188 + $3,000 = $4,200/yr, or $350/mo. 35 directors x $350 = **$12,250/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Races repeat each year and the runner list grows with every event.

**Biggest risk:** Strong incumbent registration platforms. Differentiate on referrals, series passes and sponsor reports.

**Growth path:** Race directors, then charity walks, cycling events, obstacle races and festivals.

### 2. Run-Club and Replacement Reminder Engine for Independent Running Stores

**Problem:** A running store sells shoes that wear out in 400 miles, yet nobody reminds customers. Group runs bring foot traffic but are not tracked, and customers drift to online sellers at the next purchase.

**What to build:** A customer profile with shoe model and purchase date, a "time for new shoes" text at the right interval, a run-club calendar with RSVP and a points program for attending, plus a referral bonus. Show repeat purchases and club attendance.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Bring back 15% more shoe buyers within a year or the first quarter is free." Fee: $89/mo.

**Skill fit:** Reminder logic, RSVP and loyalty points.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-60 days through store-owner groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $89/mo. 130 stores = **$11,570/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Replacement cycles are predictable, and a club builds loyalty.

**Biggest risk:** Small store base. Add outdoor, cycling and triathlon shops.

**Growth path:** Running stores, then cycling, triathlon, hiking and outdoor stores.

### 3. Game-Day Reservation and Tab Engine for Sports Bars

**Problem:** A sports bar fills on big game days and sits quiet otherwise. Groups call to reserve tables, walk-ins are turned away and loyal fans have no reason to come for lesser games.

**What to build:** A game-day calendar with table reservations and a deposit credited to the bill, a fan group text list by team, a pre-order for group platters and a "bring your crew" bonus. Show reserved covers, deposit conversions and spend per group.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Fill your top 10 game days to capacity or the first quarter is free." Fee: $99/mo plus 1% of pre-paid deposits.

**Skill fit:** Reservations, deposits, SMS lists and pre-orders.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 21-45 days through bar-owner groups, ahead of football season.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** A bar taking $10K/mo of deposits pays $99 + $100 = about $200. 60 bars x $200 = **$12,000/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Big nights drive a large share of revenue, and fan lists are reusable.

**Biggest risk:** Seasonality and general restaurant reservation tools. Differentiate on team-fan lists and group pre-orders.

**Growth path:** Sports bars, then breweries, wing shops, pubs and watch-party venues.

### 4. Fleet-Utilization and Booking Engine for Small Car-Rental and Peer-Rental Fleet Owners

**Problem:** A small operator with 5-30 rental cars takes bookings by phone and spreadsheet. Cars sit idle midweek, extensions are missed and photos at pickup and return are inconsistent.

**What to build:** An online booking page with live availability, dynamic weekday pricing, pickup and return photo checklists, an extension request link and a "book again" offer at return. Show utilization per car and revenue per car per day.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Raise fleet utilization 10 points or the first quarter is free." Fee: $159/mo flat per fleet.

**Skill fit:** Availability calendars, pricing rules and checklists.

**MVP scope:** 4 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 45-75 days through operator groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** A fleet pays a flat $159/mo. 80 fleets = **$12,720/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Each idle car day is lost revenue, and utilization is easy to measure.

**Biggest risk:** Insurance and damage disputes. Focus on booking and photo evidence only.

**Growth path:** Car rentals, then scooters, RVs, trailers, party-equipment and tool rentals.

### 5. League and Tournament Engine for Bowling Alleys and Billiard Halls

**Problem:** A bowling alley or billiard hall depends on leagues for weekday revenue, but league sign-up is paper-based. Teams drop out mid-season, standings live in spreadsheets and there is no off-season plan.

**What to build:** League and tournament pages with team registration and payment, standings and schedules, a substitute finder, a mid-season check-in for at-risk teams and an off-season series promotion. Show team retention and weekday lane revenue.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Fill 20% more league slots or the first quarter is free." Fee: $79/mo.

**Skill fit:** Team registration, standings and reminders.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days through venue-owner groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $79/mo. 150 venues = **$11,850/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Leagues bring recurring weekly revenue, and referrals fill new teams.

**Biggest risk:** Existing league-software vendors. Differentiate on the sub finder and at-risk team check-in.

**Growth path:** Bowling and billiards, then darts, pickleball, cornhole and trivia leagues.
