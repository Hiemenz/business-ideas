### 1. Escape Room & Special-Amusement-Building Venue NFPA 101 Egress & Fire Marshal Permit Compliance
**Problem:** Escape rooms, haunted attractions, and other windowless or maze-like entertainment venues fall under NFPA 101 Life Safety Code's Special Amusement Buildings provisions, which require automatic smoke detection tied to a building-wide alarm, emergency lighting along every designated escape path, and — for any room designed to disorient or conceal the exit — a staff-monitored override or "panic release" protocol that must actually work when tested, enforced through a state or local fire marshal's special-amusement-building permit that most jurisdictions require to be renewed annually alongside a documented egress-drill or system test. A venue operating past its permit's renewal date, or one unable to produce a passing egress-system test log during a fire marshal's routine inspection, is exactly the finding that shuts an attraction down mid-season — and because escape rooms are, by design, spaces a patron cannot exit through the normal door, a fire marshal's post-incident scrutiny after any fire anywhere in the "extreme attraction" industry tends to fall hardest on venues that can't immediately produce their own compliance record. Every room's props and set-dressing must also be verified against the venue's approved fire-rated materials list, since a prop or decoration using non-fire-rated foam or fabric is a separate, independently citable violation. A small operator running several rooms in one location, or a multi-location chain, manages permit renewal, per-room egress-drill documentation, and prop/material-compliance verification with no dedicated compliance function beyond a binder in the back office.
**What to build:** A special-amusement-building permit renewal tracker with a "cleared to open" gate per location; a per-room egress-system test log (smoke detector function, emergency lighting, panic-release mechanism) on a fire-marshal-mandated interval; and a prop/set-dressing fire-rated-material compliance checklist per room.
**Skill fit:** NFPA 101's special-amusement-building provisions and state/local fire marshal permit cycles are public, structured code and regulatory data with a well-defined per-venue and per-room record requirement, and the permit-renewal gate plus the egress-test log are the same deterministic "block the action until the credential clears / require the record before the action proceeds" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an experiential-entertainment trade most small operators currently track on a paper binder in the back office with no systematic cross-check between a specific room's last egress test and its continued operation.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the permit-renewal tracker, the egress-test log, and the prop/material checklist; land the first 5-8 operators through Room Escape Conference (TRECC)/Room Escape Artist industry-community outreach and state amusement/attraction association meetings.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — Room Escape Conference community and state fire marshal association public-inspection lists, where small escape-room and haunted-attraction operators already juggle annual permit renewal and periodic egress testing with no dedicated compliance staff.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249/mo base + $15/mo per room beyond the first 3. Average client (6 rooms) = $249 + $45 = $294/mo. 32-40 venues × $294/mo = **$9,408-11,760/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every room's egress-system test recurs on its own fire-marshal-mandated interval, every prop or set change reopens the fire-rated-material checklist, and the special-amusement-building permit itself renews annually — a continuous, per-room compliance stream tied directly to the venue's room count and seasonal prop turnover.
**Biggest risk:** A room opened without a documented passing egress-system test, or a special-amusement-building permit operated past its renewal date, is exactly the finding a fire marshal inspection is built to catch and can shut the attraction down mid-season — the permit gate and the egress-test log are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → permit tracker, egress-test log, and material checklist deployed, first 5-8 operator retainers through TRECC/state fire marshal outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 18-22 operators. Replaces-full-time-salary → 32-40 operators, pushing past $9.41-11.76K/mo.

### 2. Kratom Retailer State Kratom Consumer Protection Act Batch-Testing & Age-Verification Compliance
**Problem:** Retailers selling kratom products operate under a growing patchwork of state Kratom Consumer Protection Act (KCPA) statutes — enacted using American Kratom Association model language in states including Utah, Arizona, Colorado, Georgia, Nevada, and Oklahoma — that require every product sold to carry a current, batch-specific Certificate of Analysis confirming its 7-hydroxymitragynine content stays under the statutory limit and screening for contaminants such as heavy metals and salmonella, prohibit sale to anyone under 21 with documented age-verification at the point of sale, and in several states require the retailer itself to register with the state department of agriculture or health before selling. A shop that sells a batch whose COA has expired, or one that sells to a minor without a documented ID check, is exactly the finding a state agriculture-department compliance sweep or an underage-sale sting is built to catch, and repeat violations in KCPA states can result in the retailer's registration being revoked — a growing risk as more states adopt the model act each legislative session. A small shop or multi-location smoke/vape retailer carrying a rotating slate of kratom SKUs from multiple suppliers manages per-batch COA currency, per-sale age-verification documentation, and state registration renewal with no dedicated compliance function beyond a shoebox of supplier paperwork.
**What to build:** A per-SKU/per-batch Certificate of Analysis tracker flagging any product on the shelf whose COA is missing, expired, or shows an alkaloid level over the statutory limit; an age-verification incident log documenting ID checks at the point of sale; and a state registration renewal tracker for each KCPA state the retailer operates in.
**Skill fit:** State KCPA statutes and their batch-testing thresholds are public, structured state regulatory text with a well-defined per-batch record requirement, and the COA-currency tracker plus the age-verification log are the same deterministic "confirm the required record is on file / document the compliance action at the point of sale" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a fast-growing botanical-retail trade most small shops currently track on a folder of supplier PDFs with no systematic cross-check between a specific batch's COA and its continued sale.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the COA tracker, the age-verification log, and the state registration tracker for the highest-volume KCPA states; land the first 5-8 retailers through American Kratom Association GMP-qualified vendor and retailer outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — American Kratom Association's retailer and vendor network and independent smoke-shop/vape-retailer trade forums, where small shops already juggle per-batch COA currency and state registration renewal across a rotating supplier list with no dedicated compliance staff.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new batch received opens its own COA-currency event, every sale opens its own age-verification documentation requirement, and the retailer's own state registration renews on its own cycle — a continuous, per-batch and per-sale compliance stream tied directly to the shop's turnover, growing as more states adopt KCPA legislation each year.
**Biggest risk:** A product sold with an expired or missing COA, or a sale made to a minor without documented ID verification, is exactly the finding a state compliance sweep or underage-sale sting is built to catch — the COA tracker and the age-verification log are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → COA tracker, age-verification log, and registration tracker deployed, first 5-8 retailer retainers through AKA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 15-19 retailers. Replaces-full-time-salary → 27-33 retailers, pushing past $9.15-11.19K/mo.

### 3. Rendering & Animal By-Product/Deadstock Collection Route Operator State Hauler License & FDA Ruminant Feed Ban Compliance
**Problem:** Companies that collect deadstock, meat-processing byproducts, and used cooking grease from farms, butcher shops, and restaurants for rendering into tallow, meal, and other by-products operate under state Department of Agriculture inedible-products hauler licensure, a state or EPA wastewater discharge permit covering the rendering facility's own effluent, and the FDA's Ruminant Feed Ban Rule (21 CFR § 589.2000), which requires the renderer to maintain records establishing that ruminant-derived protein material is physically segregated and excluded from any product stream destined for cattle or other ruminant feed — a facility unable to produce a clean segregation record during an FDA or state feed-safety inspection is exactly the finding that can suspend its ability to sell finished product into the animal-feed market entirely, not just the specific batch in question. Every collection route also generates its own per-pickup manifest documenting the generator site, material type, and weight, since a route unable to reconcile what it collected against what it delivered to the rendering facility is the same kind of gap a state agriculture-department audit is built to expose. A small rendering/deadstock route operator running several trucks across dozens of farm and butcher-shop clients manages per-truck hauler-license currency, per-pickup manifest reconciliation, and ruminant-material segregation documentation with no dedicated compliance function beyond a paper route sheet.
**What to build:** A state inedible-products hauler license renewal tracker per vehicle with a "cleared to collect" gate; a per-pickup manifest tracker reconciling generator site, material type, and weight against what's delivered to the facility; and a ruminant-material segregation log documenting FDA feed-ban compliance for every batch processed.
**Skill fit:** State inedible-products hauler licensure and the FDA's Ruminant Feed Ban Rule's segregation record requirements are public, structured federal and state regulatory data with a well-defined per-vehicle and per-batch record requirement, and the manifest-reconciliation tracker plus the segregation log are the same deterministic "cross-check two records that must match / confirm the required record is on file" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an agricultural-byproduct trade most small operators currently track on a paper route sheet with no systematic cross-check between a specific pickup's collected material and its downstream segregation status.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the hauler license tracker, the manifest reconciliation tracker, and the segregation log; land the first 5-8 operators through National Renderers Association (NRA) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — National Renderers Association member directory and state independent renderer/deadstock-hauler association meetings, where small route operators already juggle per-vehicle license renewal and per-pickup manifest reconciliation across dozens of farm and butcher-shop clients with no dedicated compliance staff.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299/mo base + $10/mo per generator client site tracked. Average client (40 sites) = $299 + $400 = $699/mo. 13-17 operators × $699/mo = **$9,087-11,883/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every truck's hauler license renews on its own independent cycle, every pickup opens its own manifest-reconciliation event, and every batch processed generates its own ruminant-segregation documentation requirement — a continuous, per-vehicle and per-batch compliance stream tied directly to the operator's route size.
**Biggest risk:** A batch processed without a clean ruminant-material segregation record, or a pickup that doesn't reconcile against the facility's delivery log, is exactly the finding an FDA feed-safety inspection or a state agriculture-department audit is built to catch — the segregation log and the manifest-reconciliation tracker are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → hauler license tracker, manifest reconciliation tracker, and segregation log deployed, first 5-8 operator retainers through NRA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 8-10 operators. Replaces-full-time-salary → 13-17 operators, pushing past $9.09-11.88K/mo.

### 4. Paintball & Outdoor Adventure-Sport Field Operator State Safety Statute & Eye-Protection Compliance
**Problem:** Paintball fields and outdoor adventure-sport venues operate under state paintball-facility safety statutes — New Jersey's paintball-facility law (N.J.S.A. 5:14A) and similar statutes in other states among them — that require posted safety rules at the field entrance, documented verification of minimum-age and parental-consent requirements for every underage player, and ASTM F2211/F1777-compliant eye and face protection issued and logged for every player before they step onto the field, since eye injury is the single most common and most litigated paintball-related incident. A field that allows a player onto the course without a logged eye-protection issuance, or one that admits a minor without documented parental consent on file, is exactly the finding a state consumer-safety field audit — or a plaintiff's attorney after an eye-injury claim — is built to expose, and a documented gap in either record is frequently the deciding factor in a facility's liability exposure. Every marker (paintball gun) used at the facility must also be chronographed to confirm it doesn't exceed the field's velocity limit, with the result logged per session, since an over-velocity marker in play is a separate, independently citable safety-rule violation. A small field running several referees/marshals across a busy weekend schedule manages per-player waiver and eye-protection issuance, per-marker chronograph logs, and age/consent verification with no dedicated compliance function beyond a clipboard at the check-in counter.
**What to build:** A per-player waiver, age-verification, and eye-protection issuance log gating field access until all three are on file; a per-session marker chronograph log flagging any marker over the field's velocity limit; and a posted-safety-rules and staff-training currency tracker per location.
**Skill fit:** State paintball-facility safety statutes and ASTM F2211/F1777's eye-protection standards are public, structured state and standards-body data with a well-defined per-player and per-marker record requirement, and the field-access gate plus the chronograph log are the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance record clears / log the required check before play begins" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an outdoor-recreation trade most small fields currently track on a paper sign-in clipboard with no systematic cross-check between a specific player's eye-protection issuance and their field-access status.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the waiver/eye-protection field-access gate, the chronograph log, and the safety-rules/staff-training tracker; land the first 5-8 fields through PbNation (the largest independent paintball community) and regional paintball field-owner network outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — PbNation's field-owner forums and regional paintball association meetings, where small field operators already juggle per-player age/waiver verification and per-session chronograph logs during busy weekend play with no dedicated compliance staff.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249/mo base + $10/mo per referee/marshal tracked. Average client (8 marshals) = $249 + $80 = $329/mo. 28-35 operators × $329/mo = **$9,212-11,515/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every player who steps onto the field generates its own waiver/eye-protection issuance event, every session opens its own marker chronograph log, and every underage player requires its own documented parental-consent record — a continuous, per-player and per-session compliance stream tied directly to the field's weekend traffic.
**Biggest risk:** A player allowed onto the field without a logged eye-protection issuance, or a marker in play without a current chronograph result, is exactly the finding a state safety audit or an eye-injury liability claim is built to catch — the field-access gate and the chronograph log are the core protections.

### 5. Raw Milk & Herd-Share Dairy Operation State Raw-Milk Statute & Pathogen-Testing Compliance
**Problem:** Dairies selling unpasteurized milk directly to consumers operate under a patchwork of state raw-milk statutes ranging from direct retail permitting — such as California's CDFA Raw Milk Permit, which requires routine Coliform, Standard Plate Count, and pathogen (Listeria, Salmonella, E. coli O157:H7) testing on a fixed schedule with results retained for inspection — to herd-share/cow-share-only legality in states like Colorado and Utah, where raw milk can only be distributed to individuals who hold a documented ownership share in the specific animal it came from, requiring the operator to maintain a per-member herd-share contract and ownership registry that a state agriculture-department audit can demand to see at any time. A dairy that distributes milk to someone without a current, signed herd-share agreement on file — or a permitted raw-milk retailer that sells a batch past its pathogen-test result's validity window — is exactly the finding a state dairy inspector's routine or complaint-driven audit is built to catch, and because raw milk carries genuine foodborne-illness risk, a documented compliance gap is also the first thing scrutinized after any illness complaint, regardless of whether that specific batch was actually implicated. A small dairy running a rotating herd-share membership or a permitted direct-retail operation manages per-member share-contract currency, per-batch pathogen-test-result tracking, and state permit/registration renewal with no dedicated compliance function beyond a paper ledger and a filing cabinet of signed agreements.
**What to build:** A per-member herd-share contract and ownership-registry tracker (for herd-share states) with a "cleared to receive milk" gate; a per-batch pathogen-test-result tracker flagging any batch sold past its result's validity window; and a state raw-milk permit/registration renewal tracker.
**Skill fit:** State raw-milk statutes and their testing-interval requirements are public, structured state regulatory data with a well-defined per-member and per-batch record requirement, and the herd-share eligibility gate plus the pathogen-test-currency tracker are the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance record clears / confirm the required record is on file before the action proceeds" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a fast-growing direct-to-consumer dairy trade most small operators currently track on a paper ledger with no systematic cross-check between a specific member's share status and the batch they're receiving.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the herd-share registry with the eligibility gate, the pathogen-test tracker, and the state permit renewal tracker; land the first 5-8 dairies through Raw Milk Institute (RAWMI) and Weston A. Price Foundation local-chapter outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — Raw Milk Institute's listed-dairy network and Weston A. Price Foundation local chapter meetings, where small dairies already juggle per-member herd-share agreements and per-batch pathogen-test documentation with no dedicated compliance staff.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $6/mo per herd-share member or subscriber tracked. Average client (60 members) = $199 + $360 = $559/mo. 17-21 dairies × $559/mo = **$9,503-11,739/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new herd-share member opens its own contract-currency event, every batch produced opens its own pathogen-test-result cycle, and the dairy's own state permit or registration renews on its own schedule — a continuous, per-member and per-batch compliance stream tied directly to the dairy's herd size and membership growth.
**Biggest risk:** Milk distributed to someone without a current herd-share agreement on file, or a batch sold past its pathogen-test result's validity window, is exactly the finding a state dairy inspector's audit or an illness-complaint investigation is built to catch — the eligibility gate and the pathogen-test tracker are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → herd-share registry, pathogen-test tracker, and permit tracker deployed, first 5-8 dairy retainers through RAWMI/WAPF outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 10-13 dairies. Replaces-full-time-salary → 17-21 dairies, pushing past $9.50-11.74K/mo.
