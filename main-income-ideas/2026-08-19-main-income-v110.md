### 1. New Hire Paperwork Automation for Small Businesses
**Problem:** A small business with 5-40 employees hires a new person and triggers a paperwork cascade: the new hire needs to complete a federal I-9 (with physical ID verification), a W-4, a state income tax withholding form, a direct deposit authorization, an emergency contact form, and a signed employee handbook acknowledgment. The HR person (or more often, the owner) emails each form individually, the new hire fills them out on paper or in a Google Form that wasn't designed for this, and documents get returned out of order, incomplete, or not at all. By the first day of work, 30% of new hires have at least one document missing. The I-9 completion deadline is 3 days after the start date — missing it is a federal violation, but most small businesses don't track it. Enterprise HRIS platforms (Rippling, Gusto, BambooHR) solve this but cost $8-15/employee/month with minimum fees that put them out of reach for a 6-person shop.
**What to build:** A new hire onboarding document collection tool — the employer sets up their standard onboarding packet once (I-9, W-4, state tax form, direct deposit, handbook, and any custom forms they add). When a new hire is added, the tool sends them a single mobile-optimized link. The new hire completes each form in sequence: digital signature via a lightweight e-sign flow, direct deposit info captured and stored securely, handbook acknowledgment with a typed-name confirmation. The employer dashboard shows every new hire's document completion status in real time — green (all complete), yellow (some pending), red (I-9 deadline approaching with documents still missing). An I-9 deadline tracker: the tool calculates the 3-day I-9 completion window from the hire's start date and sends alerts to the employer at the 2-day mark if the form isn't yet complete. Document storage: every completed document stored as a PDF per employee, accessible forever.
**Skill fit:** E-signature via a lightweight custom implementation (typed name + timestamp + IP address, legally sufficient for most employment forms) or DocuSign API. PDF generation for completed forms. Secure file storage on S3. The I-9 deadline tracker is a date calculation. The onboarding link is a per-hire token that expires after completion. The employer dashboard is a status aggregation. No complex backend — clean form collection with a deadline layer.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — configurable onboarding packet, per-hire link generation, e-sign flow, and employer dashboard with I-9 deadline alert. An employer who goes from chasing 6 documents across 6 email threads to seeing a single completion dashboard — and who never misses an I-9 deadline again — has justified the tool on the first hire.
**Time to first $:** 14-30 days — small business owners are reachable through NFIB (National Federation of Independent Business), local chamber of commerce networks, and small business Facebook groups (Small Business Owners of America — 200,000+ members). A local approach works first: any business with 5-40 employees that hired someone in the last 6 months felt this pain. A single LinkedIn post — "built a tool that sends new hires one link for all their paperwork" — generates immediate inbound from the builder's own network.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $49-149/month per business depending on hire frequency. Average business at $89/month. 120 businesses × $89/month = **$10,680/month**. This is a high-volume, low-price product. Upsell: a 90-day new hire check-in module ($29/month add-on) — automated check-in surveys at 30, 60, and 90 days post-hire asking 3 questions about onboarding experience and role fit, giving the owner early warning of a new hire who's already disengaging.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every time the business hires someone, the tool is needed again. The onboarding packet configuration is built once and reused forever. A business owner who's used the tool for 5 hires has a document archive per employee that would take hours to reconstruct — they'll never go back to email chaos.
**Biggest risk:** I-9 ID verification requirement — the I-9 requires physical inspection of the new hire's identity documents by the employer, which no software can replace. Mitigation: the tool handles all the other paperwork digitally and provides the employer a clear I-9 instruction checklist for the physical verification step; in-person remote verification rules introduced post-COVID give additional flexibility for distributed teams.
**Growth path:** Side project → onboarding packet, per-hire link, e-sign, and dashboard live, first 10-20 business clients through local chamber of commerce outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 40-60 businesses, add state-specific tax withholding forms for top 10 states, add the 90-day check-in module. Replaces-full-time-salary → 120+ businesses, add benefits enrollment integration (connecting to common benefits providers like PlanSource or Ease), expand upmarket to 40-100-employee businesses whose HR person is managing onboarding alone.

### 2. Price Increase Communications for Service Businesses
**Problem:** A marketing agency, bookkeeper, consultant, landscaper, or house cleaner knows they need to raise prices — their costs have risen, their market rate has increased, and some clients have been paying the same rate for 3-4 years. But they don't do it because the conversation is uncomfortable, they don't know which clients to raise first, and they're afraid of losing accounts. The result: they undercharge clients who have been with them for years while charging new clients 30-40% more for identical work, compressing their overall margin and creating a quiet resentment toward their longest-tenured accounts. A service business with 40 clients, 20 of whom haven't had a price increase in 2+ years, is leaving $2,000-6,000/month on the table in margin they can't see because it's priced into old contracts.
**What to build:** A price increase planning and communications tool — the service business enters their client list with current rate, service type, last price increase date, and contract renewal date. The tool surfaces a ranked list of "repricing candidates": clients most overdue for a price increase, sorted by gap between current rate and the business's current market rate for that service tier (configurable by the owner). A staggered rollout planner: the owner selects which clients to reprice this quarter, and the tool staggers the notification dates — never sending more than 2 price increase notices in the same week so no client hears about it from another client simultaneously. A price increase notification email generator: the owner selects a client, sets the new rate and effective date, and the tool drafts a professional, relationship-preserving price increase email in the owner's voice ("Given the growth of our work together and rising costs, I'll be adjusting your monthly rate to $X effective [date]"). The owner edits and sends directly from the tool. A tracking view: which clients have been notified, which have acknowledged, which have raised concerns, and what the total monthly revenue impact will be after the rollout is complete.
**Skill fit:** The client and rate data model is simple. The repricing candidate ranking is a calculation: months since last price increase + rate gap vs. current market rate. The staggered rollout scheduler is a date allocation algorithm (spread N clients across M weeks with a max-per-week constraint). The email generator uses a template with variable substitution — no LLM required for a quality output, though an LLM call to personalize the draft per client relationship length is a natural upsell. Email sending via SendGrid or direct Gmail API.
**MVP scope:** 1-2 weekends — client list entry, repricing candidate ranking, staggered rollout planner, email draft generator, and notification tracking. A business owner who identifies 18 underpriced clients, staggers price increase notices across 9 weeks, and recovers $3,200/month in additional recurring revenue has made back the tool's annual cost in the first month.
**Time to first $:** 14-30 days — service business owners are reachable through agency owner communities (Agency Hackers, UGURUS, Smart Agency Masterclass podcast), bookkeeper and consultant Facebook groups, and freelancer communities (Freelancers Union, independent contractor LinkedIn groups). A post framed as "I built a tool that tells you which clients to raise prices on first and writes the email for you" generates immediate viral traction in agency owner Slack communities where this conversation happens weekly.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $49-129/month per business. Average client at $79/month. 140 businesses × $79/month = **$11,060/month**. Low price point, high volume. Upsell: a pricing benchmark report ($39/month add-on) — showing the owner what comparable service businesses in their category and market are charging (sourced from anonymized aggregate data across the tool's user base), giving them data to justify the new rate to skeptical clients.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Price increases are a recurring event — most service businesses should raise prices annually, making the tool useful every year. The client history and rate timeline accumulate, so the tool can flag clients automatically when they hit the 12-month mark without a price increase. The staggered rollout logic improves with each repricing wave the owner completes.
**Biggest risk:** Owners who are so conflict-averse they won't act on the tool's recommendations. Mitigation: frame the product around the email generator — the hardest part of a price increase is writing the message, and a professional, relationship-preserving draft removes the primary psychological barrier. "The hard part is done" is the pitch.
**Growth path:** Side project → client list, repricing candidate ranking, rollout planner, and email generator live, first 10-20 service business clients through agency owner Slack communities. Replaces-part-time-income → 40-60 businesses, add Gmail API integration for sending directly from the tool, add pricing benchmark report module. Replaces-full-time-salary → 140+ businesses, add annual pricing review reminder, expand to SaaS companies doing annual price increase rollouts across their subscriber base.

### 3. Weekly Project Status Report Automation for Dev Agencies
**Problem:** A web or app development agency with 8-25 clients spends 2-4 hours every Friday afternoon writing client status update emails: summarizing what was completed this week, what's in progress, what's planned for next week, and any blockers or decisions needed from the client. The project manager (or the developer themselves) opens Linear, Jira, or Asana, manually reviews the week's completed and in-progress tickets, and translates technical task names into client-readable language. At 3 hours per client per month and $100/hour project manager time, a 12-client agency is spending $3,600/month on status email production. The clients who receive these updates love them; the PMs who write them resent the time they consume.
**What to build:** A weekly project status report automation tool that integrates with Linear, Jira, and Asana — pulling completed tasks from the prior 7 days, in-progress tasks, and upcoming planned work per project per client. The tool groups and formats the tasks into a client-ready status email: "Completed this week" (tasks marked done in the prior 7 days, translated from technical ticket titles to plain-English summaries using configurable title mappings or a lightweight LLM call), "In progress" (current sprint work), "Coming up next week" (next-sprint planned tasks), and a "Decisions needed" section the PM populates manually. A PM review queue: every Friday at 9 AM, the PM receives a draft report for each client — they read it, make any edits (the "decisions needed" section, any context a task title doesn't capture), and click Send. The client receives a clean, consistently formatted email every Friday. A client engagement tracker: does the client open the report? Do they reply? Declining engagement is an early signal the relationship is cooling.
**Skill fit:** Linear has an excellent GraphQL API; Jira and Asana have REST APIs with strong documentation. The task title translation is either a configurable keyword-mapping table (the PM sets up translations once: "implement OAuth flow" → "added secure login") or a GPT-4o-mini API call per task for agencies that want natural language summaries. The report template is a structured HTML email. The PM review queue is a simple approval flow per client. The client open/reply tracking is standard email analytics via SendGrid.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — Linear API integration, weekly task pull, report template generation, PM review queue with edit and send, and client delivery. A PM who goes from spending 3 hours writing 4 client status reports on Friday afternoon to spending 25 minutes reviewing and sending 4 auto-generated drafts has reclaimed 2.5 hours of their Friday. Multiply by 4 Fridays and the math is obvious.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — dev agency owners and project managers are reachable through the Agency Hackers community, MicroConf (for developer-founders who are also their own PM), Indie Hackers, and LinkedIn agency owner communities. A tweet from a well-known agency founder saying "this tool saved my PM 3 hours of Fridays" generates hundreds of inbound inquiries from agencies with identical workflows.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-499/month per agency depending on client count. Average agency at $299/month. 35 agencies × $299/month = **$10,465/month**. Upsell: a client-facing project dashboard ($99/month add-on) — a live web portal per client showing their project's real-time task board (read-only view of their Linear/Jira board), so clients who want more than weekly emails can check progress at any time without bothering the PM.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every Friday the tool runs automatically, generating drafts for every client without the PM doing any setup. New client projects are added to the system and immediately start generating weekly reports. The client engagement tracking data — which clients open every report vs. which stop reading — becomes an early-warning system for churn that the agency didn't have before.
**Biggest risk:** Task title translation quality — if the GPT summary of "implement OAuth flow" comes out as generic or inaccurate, the PM has to rewrite it and the tool creates work rather than saving it. Mitigation: the configurable title-mapping table (no LLM) is the default for V1 — the PM sets up translations once and reuses them. The LLM option is a settings toggle for agencies that want more natural summaries and are willing to review the output.
**Growth path:** Side project → Linear integration, weekly task pull, report template, and PM review queue live, first 5-8 agency clients through Agency Hackers community. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-18 agencies, add Jira and Asana integrations, add client-facing project dashboard. Replaces-full-time-salary → 35+ agencies, add Slack delivery option (status update posted to a client's shared Slack channel instead of email), expand to marketing agencies tracking campaign deliverable status.

### 4. Testimonial and Case Study Collection for B2B Service Companies
**Problem:** B2B agencies, consultants, fractional executives, and service firms know that a well-written case study closes enterprise deals that no ad campaign can close. But 90% of them have fewer than 3 published case studies, because collecting them requires asking a client for a favor right when the project ends and everyone has moved on. The agency owner means to follow up. They don't. Six months later, the client has half-forgotten the specific outcomes and the enthusiasm has faded. Meanwhile, the agency is losing deals to competitors with better social proof. An agency with 50 completed client engagements and 2 published case studies has left 48 potential sales tools on the table.
**What to build:** A testimonial and case study collection tool — the agency owner enters completed client engagements with the project end date, service type, and client contact. At a configurable delay after project completion (default: 14 days), the tool sends the client a short satisfaction survey via email: overall rating (1-5), one quantitative outcome question ("What's the most significant business result you saw from this engagement?"), and one open-ended question ("How would you describe working with us to a peer?"). A 4 or 5 star response triggers a second email: a structured case study interview questionnaire (6 questions: what was the situation before, what did we do, what were the results, would you recommend us, may we use your responses publicly). The client's answers auto-populate a case study draft template that the agency edits and publishes with one click. A 1-3 star response goes to the owner as a private feedback alert. A testimonial library: every approved quote, stored and tagged by industry, service type, and outcome, searchable when the agency needs to pull a relevant testimonial for a proposal. A collection rate dashboard: how many engagements triggered a request, how many responded, how many became publishable case studies.
**Skill fit:** Email delivery via SendGrid. The survey is a simple multi-question form with a conditional routing layer (high score → case study flow, low score → private alert). The case study draft template is a structured text document with client answer variables inserted. The testimonial library is a tagged search index over stored quote records. No complex backend — clean form collection with a structured output.
**MVP scope:** 1-2 weekends — engagement entry, 14-day delayed satisfaction survey, conditional routing, case study questionnaire, and draft template generation. An agency that sends case study requests to 20 past clients in month 1 and gets 6 publishable case studies — which they didn't have before — has a sales asset library that will close deals for years.
**Time to first $:** 14-30 days — B2B agency owners are one of the most vocal buyer segments online. Agency Hackers, UGURUS, Lenny's Newsletter readers, and consultant communities on LinkedIn are all reachable with a single post: "built a tool that automatically collects case studies from clients while the engagement is still fresh." The tool is highly shareable because every agency owner knows the problem.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $79-199/month per agency. Average agency at $129/month. 80 agencies × $129/month = **$10,320/month**. Upsell: a proposal social proof integration ($49/month add-on) — a searchable library of testimonial snippets tagged by industry and outcome type, with a one-click "copy for proposal" button that inserts the most relevant testimonial for the specific prospect the agency is pitching.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new completed engagement creates a new case study opportunity. The testimonial library grows continuously, making it more valuable as a sales asset over time. An agency with 3 years of case study collection history in the tool — 40+ publishable case studies, organized by industry and outcome — has a competitive moat in their market that they built systematically rather than accidentally.
**Biggest risk:** Client non-response — busy clients ignore survey emails even from vendors they loved. Mitigation: the 14-day timing is specifically chosen to catch clients while the project is fresh; the survey is 2 questions (not a 20-minute form); and the case study questionnaire is framed as an opportunity for the client to showcase their own results, which is a genuine benefit to them.
**Growth path:** Side project → engagement entry, delayed survey, conditional routing, case study questionnaire, and draft template live, first 10-20 agency clients through Agency Hackers community. Replaces-part-time-income → 30-50 agencies, add the proposal social proof integration, add a public case study page generator (the tool hosts a clean case study page at the agency's subdomain). Replaces-full-time-salary → 80+ agencies, add video testimonial collection (a Loom-style self-recorded video request sent alongside the survey), expand to SaaS companies collecting customer success stories for product-led growth content.

### 5. Rent Delinquency Prediction for Property Managers
**Problem:** A property manager overseeing 50-250 units sees rent delinquency as a recurring crisis that starts the same way every month: it's the 6th, three tenants haven't paid, and nobody knows whether it's a bank delay, a money problem, or the beginning of a slow-motion eviction. By the time a tenant is 30 days late, the eviction process has already cost the property $1,500-2,500 in legal fees and lost rent — and the property manager had no warning it was coming. The signals that predict delinquency are in the payment history: a tenant who has paid on the 4th for 18 months and suddenly pays on the 9th in month 19 is showing a pattern shift. A tenant who paid partial rent twice in the last 3 months is a higher delinquency risk than one who's never been late. Property managers track this in AppFolio or Buildium but have no tool that surfaces the risk signal proactively before it becomes a 30-day delinquency.
**What to build:** A rent delinquency prediction and early intervention tool that integrates with AppFolio and Buildium (both have APIs) — pulling payment history per tenant and computing a delinquency risk score based on: days-to-pay trend (is the tenant paying later each month?), partial payment history, NSF/bounced payment events, and balance carried forward. A weekly risk dashboard: tenants ranked by delinquency risk score, with the specific signals driving their score highlighted (e.g., "paid 7 days later than average the last 2 months, 1 NSF in 90 days"). An early intervention flow: tenants who cross a configurable risk threshold get a friendly automated SMS from the property manager ("Hi [name], just a heads up that rent is due in 5 days — let us know if you have any questions"). This is not a collections tool — it's a proactive communication that prevents the delinquency from happening by making the property manager visible before the due date. A monthly portfolio report: total units, average days to pay, delinquency rate vs. prior month, and a list of tenants who crossed the risk threshold this month.
**Skill fit:** AppFolio has a REST API for lease and payment data. Buildium has a similar API surface. The risk score is a weighted combination of payment timing metrics — days-to-pay trend, partial payment frequency, NSF count — computed per tenant from the trailing 6 months of payment history. SMS via Twilio. The risk dashboard is a sorted list with expandable per-tenant payment history. This is applied data analytics on a structured financial time series — a natural fit for the builder's background.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — AppFolio API integration, trailing 6-month payment history pull, risk score calculation, weekly risk dashboard, and the automated pre-due-date SMS for high-risk tenants. A property manager who prevents 2 evictions in the first 3 months — saving $3,000-5,000 in legal and lost rent per avoided eviction — has covered the annual subscription cost many times over.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — property managers are reachable through the National Apartment Association (NAA) member community, the National Association of Residential Property Managers (NARPM), AppFolio and Buildium user communities, and property management Facebook groups (Property Management Brainstorm — 25,000+ members). A property manager who publicly attributes an avoided eviction to an early risk alert becomes a powerful case study in those communities.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-499/month per property manager depending on portfolio size. Average PM at $299/month. 35 property managers × $299/month = **$10,465/month**. Upsell: a lease renewal risk module ($79/month add-on) — identifying tenants whose lease is expiring in 90 days and who have low engagement signals (maintenance requests declining, late payment trend) as turnover risks, so the property manager can proactively offer a renewal incentive before the tenant starts searching for alternatives.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Payment history accumulates continuously, making the risk model more accurate for each individual tenant over time. A property manager whose portfolio's average days-to-pay and delinquency rate trends are tracked month-over-month has a performance metric they can show property owners in monthly reports — turning the tool's data into a client retention asset.
**Biggest risk:** AppFolio API access — AppFolio gates API access and requires a partnership application that takes time. Mitigation: Buildium has a more accessible API as the V1 integration target while the AppFolio partnership is in progress; Rent Manager is a strong third alternative with an open API. A CSV export path from AppFolio's reporting module covers the MVP period before the API partnership is approved.
**Growth path:** Side project → Buildium integration, risk score calculation, weekly dashboard, and pre-due-date SMS live, first 5-8 PM clients through NARPM outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-18 PMs, add AppFolio integration, add lease renewal risk module. Replaces-full-time-salary → 35+ PMs, add a portfolio benchmarking report (your delinquency rate vs. anonymized peer PMs in the same market), expand to HOA management companies with recurring dues collection and the same delinquency prediction dynamics.
