### 1. Freight Broker Carrier Compliance Monitoring & Onboarding Automation
**Problem:** Licensed freight brokers (FMCSA Broker Authority holders) are legally liable for cargo claims and third-party accidents when they dispatch loads to carriers whose operating authority has been revoked, whose insurance has lapsed, or whose safety fitness rating has dropped to "Unsatisfactory" — and this risk doesn't exist only at onboarding but continuously throughout the broker-carrier relationship. FMCSA safety ratings change (a carrier rated satisfactory can drop to conditional after a compliance review), insurance certificates expire without replacement, and MC authority gets revoked — none of which triggers automatic notification to every broker using that carrier. Most small freight brokers handle this with a one-time FMCSA Safer Web lookup at carrier signup, then rely on the carrier to self-report changes, which doesn't happen. A monitoring service continuously checks each broker's carrier base against live FMCSA data and alerts before the broker dispatches a load to a non-compliant carrier.
**What to build:** A carrier compliance monitoring platform — a carrier onboarding portal (collecting USDOT number, MC number, and carrier contact for each new carrier; automating the FMCSA SAFER lookup for operating authority, safety fitness rating, and insurance on file at onboarding), a continuous compliance monitoring engine (polling FMCSA's public SAFER API for each carrier in the broker's active base daily, flagging any change in authority status, safety rating, or insurance certificate expiration), a carrier status alert system (sending an immediate alert when a carrier's status changes — authority revoked, insurance lapsed, safety rating downgraded — with the carrier identity and load implications), a carrier compliance dashboard (showing the full carrier base ranked by compliance risk: carriers with expiring insurance, conditional safety ratings, and authority anomalies), and an optional load dispatch block (configurable to prevent assigning a new load to a flagged carrier until the compliance issue is resolved) — charged as a monthly SaaS subscription tiered by carrier base size.
**Skill fit:** FMCSA's SAFER API is a publicly available REST API returning operating authority, safety fitness rating, and insurance data by USDOT number — building a daily polling job that tracks changes for a broker's carrier roster and triggers alerts is a data pipeline and monitoring problem squarely within the builder's engineering skills. The risk-ranked carrier dashboard (days-until-insurance-expiration, safety rating history, authority status timeline) is a data visualization product built on top of a straightforward API integration. No freight industry licensing is required to operate a compliance monitoring SaaS platform.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build FMCSA SAFER API integration for carrier authority/insurance/safety rating lookup, a carrier database with USDOT/MC tracking, a daily polling job with change detection, and an email alert system for compliance changes; first 5-10 broker accounts through freight broker Facebook groups and transportation association communities while validating the alert logic against real carrier status change events.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through freight broker Facebook groups (the primary community for independent freight brokers), TIA (Transportation Intermediaries Association) member communications, Freight 360 and DAT broker training program alumni communities, and directly through the public FMCSA broker authority registry (which lists every active broker authority holder by company name and state — a built-in prospecting list).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly SaaS subscription: $150-400/mo per broker (tier by carrier base size — up to 50 carriers: $150/mo; 51-200 carriers: $250/mo; 200+ carriers: $400/mo). Reaching 35-40 active broker accounts at $275/mo average = **$9,625-11,000/mo**. FMCSA lists approximately 25,000 active broker authority holders; reaching 40 accounts is less than 0.2% penetration. Carrier base growth among active brokers drives natural upsells to higher tiers as brokers scale their networks.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Freight brokers cannot turn off carrier compliance monitoring once they rely on it — a single missed carrier authority revocation resulting in a cargo claim or accident creates direct broker liability under FMCSA regulations, making the service a permanent operating cost, not a discretionary tool. Every load dispatched to a non-compliant carrier is a live liability event; the service's value is concrete legal protection brokers can quantify against their cargo insurance premiums and legal exposure. Broker authority holders who scale their carrier base drive revenue growth automatically without any upsell conversation.
**Biggest risk:** FMCSA SAFER API data has known latency — insurance certificate updates and safety rating changes sometimes take 24-48 hours to propagate, meaning the monitoring may not catch a change before the broker dispatches a load. Build an explicit service-level disclosure acknowledging the monitoring is based on FMCSA's public data and is not a substitute for manual verification before dispatch of high-value freight, positioning the service as systematic risk reduction rather than a guarantee of real-time status.
**Growth path:** Side project → FMCSA SAFER API integration validated, carrier monitoring for 5-10 broker accounts, alert logic tested against real carrier status change events. Replaces-part-time-income → 15-20 active broker accounts, TIA and training community referrals generating consistent signups, first tier upgrades as broker carrier bases grow (~$3-6K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 35-45 broker accounts, dispatch-block integration with popular TMS platforms under development, pushing past $9-12K/mo.

### 2. FDA Food Facility Registration & FSMA Preventive Controls Compliance Management
**Problem:** Any facility that manufactures, processes, packs, or holds food for US consumption must be registered with FDA under 21 CFR Part 1 — and that registration must be renewed every two years during the October 1–December 31 renewal window. A lapsed registration renders the facility legally unable to introduce food into US commerce until reinstated. Beyond registration, FSMA's Preventive Controls for Human Food rule (21 CFR Part 117) requires registered facilities to maintain a written Food Safety Plan: hazard analysis, preventive controls (process, allergen, sanitation, supply chain), monitoring procedures, corrective action records, and verification activities. Artisan food manufacturers, contract co-packers, and specialty food importers typically get their initial plan written by a PCQI consultant at significant expense, then let it go stale — missing the biennial registration renewal, skipping required records reviews, or failing FDA inspections because the written plan no longer matches their actual process after equipment or ingredient changes.
**What to build:** An FDA food facility compliance management service — a facility registration tracking system (monitoring each client's FDA registration status, filing the biennial renewal through FDA's FURLS portal, and confirming reinstatement), a Food Safety Plan maintenance workflow (maintaining the client's FSMA Preventive Controls plan as a living document: tracking process changes that trigger plan revision requirements, managing the annual reanalysis required by 21 CFR 117.170, and updating hazard analysis and control documentation when the client changes ingredients, equipment, or processes), a corrective action and records review calendar (scheduling monthly monitoring records reviews and generating corrective action documentation when a preventive control is not properly implemented), a supply chain program management tool (tracking the approved supplier list, supplier verification activities, and incoming material inspection records required for supply chain controls), and an FDA inspection readiness report (generating a pre-inspection checklist showing which records are current, which are missing, and which plan sections need updating) — charged as a monthly retainer per facility.
**Skill fit:** The FDA registration renewal calendar, FSMA plan revision tracking, records review scheduling, and supplier verification documentation are structured document management and compliance calendar problems directly within the builder's data engineering skills. The FURLS portal accepts registration submissions through a defined web interface. FDA's publicly available FSMA Food Safety Plan Builder defines every required plan element precisely, making the plan template a well-defined regulatory-to-document mapping with no ambiguity about required fields.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build an FDA registration renewal calendar and FURLS submission workflow, a Food Safety Plan template from FDA's FSMA Plan Builder structure, a records review scheduling system with email reminders, and an inspection readiness checklist generator; first 3-5 facility clients through PCQI certification program graduate communities and specialty food associations while the plan maintenance workflow is validated against real FDA inspection preparation scenarios.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through FSPCA PCQI training program graduate communities (FDA-recognized training programs train thousands of food safety managers annually; those managers become clients or referral sources), the Specialty Food Association (whose member companies are exactly the small food manufacturers who struggle with FSMA maintenance), food business incubator programs (commercial kitchen incubators whose tenants are small food manufacturers who need FSMA support but can't afford ongoing consultant fees), and food co-packer networks (co-packers face FSMA compliance for every product under contract — compliance burden scales with client count, making the service high-value).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per facility: $350-750/mo (depending on process complexity and number of product categories — a single-SKU hot sauce manufacturer is simpler than a multi-category co-packer running 50 customer products). Managing 20-25 facility retainers at $500/mo average = **$10,000-12,500/mo**. FDA biennial registration renewals (October-December, even-numbered years) generate a coordination fee spike on top of monthly retainer income for each renewal cycle. Reaching 20-25 facilities requires 4-6 PCQI consultant relationships and 2-3 specialty food association marketing channels.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** FSMA compliance is a permanent regulatory requirement — registered facilities must maintain their Food Safety Plans indefinitely, conduct annual reanalysis, and renew their FDA registration every two years, making each facility a multi-year retainer commitment with no natural end date. FDA inspections (Risk-Based Preventive Controls inspections triggered by FDA's facility risk ranking) can occur at any time and require the Food Safety Plan to be current and records complete — the service's value is highest exactly when it's most needed, making clients highly retention-resistant. The biennial registration window creates a predictable annual revenue spike.
**Biggest risk:** A facility that fails an FDA inspection or receives a Warning Letter while under the service's management creates reputational risk and potential client claims about incomplete plan maintenance. Build a clear service agreement delineating the service's scope (plan maintenance and records calendar) from the facility's obligation (actually implementing controls and generating records), and include a quarterly records audit requiring the client to confirm their actual process matches the documented plan — catching plan-to-process gaps before FDA does.
**Growth path:** Side project → FDA registration renewal workflow built and FURLS submission process documented, FSMA plan template validated against FDA inspection criteria, first 3-5 facility retainers through PCQI training community referrals. Replaces-part-time-income → 10-12 facility retainers, PCQI consultant referral relationships established, first biennial registration renewal cycle managed (~$4-7K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 20-25 facility retainers, specialty food association marketing channel producing consistent inbound, inspection readiness add-on consulting at premium hourly rate for clients with imminent FDA inspections, pushing past $10-13K/mo.

### 3. Community Solar Subscriber Operations & Bill Credit Reconciliation Service
**Problem:** Community solar projects — ground-mount or rooftop solar arrays sized 1-5 MW that sell subscriptions to local utility customers who receive proportional bill credits — require ongoing subscriber operations that most developers are poorly equipped to manage: subscriber enrollment, utility account verification, production-to-credit allocation calculations, monthly bill credit reconciliation against utility interconnection reports, churn management when a subscriber moves or cancels, and replacement subscriber enrollment to maintain full allocation. A project with 300-500 residential subscribers generates hundreds of utility account changes, credit discrepancies, and lifecycle events per year. Community solar developers (typically focused on project finance and grid interconnection, not subscriber operations) either manage this with spreadsheets and a part-time hire, pay expensive billing platforms designed for utilities rather than small project operators, or outsource to national subscriber management companies that charge minimums eroding returns on smaller projects.
**What to build:** A community solar subscriber operations platform — a subscriber enrollment and onboarding workflow (collecting utility account information, validating eligibility for the relevant utility territory's community solar program, and communicating enrollment timelines to subscribers), a production data integration layer (connecting to the project's inverter monitoring system — Enphase, SolarEdge, or DAS platforms — to pull monthly production and calculate each subscriber's proportional bill credit based on their subscription percentage), a bill credit reconciliation tool (comparing calculated credit amounts against the utility's monthly production credit report, flagging discrepancies, and generating subscriber-level credit summaries), a churn management workflow (tracking cancellation requests, processing within the utility's required notice window, and flagging vacated subscription allocation for replacement enrollment), and a subscriber communication system (generating monthly production statements and handling billing inquiries about utility bill credits) — charged as a monthly operations fee per project scaled by subscriber count.
**Skill fit:** The production data API integration (inverter monitoring systems expose REST or SFTP data feeds), the credit allocation calculation model (subscriber credit = their subscription percentage × monthly production kWh × applicable utility credit rate), the reconciliation comparison against utility reports, and the subscriber lifecycle workflow are data engineering and operations workflow problems the builder constructs precisely. Community solar billing rules vary by utility interconnection agreement but follow a predictable structure — expertise in 2-3 utility territories' credit programs makes the builder the go-to operator for developers active in those territories.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a subscriber database with utility account tracking, production data import from an Enphase or SolarEdge API, a credit allocation calculation model, and a subscriber enrollment and churn workflow; first project management contract through direct outreach to community solar developers in states with active programs (Illinois, Minnesota, New York, Massachusetts, Colorado) while the production reconciliation logic is validated against real utility monthly reports.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through SEIA's active community solar working group (project developers are concentrated in 6-8 states with active community solar legislation), utility interconnection consultants (who manage grid interconnection for projects and refer developers to operations services), and renewable energy project finance attorneys (who structure community solar operating agreements and identify developers needing subscriber management).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly operations fee: $800-2,000/mo per project (scaled by subscriber count — 100 subscribers: $800/mo; 300 subscribers: $1,400/mo; 500 subscribers: $2,000/mo). Managing 8-10 active projects at $1,400/mo average = **$11,200-14,000/mo**. Each project generates recurring monthly operations income for the 20-25 year life of the solar asset. Reaching 8-10 projects requires relationships with 5-7 active community solar developers. No licensing is required to operate a subscriber management service.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Community solar projects operate for 20-25 years with subscriber operations required every month of that lifespan — each project managed is a 20-year recurring revenue relationship. Developers are structurally motivated to keep subscriptions fully allocated because unsubscribed capacity generates no revenue, creating strong alignment between the developer's financial interest and the service's subscriber retention function. The community solar market is in early-stage rollout in most states — the window to establish territory-specific expertise before large national operators dominate subscriber management in each utility territory is open now.
**Biggest risk:** Utility interconnection agreements and bill credit programs vary significantly by territory — credit calculation methodology that works for one utility's community solar tariff may not match another utility's program rules, creating reconciliation errors that generate subscriber complaints and developer liability. Build territory-specific credit calculation logic for each utility before onboarding projects there, and maintain a changelog of tariff changes that affect calculations (utility community solar tariffs are amended periodically as program rules evolve).
**Growth path:** Side project → production data API integration validated against one inverter platform in one utility territory, first community solar project contract through developer direct outreach, subscriber database and credit reconciliation operating for 100-200 subscribers. Replaces-part-time-income → 3-4 active projects across 2-3 utility territories, churn management workflow refined against real subscriber lifecycle events (~$4-7K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 8-10 active projects, 4-6 developer client relationships, territory expertise established in 4-5 active community solar states, pushing past $11-14K/mo.

### 4. Independent Insurance Claims Adjuster Multi-State License & CE Compliance Management
**Problem:** Independent insurance claims adjusters working catastrophe response, daily claims, or specialty lines must hold licenses in every state where they adjust. Multi-state adjusters commonly hold 20-40 state licenses simultaneously, each with different CE requirements (12-24 hours per 2-year cycle), different approved course provider lists, different renewal deadlines, and complex reciprocity rules. Missing a renewal deadline in a high-demand state — Florida after a hurricane, California after a wildfire — means the adjuster cannot work the deployment at exactly the moment they need to most. Most adjusters manage this with a personal spreadsheet that misses state-specific CE topic mandates (some states require flood CE hours, ethics hours, or surplus lines CE for renewal), causing license lapse when the CE completed doesn't satisfy the state's specific requirements even though the required number of hours was met.
**What to build:** A multi-state adjuster license compliance management service — a license portfolio registry (cataloging every state license: license number, type, issue date, expiration date, CE hours required, CE hours completed, approved provider requirement, and state-specific topic mandates), a CE requirement tracking engine (mapping each state's specific CE requirements against the adjuster's CE completion record and flagging gaps at 6 months, 3 months, and 30 days before renewal), a renewal calendar management system (generating the full multi-state renewal calendar with deadlines, CE completion windows, and application submission timing), a reciprocity monitoring tool (tracking which states offer non-resident license reciprocity with the adjuster's resident state, alerting when reciprocity rules change and affect non-resident licenses), and a license renewal coordination service (managing renewal application submission for each state, collecting CE certificates, and confirming receipt from each state DOI) — charged as a monthly SaaS subscription per adjuster.
**Skill fit:** The license registry database, CE requirement mapping engine (each state DOI publishes CE requirements publicly; mapped into structured data by state, license type, and renewal period), renewal deadline calendar, and NIPR-integrated license status monitoring (many states expose adjuster license status through the National Insurance Producer Registry) are data modeling problems applied to a regulatory compliance domain with fixed, well-documented rules. No insurance license is required to manage license compliance data for insurance adjusters.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a license portfolio database covering 30-40 active adjuster licensing states, a CE requirement data model from each state DOI's published requirements, a renewal calendar generator, and an email alert system; first 10-15 adjuster clients through NACA communities and catastrophe adjuster Facebook groups while the CE requirement database is validated against real adjuster license renewal filings.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through NACA (National Association of Catastrophe Adjusters) member communications (primary professional association for independent cat adjusters), catastrophe adjuster Facebook groups (very active communities where license management problems surface constantly — missed CE deadlines, reciprocity changes, and state DOI requirements are discussed regularly), cat management company vendor networks (who maintain adjuster pools and can refer license management services to their adjusters), and CE course provider partner programs (course providers selling to adjusters have direct access to the exact population with the problem).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly SaaS subscription: $40-80/mo per adjuster ($40/mo for under 20 licenses; $80/mo for 30+ licenses in complex states). Volume model: 200 active adjuster clients at $60/mo average = **$12,000/mo**. Reaching 200 clients is a small fraction of the estimated 50,000+ independent adjusters in the US. The low per-seat price creates self-service acquisition with minimal sales friction — adjusters who have experienced a license lapse convert quickly. Catastrophe season (June-November) creates natural urgency cycles driving signup spikes.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Multi-state license portfolios don't shrink — adjusters who add states add complexity and drive upsells to higher tiers. License lapse events (a missed renewal that bars the adjuster from a paid deployment) are costly and traumatic enough that any adjuster who has experienced one becomes a permanent customer. Reciprocity rules change when state DOIs revise their non-resident programs — ongoing monitoring value compounds as portfolios grow. Catastrophe adjuster deployments (the highest-income opportunities for independent adjusters) create urgency around maintaining licenses in deployment target states like Florida, Texas, and Louisiana.
**Biggest risk:** State DOI CE requirements and renewal deadlines change — a CE requirement database accurate today may be wrong in 6 months when a state revises its schedule or topic mandates. Build a semi-annual state-by-state CE requirement audit into the product roadmap, subscribe to each state DOI's regulatory bulletin, and include an explicit service-level disclosure that the client is responsible for verifying CE completion with each state DOI at renewal time — the service tracks and reminds, the adjuster owns the final verification.
**Growth path:** Side project → license portfolio database built for 30-40 active licensing states, CE requirement data model validated against 3-5 active adjuster client portfolios, renewal calendar and alerts delivering timely notifications. Replaces-part-time-income → 80-100 active adjuster clients, NACA member marketing established, cat adjuster community referrals compounding (~$4-7K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 180-220 active adjuster clients, catastrophe season marketing campaigns driving annual signup spikes, pushing past $10-14K/mo.

### 5. DOT Hazardous Materials Shipper Registration & Employee Training Compliance (49 CFR)
**Problem:** Any company that ships hazardous materials by ground — including common business shipments of lithium batteries, cleaning chemicals, paint and coatings, compressed gases, and laboratory reagents — must comply with PHMSA's hazardous materials regulations under 49 CFR Parts 171-180. Companies shipping specified quantities of Tier 1 or Tier 2 hazmat must register annually with PHMSA (fees: $250-3,000/yr depending on size) and maintain a Hazmat Safety Plan; all employees who prepare hazmat for transportation, handle hazmat shipments, or operate vehicles transporting hazmat must receive DOT hazmat training under 49 CFR 172.700 (general awareness, function-specific, safety, and security training) and be retrained at least every 3 years; training records must be maintained per employee for 3 years after employment ends. PHMSA inspectors conduct roadside and facility inspections; civil penalties reach $87,421 per violation per day for wilful violations. Most small manufacturers, chemical distributors, and e-commerce companies shipping regulated products manage this informally — periodic PHMSA registration that's sometimes renewed and sometimes forgotten, hazmat training that happened once years ago and was never refreshed, and training records in a paper file that gets lost with turnover.
**What to build:** A hazmat shipper compliance management service — a PHMSA registration tracking system (identifying which clients require registration based on hazmat shipping classification and tonnage, managing annual PHMSA registration renewal through the PHMSA Portal, and filing the Hazmat Safety Plan documentation), an employee training compliance tracker (maintaining a training record for each employee who handles hazmat: initial training date, functions trained, and 3-year retraining deadline — with reminders at 12 and 6 months before retraining is due), a hazmat classification review service (reviewing the client's shipped commodities against the 49 CFR Hazardous Materials Table to confirm correct classification, packing group, and shipping description — the most common source of PHMSA violations), a shipping paper compliance audit (reviewing the client's hazmat shipping paper format against 49 CFR 172.200 requirements), and a new employee training coordination workflow (triggering a training requirement record for each new hire in a hazmat-handling role and tracking completion before the employee works with hazmat unsupervised) — charged as a monthly retainer per facility.
**Skill fit:** The PHMSA registration renewal calendar, employee training record database (name, training date, functions trained, retraining due date), hazmat classification lookup against the 49 CFR Hazardous Materials Table (a structured data table available in the Electronic Code of Federal Regulations), and shipping paper compliance checklist are data management and regulatory rule application problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. PHMSA's registration portal and 49 CFR training regulations are fully public, and the Hazardous Materials Table is machine-parseable — building a commodity classification lookup is a data engineering problem with well-defined inputs and outputs.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a PHMSA registration renewal tracking and submission workflow, an employee training record database with 3-year retraining deadline alerts, a hazmat commodity classification lookup against the 49 CFR Hazardous Materials Table, and a shipping paper compliance checklist; first 3-5 client facilities through EHS consultant referrals and small manufacturer communities while the registration and training tracking workflows are validated against real client PHMSA compliance records.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through EHS consultant networks (EHS consultants work with manufacturers and distributors on OSHA, EPA, and DOT compliance and regularly refer out DOT-specific hazmat work they don't specialize in), NACD (National Association of Chemical Distributors) member communications (whose members are exactly the shippers with active hazmat programs), e-commerce fulfillment and 3PL networks (3PLs shipping consumer electronics with lithium batteries or flammable products have direct hazmat compliance obligations and face PHMSA inspection risk on their facility operations), and directly through PHMSA's public registration database (which lists every registered hazmat shipper by company and state — a built-in prospecting list for the service).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per facility: $300-600/mo (depending on employee count and commodity complexity — a small chemical distributor with 10 hazmat employees is simpler than a 3PL with 50 employees across multiple hazmat classifications). Managing 25-30 facility retainers at $425/mo average = **$10,625-12,750/mo**. PHMSA annual registration renewal generates a coordination fee on top of monthly retainers. Reaching 25-30 facilities requires 4-6 active EHS consultant referral relationships. The 3-year employee retraining cycle generates a recurring add-on revenue event for every client facility every 3 years.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** PHMSA hazmat compliance requirements don't end — companies shipping regulated products face permanent annual registration and ongoing employee training obligations. The 3-year retraining cycle creates a predictable recurring engagement: every 3 years, the entire hazmat-handling employee base must be retrained, generating either a coordination fee or an upsell to in-person training facilitation. PHMSA enforcement has increased as e-commerce growth has expanded the number of hazmat shippers (lithium battery regulations cover nearly every consumer electronics shipper), growing the addressable market without requiring the service to manufacture demand.
**Biggest risk:** Incorrect hazmat classification guidance — if the service incorrectly classifies a commodity as not requiring registration or not triggering training requirements, and PHMSA subsequently cites the client, the service faces liability claims. Build the hazmat classification tool as a reference lookup that surfaces the specific 49 CFR Hazardous Materials Table entry with its classification, packing group, and applicable special provisions, and include a disclaimer that final classification decisions are the shipper's responsibility and should be confirmed with a DOT-certified hazmat trainer or attorney for novel commodities. Carry E&O insurance from the first client engagement.
**Growth path:** Side project → PHMSA registration renewal workflow validated, employee training record database tracking 3-year cycles for first 3-5 client facilities, hazmat classification lookup built and tested against the 49 CFR Hazardous Materials Table. Replaces-part-time-income → 10-12 facility retainers, EHS consultant referral relationships established, first 3-year retraining cycle coordination for initial client employees (~$3-6K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 25-30 facility retainers, NACD member marketing channel established, 3PL and e-commerce fulfillment client tier generating higher-employee-count facilities at premium rates, pushing past $10-13K/mo.
