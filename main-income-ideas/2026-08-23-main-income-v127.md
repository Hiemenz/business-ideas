The regulator (or accrediting body) creates the deadline; you sell the peace of mind of never missing it.

### 1. Land Mobile Radio (Two-Way Radio) Dealer & Installer FCC Part 90 License Renewal & Frequency-Coordination Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Companies that sell, install, and program commercial two-way radios for businesses, municipalities, and industrial clients operate at the center of a licensing structure their customers rarely understand and almost never track themselves: every radio system operating on a licensed frequency must hold its own FCC Part 90 license issued through the Universal Licensing System (ULS), renewed on a 10-year term, and before a dealer can even file a new license application, the requested frequency assignment must clear review by an FCC-certified frequency coordinator such as the Enterprise Wireless Alliance (EWA) — a coordination step that can take weeks and must be re-run whenever a client adds a new site, changes power output, or modifies their coverage area. A dealer managing license renewals and frequency coordination as a value-added service for dozens or hundreds of client radio systems — a school district's bus fleet, a golf course's grounds crew, a warehouse operator's forklift dispatch channel — tracks each client's ULS registration number, license expiration date (a full decade out, easy to lose track of amid day-to-day sales and installs), and the specific frequency/coordinator record tied to every site, usually on a spreadsheet of client call signs with no systematic renewal alert. A client radio system caught transmitting on an expired license, or a new site added without re-running frequency coordination, is exactly the finding an FCC field inspection or an interference complaint investigation is built to catch, and can result in the license being cancelled outright — silencing the client's entire radio fleet.

**What to build:** A per-client ULS license renewal tracker computing each system's 10-year expiration from its grant date, with tiered alerts starting a year out; a per-site frequency-coordination record log tied to each license, flagging any site added or modified without a corresponding coordinator record on file; and a dealer-facing bulk-renewal dashboard letting the dealer manage and bill the renewal-filing service across its entire client base from one view.

**Skill fit:** FCC's ULS database is a public, structured, queryable federal dataset a developer can pull directly to validate license status and expiration dates, and the per-client renewal-countdown-plus-coordination-record-check is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a licensing structure this repo has never addressed.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the ULS renewal tracker with automated status lookups, the per-site frequency-coordination record log, and the bulk dealer dashboard. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent two-way radio dealers.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Enterprise Wireless Alliance (EWA) runs an active member directory of independent land mobile radio dealers and installers, reaching exactly the small dealers currently tracking client license renewals on a decade-long clock with no systematic alerting.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $5/mo per client license tracked. Average client (a dealer managing 45 client licenses) = $199 + $225 = $424/mo. 25-33 dealers × $424/mo avg = $10,600-13,992/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every client license renews on its own 10-year clock spread unevenly across the dealer's book, every new site or coverage change reopens a fresh frequency-coordination event, and the dealer's client base only grows as it wins new installation contracts — a continuous, license-portfolio-driven compliance stream that scales directly with how many client systems the dealer manages.

**Biggest risk:** A dealer with a small, stable client base whose licenses were all issued around the same time has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need for a few years. Mitigate by qualifying leads for dealers managing a large, staggered client license portfolio built up over many years of sales, where renewal dates are already scattered unpredictably across the calendar.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 dealers ($6.4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 25 dealers ($10.6K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 33+ dealers plus a client-facing renewal-status portal dealers can offer their largest municipal and industrial accounts as a premium add-on.

### 2. Small-Batch Firearms & Ammunition Manufacturer ATF Type 06/07 Marking Record & TTB Firearms Excise Tax (FAET) Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Small manufacturers producing firearms or ammunition under an ATF Type 07 (firearms manufacturer) or Type 06 (ammunition manufacturer) license carry an entirely different compliance load than a retail dealer's Form 4473 paperwork: every firearm produced must be marked with a serial number and manufacturer identification per 27 CFR 478.92 before it can leave the shop, and the manufacturer must maintain production and disposition records under 27 CFR 478.123 tracking every unit from raw receiver or component through finished, marked product — records an ATF compliance inspection reviews unit-by-unit, with an unmarked or improperly recorded firearm treated as a fact pattern serious enough to jeopardize the manufacturing license itself. Layered on top of ATF's marking and recordkeeping rules, every firearms and ammunition manufacturer must separately register with the Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau (TTB) and file the Firearms and Ammunition Excise Tax (FAET) return under 26 U.S.C. § 4181 — an 11% (firearms and ammunition) or 10% (pistols and revolvers) federal excise tax on units sold, filed quarterly on TTB Form 5300.26, with a small manufacturer eligible for less-frequent filing only after meeting a trailing tax-liability threshold the manufacturer must track itself. A small manufacturer running a handful of product lines through a growing production run tracks per-unit marking and disposition records for ATF and a running excise-tax liability calculation for TTB with no dedicated compliance function, usually reconciling both from the same shop-floor production log by hand — a mismatch between units marked and units reported for excise tax is exactly the cross-reference an ATF or TTB audit is built to catch.

**What to build:** A per-unit production and disposition tracker recording the serial number, marking date, and disposition (sold, transferred, or in inventory) for every firearm or ammunition lot produced, structured to satisfy 27 CFR 478.123's recordkeeping requirements; a running FAET liability calculator that totals taxable sales by quarter and flags the exact Form 5300.26 filing deadline and the manufacturer's current filing-frequency eligibility; and a reconciliation view cross-checking units marked against units reported for excise tax to catch any gap before an auditor does.

**Skill fit:** ATF's Type 06/07 marking and recordkeeping requirements and TTB's FAET filing structure are both public, well-documented federal regulatory text with concrete numeric thresholds and deadlines, and the per-unit production-to-disposition reconciliation against a running tax-liability calculation is the same deterministic ledger-matching pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a manufacturing license class this repo has never addressed.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the per-unit production/disposition tracker, the FAET liability calculator with the Form 5300.26 deadline tracker, and the marking-to-tax reconciliation view. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small firearms and ammunition manufacturers.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the National Shooting Sports Foundation (NSSF), the firearms and ammunition industry's trade association, runs an active member directory reaching exactly the small manufacturers currently reconciling ATF marking records against TTB excise-tax filings by hand.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249/mo base + $10/mo per product line (SKU) tracked. Average client (8 product lines) = $249 + $80 = $329/mo. 30-40 manufacturers × $329/mo avg = $9,870-13,160/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every unit produced opens its own marking-and-disposition record, every quarter reopens the FAET filing obligation regardless of production volume, and a growing manufacturer's filing frequency itself changes as trailing tax liability crosses TTB's thresholds — a continuous, production-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to how many units the manufacturer ships.

**Biggest risk:** A very small manufacturer producing only a few hundred units a year under TTB's small-manufacturer safe harbor has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need a spreadsheet can still handle. Mitigate by qualifying leads for manufacturers running multiple product lines at a production volume approaching or crossing TTB's filing-frequency thresholds, where the quarterly reconciliation genuinely outgrows manual tracking.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 manufacturers ($4.9K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 28 manufacturers ($9.2K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 30-40 manufacturers plus a multi-state dealer-network shipping compliance add-on for manufacturers expanding direct-to-dealer distribution.

### 3. Water Park & Aquatic-Attraction Operator ASTM F2376 Slide Inspection & Lifeguard Certification Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Water parks and aquatic attractions operating water slides, flumes, wave pools, and lazy rivers fall under ASTM F2376, the standard specific to water slide system design, construction, and operation, which most states implement by requiring an annual slide/flume inspection — performed by a state inspector or a NAARSO-affiliated private inspector depending on the jurisdiction — before the attraction can reopen each season, on top of the separate pool-code water-chemistry requirements a standard swimming pool already carries. Every attraction also requires certified lifeguard staffing at a ratio the state bather-load code sets per feature — commonly through Ellis & Associates' International Lifeguard Training Program or YMCA certification — renewed on each guard's own multi-year cycle, and a park's severe-weather and lightning-closure protocol must be documented and followed for every open-water attraction, since a guard posted at an under-staffed feature or a slide reopened without its annual inspection on file is exactly the finding a state amusement-ride inspector or a post-incident liability investigation is built to catch. A seasonal water park running a dozen or more slides and features alongside a large seasonal lifeguard roster tracks each attraction's inspection currency, each guard's certification renewal, and the park's weather-closure log — usually on a facilities manager's binder rebuilt from scratch every spring reopening, with no cross-check between which attractions are cleared to open and which guards are cleared to staff them.

**What to build:** A per-attraction ASTM F2376 inspection tracker with a "cleared to open" gate blocking any slide or flume from being scheduled operational until its current-season inspection is on file; a per-lifeguard certification renewal tracker with a staffing-eligibility gate matched against each attraction's required ratio; and a severe-weather closure log timestamping every closure and reopening decision against the park's documented protocol.

**Skill fit:** ASTM F2376's inspection structure and lifeguard-certification renewal cycles are published, structured standards-body data, and the per-attraction "cleared to open" gate plus the per-guard staffing-eligibility check are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a water slide and wave-pool attraction class distinct from the pool-chemistry service, trampoline park, and go-kart track ideas already in this repo.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the attraction inspection tracker with the open-eligibility gate, the lifeguard certification tracker with the staffing gate, and the weather-closure log. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent and regional water park operators.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the World Waterpark Association (WWA) runs an active member directory and annual trade show reaching exactly the small and regional water park operators rebuilding their inspection and staffing compliance binder from scratch every season.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299/mo base + $30/mo per slide/attraction tracked. Average client (a park with 5 major attractions) = $299 + $150 = $449/mo. 25-32 parks × $449/mo avg = $11,225-14,368/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every attraction's inspection recurs on its own annual pre-season clock, every lifeguard's certification renews on an independent multi-year cycle across a large seasonal roster, and every operating day generates a fresh weather-closure documentation event — a continuous, attraction-and-staffing-driven compliance stream tied directly to the park's seasonal operating calendar.

**Biggest risk:** A small operator running only one or two water features alongside a standard pool has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads for parks with a dozen or more attractions and a large seasonal lifeguard roster, where the pre-season inspection-and-staffing scramble genuinely outgrows a facilities binder.

**Growth path:** Side project → 14 parks ($6.3K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 22 parks ($9.9K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 25-32 parks plus a pre-season reopening-audit package sold as a one-time add-on every spring.

### 4. Home Elevator, Stairlift & Platform Lift Installer ASME A18.1 Manufacturer Certification & Accessibility-Lift Licensure Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Installers of residential accessibility equipment — stairway chairlifts, vertical platform lifts, and low-speed home elevators — operate under ASME A18.1, a safety standard entirely separate from ASME A17.1's elevator and escalator rules, and can only install a given manufacturer's product — Bruno, Stannah, or Acorn among the major stairlift and platform lift makers — after completing that manufacturer's own installer-certification course, since warranty coverage and code compliance both depend on the equipment being installed exactly per the manufacturer's certified method. Many states additionally require the installer or the installing company to hold a distinct "limited-use/accessibility lift" license tier under the state's elevator-safety program — a separate classification from the full elevator-mechanic license a commercial elevator contractor holds — renewed on its own cycle and often requiring proof of current manufacturer certification as a condition of the license itself. A small installer running crews certified across two or three manufacturer product lines, working jobs in several states with different accessibility-lift licensing rules, tracks each installer's manufacturer-specific certification and renewal date, the company's own state accessibility-lift license per operating state, and per-job documentation confirming the correct certified installer and manufacturer model were used — usually on a spreadsheet with no systematic cross-check, and a lift installed by an installer whose manufacturer certification lapsed, or a job performed in a state where the company's accessibility-lift license was never obtained, is exactly the finding a state elevator-safety inspection or a warranty-claim dispute is built to catch.

**What to build:** A per-installer, per-manufacturer certification renewal tracker covering every product line the company installs, with an "cleared to install" gate; a per-state accessibility-lift license renewal tracker for every state the company operates in; and a per-job as-built log recording the certified installer of record, manufacturer, and model for every unit installed.

**Skill fit:** Manufacturer installer-certification cycles and state accessibility-lift licensing rules are structured, trackable business data with concrete renewal dates, and the per-installer "cleared to install" gate plus the per-state license tracker are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a residential accessibility-equipment trade distinct from the commercial elevator and escalator maintenance idea already in this repo.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the manufacturer-certification tracker with the install-eligibility gate, the per-state accessibility-lift license tracker, and the as-built job log. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent stairlift and home elevator installers.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the National Association of Elevator Contractors (NAEC), which also serves the accessibility-lift installer community, runs an active member directory reaching exactly the small installers juggling multiple manufacturer certifications and multi-state licensing.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $15/mo per certified installer tracked. Average client (a company with 5 certified installers) = $199 + $75 = $274/mo. 35-45 companies × $274/mo avg = $9,590-12,330/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every installer's manufacturer certification renews on its own independent cycle across every product line the company carries, every operating state's accessibility-lift license renews on its own cycle, and every new job generates a fresh as-built documentation record — a continuous, installer-and-state-license-driven compliance stream tied directly to the company's crew size and operating footprint.

**Biggest risk:** A small installer certified on a single manufacturer's product line operating in one state has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads for installers certified across multiple manufacturer product lines and operating across state lines, where the staggered certification-and-license calendar genuinely outgrows manual tracking.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 companies ($4.1K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 28 companies ($7.7K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 35-45 companies plus a multi-state licensing-expansion concierge tier for companies growing into new states.

### 5. Independent Industrial & Medical Gas Distributor DOT Cylinder Requalification & FDA Medical-Gas Drug Establishment Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent distributors who fill and sell cylinders of oxygen, nitrogen, acetylene, argon, and similar compressed gases to welding shops, manufacturers, and healthcare facilities operate under DOT 49 CFR Part 180's periodic cylinder-requalification requirements — every cylinder in the fleet must be hydrostatically retested and requalified on an interval set by cylinder type, with an overdue cylinder legally barred from being filled or shipped — layered with Compressed Gas Association (CGA) handling and visual-inspection standards a distributor's filling operation must follow. Distributors who additionally sell medical-grade oxygen, nitrous oxide, or medical air to hospitals, surgery centers, and home-care patients carry an entirely separate obligation the industrial side of the business never touches: medical gas is legally regulated as a drug under the FDCA, so the distributor itself — not the hospital's piping installer — must hold FDA drug establishment registration and National Drug Code (NDC) listing, follow current Good Manufacturing Practice tailored to medical gas production, and perform a documented positive-identification test on every cylinder before it is dispensed as a medical product. A small independent distributor running a fleet of hundreds or thousands of cylinders across industrial and medical accounts tracks each cylinder's requalification due date, the facility's FDA registration and NDC listing currency, and per-cylinder positive-identification test records for medical-gas cylinders — usually on a paper cylinder-tracking card system with no systematic fleet-wide view, and a cylinder filled and shipped past its requalification date, or a medical-oxygen cylinder dispensed without a documented identification test, is exactly the finding a DOT hazmat audit or an FDA drug-establishment inspection is built to catch.

**What to build:** A fleet-wide cylinder requalification tracker computing each cylinder's next-due hydrostatic test from its type and last-test date, with a hard block on any cylinder marked "filled" past its due date; an FDA drug establishment registration and NDC listing renewal tracker for distributors handling medical gas; and a per-cylinder positive-identification test log gating whether a medical-gas cylinder can be marked ready for dispensing.

**Skill fit:** DOT's cylinder-requalification intervals and FDA's medical-gas drug-establishment requirements are both public, structured federal regulatory data, and the fleet-wide requalification gate plus the per-cylinder identification-test gate are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a compressed-gas distribution trade distinct from both the hospital-piping installer and the LP-gas dealer ideas already in this repo.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the fleet-wide cylinder requalification tracker with the fill-eligibility gate, the FDA registration/NDC tracker, and the positive-identification test log. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent industrial and medical gas distributors.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Gases and Welding Distributors Association (GAWDA) runs an active member directory and annual conference reaching exactly the small independent distributors juggling cylinder-fleet requalification and, for those serving healthcare accounts, FDA drug-establishment obligations with no dedicated compliance staff.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per distributor depending on cylinder-fleet size and medical-gas registration status. 25-32 distributors × $429/mo avg = $10,725-13,728/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every cylinder in the fleet carries its own independent requalification clock regardless of how often it's filled, every medical-gas cylinder dispensed requires a fresh positive-identification test event, and the distributor's FDA registration and NDC listing renew on their own federal cycle — a continuous, fleet-and-account-driven compliance stream tied directly to the distributor's cylinder volume and customer mix.

**Biggest risk:** A distributor selling only industrial gas to welding shops, with no medical-gas accounts, has a comparatively lower-stakes compliance surface than one also serving healthcare facilities. Mitigate by leading with the cylinder-fleet requalification tracker as the core offer for every distributor, with the FDA registration and identification-test tracker as a differentiator for distributors serving medical accounts.

**Growth path:** Side project → 14 distributors ($6K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 22 distributors ($9.4K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 25-32 distributors plus a healthcare-account onboarding package for industrial distributors expanding into medical-gas sales.
