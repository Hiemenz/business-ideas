### 1. Nuclear Pharmacy Authorized Nuclear Pharmacist, Dose Calibrator QC & USP <825> Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Nuclear pharmacies that compound and dispense radiopharmaceuticals to hospitals and imaging centers hold their own NRC or Agreement State medical-use byproduct material license under 10 CFR Part 35, distinct from a standard state Board of Pharmacy license, and must designate one or more Authorized Nuclear Pharmacists (ANPs) — each individually authorized under 35.55 for the specific radionuclides and specific uses (diagnostic imaging doses, therapeutic doses) they're permitted to compound — meaning a pharmacist compounding a therapeutic Lu-177 dose without that specific authorization on file is exactly the finding an NRC or Agreement State inspection is built to catch, and a lapse voids every dose that pharmacist prepared during the gap. Every dose calibrator used to measure radioactivity before release must undergo daily constancy checks and periodic accuracy, linearity, and geometry checks under 10 CFR 35.60, with results logged against tolerance limits — a calibrator used past a missed constancy check invalidates every dose measured on it that day, a defect that isn't discovered until an inspector pulls the log. Because most clinically used isotopes have short half-lives (technetium-99m at roughly 6 hours, fluorine-18 at about 110 minutes), every unit dose compounded under USP <825>'s radiopharmaceutical compounding standard must carry a beyond-use time computed from radioactive decay back to a specific calibration time rather than ordinary chemical stability — a dose released without its decay-corrected activity and beyond-use time both verified is either a wasted, sub-potency dose sent to a hospital's nuclear medicine department or an over-exposure risk. Shipping containers must also meet DOT Type A packaging performance testing under 49 CFR Part 173 Subpart I, recertified on the manufacturer's testing cycle. A nuclear pharmacy running several ANPs across rotating shifts to hit early-morning hospital delivery windows manages per-pharmacist isotope authorization, dose calibrator QC schedules, and per-batch decay/beyond-use calculations on a mix of a paper QC binder and a compounding-log spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific batch's compounder, calibrator, and beyond-use time are all actually valid before it ships.

**What to build:** A per-ANP isotope-and-use authorization tracker with a "cleared to compound" gate blocking any batch from being logged against a pharmacist not currently authorized for that isotope; a dose calibrator constancy/accuracy/linearity QC scheduler flagging any instrument past its check-due date; and a per-batch decay-correction and beyond-use-time calculator tied to DOT Type A container recertification status.

**Skill fit:** 10 CFR Part 35's per-pharmacist authorization structure and USP <825>'s decay-driven beyond-use calculation are public, numerically precise regulatory and pharmacopeial data, and the "cleared to compound" gate plus the decay calculator are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a radiopharmaceutical-compounding niche this repo has never addressed and that shares no license type, regulator, or QC structure with the industrial-radiography or medical-imaging ideas already in this repo.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the ANP authorization gate, the dose calibrator QC scheduler, and the batch decay/beyond-use calculator. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent (non-hospital-owned) nuclear pharmacies.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Society of Nuclear Medicine and Molecular Imaging's (SNMMI) Pharmacy Practice section and its annual meeting reach exactly the small independent nuclear pharmacy owners currently tracking ANP authorizations and dose calibrator QC on a paper binder with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $399-799/mo per pharmacy depending on ANP count and isotope menu. 18-24 pharmacies × $549/mo avg = $9,882-13,176/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every batch compounded generates its own decay-correction-and-beyond-use event regardless of how routine the isotope, every dose calibrator recurs on its own daily/quarterly QC clock for as long as the pharmacy operates, and every new ANP hired reopens the per-isotope authorization question — a continuous, batch-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the pharmacy's delivery schedule.

**Biggest risk:** A dose released on an out-of-tolerance calibrator or from an unauthorized pharmacist is a severe radiation-safety and licensing event, not a routine paperwork gap. Mitigate by making the compounding gate a hard block rather than an advisory flag, and by qualifying leads toward pharmacies running multiple ANPs and a broad isotope menu where the daily QC-and-authorization load genuinely outgrows a paper binder.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10 pharmacies ($5.5K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 16 pharmacies ($8.8K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 18-24 pharmacies plus an NRC/Agreement-State-inspection-ready export pharmacies can hand an inspector during a routine license review.

### 2. CFTC/NFA-Registered Commodity Trading Advisor & Introducing Broker Registration, Disclosure & Net-Capital Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Small Commodity Trading Advisors (CTAs) who give trading advice on futures, options on futures, and swaps, and Introducing Brokers (IBs) who solicit and accept futures orders on behalf of clients, register not with the SEC but with the CFTC through the National Futures Association (NFA), each Associated Person passing the Series 3 exam before soliciting or advising — an AP whose Series 3 currency has lapsed, or who was never registered at all, is exactly the finding an NFA compliance audit or a client-complaint arbitration is built to catch. Every CTA must maintain and update its CFTC Rule 4.31 disclosure document — covering trading program performance, fees, and conflicts of interest — at least once every 12 months, or immediately upon any material change, and a document distributed to a new client past its update deadline is an independently citable violation regardless of how the trading program actually performed. An Introducing Broker operates under a written guarantee agreement with a sponsoring Futures Commission Merchant (FCM), and unless fully guaranteed, must meet and continuously demonstrate its own minimum net capital requirement, filing periodic financial reports with the NFA — an IB whose net capital slips below the threshold between filing periods, with nobody tracking it, is exactly the gap an NFA financial-surveillance review is built to catch, and can trigger a restriction on new business or membership suspension. Every firm must also complete NFA's annual ethics/AML training requirement under NFA Compliance Rule 2-9 for each registered AP. A small CTA/IB firm running several APs across a rotating client roster manages per-AP Series 3 currency, disclosure-document update deadlines, net-capital computation, and annual ethics-training completion on a compliance officer's spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific AP's registration and the firm's own disclosure document are both actually current before a new client is solicited.

**What to build:** A per-AP Series 3/NFA registration and annual ethics-training currency tracker with a "cleared to solicit" gate; a disclosure-document update-deadline tracker computing the 12-month clock from the last update and flagging any material change requiring an off-cycle revision; and a net-capital computation and NFA financial-report filing tracker for non-guaranteed IBs.

### 3. SEC-Registered Securities Transfer Agent Turnaround-Time, Lost-Holder-Search & Recordkeeping Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Small independent transfer agents — maintaining the official shareholder-of-record ledger and executing dividend payments, transfers, and corporate-action processing on behalf of small-cap, OTC, and privately held issuers — register with the SEC under Exchange Act §17A on Form TA-1 and operate under a set of hard numeric processing deadlines rather than a general fiduciary standard: Rule 17Ad-2 requires routine items (a straightforward transfer, a dividend disbursement) to be processed within a specific business-day turnaround window, and an agent that misses that window on even a portion of its transaction volume in a reporting period must disclose the deficiency to the SEC — a disclosure that is itself the finding an SEC examination is built to catch, since a pattern of missed turnaround windows can trigger a formal SEC enforcement inquiry. Rule 17Ad-17 separately requires the agent to run a database search for every "lost securityholder" — an address on file that mail has been returned from as undeliverable — within a defined timeframe using an approved search provider, and a lost-holder account that sits unsearched past its deadline is exactly the gap an SEC compliance sweep targets, since the rule exists specifically to prevent shareholders from silently losing contact with their own holdings. Every agent must also retain specific transaction and correspondence records for a multi-year period under Rules 17Ad-6 and 17Ad-7, and file an annual Form TA-2 report of its transfer activity. A small transfer agent servicing dozens of issuer clients and their combined shareholder-of-record base manages per-transaction turnaround-time computation, per-account lost-holder-search deadlines, and multi-year record retention on a transfer-processing system built for ledger accuracy rather than regulatory-deadline tracking, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific lost-holder search or a specific transaction's turnaround compliance is actually current before the next SEC examination.

**What to build:** A per-transaction turnaround-time tracker computing each item's processing deadline under Rule 17Ad-2 and flagging any item approaching or past its window; a per-account lost-securityholder search tracker with a due-date countdown tied to the required search-provider database; and a Form TA-2 annual filing tracker aggregating turnaround-compliance and search-completion statistics into a single examination-ready report.

**Skill fit:** Rule 17Ad-2's turnaround-time standard and Rule 17Ad-17's lost-holder-search deadline are public, numerically precise SEC regulatory data with concrete per-transaction and per-account triggers, and the deadline-tracker-plus-compliance-report pattern is the same deterministic system this series builds repeatedly, applied to a shareholder-recordkeeping niche entirely distinct from the RIA, broker-dealer, and non-depository trust company ideas already in this repo, none of which registers under §17A or tracks a lost-securityholder search obligation.

### 4. Research Biobank & Biorepository Consent-Scope, CAP Accreditation & Freezer-Failure Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Research biobanks that collect, store, and distribute blood, tissue, and DNA specimens for outside investigators operate under an IRB-approved protocol governed by the Common Rule (45 CFR 46), and every specimen carries its own linked informed-consent scope — a donor may have consented to cancer research but not to unrestricted commercial use, or to a specific study but not broad future use — and distributing a specimen for a purpose outside its documented consent scope is a federal human-subjects-protection violation independent of anything about the specimen's physical handling, exactly the finding an OHRP or institutional IRB audit is built to catch. Biobanks pursuing CAP Biorepository Accreditation or following ISBER Best Practices must maintain continuous temperature monitoring on every ultra-low-temperature freezer and liquid-nitrogen storage tank, with a documented, time-stamped response protocol for any freezer-failure or temperature-excursion event — a specimen collection that sat above its validated storage temperature for hours before anyone noticed is a research-integrity failure that can invalidate every downstream study using that collection, and a biobank that can't produce its failure-response documentation on demand is exactly the gap a CAP reaccreditation inspection targets. Every specimen distributed to an outside researcher must also be tracked against a negotiated Material Transfer Agreement (MTA) specifying permitted use, and a shipment sent before its MTA is fully executed is an unauthorized transfer of human specimens the biobank's own institution can be held responsible for. A biobank managing tens of thousands of specimens across multiple donor consent protocols and active researcher relationships tracks per-specimen consent scope, freezer/tank temperature-monitoring status, and per-shipment MTA execution on a mix of a laboratory information system built for specimen location rather than consent-and-agreement tracking and a biobank manager's spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific specimen's consent scope actually covers a specific researcher's request before it ships.

**What to build:** A per-specimen consent-scope tracker linking each specimen to its source donor protocol and flagging any distribution request outside the documented consent boundary; a continuous freezer/tank temperature-monitoring integration with a time-stamped failure-response documentation log; and a per-shipment MTA tracker with a "cleared to ship" gate blocking any distribution until the receiving researcher's agreement is fully executed.

**Skill fit:** The Common Rule's consent-scope requirements and CAP/ISBER's freezer-monitoring and failure-documentation standards are public, structured regulatory and accrediting-body data with a well-defined per-specimen record requirement, and the consent-scope-matching and shipment-eligibility gates are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a research-specimen-storage niche entirely distinct from every clinical blood, tissue, and milk bank idea already in this repo, none of which tracks a consent-scope-to-research-use match or an MTA-gated distribution at all.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the International Society for Biological and Environmental Repositories (ISBER) member directory and its annual meeting reach exactly the small biobank managers currently reconciling consent scope and MTA status against a specimen-tracking LIMS with no dedicated compliance layer.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $349-699/mo per biobank depending on specimen volume and active researcher-relationship count. 20-26 biobanks × $479/mo avg = $9,580-12,454/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every specimen collected carries its own consent-scope record for the life of the collection, every freezer and tank recurs on its own continuous monitoring cycle for as long as the biobank operates, and every new researcher relationship reopens the MTA-tracking requirement — a continuous, specimen-and-relationship-driven compliance stream tied directly to the biobank's collection growth.

### 5. Recreational Boat Manufacturer USCG Hull Identification Number & Flotation Self-Certification Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Small recreational boat builders must obtain a Manufacturer Identification Code (MIC) from the U.S. Coast Guard and assign a unique Hull Identification Number (HIN) to every hull produced under 33 CFR Part 181 — a boat sold without a properly formatted, permanently affixed HIN is an unregistrable vessel the buyer's own state titling agency will reject, a defect discovered only after the sale closes. Every model a manufacturer produces must be self-certified against 33 CFR Part 183's boating safety standards before it can legally be sold: Subpart D requires boats under 20 feet to meet a mandatory flotation standard, proven through an actual swamp/flotation test whose results the manufacturer must retain as its compliance record — a model sold without that underlying test documentation on file is exactly the finding a Coast Guard compliance investigation (often triggered by an accident or a random audit) targets, and a manufacturer that can't produce it faces a recall order covering every hull built to that specification. Subpart C requires a capacity plate on every boat computed from the model's safe-loading and horsepower-capacity calculation, and Subparts I and J require ignition-protected electrical components and compliant fuel-system installation, each with its own component-level documentation a manufacturer must be able to trace back to a specific hull. A small manufacturer running several boat models through a production line manages per-hull HIN assignment and sequencing, per-model flotation/capacity/electrical/fuel self-certification documentation, and capacity-plate accuracy on a shop-floor production log and a filing cabinet of model-certification binders, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific hull leaving the shop actually has its HIN assigned and its model's full self-certification package on file.

**What to build:** A per-hull HIN assignment and sequencing tracker tied to the manufacturer's MIC, preventing a duplicate or malformed HIN from being assigned; a per-model self-certification documentation tracker covering flotation test results, capacity-plate calculations, and electrical/fuel-system compliance records, with a "cleared to sell" gate blocking any hull from shipping until its model's full certification package is on file; and a component-to-hull traceability log for recall-readiness.

**Skill fit:** 33 CFR Part 181's HIN-formatting rules and Part 183's model-level self-certification standards are public, structured Coast Guard regulatory data with a well-defined per-hull and per-model record requirement, and the HIN-sequencing tracker plus the "cleared to sell" gate are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a boat-manufacturing niche this repo has never addressed and that shares no regulator, permit type, or self-certification structure with the marina, vessel-documentation, or boat-dealer ideas already in this repo.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the HIN sequencing tracker, the per-model self-certification documentation tracker with the shipping gate, and the component traceability log. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small independent boat builders.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the National Marine Manufacturers Association's (NMMA) small-builder member segment and its annual boat-show exhibitor network reach exactly the small manufacturers currently reconciling per-hull HIN assignment and model-certification documentation on a shop-floor log with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per manufacturer depending on model count and annual hull-production volume. 22-28 manufacturers × $429/mo avg = $9,438-12,012/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every hull produced generates its own HIN-assignment event regardless of how routine the model, every new model the manufacturer develops reopens the full flotation/capacity/electrical self-certification cycle from scratch, and every component substitution mid-production reopens the traceability question — a continuous, production-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the manufacturer's output.
