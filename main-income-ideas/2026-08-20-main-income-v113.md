# Main Income Ideas - 2026-08-20 (Session 113)

## Who this is for

A 29-year-old with software engineering and data analytics skills who wants to replace a full-time salary with a solo-buildable SaaS business, working evenings and weekends, with no outside funding. The target is $8-15K+/mo within 12-24 months, with a realistic path to first revenue inside 90 days. Every idea below is a monthly-retainer compliance product for small operators in a regulated industry — the builder never touches the regulated activity itself, only the tracking, deadline, and documentation layer around it.

## Ground rules for every idea in this series

- Solo-buildable start. One person, evenings/weekends, no funding, no employees required to reach the first $8-15K/mo.
- Realistic path to $8-15K+/mo, shown with explicit per-client pricing math.
- Time to first dollar under 90 days.
- Named distribution channel — a specific professional association, trade group, or referral network, not "cold outreach" in the abstract.
- No repeats. This session's five regulated industries are: (1) municipal solid waste landfill & transfer station operators, (2) health club/fitness studio operators under state health studio acts, (3) speech-language pathology private practices, (4) small e-commerce/SaaS companies subject to multi-state consumer data privacy law, and (5) domestic staffing/nanny placement agencies. Each is distinct from anything already in this repo:
  - **Landfill & transfer station operators** regulate the *disposal facility itself* under RCRA Subtitle D and state solid-waste-facility permits (siting, financial assurance for closure/post-closure care, groundwater and landfill-gas monitoring). This repo already has hazardous waste *generator* manifest tracking (RCRA Subtitle C, for companies shipping waste off-site) and scrap tire hauler registration (a single waste stream's transport chain) — neither touches facility-level permitting, financial assurance instruments, or post-closure monitoring, which is the entire scope of this idea.
  - **Health club/fitness studio compliance** covers the legal contract and licensing layer — state Health Studio/Health Club Services Acts (bonding requirements, mandatory contract disclosures, statutory rescission windows) and the FTC's 2024 "click-to-cancel" negative option rule. The repo's existing gym ideas (churn prediction, win-back campaigns, membership freeze tracker) are growth/retention tools with zero connection to a gym's legal exposure on contract enforceability, bonding, or cancellation-rule compliance — this idea is a legal-compliance retainer, not a marketing tool.
  - **Speech-language pathology private practice** compliance tracks state SLP licensure board renewal, ASHA Certificate of Clinical Competence (CCC-SLP) maintenance, Clinical Fellowship Year (CFY) supervision-hour documentation, and state Medicaid/school-based billing certification (many states require SLPs billing school-based Medicaid services to complete a separate state DOE/Medicaid certification and participate in Random Moment Time Studies). The repo mentions SLPs only as one line item inside two broader ideas — a multi-profession Medicare/Medicaid enrollment service and a multi-profession therapy billing/RCM service — neither of which builds SLP-specific licensure, CE, or CFY-supervision tracking as a standalone product.
  - **Multi-state consumer data privacy law compliance** targets small e-commerce and SaaS businesses that collect personal data from residents of the 19+ states with comprehensive privacy statutes (CCPA/CPRA, VCDPA, CPA, CTDPA, UCPA, and others) — tracking data subject access/deletion request (DSAR) response deadlines, opt-out/Do-Not-Sell mechanism verification, and vendor data processing agreements. This is a first-appearance regulatory category in the repo; the existing "Data Broker" idea targets companies that register as data brokers specifically (a narrow subset), not the much larger population of ordinary online businesses that must honor consumer privacy rights regardless of broker status.
  - **Domestic staffing/nanny placement agencies** operate under state employment agency licensing plus, in a growing list of states (NY, CA, IL, MA, NM, OR, CT, NV, and Philadelphia's city ordinance), a Domestic Worker Bill of Rights statute mandating specific written-contract terms, overtime/rest-break provisions, and household-employer payroll tax disclosure. No staffing or placement idea already in the repo (temp staffing, PEO, locum tenens staffing, travel nurse registries, court reporter staffing) covers domestic worker protections or the household-employer "nanny tax" compliance layer, which is a completely separate statutory regime.

### 1. Municipal Solid Waste Landfill & Transfer Station Operator RCRA Subtitle D, Financial Assurance & Monitoring Compliance

**Problem:** Municipal solid waste (MSW) landfills and transfer stations operate under EPA's RCRA Subtitle D criteria (40 CFR Part 258) as implemented through state solid waste permits, which require the facility to maintain a demonstrated financial assurance instrument (a trust fund, surety bond, letter of credit, or state-approved mechanism) covering the full cost of closure and 30-year post-closure care — recalculated annually as the facility's remaining capacity and inflation-adjusted cost estimate change, with the recalculation and updated instrument due to the state solid waste agency by a fixed annual deadline. Facilities must also run a groundwater monitoring program (a fixed network of upgradient/downgradient wells sampled on a quarterly or semiannual schedule, with statistical trend analysis submitted to the state) and, for facilities that meet a design-capacity threshold, a landfill gas (methane) monitoring program with quarterly perimeter probe readings and a corrective action trigger if methane concentrations exceed the regulatory limit. A small, independently or family-owned landfill or transfer station — common in rural counties and smaller municipalities that contract out landfill operation rather than running it as a government department — manages financial assurance recalculation, well-sampling schedules, gas-probe readings, and the annual state compliance report with a site manager who has no dedicated environmental compliance staff, and a missed financial assurance update or an unreported groundwater exceedance is precisely the finding a state inspection or EPA audit is built to catch, carrying penalties that can escalate to permit suspension.

**What to build:** A financial assurance tracker (current instrument type, coverage amount, annual recalculation deadline, and a cost-estimate calculator that rolls remaining capacity and current unit-cost data into next year's required coverage amount); a groundwater monitoring calendar (per-well sampling schedule, lab result log, and a statistical trend flag when a downgradient well begins drifting from its upgradient baseline); a landfill gas monitoring log (per-probe quarterly reading history with an automatic corrective-action-trigger alert when a reading crosses the regulatory threshold); and a state annual compliance report generator that rolls all three data sets into the format the state solid waste agency requires.

**Skill fit:** RCRA Subtitle D's Part 258 criteria and each state's solid waste facility permit conditions are public regulatory text, and the financial assurance cost-estimate calculation, well-network sampling schedule, and gas-probe threshold monitoring are exactly the kind of deterministic "compute the deadline, track the reading, flag the exceedance" data problems this series builds repeatedly — applied here to a facility-level environmental permit instead of a waste-generator or hauler manifest.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the financial assurance recalculation calculator and annual-deadline tracker, the groundwater well-sampling calendar with trend flagging, and the landfill gas quarterly-reading log with threshold alerts; first 5-8 clients from state solid waste association member directories (most states have a Solid Waste Association of North America — SWANA — chapter) and referrals from environmental engineering firms that already do the underlying well-network design work but don't offer ongoing compliance tracking.

**Time to first $:** 30-60 days — SWANA state chapters and the National Waste & Recycling Association (NWRA) both maintain member directories of small landfill and transfer station operators, and SWANA's annual state conferences are a concentrated venue to reach site managers directly.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per facility: $400-900/mo (a small rural transfer station with no groundwater network: $400/mo; a Subtitle D landfill with a full well network and active gas monitoring: $900/mo). Managing 15-22 facility retainers at $650/mo average = **$9,750-14,300/mo**. There are an estimated 1,900+ active MSW landfills and thousands of transfer stations in the US, the large majority operated by small counties, municipalities, or family-owned waste companies without dedicated compliance staff.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Financial assurance recalculation is annual and non-negotiable for every permitted facility. Groundwater and gas monitoring are continuous, recurring obligations for the life of the facility plus 30 years of post-closure care — a landfill that closes doesn't stop being a client, since post-closure monitoring is often the longest-running compliance obligation in this entire repo.

**Biggest risk:** Financial assurance cost-estimate errors have real financial consequences (undercoverage can trigger a state finding; overcoverage ties up client capital unnecessarily) — mitigate by building the calculator directly from each state's published cost-estimate methodology and having a state-certified engineer spot-check the first several client calculations before the tool is positioned as authoritative.

**Growth path:** Side project → financial assurance calculator, well-monitoring calendar, and gas-monitoring log live, first 5-8 transfer station and small landfill retainers through SWANA chapter outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-15 facilities, add a state annual report auto-generator. Replaces-full-time-salary → 15-22 facilities, expand into post-closure-only facilities (lower-touch, long-duration contracts) as a second product tier.

### 2. Health Club & Fitness Studio State Health Studio Act & Membership Contract Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent gyms, yoga studios, martial arts academies, and boutique fitness studios that sell prepaid or auto-renewing memberships operate under state health studio/health club services statutes (e.g., New York's General Business Law Article 26, Texas's Health Spa Act, Florida's Health Studio Services Act, California Civil Code §1812.80-.97) that mandate specific contract disclosures, a statutory 3-to-5-business-day right of rescission, caps on prepayment terms, and — in many states — a financial security bond or trust requirement sized to the studio's total membership liability, filed with the state and renewed annually. Layered on top, the FTC's 2024 "click-to-cancel" negative option rule (16 CFR Part 425) now requires any business selling auto-renewing memberships to make cancellation at least as easy as signup, in the same medium, with no added retention steps — a rule most independent studio owners learned about from a competitor's FTC complaint rather than from legal counsel. A studio owner managing member contracts through a generic gym-management platform (built for scheduling and billing, not legal compliance) has no systematic way to confirm every new contract includes the state-required disclosures, that the bonding amount still covers current membership liability as the roster grows, or that the studio's actual cancellation flow matches what the FTC rule requires — and a state attorney general inquiry or an FTC complaint targets exactly this gap.

**What to build:** A membership contract compliance checker (a state-specific disclosure checklist confirmed present in the studio's actual contract template, with an alert when a state statute changes); a rescission-period tracker (flagging any membership charged before its statutory rescission window closes); a bonding/financial-security calculator (current total prepaid membership liability compared against the studio's filed bond amount, with a renewal alert before the bond's annual filing deadline); and a cancellation-flow auditor (a periodic checklist confirming the studio's actual online and in-person cancellation process meets the FTC click-to-cancel standard — same number of steps, same medium, no forced retention call).

**Skill fit:** State health studio act text and the FTC's click-to-cancel rule are public regulatory sources, and the bonding-liability calculation (total prepaid contract value vs. required coverage) is a straightforward data aggregation problem — pulling active membership contract values from the studio's existing billing system and comparing the total against the filed bond amount is exactly the kind of engineering problem this series solves repeatedly, applied here to a business type (gyms) the repo has only ever addressed from a growth/retention angle, never a legal-compliance one.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the state disclosure checklist for the 15 states with the most detailed health studio statutes, the rescission-window tracker, the bond-liability calculator, and the click-to-cancel flow checklist; first 5-8 studio clients through direct outreach to independent (non-franchise) gym and studio owners in states with active AG enforcement history around gym contracts.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the International Health, Racquet & Sportsclub Association (IHRSA, now the Health & Fitness Association) and state boutique fitness owner Facebook/Slack communities are both active distribution channels; state AG consumer-protection press releases about gym contract violations (published periodically in most states) are a ready-made pitch hook for outreach to studios in that state.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per studio: $150-350/mo (a single-location studio with straightforward month-to-month billing: $150/mo; a multi-location club with prepaid annual contracts and an active bond filing: $350/mo). Managing 40-55 studio retainers at $250/mo average = **$10,000-13,750/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new membership sold is a fresh contract that must meet the disclosure and rescission requirements, every member cancellation is a test of the click-to-cancel flow, and the bond-liability calculation shifts every month as membership roster and prepayment mix change — this is continuous, transaction-level compliance monitoring, not a one-time contract review.

**Biggest risk:** A studio that ignores the tool's flags and gets hit with an AG inquiry may (unfairly) blame the compliance tool rather than their own contract practices — mitigate by keeping the product scoped to flagging and documentation, with an explicit engagement-letter disclaimer that the studio's legal counsel, not the software, makes final compliance determinations.

**Growth path:** Side project → disclosure checklist, rescission tracker, and bond calculator live for the top 15 states, first 5-8 studio retainers through IHRSA/Health & Fitness Association outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 20-25 studios, add the click-to-cancel flow auditor as a differentiator post-2024-rule enforcement wave. Replaces-full-time-salary → 40-55 studios, expand state coverage to all 50 and add a multi-location franchise tier.

### 3. Speech-Language Pathology Private Practice State Licensure, ASHA CCC-SLP & Clinical Fellowship Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent speech-language pathologists in private practice hold a state SLP licensure board credential requiring biennial renewal with continuing-education hours (typically 20-30 hours per cycle, with several states mandating specific hours in ethics or ASHA-designated topic areas) and, separately, maintain their ASHA Certificate of Clinical Competence (CCC-SLP), which carries its own CE-hour maintenance requirement tracked on a different renewal cycle than the state license. An SLP still completing their Clinical Fellowship Year (CFY) — the mandatory supervised post-graduate year required before full, unrestricted licensure — must log a specific number of supervised direct-service hours across defined skill categories, with the supervising SLP submitting periodic formal evaluations to both the state board and ASHA before the CFY can be certified complete, and a fellowship that lapses without timely evaluation submission can force the fellow to restart hours. SLPs providing school-based services billed to Medicaid face a third, separate compliance track in many states: a state Department of Education or Medicaid-specific SLP certification, participation in Random Moment Time Studies (RMTS) that determine the school district's Medicaid cost-report reimbursement, and service documentation formatted to the state's school-Medicaid billing manual — a requirement entirely distinct from clinic-based private-pay or insurance billing. A solo or small-group SLP practice supervising one or two CFY fellows while also billing school-district Medicaid contracts tracks three independent regulatory clocks — state license CE, ASHA CCC-SLP CE, and CFY hour/evaluation deadlines — with no single system connecting them, and a fellow's evaluation submission missed by even a few days can force a hiring practice to restart a supervision relationship it already invested months in.

**What to build:** A dual-track CE tracker (state license renewal deadline and ASHA CCC-SLP renewal deadline tracked independently, since the cycles rarely align, with category-specific hour requirements — ethics, telepractice, etc. — flagged separately from general CE hours); a CFY supervision tracker (per-fellow supervised hour log by skill category, a supervisor-evaluation submission calendar with the exact state-board and ASHA deadlines, and a "fellowship on track" dashboard showing hours completed against the requirement with time remaining); and a school-Medicaid billing compliance module (state SLP-Medicaid certification renewal tracker, RMTS participation calendar, and a documentation format checker confirming session notes meet the state's school-Medicaid billing manual requirements before a claim is submitted).

**Skill fit:** State SLP board CE requirements, ASHA's CCC-SLP maintenance standards, and each state's CFY supervision-hour and evaluation-submission rules are public, structured regulatory data, and the multi-cycle deadline tracking (three independent renewal clocks per practitioner) plus the category-specific hour requirement follows the same deterministic tracking pattern this series builds for other licensed-professional compliance products — applied here as a standalone SLP product rather than as one line item inside a larger multi-profession billing or enrollment service.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the dual CE-cycle tracker with category-specific hour flags for the 10 highest-SLP-density states, the CFY hour and evaluation-deadline tracker, and a school-Medicaid documentation checklist for 5-8 states with active RMTS programs; first 5-8 practice clients through outreach to small group practices that supervise CFY fellows (a strong initial buyer, since a botched CFY restart is expensive for the hiring practice).

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through the American Speech-Language-Hearing Association's (ASHA) Private Practice Special Interest Group, state SLP association member directories, and school-based SLP Facebook groups where RMTS and school-Medicaid documentation burden is a constant discussion topic.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per practice: $120-280/mo (a solo SLP with no CFY supervision and private-pay-only billing: $120/mo; a small group practice supervising 1-2 fellows and billing school-district Medicaid contracts: $280/mo). Managing 45-60 practice retainers at $220/mo average = **$9,900-13,200/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** State license and ASHA CCC-SLP renewal cycles recur every 1-2 years for every practitioner without exception, CFY supervision generates a dense sequence of hour-logging and evaluation-submission events for as long as the practice hires new graduates, and school-Medicaid billing compliance is a continuous per-session documentation requirement for any practice with a school contract — three separately-recurring obligation streams inside a single client relationship.

**Biggest risk:** A missed CFY evaluation deadline is high-stakes for the client (forcing a fellow to restart supervised hours) — mitigate by building redundant alerting (email plus SMS) at multiple lead times before each evaluation deadline rather than a single notification, since this is the one failure mode that would most damage client trust in the product.

**Growth path:** Side project → dual CE tracker and CFY supervision module live for the top 10 states, first 5-8 practice retainers through ASHA Private Practice SIG outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 20-25 practices, add the school-Medicaid RMTS/documentation module. Replaces-full-time-salary → 45-60 practices, expand to audiology private practices (a closely adjacent licensed profession with a similar dual-CE-cycle structure) as a second product line.

### 4. Multi-State Consumer Data Privacy Law (CCPA/CPRA & State Comprehensive Privacy Acts) Compliance for Small E-Commerce & SaaS Companies

**Problem:** Nineteen states and counting have enacted comprehensive consumer data privacy statutes — California's CCPA/CPRA, Virginia's VCDPA, Colorado's CPA, Connecticut's CTDPA, Utah's UCPA, and others enacted through 2024-2026 — each granting residents rights to access, correct, delete, and opt out of the sale/sharing of their personal data, with the business required to respond to a verified consumer request within a fixed statutory window (typically 45 days, often extendable once by 45 more days with notice). A small e-commerce store or B2B SaaS company collecting customer data from residents of multiple states must track which state laws it's subject to (thresholds vary by revenue and by number of state residents' data processed — a company can be covered in Colorado at 100,000 residents' data but exempt in Virginia until 100,000 residents or 25,000 residents plus revenue from data sales), verify and respond to each incoming data subject access/deletion request (DSAR) within that state's specific deadline, maintain a "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" mechanism that actually works end-to-end, and keep signed data processing agreements (DPAs) on file with every third-party vendor that touches customer data (required under most of these statutes as a condition of using a processor at all). A small company's founder or ops lead currently fields DSARs by email with no deadline tracking, has no reliable list of which vendors have signed DPAs on file, and often doesn't know which state laws actually apply to their specific size and data footprint — exactly the gap a state attorney general's office (several of which have begun proactively investigating small businesses, not just large ones) is positioned to find.

**What to build:** A state-applicability calculator (company enters revenue, data-subject count by state, and whether it sells/shares data; the tool determines which of the 19+ state laws currently apply, updated as new state laws take effect and thresholds change); a DSAR intake and deadline tracker (each incoming request logged with identity-verification status, the applicable state's response deadline auto-computed, and an internal task checklist — locate the data, prepare the export or deletion, respond — gated behind verification); a vendor DPA registry (every third-party data processor tracked with DPA signed/expired status and a renewal alert); and an opt-out mechanism auditor (a periodic automated check confirming the company's "Do Not Sell/Share" link and cookie-consent banner actually function as required).

**Skill fit:** Each state's privacy statute text, applicability thresholds, and DSAR response deadlines are public legislative text, and the state-applicability calculator (a rules-engine problem mapping company attributes to a matrix of 19+ state threshold tests), the deadline-computation-from-trigger-event pattern, and the vendor-DPA registry are exactly the kind of structured data-engineering problems this series solves repeatedly — applied here to a cross-industry compliance obligation rather than a single regulated trade, which widens the addressable market considerably since almost any e-commerce or SaaS business with US customers is a potential client.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the state-applicability calculator covering the 12 states with active enforcement history, the DSAR intake/deadline tracker with auto-computed response windows, and the vendor DPA registry; first 5-8 clients from small e-commerce and SaaS communities where a founder has already fielded a confusing DSAR email and has no process for it.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through Indie Hackers, small SaaS founder Slack/Discord communities, and Shopify/e-commerce operator forums, where "what do I actually have to do about CCPA" is a recurring, unresolved question; a free state-applicability quiz (lead magnet) that tells a founder whether they're even covered is a strong low-friction entry point into the paid DSAR/DPA tracking product.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per company: $99-249/mo (a small Shopify store with occasional DSARs and few vendors: $99/mo; a growth-stage SaaS company with regular DSAR volume and 20+ vendor DPAs to track: $249/mo). Managing 55-75 clients at $170/mo average = **$9,350-12,750/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** New states add comprehensive privacy statutes on an ongoing basis (several more are scheduled to take effect through 2026-2027), meaning the state-applicability calculator requires continuous maintenance and every client's coverage can change without the client doing anything — a structural reason the product never becomes "finished," and DSARs and vendor onboarding are continuous business-as-usual events for any growing company.

**Biggest risk:** Getting a state-applicability determination wrong has real legal exposure for the client — mitigate by keeping the calculator's output framed as an initial screening tool with a clear "confirm with counsel" disclaimer, and by sourcing thresholds directly from each state AG's published guidance rather than secondary summaries.

**Growth path:** Side project → state-applicability calculator and DSAR tracker live for the top 12 states, first 5-8 clients through founder-community outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 25-30 clients, add the vendor DPA registry and opt-out auditor. Replaces-full-time-salary → 55-75 clients, expand state coverage to all 19+ and add a children's-data (COPPA) compliance module as a natural adjacent add-on.

### 5. Domestic Staffing & Nanny Placement Agency State Employment Agency License & Domestic Worker Bill of Rights Compliance

**Problem:** Nanny and household-staff placement agencies operate under state private employment agency licensing (fee caps, bonding requirements, and placement-fee refund rules that vary significantly by state) and, in a growing list of jurisdictions — New York, California, Illinois, Massachusetts, New Mexico, Oregon, Connecticut, Nevada, and Philadelphia's city-level ordinance — a Domestic Worker Bill of Rights statute that mandates specific written-contract terms between the household employer and the placed worker (rest breaks, overtime calculation for live-in workers, advance notice of termination, and in some states a required written agreement the agency must confirm exists before placement is considered complete). Layered on top, agencies routinely advise household employers on "nanny tax" obligations — household employer federal and state payroll tax registration, quarterly estimated tax payments, and the Schedule H filing requirement — even though the tax obligation legally sits with the family, not the agency, creating liability exposure if an agency gives incomplete guidance and a placed worker later disputes wages or benefits. A small agency running 20-40 active placements manages agency license renewal, per-placement domestic-worker contract compliance checks across whichever of the 8+ states with a Domestic Worker Bill of Rights the placement falls in, and household-employer tax guidance documentation with no dedicated compliance function — and a placement made without the state-required written agreement on file is exactly the gap a worker complaint or state labor department investigation targets.

**What to build:** A state employment agency license tracker (renewal deadline, bonding amount, and fee-cap compliance check against the agency's actual placement fee structure); a Domestic Worker Bill of Rights contract checker (state-specific — confirming the required written agreement terms are present before a placement is marked complete, for any of the 8+ states with an active statute); a household-employer tax guidance document log (a standardized disclosure packet given to every placing family, timestamped and stored, so the agency has documented proof it provided the required guidance without taking on the family's actual tax-filing liability); and a placement compliance dashboard showing every active placement's contract and license status at a glance.

**Skill fit:** State employment agency licensing statutes and the text of each state's Domestic Worker Bill of Rights are public regulatory sources, and the per-placement contract-term checklist (gated behind a "placement complete" flag, matching the same work-eligibility-gate pattern this series builds repeatedly for other license-and-transaction-driven businesses) is a structured compliance problem — applied here to a staffing niche the repo has only addressed through general PEO, temp staffing, and specialty healthcare staffing angles, never domestic/household placement.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the agency license renewal tracker, the Domestic Worker Bill of Rights contract checklist for the 8 states with active statutes, and the household-employer tax guidance document log; first 5-8 agency clients through outreach to independent (non-franchise) nanny and household staffing agencies in New York, California, and Illinois, where the Domestic Worker Bill of Rights compliance burden is newest and least systematized.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the International Nanny Association (INA) and the Alliance for Professional Nanny Agencies (APNA) both maintain member directories of independent placement agencies, and INA's annual conference is a concentrated venue for direct outreach to agency owners.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per agency: $150-350/mo (a small agency with fewer than 15 active placements in a single state: $150/mo; a multi-state agency with 40+ active placements spanning several Domestic Worker Bill of Rights states: $350/mo). Managing 35-50 agency retainers at $260/mo average = **$9,100-13,000/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new placement is a fresh contract-compliance check, agency license renewal is annual and non-negotiable, and the list of states with a Domestic Worker Bill of Rights is actively growing — meaning the compliance checklist itself requires ongoing maintenance and every agency client's obligations can expand without the agency doing anything.

**Biggest risk:** A worker-employer dispute that escalates to a state labor department investigation could pull the placement agency in as a party even with full contract documentation on file — mitigate by scoping the product explicitly around contract-term presence and license/tax-disclosure documentation, with a clear disclaimer that the tool confirms paperwork exists, not that any individual placement is dispute-proof.

**Growth path:** Side project → agency license tracker and Domestic Worker Bill of Rights contract checklist live for the top 8 states, first 5-8 agency retainers through INA/APNA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 18-22 agencies, add the household-employer tax guidance document log as a differentiator. Replaces-full-time-salary → 35-50 agencies, expand into senior/elder-care companion placement agencies (a closely adjacent licensing and worker-protection structure) as a second vertical.

## This Session's Pick: #4 for the largest addressable market; #1 for the longest-duration client relationships

**Largest addressable market:** Idea #4 (multi-state consumer data privacy law compliance) is the standout in this session because its buyer isn't confined to a single regulated trade — nearly any small e-commerce store or SaaS company with US customers is a potential client, and the number of states with comprehensive privacy statutes is still growing. That combination of a wide buyer base and a genuinely confusing, actively-changing regulatory landscape is the strongest setup in this session for fast client acquisition through founder communities that already feel this pain acutely.

**Longest-duration relationships:** Idea #1 (landfill and transfer station compliance) stands out for durability — post-closure groundwater and gas monitoring obligations run for 30 years after a facility stops accepting waste, meaning a client relationship that starts with an active landfill can keep generating revenue for decades after the facility itself closes. Combined with a small, well-defined buyer population (SWANA chapter membership) and high per-client pricing driven by genuine regulatory complexity, it's the session's strongest pick for a builder who values contract stability over rapid client growth.
