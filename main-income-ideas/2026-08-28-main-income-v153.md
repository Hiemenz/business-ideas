### 1. Marine & Waterway Dredging Contractor CWA §404/§10 Permit, In-Water Work Window & Turbidity Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Small dredging contractors maintaining marina channels, private dock approaches, agricultural drainage canals, and small port berths operate under a US Army Corps of Engineers permit — a Clean Water Act §404 dredge-and-fill authorization layered with a Rivers and Harbors Act §10 navigable-waters permit, plus a state coastal-zone-management consistency determination and a CWA §401 water-quality certification — and every one of those permits carries its own conditions that don't expire on a simple renewal date but instead gate *when and how* the work can happen: a seasonal in-water work window set by the state wildlife agency to protect fish spawning and migration (a window that can be as short as six to ten weeks a year and varies waterbody by waterbody), a turbidity-monitoring requirement enforced with a specific NTU threshold during active dredging, and a sediment-testing-to-disposal-method rule requiring lab results before spoils can be placed in open water versus hauled to an upland site. A contractor who starts dredging even one day before the seasonal window opens, or who dumps spoils to open water without the required sediment test on file, is exactly the finding a Corps compliance inspection or a state environmental agency site visit is built to catch, and a violation can suspend the permit for that waterbody entirely — shutting down the job mid-contract with equipment already mobilized. A contractor running several concurrent jobs across different waterbodies and permit jurisdictions currently tracks work-window dates, turbidity readings, and sediment-disposal documentation on a paper site log and a stack of permit letters, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific day's dredging is actually inside its permitted window and turbidity is holding under threshold before the crew starts the excavator.

**What to build:** A per-permit in-water work window calculator flagging any scheduled dredge date outside that specific waterbody's permitted seasonal window with a hard "not clear to dredge" flag; a turbidity-monitoring log with a threshold alert during active work; and a sediment-testing-to-disposal-method tracker gating a load from being marked "clear to dispose" until lab results confirming the appropriate disposal method are on file.

**Skill fit:** Army Corps district permit conditions, state seasonal fish-window restrictions, and CWA §401 turbidity thresholds are public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-job, per-day compliance trigger, and the "not clear to dredge / not clear to dispose" gates are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a physical-excavation niche entirely distinct from the Wetland Delineation & CWA §404 Permitting Consultant idea already in this repo (Session 128), whose jurisdictional-determination and mitigation-monitoring paperwork practice never itself operates a dredge or holds a permit's own operating conditions.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the work-window calculator, the turbidity-monitoring log, and the sediment-disposal tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small dredging contractors.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Western Dredging Association's (WEDA) member directory reaches exactly the small contractors currently reconciling seasonal work windows and turbidity thresholds on a paper site log with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-399/mo per contractor depending on the number of concurrent permits and waterbodies tracked. 30-40 contractors × $280/mo avg = $8,400-11,200/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new job opens its own independent work-window-and-turbidity clock for that specific waterbody, every load of spoils reopens the sediment-disposal question, and every new permit jurisdiction a contractor expands into compounds the number of simultaneously tracked seasonal windows — a continuous, job-and-jurisdiction-driven compliance stream tied directly to the contractor's own growth.

**Biggest risk:** A contractor running one small, stable channel-maintenance contract in a single waterbody with a wide seasonal window has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward contractors running multiple concurrent jobs across several waterbodies with tight, fish-window-restricted seasons, where the compliance load genuinely outgrows a paper site log.

**Growth path:** Side project → 14 contractors ($3.9K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 26 contractors ($7.3K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 30-40 contractors plus a Corps-inspection-ready work-window-and-turbidity export a contractor can produce the moment a site visit happens.

### 2. Independent CRNA (Certified Registered Nurse Anesthetist) Practice Multi-Facility Privileging, NBCRNA CPC & DEA Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent Certified Registered Nurse Anesthetists — contracting with ambulatory surgery centers, rural hospitals, and office-based practices (dental anesthesia, plastic surgery, GI endoscopy) rather than working as a single hospital's employee — hold state Board of Nursing APRN licensure renewed on a state-specific cycle, alongside national certification through NBCRNA's Continued Professional Certification (CPC) Program: a rolling four-year cycle requiring a specific split of Class A (core) and Class B (elective) continuing-education credits, nested inside an eight-year cycle that adds a core-module completion and a proctored assessment — a CRNA who lets either cycle lapse loses the national certification the state license itself often depends on. In the roughly 20 states where CRNAs practice without physician supervision under a state's Medicare Part A hospital-anesthesia opt-out election, a CRNA must also independently hold and maintain clinical privileges at *every single facility* where they provide anesthesia — each ASC or hospital runs its own credentialing committee on its own renewal cycle (commonly every two years), verifying license, DEA registration, and malpractice coverage independently of the state board — and a CRNA who provides anesthesia at a facility where privileges have lapsed is practicing outside that facility's own credentialing authority, exactly the gap a Joint Commission survey or a malpractice claim's discovery process is built to catch. DEA registration for administering and ordering controlled substances runs on its own three-year renewal, independent of both the state license and the facility privileging cycle. A CRNA contracting across four or five facilities currently tracks state license renewal, NBCRNA CPC module and credit-hour progress, DEA registration, and per-facility privileging deadlines on a personal spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming every one of those records is simultaneously current before the next scheduled case at a specific facility.

**What to build:** A multi-facility clinical-privileging tracker with a "cleared to work this facility" gate flagging any site where privileges are approaching or past renewal; an NBCRNA CPC four-year/eight-year cycle tracker splitting Class A/B credit progress; and a DEA registration renewal tracker cross-referenced against every facility contract currently active.

**Skill fit:** NBCRNA's published CPC cycle requirements, state APRN license renewal cycles, and DEA registration expiration data are public, structured data with a concrete per-facility trigger, and the "cleared to work this facility" gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an anesthesia-specific APRN niche entirely distinct from the Independent PMHNP Practice idea already in this repo (Session 148), whose collaborative-practice-agreement and PDMP-query compliance core governs psychiatric prescribing with no facility-privileging mechanic of any kind, and from the Ketamine & Esketamine Clinic idea (Session 89), whose FDA REMS/DEA compliance governs the clinic's own controlled-substance program rather than an independent CRNA's own multi-facility credentialing.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the multi-facility privileging tracker, the NBCRNA CPC cycle tracker, and the DEA registration tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent CRNA groups contracting across multiple facilities.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the American Association of Nurse Anesthesiology's (AANA) independent-practice section reaches exactly the small CRNA groups currently reconciling multi-facility privileging deadlines on a personal spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149-299/mo per CRNA practice depending on provider count and number of facility contracts tracked. 45-60 practices × $210/mo avg = $9,450-12,600/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new facility contract reopens the privileging question on that facility's own independent renewal clock, the NBCRNA CPC cycle recurs on its own rolling four/eight-year schedule for as long as the CRNA holds certification, and every additional provider added to a growing practice compounds the number of simultaneously tracked facility-and-certification records — a continuous, contract-and-headcount-driven compliance stream tied directly to the practice's own growth.

**Biggest risk:** A hospital-employed CRNA working a single facility isn't the buyer for a multi-facility compliance tool. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward independent CRNA groups contracting across several ASCs or facilities, especially in opt-out states, where the multi-facility privileging load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 16 practices ($3.4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 32 practices ($6.7K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 45-60 practices plus a credentialing-audit-ready privileging export a practice can hand a facility's medical staff office the moment a renewal cycle opens.

### 3. Certified Diabetes Care and Education Specialist (CDCES) DSMT Program Accreditation & Benefit-Utilization Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Small diabetes education programs delivering Medicare's Diabetes Self-Management Training (DSMT) benefit depend entirely on the program itself holding current ADA Recognition or ADCES Accreditation — not just the individual educator's own credential — because Medicare will only reimburse DSMT sessions billed under codes G0108 (individual) and G0109 (group) when the delivering program's accreditation status is active, and a program whose ADA Recognition lapses (renewed on a multi-year cycle requiring an ongoing continuous quality improvement data submission tracking patient outcomes across the program) loses its ability to bill Medicare for every session performed under it starting the day it lapses, not just going forward — a finding a Medicare Administrative Contractor's post-payment audit is built to catch. Every individual Certified Diabetes Care and Education Specialist on staff separately holds NCBDE certification on its own five-year recertification cycle requiring continuing education and documented practice hours, and a CDCES delivering sessions on a lapsed individual certification compounds the program's exposure on top of any program-level accreditation gap. The DSMT benefit itself is capped and structured — 10 hours of initial training in the year following diagnosis or a qualifying event, plus 2 hours of follow-up training in each subsequent year — and a program that bills a patient's 11th hour of initial training, or a second year's follow-up hours before the calendar year resets, is billing outside the benefit structure Medicare actually covers, an overbilling pattern that surfaces in exactly the kind of claims-data review that triggers a program audit. A small program running several CDCES educators across a rolling patient caseload currently tracks program-accreditation renewal, individual NCBDE recertification, and per-patient DSMT hour utilization on a shared spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific billed session falls within both the program's current accreditation window and that patient's remaining benefit hours.

**What to build:** A program-level ADA Recognition/ADCES Accreditation renewal and CQI-submission tracker with a "cleared to bill Medicare" gate for the entire program; a per-CDCES NCBDE five-year recertification tracker; and a per-patient DSMT benefit-hour utilization tracker enforcing the 10-hour-initial/2-hour-annual structure before a session is marked billable.

**Skill fit:** ADA/ADCES accreditation-cycle requirements, NCBDE's recertification rules, and CMS's DSMT benefit-structure rules are public, structured regulatory and accrediting-body data with a concrete program-level and per-patient trigger, and the "cleared to bill" gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a diabetes-education niche entirely distinct from the Registered Dietitian/Nutritionist Private Practice idea already in this repo (Session 118), whose CDR CPE-cycle and Medicare Medical Nutrition Therapy referral-documentation compliance governs a completely separate Medicare benefit, billing-code family, and accrediting body with no DSMT program-accreditation or benefit-hour-utilization field of any kind.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the program-accreditation tracker, the CDCES recertification tracker, and the per-patient benefit-hour utilization tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small independent diabetes education programs.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Association of Diabetes Care & Education Specialists (ADCES) provider network reaches exactly the small programs currently reconciling accreditation status and benefit-hour utilization on a shared spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $99-199/mo per program depending on educator count and patient caseload. 55-70 programs × $150/mo avg = $8,250-10,500/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new patient enrolled opens its own independent DSMT benefit-hour clock, the program's own accreditation cycle recurs with its ongoing CQI-submission requirement for as long as the program bills Medicare, and every new CDCES hired reopens the individual recertification question — a continuous, caseload-and-staffing-driven compliance stream tied directly to the program's own growth.

**Biggest risk:** A very small program with one educator and a light, self-pay-only caseload has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward programs billing Medicare at volume across multiple CDCES educators, where the accreditation-and-benefit-hour load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 18 programs ($2.7K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 38 programs ($5.7K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 55-70 programs plus a MAC-audit-ready accreditation-and-benefit-utilization export a program can produce the moment a post-payment review arrives.

### 4. Court-Appointed Child Custody Evaluator & Guardian ad Litem Training-Hour, Conflict-Disclosure & Report-Deadline Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Licensed psychologists, MFTs, and clinical social workers who accept court appointments as child custody evaluators or guardians ad litem in contested family-court cases operate under state family-court evaluator rules — California Rule of Court 5.220 is the leading model, requiring an initial 16-hour training curriculum under Family Code §3110.05 plus 8 hours of annual continuing education specific to custody evaluation before an evaluator may accept new court appointments — layered with the Association of Family and Conciliation Courts' (AFCC) model standards requiring a documented conflict-of-interest disclosure filed for every single case before the evaluation begins; an evaluator who accepts an appointment without a current training-hour record, or who fails to file the conflict disclosure before starting interviews, has produced a report a parent's attorney can move to strike entirely, undoing months of work and exposing the evaluator to a licensing board complaint from the losing party — one of the single largest sources of board discipline against family-law-adjacent psychologists. Every appointment order also sets its own judicially-imposed report-delivery deadline, and a late-filed custody evaluation is exactly the failure that draws a judicial sanction or its own separate board referral, since the entire custody proceeding can stall waiting on the report a judge needs before setting a hearing date. Underneath both of those, the evaluator's own underlying professional license carries its own renewal cycle, and a lapsed underlying license immediately voids the evaluator's eligibility to accept any court appointment at all, regardless of how current their custody-specific training hours are. An evaluator working appointments across several counties — each often with its own local court rule stacked on top of the state requirement — currently tracks training-hour currency, per-case conflict disclosures, and appointment-order deadlines on a personal calendar and a case-file folder, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific case's disclosure was actually filed and its report deadline is still achievable before the judge's hearing date arrives.

**What to build:** A multi-jurisdiction training-hour and CE tracker computing each county or state's specific curriculum-hour requirement with a "cleared to accept" gate; a per-case conflict-of-interest disclosure and judicially-ordered report-deadline tracker with a countdown to the filing date; and an underlying-license renewal tracker feeding directly into the accept-new-appointments gate.

**Skill fit:** State family-court evaluator training-hour rules and AFCC's published model standards are public, structured regulatory and professional-standards data with a concrete per-case and per-jurisdiction trigger, and the "cleared to accept" gate plus the report-deadline countdown are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a family-court-evaluative niche entirely distinct from the Professional Fiduciary Guardian & Conservator idea already in this repo (Session 88), whose probate-court appointment manages an already-incapacitated adult's entire estate under a state fiduciary-licensing regime with no custody-evaluation training-hour or conflict-disclosure mechanic of any kind, and from the Court-Connected & Certified Mediator idea (Session 149), whose CME/ethics-hour roster-eligibility compliance governs a neutral facilitating a settlement between consenting parties rather than an evaluator producing an investigative recommendation to the judge under a fixed report deadline.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the multi-jurisdiction training-hour tracker, the per-case conflict-disclosure and report-deadline tracker, and the underlying-license renewal gate. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent custody evaluators and GALs.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Association of Family and Conciliation Courts' (AFCC) member directory and state chapter conferences reach exactly the small evaluator practices currently reconciling training-hour currency and report deadlines on a personal calendar with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $79-149/mo per evaluator. 70-90 evaluators × $115/mo avg = $8,050-10,350/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new court appointment reopens its own conflict-disclosure-and-report-deadline event, every county an evaluator works in adds its own training-hour variant to track, and the underlying-license renewal cycle recurs independently of any single case — a continuous, caseload-and-jurisdiction-driven compliance stream tied directly to the evaluator's own practice footprint.

**Biggest risk:** An evaluator accepting only an occasional appointment in a single county has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward evaluators working a steady court-referral caseload across multiple counties, where the training-hour-and-deadline load genuinely outgrows a personal calendar.

**Growth path:** Side project → 20 evaluators ($2.3K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 45 evaluators ($5.2K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 70-90 evaluators plus a board-complaint-ready training-hour-and-disclosure export an evaluator can produce the moment a parent's attorney challenges the report.

### 5. Neuropsychological & Psychological Testing Practice Licensure, Test-Publisher Access & Time-Unit Billing Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Neuropsychologists and testing psychologists running an assessment-only practice — cognitive, personality, and diagnostic testing for schools, courts, insurers, and referring physicians rather than ongoing psychotherapy — hold state psychology board licensure through a postdoctoral supervised-hours pathway (commonly 1,500-2,000 hours) that's tracked and renewed separately from a general clinical-license track, on a biennial CE cycle. Test publishers — Pearson, WPS, and PAR among them — gate access to restricted (Level B and C) test materials and to the digital platforms used to score and generate reports behind an actively verified license and qualification level on file with the publisher, and a practice whose license status isn't current in the publisher's own records can find its scoring-platform account frozen mid-report, unable to even finish scoring a test already administered to a patient. Medicare and commercial payers reimburse testing under time-based CPT codes — 96130-96133 for psychological testing evaluation and interpretation, 96136-96139 for neuropsychological testing, and 96146 for automated scoring — each requiring documented face-to-face time with the patient and separate technician time logged in specific unit increments, and a claim billed without that exact time-unit documentation on file is exactly the finding a payer's post-payment audit is built to catch, triggering recoupment across every similarly-billed claim in the audit window. A practice running several concurrent evaluations a week currently tracks license/CE status, test-publisher qualification records, and per-case time-unit documentation on a front-office spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific billed evaluation has both a current publisher-verified license on file and properly documented time units before the claim goes out.

**What to build:** A license and CE-cycle tracker with a "cleared to test" gate tied to publisher qualification-level status; a per-case time-unit billing log enforcing each CPT code's documented-minute requirements before a claim is marked billable; and a test-publisher access renewal tracker flagging any account approaching a qualification-verification lapse.

**Skill fit:** State psychology board CE rules, test-publisher qualification-verification requirements, and CMS's time-based testing CPT code rules are public and contractual structured data with a concrete per-case billing-eligibility trigger, and the "cleared to test / cleared to bill" gates are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an assessment-only psychology niche entirely distinct from the Independent PMHNP Practice idea already in this repo (Session 148), whose DEA registration and PDMP query obligations govern prescriptive authority a testing psychologist never holds, and from every general psychotherapy-focused licensure idea in this repo, none of which tracks a test-publisher qualification gate or a time-unit testing-code billing rule of any kind.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new evaluation reopens the time-unit billing documentation question, every test-publisher relationship recurs its own qualification-verification cycle, and every evaluator added to a growing practice compounds the number of simultaneously tracked licenses and publisher accounts — a continuous, caseload-and-headcount-driven compliance stream tied directly to the practice's own growth.

## This Session's Pick: #2 (Independent CRNA Practice) for the highest-value, cleanest multi-facility mechanic; #4 (Court-Appointed Custody Evaluator) for the sharpest professional-liability stakes

Independent CRNA practice compliance stands out because the multi-facility privileging gate is genuinely load-bearing rather than a scheduling nicety — a CRNA who provides anesthesia at a facility where privileges have lapsed is practicing outside that facility's own credentialing authority the moment the case starts, and the American Association of Nurse Anesthesiology's active independent-practice section gives direct, credible access to exactly the CRNA groups juggling several facility contracts with no dedicated system for it, at the highest per-account pricing in this session.

Court-appointed custody evaluator compliance has the sharpest professional-liability stakes: a missed conflict-of-interest disclosure or a blown report deadline doesn't just risk a fine, it can get an evaluator's entire report struck from a custody case and trigger the exact licensing-board complaint pattern that ends careers, which makes the training-hour-and-deadline tracker genuinely load-bearing for the evaluator's own professional standing, and AFCC's dense national and state-chapter network gives a builder direct access to almost the entire addressable market of evaluators who live this exposure on every single case.
