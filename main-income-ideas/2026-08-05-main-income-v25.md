### 1. ERISA Employee Benefit Plan Form 5500 Filing & Compliance Management
**Problem:** Employers sponsoring ERISA-covered benefit plans — group health plans with 100+ participants, 401(k) plans, FSA plans, and other welfare and retirement plans — must file Form 5500 annually with the Department of Labor and IRS, distribute Summary Plan Descriptions (SPDs) to all participants, maintain plan documents reflecting current design, and issue required participant notices (COBRA, HIPAA, CHIP, Medicare Part D creditable coverage notices) on specific annual schedules. Penalties for missing Form 5500 deadlines are $250/day up to $150,000 per plan, and the DOL's Voluntary Correction Program for late filers charges a penalty of $10 per participant per day. Beyond the annual 5500, ERISA plan sponsors must maintain a plan document (which must be restated every 6 years for prototype 401(k) plans), distribute a Summary of Material Modifications (SMM) within 60 days of any plan amendment, and conduct annual non-discrimination testing for health FSAs and retirement plans. Most employers with 20-150 employees manage this compliance through their payroll company (which handles 401(k) 5500 filings but not health plan 5500 filings), their health insurance broker (who may distribute some notices but doesn't manage plan documents), and their HR generalist (who doesn't know ERISA's specific compliance requirements) — resulting in systematic gaps across all three compliance obligations simultaneously.
**What to build:** An ERISA benefit plan compliance management service — a plan inventory and 5500 filing calendar (cataloging every benefit plan the employer sponsors, its participant count, plan year, applicable 5500 variant, and filing deadline; preparing the Form 5500 with required schedules — Schedule A for insurance information, Schedule C for service provider fees, Schedule H for financial information on large plans — and filing through EFAST2), a plan document maintenance workflow (tracking the plan document's last restatement date, required restatement deadlines for pre-approved plans, and plan amendments that require SPD updates or SMMs), a required participant notice calendar (generating the annual notice delivery calendar: COBRA election notices within 14 days of qualifying events, CHIP notice annually before the plan year begins, Medicare Part D creditable coverage determination and notice annually before October 15, HIPAA special enrollment rights notice at enrollment), a non-discrimination testing coordination service (gathering the compensation and participation data needed for 401(k) ADP/ACP testing and health FSA non-discrimination testing and coordinating with the plan's TPA for test results), and a DOL audit readiness checklist (generating the plan document and participant notice records required in any DOL or IRS employee benefit plan audit).
**Skill fit:** The Form 5500 preparation workflow (EFAST2 accepts structured electronic filings with well-defined field schemas for each schedule; the data gathering and form completion is a structured document preparation problem), the participant notice delivery calendar (each notice has a statutory deadline and required content defined by ERISA and its implementing regulations), the plan document restatement tracking, and the DOL audit readiness checklist are compliance calendar and document management problems the builder's data engineering skills address directly. The DOL publishes Form 5500 instructions, required notice templates, and plan document content requirements publicly.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a benefit plan inventory database (capturing plan type, participant count, plan year, and 5500 variant), a Form 5500 data gathering questionnaire covering the most common small and mid-size employer plan schedules (Schedule A and Schedule C for most plans), a required participant notice delivery calendar with annual notice triggers, and a plan document amendment tracking system; first 3-5 employer clients through employee benefits attorney referrals and HR consulting firm partnerships while the 5500 preparation workflow is validated against real EFAST2 acceptance confirmations.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through employee benefits attorneys (who draft plan documents and amendments for employers and regularly refer 5500 filing and annual compliance management to a specialist service), HR consulting firms (who provide HR support to employers of 20-200 employees and frequently encounter benefit plan compliance gaps their scope doesn't cover), and payroll company partner referral programs (payroll companies handle 401(k) 5500 filings for some clients but routinely leave health plan 5500 filing, participant notices, and welfare plan compliance to others — a natural referral dynamic).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per employer: $350-700/mo (depending on plan count and plan type complexity — an employer with a group health plan + 401(k) is $350/mo; an employer with a self-insured health plan, 401(k), FSA, and life/disability plans is $700/mo). Managing 22-28 employer retainers at $475/mo average = **$10,450-13,300/mo**. Form 5500 filing deadlines (July 31 for calendar-year plans, extendable to October 15) create predictable annual revenue spikes. Reaching 22-28 employer retainers requires 4-6 employee benefits attorney relationships and 2-3 HR consulting firm partner channels.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** ERISA compliance is a permanent annual obligation — every year, the 5500 must be filed, notices must be distributed, and plan documents must be current. Participant counts don't shrink for employers with stable workforce sizes, and plan counts grow as employers add benefit programs. DOL audit risk (the DOL's Employee Benefits Security Administration conducts targeted 5500 audit programs) creates urgency and willingness to pay that is structural, not discretionary. Employers who outsource ERISA compliance management rarely bring it back in-house because the regulatory complexity grows rather than simplifies.
**Biggest risk:** A Form 5500 filed with incorrect participant counts, financial data, or missing required schedules triggers DOL deficiency notices and potential civil penalties. Build a pre-filing review checklist that cross-references the 5500's participant count against the employer's plan enrollment records and verifies that all required schedules are attached before EFAST2 submission. Include an explicit engagement letter clause that the employer's plan administrator is responsible for certifying the accuracy of plan data before the 5500 is submitted — the service prepares and files, the plan administrator certifies.
**Growth path:** Side project → Form 5500 preparation workflow built for the most common plan variants (small plan health + 401(k)), participant notice delivery calendar generating annual notice triggers, plan document tracking system deployed, first 3-5 employer retainers through benefits attorney referrals. Replaces-part-time-income → 10-12 employer retainers, HR consulting firm partner channel established, first DOL audit readiness engagements completed (~$4-7K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 22-28 employer retainers, self-insured health plan client tier generating higher retainer rates, non-discrimination testing coordination add-on deployed, pushing past $10-13K/mo.

### 2. Specialty Food Brand Distributor Invoice Deduction Audit & Recovery
**Problem:** Specialty food and beverage brands selling through national distributors (KeHE, UNFI) and regional distributors receive monthly or bi-weekly payment remittances that are riddled with deductions applied against their invoice amounts: spoilage and damage claims (sometimes applied to product the distributor actually sold), promotional allowances for programs the brand never authorized, shortage deductions for alleged delivery discrepancies, administrative handling fees calculated incorrectly, and price protection credits for inventory the distributor bought at a higher price before a brand price change. Many of these deductions are invalid — fabricated, miscalculated, or applied to promotional programs the brand's sales team never approved — but brands don't dispute them because the dispute process is time-consuming, the documentation is complex, and the risk of straining the distributor relationship feels higher than the deduction amount. Across a $2M/year distributor relationship, uncontested invalid deductions of 3-5% represent $60,000-100,000 in annual revenue that brands simply absorb. A deduction audit service identifies disputable deductions, prepares the supporting documentation, and submits disputes on the brand's behalf.
**What to build:** A distributor deduction audit and recovery service — a remittance reconciliation tool (importing the distributor's payment remittance files — KeHE and UNFI both provide structured remittance data formats — and reconciling each deduction against the brand's invoice history, promotional calendar, and authorized trade spend), a deduction classification and disputability analysis (categorizing each deduction by type: authorized promotional allowance, potentially invalid damage claim, disputable shortage, unauthorized administrative fee — and flagging deductions above a minimum dispute threshold as worth challenging), a dispute documentation generator (building the supporting documentation for each disputed deduction: original invoice, proof of delivery, promotional authorization records, and the distributor's own claims documentation if obtainable), a dispute submission and tracking system (submitting disputes through the distributor's vendor portal or deduction dispute process and tracking each dispute's status — approved, denied, pending — with escalation triggers for disputes that age beyond the distributor's stated resolution window), and a recovery analytics dashboard (showing the brand's deduction rate by type, dispute win rate by category, and recovered revenue by period — quantifying the service's ongoing value in recovered dollars).
**Skill fit:** The remittance reconciliation tool (distributor remittance files follow structured formats with invoice reference, deduction type code, and deduction amount fields — building a parser and reconciliation engine against the brand's invoice history is a data matching problem), the deduction classification logic (each deduction type code maps to a specific dispute likelihood based on the brand's promotional calendar and delivery records), and the recovery analytics dashboard are data engineering problems the builder can systematize. KeHE and UNFI both have vendor portal systems that structure the deduction dispute process, making the dispute submission workflow a well-defined external interface.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a remittance file parser for KeHE and UNFI's most common remittance formats, a deduction classification engine mapping deduction type codes to disputable categories, a dispute documentation template for the most common dispute types (shortage, unauthorized deduction, promo allowance discrepancy), and a dispute tracking dashboard; first 3-5 food brand clients through specialty food industry communities and natural products broker networks while the deduction classification logic is validated against real remittance files.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through the Specialty Food Association and Natural Products Expo trade show communities (where emerging food brands are concentrated and deduction frustration is a constant discussion topic), food broker networks (food brokers represent brands at distributors and frequently witness deduction disputes that they can't resolve — a natural referral source for an audit service), and DTC food brand communities shifting to retail distribution (brands entering wholesale distribution for the first time quickly discover deduction complexity and need help immediately).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer: $400-700/mo per brand (depending on distributor relationship volume and deduction complexity). Contingency recovery fee: 15-20% of deduction amounts successfully recovered through disputes (applied to the incremental recovery above the brand's baseline dispute rate). A brand with $150,000/month in distributor invoices and a 4% invalid deduction rate has $6,000/month in disputable deductions — recovering 60% of those through disputes generates $3,600/month × 17.5% contingency = $630/month in contingency fees on top of the retainer. Managing 15-20 brand retainers at $525/mo = **$7,875-10,500/mo** in retainer base plus contingency recovery fees pushing toward $11-13K/mo at scale.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Distributor deductions are a permanent, recurring feature of wholesale distribution relationships — every remittance cycle generates new deductions that must be audited. Brands that grow their distributor revenue drive automatic growth in the deduction audit volume (and contingency recovery fees) without any account expansion effort. The service's recovery dashboard makes its ROI explicitly measurable — brands can see the recovered revenue against the retainer cost, making renewal decisions easy.
**Biggest risk:** Submitting a deduction dispute that turns out to be legitimate — disputing a valid promotional allowance that the brand's own sales team authorized but didn't communicate internally — damages the distributor relationship and the brand's credibility in the dispute system. Build an internal authorization verification step: before submitting any promotional allowance dispute, confirm with the brand's sales team that no promotion matching the deduction description was authorized during the relevant period. The additional 24-hour verification step before submission prevents the most common dispute error.
**Growth path:** Side project → remittance file parser validated against real KeHE and UNFI remittance formats, deduction classification engine tested against 2-3 real brand remittance histories, first 3-5 brand retainers through specialty food community outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 8-10 brand retainers, natural products broker referral channel established, contingency recovery revenue beginning to compound alongside retainers (~$4-7K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 15-20 brand retainers, regional distributor deduction workflows added beyond KeHE and UNFI, recovery analytics dashboard becoming a client retention driver, pushing past $10-13K/mo.

### 3. Multi-Location Corporate Tenant Commercial Lease Abstraction & Critical Date Management
**Problem:** Companies operating from 5-50 leased locations — regional retailers, franchise operators, medical group practices, professional service firms — have their lease portfolios scattered across paper files, email attachments, and legal department folders with no structured data layer. The result: missed renewal option deadlines (typically requiring 6-12 months' advance notice — missing one means the landlord is not obligated to renew on favorable terms), overlooked rent escalation dates (missing an escalation means overpaying or underpaying, with catch-up obligations), forgotten co-tenancy clause triggers, unclaimed tenant improvement allowance deadlines, and lease expirations that catch the operations team off-guard. A single missed lease renewal option at a profitable location can cost the company the right to renew at all, or force a below-market renewal on the landlord's terms. Most companies in this size range don't have an in-house real estate department — they rely on their real estate attorney to handle individual lease transactions but have no systematic portfolio-level visibility between transactions.
**What to build:** A commercial lease abstraction and critical date management service — a lease document review and abstraction workflow (reading each lease in the client's portfolio and extracting the structured data layer: base rent by period, rent escalation schedule, lease expiration date, renewal option terms and notice deadline, tenant improvement allowance amount and expiration, permitted use restrictions, co-tenancy requirements, landlord maintenance obligations, and operating expense cap provisions), a critical date calendar (mapping every actionable date across the portfolio: renewal option notice deadlines, rent escalation effective dates, TI allowance expiration dates, lease expiration dates, and option exercise windows), a portfolio dashboard (showing all active locations with their critical dates, base rent, and lease status — giving the CFO or COO a single view of the company's real estate obligations for the first time), an amendment tracking system (when a lease is amended, capturing the changed terms and updating the abstracted data layer and critical date calendar), and a critical date alert workflow (sending the internal real estate decision-maker advance alerts at 12, 6, 3, and 1 month before each critical deadline with the specific decision required and the consequences of inaction).
**Skill fit:** The lease abstraction process (reading each lease document and extracting structured data fields — rent schedule, option terms, escalation provisions — is a document review and data extraction problem that the builder can systematize with a consistent extraction template), the critical date calendar generator (computing notice deadlines from lease execution date plus contractual notice period), the portfolio dashboard (a data visualization layer on top of the structured lease database), and the amendment tracking system are structured document management and deadline tracking problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. Commercial lease abstraction follows a predictable field structure because commercial leases are standardized enough that the same data fields apply across the vast majority of leases in any portfolio.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a lease abstraction template covering the 15-20 most critical lease data fields for any commercial portfolio, a critical date calendar generator with configurable advance notice alert windows, a portfolio dashboard with lease status visualization, and an amendment tracking workflow; first 3-5 corporate tenant clients through tenant representation broker referrals and commercial RE attorney introductions while the abstraction template is validated against real lease documents.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through tenant representation brokers (who manage lease transactions for multi-location companies and have direct relationships with the real estate decision-makers at exactly the companies the service targets), commercial real estate attorneys (who handle lease negotiations for multi-location clients and recognize the portfolio management gap between transactions), and CFO/COO communities for growing companies (multi-location operators who have recently crossed the threshold where their portfolio is too complex to manage informally).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer: $500-1,200/mo per corporate tenant (depending on portfolio size — 5-10 locations: $500/mo; 11-25 locations: $800/mo; 26-50 locations: $1,200/mo). One-time abstraction fee for initial portfolio setup: $150-300 per lease abstracted (a one-time data extraction fee charged at engagement start, before monthly retainer begins). Managing 12-18 corporate tenant retainers at $750/mo average = **$9,000-13,500/mo**, plus abstraction setup fees on new client onboarding ($1,500-7,500 per new portfolio depending on location count). Reaching 12-18 tenants requires 4-6 active tenant rep broker relationships in the target metro market.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Commercial leases run 3-10 years, making each corporate tenant a 3-10 year retainer relationship with no natural churn point. Every new location the client opens generates a new abstraction engagement and increases their portfolio tier. The critical date calendar becomes the client's single source of truth for real estate obligations — once the service owns this data layer, the switching cost (re-abstracting all leases to a new provider) is high enough that clients stay. Tenant rep brokers who refer the service benefit because the portfolio visibility the service provides surfaces renewal and relocation decisions earlier, generating more transaction business for the broker — creating genuine referral alignment.
**Biggest risk:** An abstraction error that causes a client to miss a critical deadline — particularly a renewal option notice deadline — creates significant liability if the client loses their renewal right as a result. Build a two-reviewer abstraction workflow: every lease goes through an initial extraction pass plus a second-pass verification against the lease document before the critical date is entered into the calendar. Flag any ambiguous or unusual deadline clause for explicit client confirmation before the date is finalized.
**Growth path:** Side project → lease abstraction template validated against 20-30 real lease documents across different property types, critical date calendar generating advance alert notifications, first 3-5 corporate tenant retainers through tenant rep broker referrals. Replaces-part-time-income → 6-8 corporate tenant retainers, abstraction setup fees compounding alongside monthly retainer base, tenant rep broker referral relationships established (~$4-8K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 12-18 corporate tenant retainers, amendment tracking service deployed, portfolio dashboard becoming a strategic planning tool for CFOs managing lease renewal decisions, pushing past $9-13K/mo.

### 4. Pesticide Applicator License Renewal & EPA Worker Protection Standard Compliance for Pest Management Companies
**Problem:** Commercial pest management companies employing licensed pesticide applicators must maintain each technician's state pesticide applicator license in active standing — requiring state-specific continuing education credits (typically 15-30 hours per renewal cycle, varying by state and license category), annual or biennial renewal filings with the state department of agriculture, and insurance bond verification. Additionally, pest management companies applying pesticides on agricultural premises (farms, nurseries, greenhouses) must comply with EPA's Worker Protection Standard (WPS): providing pesticide safety training to agricultural workers and early-entry workers, posting WPS information at a central location, maintaining pesticide application records for 2 years, and providing decontamination supplies during re-entry intervals. Most pest management companies with 5-25 technicians manage license renewals informally — tracking renewal dates in a spreadsheet that misses CE topic requirements (many states require specific CE categories: new pesticide chemistry, laws and regulations, integrated pest management), and outsourcing WPS compliance to a one-time training without tracking the annual re-training requirement or maintaining the pesticide application records required by WPS regulation.
**What to build:** A pesticide applicator compliance management service — a technician license portfolio database (cataloging every technician's pesticide applicator license: state, license category, license number, expiration date, CE hours required, CE hours completed by category, and approved CE provider requirement), a CE requirement tracking engine (mapping each state's pesticide CE requirements by license category — structural pest control licenses have different CE requirements than ornamental/turf licenses; agricultural pest management has different requirements than public health — against each technician's CE completion record and flagging gaps before renewal deadlines), a license renewal calendar and coordination workflow (preparing renewal applications for each technician before their state's renewal deadline, tracking state department of agriculture acceptance, and confirming renewed license receipt), a WPS compliance management tool (for clients applying pesticides on agricultural premises: tracking the annual WPS safety training requirement for each agricultural worker and early-entry worker, maintaining the required pesticide application records — product name, EPA registration number, application date, location, rate, and restricted-entry interval — and generating the WPS compliance documentation for state department of agriculture or EPA review), and a new technician onboarding compliance workflow (triggering the license verification, state registration, and WPS training requirements for each newly hired technician before they apply pesticides independently).
**Skill fit:** The technician license database, CE requirement tracking by state and license category (each state department of agriculture publishes CE requirements publicly; building a structured database of state-by-state requirements by license category is a regulatory mapping problem), the license renewal calendar, and the WPS application record database are data management and compliance calendar problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. State departments of agriculture maintain online license lookup databases that can be monitored for license status changes, and WPS's application record requirements are explicitly defined in 40 CFR Part 170.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a technician license database for the 5-6 most active pest management states (FL, TX, CA, GA, NC, OH — large pest management markets with active state licensing programs), a CE requirement data model by state and license category, a license renewal calendar with advance alert notifications, and a WPS application record database and training tracker; first 3-5 pest management company clients through NPMA (National Pest Management Association) community outreach while the license tracking and CE requirement database is validated against real technician renewal histories.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through NPMA (National Pest Management Association) member communications and state pest management associations (every state has a pest management association with an active member directory of licensed pest control companies), pest management CE course provider partner programs (CE providers who sell to pest control technicians have direct relationships with the company managers who manage license compliance), and pest management business coaching and franchise communities (franchise pest control brands — Orkin, Terminix franchisees, and regional brands — have multi-technician license compliance obligations and benefit from systematic tracking).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per company: $250-500/mo (depending on technician count and license complexity — a 5-technician residential pest control company is $250/mo; a 25-technician company with multiple license categories and WPS compliance is $500/mo). Managing 30-40 pest management company retainers at $350/mo average = **$10,500-14,000/mo**. The pest management industry in the US includes over 20,000 licensed pest control companies; reaching 35 clients is less than 0.2% penetration. License renewal cycles (annual or biennial, state-dependent) create predictable revenue spikes coinciding with renewal seasons.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Pesticide applicator licenses are a permanent, mandatory operating requirement — a pest management company cannot legally operate without licensed technicians, and license lapses (from missed CE or missed renewal) require the technician to stop applying pesticides immediately, disrupting the company's service schedule. The WPS compliance obligation is permanent for any company with agricultural premises clients. Technician turnover (the pest management industry has high turnover) creates recurring new technician onboarding compliance engagements that compound the service's ongoing value without additional client acquisition.
**Biggest risk:** A technician who applies pesticides after their license has lapsed — or applies on an agricultural premises without current WPS training — creates significant regulatory liability for the company. Build an active license verification check (using each state's online license lookup database) that runs monthly for every technician in the client's roster and alerts immediately if any license shows as expired or inactive, so the company can pull that technician from pesticide applications before a violation occurs.
**Growth path:** Side project → technician license database and CE requirement tracker validated for 4-5 target states, WPS application record database deployed, license renewal calendar generating advance alerts, first 3-5 pest management company retainers through NPMA community outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-15 company retainers, state pest management association member marketing established, first WPS compliance audit readiness engagements for agricultural pest management clients (~$4-6K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 30-40 company retainers, multi-state license tracking for companies operating in multiple states at higher retainer tiers, pushing past $10-14K/mo.

### 5. Workers' Compensation Premium Audit Defense & Job Classification Review
**Problem:** Workers' compensation insurers conduct mandatory annual premium audits at the end of each policy year, reviewing the employer's actual payroll to determine the final premium owed based on actual (not estimated) payroll and the applicable classification codes for each job category. These audits frequently generate significant additional premium assessments — sometimes tens of thousands of dollars — because auditors: assign employees to higher-rated job classification codes than their actual duties warrant, fail to apply subcontractor exclusions correctly, count payroll for executive officers above the state's maximum officer payroll cap, or misclassify clerical and sales employees into construction or manufacturing codes based on the employer's general industry rather than the employee's actual duties. Employers who receive an unexpected premium audit bill almost never challenge it — they don't know that classification decisions are contestable, don't know the NCCI classification rules that define which code applies to which job duty, and don't have time to research the dispute process while running their business. A defense service reviews the audit results, identifies misclassifications and improper payroll allocations, and submits a formal dispute that can eliminate or significantly reduce the additional premium assessed.
**What to build:** A workers' compensation premium audit defense service — a premium audit review and classification analysis tool (reviewing the insurer's audit report against the employer's actual payroll records and job descriptions, identifying each classification code assigned and cross-referencing it against NCCI's Scopes of Basic Manual Classifications to determine whether the assigned code accurately describes the employees' actual duties), a disputed classification documentation system (for each disputed code, preparing the supporting documentation: detailed job duty descriptions for each employee category, NCCI classification rule citations supporting the alternative code, and payroll segregation evidence showing which payroll is correctly classifiable under which code), a payroll exclusion and limitation review (identifying executive officer payrolls above the state's statutory maximum, subcontractor payroll that qualifies for exclusion, overtime premium exclusion opportunities, and employee vs. contractor classification issues that affect the audit basis), a formal dispute submission workflow (preparing the dispute letter and supporting documentation in the format required by the insurer's audit review process or state insurance department complaint process), and a premium savings calculator (projecting the premium reduction achievable from each disputed classification change, allowing the employer to evaluate the dispute value against the service fee).
**Skill fit:** The NCCI classification rules database (NCCI publishes Scopes of Basic Manual Classifications — the definitive reference for which classification code applies to which job duty — as a structured reference document; building a searchable classification lookup and dispute analysis workflow is a regulatory reference data problem), the payroll exclusion rules (each state's workers' comp statute defines executive officer payroll caps and overtime premium exclusion rules — structured state-by-state data), and the premium savings calculator are data analysis and structured reference application problems the builder can systematize. No insurance license is required to provide premium audit dispute consulting in most states (it is a consulting and advocacy service, not insurance product sales).
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a NCCI classification code lookup tool (using NCCI's published Scopes descriptions), a premium audit review checklist identifying the most common misclassification patterns (clerical employees coded to manual labor codes, construction supervisors coded to laborer codes, technology company employees coded to their clients' industries), a payroll exclusion analysis tool, and a dispute letter template; first 5-10 audit defense engagements through workers' comp broker referrals and employer association communities while the classification analysis framework is validated against real audit dispute outcomes.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through independent workers' compensation insurance brokers (who have direct relationships with every employer client receiving an audit result and can refer defense services when audit bills arrive), employer association communities (NFIB, regional employer associations — employers who receive unexpected audit bills are motivated buyers who are actively searching for help at the moment the service is most relevant), and payroll company partner programs (payroll companies whose clients receive audit assessments are a natural referral channel — the employer calls their payroll company asking "why is my premium so high?" and the payroll company can refer the audit defense service).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Per-engagement defense fee: $500-2,000 per audit defense engagement (depending on the size of the disputed premium — an employer with a $5,000 additional premium assessment warrants $500-750 in defense fees; an employer with a $50,000 assessment warrants $1,500-2,000). Contingency option: 20-25% of premium savings achieved through the dispute (more attractive to employers who can't pay a flat fee). Processing 8-12 audit defense engagements per month at $1,000 average = **$8,000-12,000/mo**. Workers' compensation premium audits happen annually for every insured employer, and additional premium assessments are common enough that a steady pipeline of 8-12 monthly engagements is achievable through 4-6 active workers' comp broker referral relationships. Annual audit season (audits typically occur 60-90 days after policy expiration) creates predictable seasonal volume spikes.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Workers' comp audits happen every year for every insured employer — the service's buyer population regenerates annually without any new market development required. Employers who have had their audit successfully defended once become permanent referral sources: they tell every business-owner peer who mentions an unexpected workers' comp bill about the service, generating word-of-mouth referrals at exactly the moment the referred employer has maximum urgency and willingness to pay. Workers' comp broker referral relationships generate consistent pipelines once established because brokers can proactively notify every client who receives an audit result — turning each broker into an active referral channel rather than a passive one.
**Biggest risk:** A dispute that fails — where the insurer upholds the original audit result — with an employer who expected a specific recovery creates client satisfaction risk. Build a pre-engagement assessment step: before accepting a defense engagement, review the audit report and payroll records to estimate the dispute success likelihood and potential premium savings. Decline engagements where the audit appears accurate and the payroll is correctly classified, and communicate the assessment honestly before the engagement begins. A contingency option (no fee if no recovery) removes this risk for the employer and concentrates the service's work on the highest-probability disputes.
**Growth path:** Side project → NCCI classification code database and misclassification pattern library built, payroll exclusion rules mapped by state, dispute letter template validated against real audit dispute outcomes, first 5-8 audit defense engagements through workers' comp broker referrals. Replaces-part-time-income → 4-5 audit defense engagements per month, workers' comp broker referral channel established, contingency option attracting larger-premium employers (~$4-7K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 8-12 engagements per month, broker referral network across 5-8 active workers' comp specialists, annual audit season marketing campaigns driving volume spikes, pushing past $8-12K/mo.
