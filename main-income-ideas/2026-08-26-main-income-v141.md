### 1. Geotechnical Engineering Firm State DOT Prequalification, Field-Crew OSHA Competent-Person & Boring-to-Report Chain-of-Custody Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Small geotechnical engineering firms that design foundations, retaining walls, and slope-stability solutions for public transportation projects must hold their own firm-level prequalification certification with every state DOT they bid work in — Caltrans, TxDOT, PennDOT, and NYSDOT among them — renewed annually or biennially against a demonstrated project-experience history, a written QA/QC manual, and a roster of specific NICET-certified soils technicians the DOT verifies is actually current; a firm whose prequalification lapses is locked out of bidding public work entirely until it's reinstated, regardless of how strong its engineering is. Every field crew member who opens a test pit or supervises a drill rig on a boring program must independently hold current OSHA 29 CFR 1926 Subpart P excavation competent-person qualification, since any excavation deeper than five feet legally requires a documented competent-person inspection and protective system before a technician enters — a crew member sent into the field with a lapsed competent-person qualification is exactly the finding an OSHA inspection or a DOT project audit is built to catch. Every soil sample a boring crew pulls from the ground must also carry its own chain-of-custody record — sample ID, depth, boring-log reference — traceable through lab testing all the way to the specific PE-stamped geotechnical report a foundation design depends on, and many DOT specifications require the physical samples themselves to be retained for a fixed period after the report is issued in case of a dispute; a report issued without a reconcilable sample trail is exactly the gap a peer-review audit or a construction-dispute discovery request targets. A small firm running several field crews and multiple DOT prequalifications across neighboring states manages per-state prequalification renewal, per-technician competent-person and NICET currency, and per-project sample chain-of-custody on a project manager's spreadsheet and a filing cabinet of boring logs, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific project's crew, samples, and prequalification status are all actually current before the report ships.

**What to build:** A per-state DOT prequalification renewal tracker with a bid-eligibility gate flagging any state where certification has lapsed; a per-technician OSHA competent-person and NICET certification tracker with a "cleared to supervise" gate blocking any field crew assignment once a qualification lapses; and a per-project sample chain-of-custody log tying every boring's sample IDs through lab testing to the final PE-stamped report, with a retention-deadline tracker for physical sample disposal.

### 4. Immigration Bond Agent ICE Form I-352, Treasury-Listed Surety Appointment & Federal Bond-Breach/Cancellation Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Immigration bond agents post federal delivery bonds under INA §236 or voluntary-departure bonds under §241 on ICE Form I-352, underwritten only by a surety company listed on U.S. Treasury Circular 570 — an entirely separate underwriting-authority chain from a state Department of Insurance-licensed criminal bail surety, and an agent who lets a Treasury-listed surety's own appointment or capacity limit go stale is exactly the finding an ICE bond office rejects a filing over. Unlike a criminal bail bond, whose condition is a single court date, an immigration bond's condition is the respondent's continued appearance at every hearing in a removal proceeding and, ultimately, actual physical surrender for removal or a granted immigration benefit — a compliance period that can run for years given immigration court backlogs, meaning an agent's open-bond book has to track respondents whose obligations are still active long after a typical criminal bail bond would have closed. When a respondent misses a required appearance or fails to surrender, ICE issues its own Bond Breach Determination through a federal process entirely separate from a county court's forfeiture notice, with its own contest-and-appeal deadline the agent must meet to avoid losing the full bond amount — a determination an agent tracking dozens of open bonds across different ICE field offices on a paper ledger can easily miss until the demand for payment arrives. Just as consequential on the other side: when a respondent's case actually resolves favorably (removal completed, relief granted, proceedings terminated), the agent must affirmatively file for bond cancellation — commonly Form I-391 — to release the tied-up capital back to the surety's underwriting capacity, and an agent who doesn't track case-resolution events leaves capital needlessly locked up for months or years past when it could have been freed. A small immigration bond agency running dozens to hundreds of open bonds across multiple ICE field offices and immigration courts manages per-bond respondent-appearance status, breach-determination deadlines, and cancellation-eligibility events on a paper ledger, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific bond's aggregate exposure against the surety's capacity limit before the next bond is written.

**What to build:** A Treasury-listed surety appointment and underwriting-capacity tracker; a per-bond respondent-status ledger tracking hearing appearances and flagging any bond approaching a breach-determination risk; a Bond Breach Determination deadline calculator with contest/appeal countdown alerts; and a case-resolution-triggered cancellation-filing tracker (Form I-391) to flag capital eligible for release.

**Skill fit:** ICE's bond-posting and breach-determination process and Treasury Circular 570's surety-listing requirements are public, structured federal regulatory data with concrete per-bond triggers, and the breach-deadline calculator plus the cancellation-eligibility tracker are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a federal-immigration-bond niche explicitly distinct from the state-court criminal bail bondsman idea already in this repo, which tracks an entirely different regulator, surety-authority chain, and forfeiture-deadline mechanism.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the surety appointment/capacity tracker, the per-bond respondent-status ledger with the breach-deadline calculator, and the cancellation-filing tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent immigration bond agencies.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Professional Bail Agents of the United States (PBUS) immigration-bond-specialist members and state bail-agent associations with an immigration-bond practice area reach exactly the small agencies currently reconciling open-bond status and breach deadlines on a paper ledger with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $349-699/mo per agency depending on open-bond volume and number of ICE field offices served. 20-26 agencies × $479/mo avg = $9,580-12,454/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every bond written opens its own multi-year respondent-status tracking obligation regardless of how routine the case, every hearing cycle reopens the breach-risk question, and every case resolution generates its own cancellation-filing event — a continuous, open-bond-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the agency's book of business.

## This Session's Pick: #2 (Mobile Mammography & Mobile Imaging Unit) for the sharpest per-unit federal certification hook; #4 (Immigration Bond Agent) for the cleanest, most legally load-bearing federal process to formalize

Mobile mammography compliance stands out because the FDA's per-unit certification structure means every single truck in an operator's fleet carries its own independent certification-and-survey obligation regardless of how many addresses it visits — a rare case in this series where the regulator's own definition of "facility" maps directly onto the exact unit-by-unit tracking a builder would want to sell anyway, and a missed survey carries the unusually sharp consequence of a mandatory patient-notification letter, which gives the pitch real urgency. ACR's mobile-facility accreditation roster and the Mobile Healthcare Association give direct, credible access to exactly the small operators who own this problem.

Immigration bond agent compliance has the cleanest underlying legal process in this session to formalize into software: ICE's Form I-352 bond, its Bond Breach Determination, and Form I-391 cancellation filing are all federal, numbered, and procedurally exact, which means the product's core value — tracking a bond's condition across a multi-year immigration-court timeline that a paper ledger simply isn't built for — maps onto a real and growing population of small agencies as immigration-court backlogs keep bonds open for longer than ever before.
