### 1. Barbershop & Barber State Board License, Apprentice-Hour & Bloodborne Pathogen Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Barbershops operate under state barber boards that require every working barber to hold an individually renewed license earned through a fixed apprenticeship-hour requirement (commonly 1,000-1,500 hours logged under a licensed master barber before the state licensing exam), a separate shop/establishment license per physical location, and continuing-education hours tied to each barber's own renewal date. Because straight-razor shaves and close fades routinely nick skin, state barbering rules mirror OSHA's Bloodborne Pathogens Standard (29 CFR 1910.1030), requiring a documented exposure-control plan, implement-disinfection logs, and — for shops running autoclaves — periodic spore-testing records proving the sterilizer actually works. A shop that lets an apprentice work unsupervised past their logged-hour threshold, dispatches a barber on a lapsed license, or can't produce a current spore-test log during a state board's unannounced inspection is exactly the finding that draws a citation, a fine, or a shop shutdown. A multi-chair barbershop running 6-12 barbers and apprentices manages apprentice-hour logs, shop and individual license renewal dates, and bloodborne-pathogen sanitation records across a paper logbook and a shop owner's memory, with no systematic cross-check between a specific barber's license status and who's actually working the floor that day.
**What to build:** A per-barber apprenticeship-hour tracker counting logged hours toward the state's licensure threshold; a shop and individual license renewal calendar with a "cleared to work" gate blocking chair assignment for any lapsed license; and a bloodborne-pathogen compliance log tracking implement-disinfection records and autoclave spore-test results with due-date alerts.
**Skill fit:** State barber-board licensure-hour thresholds and OSHA's bloodborne-pathogen recordkeeping requirements are public, structured regulatory data with a well-defined per-barber and per-shop record model, and the apprentice-hour counter plus the work-eligibility gate are the same deterministic "count toward the threshold / block the action until the credential clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a personal-care trade most independent shops currently track through a paper logbook with no systematic link between a barber's actual license status and the chair they're working.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the apprentice-hour tracker, the license renewal gate, and the bloodborne-pathogen sanitation log; land the first 6-10 shops through National Association of Barber Boards of America (NABBA) state-affiliate outreach and Professional Beauty Association (PBA) member channels.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — state barber association chapters and PBA's shop-owner directory, where multi-chair shops already juggle apprentice-hour thresholds and spore-test logs with no dedicated compliance software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $99/mo base (up to 6 chairs/apprentices tracked) + $8/mo per additional chair. Average client (10 chairs, 4 over base) = $99 + 4×$8 = $131/mo. 69-92 shops × $131/mo = **$9,039-12,052/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every apprentice logging hours toward licensure reopens the hour-counter daily, every barber's individual license renews on its own cycle, and every implement-disinfection cycle generates a fresh log entry — a continuous, per-barber and per-shift compliance stream tied to a shop's daily chair count rather than an annual renewal.
**Biggest risk:** A lapsed license worked unnoticed, or a missing spore-test log during a surprise board inspection, is exactly the finding that draws a citation or shutdown — the work-eligibility gate and the sanitation log are the core protections, built to catch the gap before the inspector does.
**Growth path:** Side project → apprentice-hour tracker, license gate, and sanitation log deployed, first 6-10 shop retainers through NABBA and PBA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 35-45 shops. Replaces-full-time-salary → 69-92 shops, pushing past $9.04-12.05K/mo.

### 2. Payday & Auto Title Loan Lender State Rollover-Limit & Database-Verification Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Payday and auto title lenders operate under state short-term/deferred-presentment lending statutes — Texas's Credit Access Business license under Finance Code Chapter 393, Georgia's Industrial Loan Act for title lending, California's Deferred Deposit Transaction Law licensed through the DFPI — that cap or outright ban loan rollovers, require a mandatory cooling-off period between a paid-off loan and a new one, and, in states running a centralized lending database (Florida's Veritec system, Illinois's statewide database, Oklahoma's database), require real-time verification before issuing a new loan that the borrower doesn't already carry an outstanding loan elsewhere in the state. Title lenders carry an added layer: a lien on the vehicle collateral that must be properly released once the loan is paid, and a repossession-notice process that must follow state-mandated timelines before a vehicle can be sold. A location that issues a rollover past the state's cap, skips the database check before funding a new loan, or mishandles a title lien-release is exactly the finding a state financial-regulation examiner's audit is built to catch, and license revocation is a real consequence for repeat violations. A small payday or title-loan operator running 2-4 storefront locations manages rollover counts, database-check timing, and title-lien status with a teller-terminal spreadsheet and no systematic gate stopping a new loan from funding before the database check clears.
**What to build:** A per-loan rollover counter enforcing the state's cap with a hard stop once the limit is reached; a pre-funding database-verification gate blocking loan issuance until the state database check confirms no outstanding loan elsewhere; and, for title lenders, a lien-release and repossession-notice tracker with statutory timeline countdowns.
**Skill fit:** State rollover-cap rules and centralized lending-database check requirements are public, structured regulatory text with a well-defined per-loan and per-borrower record model, and the rollover counter plus the pre-funding gate are the same deterministic "count toward the cap / block the action until the check clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a short-term-lending trade most small operators currently track on a teller-terminal spreadsheet with no systematic pre-funding gate tied to the state database.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the rollover-cap counter, the database-verification gate, and the title lien-release tracker; land the first 5-8 operators through Online Lenders Alliance and state small-dollar-lending trade association outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — state consumer-lending trade associations and the Online Lenders Alliance member directory, where small storefront operators already juggle per-state rollover caps and database-check timing with no dedicated compliance software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299/mo base per licensed location (up to 500 active loans/mo) + $0.75/mo per additional loan. Average client (1,200 loans/mo, 700 over base) = $299 + 700×$0.75 = $824/mo. 11-15 operators × $824/mo = **$9,064-12,360/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new loan reopens its own rollover-count check and database-verification gate before it can fund, and every title loan carries its own lien-release timeline — a continuous, per-loan compliance stream tied directly to daily loan volume rather than a single annual license renewal.
**Biggest risk:** A rollover issued past the state cap, or a loan funded without the required database check, is exactly the finding that draws license revocation on audit — the rollover counter and the pre-funding gate are the core protections, built to make the compliant path the only path the terminal allows.
**Growth path:** Side project → rollover counter, database gate, and lien-release tracker deployed, first 5-8 operator retainers through OLA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 7-9 operators. Replaces-full-time-salary → 11-15 operators, pushing past $9.06-12.36K/mo.

### 3. Foster Care & Child-Placing Agency State License, Home-Study & Caseworker-Ratio Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Private foster care and child-placing agencies operate under state child-welfare licensing bodies that require every foster home under the agency's supervision to hold its own renewable license — contingent on fingerprint-based background checks for every adult household member under the federal Adam Walsh Act, a documented home-safety inspection, and a fixed pre-service training-hour requirement (commonly 30 hours via a curriculum such as PRIDE or MAPP) — plus a state- or accreditor-mandated caseworker-to-foster-home ratio ceiling the agency can't exceed without triggering a licensing violation. Any placement crossing state lines requires Interstate Compact on the Placement of Children (ICPC) paperwork approved before the child moves, and any critical incident (an injury, an allegation, a placement disruption) must be reported to the state within a fixed window, commonly 24-48 hours. An agency that lets a foster home's license lapse, exceeds its caseworker-ratio ceiling, or misses a critical-incident reporting deadline is exactly the finding a state licensing-division audit is built to catch, and a licensing violation on a child-placing agency draws far more scrutiny than a typical business compliance gap because a child's safety is the direct stake. A small independent agency supervising 30-70 foster homes across a caseworker team of 4-8 manages home-license renewal dates, training-hour completion, and caseworker caseloads with a shared spreadsheet and no systematic alert when a specific caseworker's home count creeps past the state's ratio ceiling.
**What to build:** A per-foster-home license renewal tracker with background-check and training-hour completeness gates; a caseworker-to-home ratio monitor flagging any caseworker approaching or exceeding the state ceiling; and a critical-incident reporting countdown starting the moment an incident is logged, with escalating alerts as the 24-48 hour window closes.
**Skill fit:** State foster-home licensing cycles, Adam Walsh Act background-check requirements, and ICPC placement rules are public, structured child-welfare regulatory data with a well-defined per-home and per-caseworker record model, and the ratio monitor plus the incident-reporting countdown are the same deterministic "flag the threshold / compute the deadline from the trigger event" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a child-welfare trade most small agencies currently track through a shared spreadsheet with no systematic ratio alert.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the home-license tracker, the caseworker-ratio monitor, and the incident-reporting countdown; land the first 5-8 agencies through National Foster Parent Association and state foster care & adoption association outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — state child-placing agency associations and the Child Welfare League of America's (CWLA) member directory, where small independent agencies already juggle per-home license renewals and caseworker ratios with no dedicated compliance software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $349/mo base (up to 40 licensed foster homes) + $6/mo per additional home. Average client (90 homes, 50 over base) = $349 + 50×$6 = $649/mo. 14-18 agencies × $649/mo = **$9,086-11,682/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every foster home under supervision carries its own independent license-renewal clock, every caseworker's home count shifts as placements move, and every incident reopens its own reporting countdown — a continuous, per-home and per-caseworker compliance stream tied directly to the agency's placement volume.
**Biggest risk:** A caseworker's ratio quietly exceeding the state ceiling, or a critical incident reported past the 24-48 hour window, is exactly the finding that draws a licensing violation with a child's safety as the direct stake — the ratio monitor and the incident countdown are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → home-license tracker, ratio monitor, and incident countdown deployed, first 5-8 agency retainers through NFPA and CWLA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 9-11 agencies. Replaces-full-time-salary → 14-18 agencies, pushing past $9.09-11.68K/mo.

### 4. Polygraph Examiner State Licensing, APA Chart-Retention & EPPA Exam-Record Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Polygraph examiners in the roughly 29 states that regulate the profession operate under a dedicated state polygraph board or licensing bureau (such as the Texas Polygraph Examiners Board under Occupations Code Chapter 1703), which requires a supervised internship under a licensed examiner before independent licensure and fixed continuing-education hours for renewal. Layered on top, the American Polygraph Association (APA) sets chart- and exam-record retention standards examiners must follow to keep their APA credential current, and the federal Employee Polygraph Protection Act (EPPA, 29 U.S.C. §2001) bars most private employers from polygraph-testing employees except narrow exemptions — security-service firms, controlled-substance handlers, and employers conducting an ongoing workplace-theft investigation. Every EPPA-exempt exam requires the examiner to deliver statutorily specific written notice to the examinee before testing and to retain the exam record for a minimum multi-year period under EPPA's implementing regulations. An examiner who runs an EPPA-exempt exam without the required notice, or who can't produce a retained chart when a state board audit or a legal discovery request comes in, is exactly the finding that draws a licensing complaint or invalidates the exam's legal standing entirely. A solo or small-practice examiner running 20-50 exams a month across pre-employment security screening, ongoing-investigation exams, and private client work manages licensing renewal dates, EPPA notice compliance, and chart retention with a filing cabinet and a paper case log, with no systematic link between a specific exam's EPPA-exemption category and whether the required notice was actually delivered before testing.
**What to build:** A per-exam EPPA-exemption classifier gating test administration until the required written notice is confirmed delivered; a chart and exam-record retention tracker enforcing the multi-year minimum with searchable case-ID lookup; and a state license and APA continuing-education renewal tracker.
**Skill fit:** EPPA's exemption categories and notice requirements, plus state polygraph-board licensing cycles, are public, structured federal and state regulatory text with a well-defined per-exam record model, and the exemption gate plus the retention tracker are the same deterministic "classify the trigger category / retain the record for the mandated period" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an investigative-credentialing trade most solo examiners currently track through a filing cabinet with no systematic link between an exam's legal basis and its notice compliance.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the EPPA-exemption gate, the chart-retention tracker, and the license/CE renewal tracker; land the first 8-12 examiners through American Polygraph Association (APA) member outreach and state polygraph examiner association chapters.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the APA's member directory and state polygraph association meetings, where solo and small-practice examiners already juggle EPPA notice requirements and chart retention with no dedicated compliance software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $79/mo base (up to 15 exams/mo) + $4/mo per additional exam. Average client (40 exams/mo, 25 over base) = $79 + 25×$4 = $179/mo. 51-68 examiners × $179/mo = **$9,129-12,172/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every exam scheduled reopens its own EPPA-exemption classification and notice-compliance check, and every completed chart adds to a retention queue that never shrinks until the statutory period expires — a continuous, per-exam compliance stream tied directly to an examiner's monthly caseload.
**Biggest risk:** An EPPA-exempt exam run without the required written notice, or a chart that can't be produced on audit, is exactly the gap that voids the exam's legal standing and draws a licensing complaint — the exemption gate and the retention tracker are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → EPPA gate, chart-retention tracker, and license/CE tracker deployed, first 8-12 examiner retainers through APA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 30-38 examiners. Replaces-full-time-salary → 51-68 examiners, pushing past $9.13-12.17K/mo.

### 5. Manufactured & Modular Home Retailer State Dealer License, HUD Installation & Warranty-Response Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Manufactured and modular home retailers sell homes built to HUD's Manufactured Home Construction and Safety Standards (24 CFR Part 3280, the "HUD Code"), with site setup governed by HUD's Model Manufactured Home Installation Standards (24 CFR Part 3285) or a state's own HUD-approved installation program — requiring a separately licensed installer for every home placed, distinct from the dealer's own state manufactured-housing board license and per-location surety bond. State manufactured-housing acts also mandate specific consumer purchase-agreement disclosures at time of sale and, in many states, a statutory window — commonly around 20 days — within which the dealer must initiate manufacturer warranty service after a customer reports a defect. A dealer that sells a home without a licensed installer assigned, misses a purchase-agreement disclosure, or lets a warranty-service request sit past the statutory response window is exactly the finding a state manufactured-housing board audit or a consumer complaint investigation is built to catch, and warranty-response violations are a routine source of state enforcement actions against small dealers. A small independent manufactured-home retailer running one or two sales lots manages installer assignment, disclosure paperwork, and warranty-response deadlines with a paper sales-file folder and no systematic countdown from a defect report to the statutory response window.
**What to build:** A per-sale installer-assignment tracker confirming a licensed installer is assigned before a home ships to site; a purchase-agreement disclosure checklist gating sale completion until every state-mandated disclosure is on file; and a warranty-service response-deadline countdown starting the moment a defect is reported, with escalating alerts as the statutory window closes.
**Skill fit:** HUD Code installation standards and state manufactured-housing disclosure and warranty-response rules are public, structured federal and state regulatory text with a well-defined per-sale record model, and the disclosure checklist plus the warranty countdown are the same deterministic "gate the action until the requirement clears / compute the deadline from the trigger event" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a housing-retail trade most small dealers currently track through a paper sales file with no systematic countdown from a defect report to the statutory response deadline.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the installer-assignment tracker, the disclosure checklist, and the warranty-response countdown; land the first 6-9 dealers through Manufactured Housing Institute (MHI) state-affiliate outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — state manufactured housing associations (such as the Texas Manufactured Housing Association) and MHI's dealer-member directory, where small independent lots already juggle installer licensing and warranty-response deadlines with no dedicated compliance software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249/mo base (up to 15 active warranty-service cases/mo) + $10/mo per additional case. Average client (35 cases/mo, 20 over base) = $249 + 20×$10 = $449/mo. 20-27 dealers × $449/mo = **$8,980-12,123/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every home sold reopens its own installer-assignment and disclosure check, and every warranty defect reported starts its own independent response-deadline countdown — a continuous, per-sale compliance stream tied directly to a dealer's sales and service volume rather than a single annual license renewal.
**Biggest risk:** A warranty-service request left past the statutory response window, or a home installed without a licensed installer of record, is exactly the finding that draws a state enforcement action — the installer-assignment tracker and the warranty countdown are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → installer-assignment tracker, disclosure checklist, and warranty countdown deployed, first 6-9 dealer retainers through MHI outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 13-16 dealers. Replaces-full-time-salary → 20-27 dealers, pushing past $8.98-12.12K/mo.
