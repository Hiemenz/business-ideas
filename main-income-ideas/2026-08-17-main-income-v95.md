### 1. Tattoo & Permanent Cosmetics Studio Body-Art Licensure, Bloodborne Pathogen & Autoclave Spore-Testing Compliance
**Problem:** Tattoo, piercing, and permanent-cosmetics/microblading studios operate under state and county body-art licensing statutes — California's Safe Body Art Act (Health & Safety Code §119300 et seq.), Oregon's OAR 333-015, and Texas DSHS Chapter 146 among them — requiring both a facility license and an individual artist registration or certificate renewed annually, each tied to proof of current bloodborne pathogen (BBP) training under OSHA's Bloodborne Pathogens Standard (29 CFR 1910.1030), which mandates initial and annual refresher training for anyone with occupational exposure to blood; an artist working past a lapsed registration, or one without current BBP training on file, is exactly the finding a health-department inspection targets first. Every steam autoclave sterilizer used to reprocess reusable equipment must pass a periodic biological (spore) test — commonly weekly, and every load in some states — with the result documented and retained for the period the health department can request during an inspection, and a spore test that fails, or is simply missing from the studio's records, is exactly the finding that can suspend the studio's ability to legally sterilize equipment and trigger an immediate closure order. Every single-use needle and sharps item must be logged and its disposal documented through a sharps-disposal manifest, and permanent-cosmetics/microblading in several states requires its own separate license category with its own CE and renewal cycle distinct from a standard tattoo license. A studio running 4-8 artists across tattoo, piercing, and permanent-cosmetics services manages per-artist BBP training/registration currency, weekly autoclave spore-test documentation, and sharps-disposal manifest tracking with no dedicated compliance function beyond a logbook next to the sterilizer.
**What to build:** A per-artist license/registration and BBP training-currency tracker with a "cleared to work" gate; a weekly (or per-load) autoclave spore-test log flagging any missed or failed test before equipment can be marked sterile-ready; and a sharps-disposal manifest and consent-form archive tied to each client procedure.
**Skill fit:** State body-art statutes and OSHA's BBP training cycle are public, structured regulatory data with a well-defined per-artist and per-sterilizer record requirement, and the work-eligibility gate plus the spore-test log are the same deterministic "block the action until the credential clears / require the periodic test result before the equipment is marked usable" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a body-art trade most small studios currently track in a logbook next to the autoclave with no systematic cross-check between a specific artist's training currency and today's client schedule.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the artist license/BBP tracker with the work-eligibility gate, the spore-test log, and the sharps/consent archive; land the first 5-8 studios through Alliance of Professional Tattooists (APT) and Society of Permanent Cosmetic Professionals (SPCP) outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — APT's studio-owner member network and SPCP's permanent-cosmetics practitioner directory, where small studios already juggle per-artist BBP renewal and weekly spore testing with no dedicated compliance software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $15/mo per artist tracked. Average client (6 artists) = $199 + $90 = $289/mo. 32-40 studios × $289/mo = **$9,248-11,560/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new client procedure logs its own sharps/consent record, every sterilizer load reopens the spore-test cycle, and every artist's BBP training and license renew on independent annual clocks — a continuous, per-artist and per-load compliance stream tied directly to the studio's daily appointment volume.
**Biggest risk:** A studio using equipment sterilized by an autoclave with no current passing spore test, or an artist working past a lapsed BBP/registration renewal, is exactly the finding a health-department inspection is built to catch — the work-eligibility gate and the spore-test log are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → artist license/BBP tracker, spore-test log, and sharps/consent archive deployed, first 5-8 studio retainers through APT/SPCP outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 16-20 studios. Replaces-full-time-salary → 32-40 studios, pushing past $9.25-11.56K/mo.

### 2. Auctioneer & Auction House State Licensing, Surety Bond & Consignor Trust Account Compliance
**Problem:** Auctioneers and auction houses operate under state auctioneer licensing boards — the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation, the Ohio Auctioneers Commission, and the Tennessee Auctioneer Commission among the roughly 30 states that license the trade — requiring every practicing auctioneer to hold a current license backed by a surety bond (commonly $10,000-$50,000 depending on the state) and complete continuing-education hours on an annual or biennial renewal cycle before conducting a licensed sale, and an auctioneer who calls a sale on a lapsed license or an expired bond is exactly the finding a state licensing-board complaint or field audit is built to catch, exposing the auctioneer to fines and, on repeat violations, license revocation. Every consignment auction — estate sales, farm equipment, benefit auctions — requires the auction house to hold consignor proceeds in a segregated trust or escrow account until settlement, with a documented per-consignor accounting of hammer price, buyer's premium, commission, and net proceeds owed, and a commingled or unreconciled trust account is exactly the fiduciary-breach finding a state audit targets, with several states requiring an annual trust-account reconciliation filed with the licensing board. A small auction house running a rotating calendar of a dozen or more consignment sales a year manages per-auctioneer license/bond currency, per-sale consignor trust reconciliation, and settlement-deadline tracking — many states require consignor payout within a statutory window, commonly 30 days after the sale — with no dedicated compliance function beyond a bookkeeper's spreadsheet.
**What to build:** A per-auctioneer license and bond renewal tracker with a "cleared to call" gate; a per-sale consignor trust-account ledger reconciling hammer price, commission, and net proceeds per consignor; and a settlement-deadline tracker computing each state's statutory payout window from the sale date.
**Skill fit:** State auctioneer licensing cycles and consignor trust-accounting rules are public, structured regulatory data with a well-defined per-license and per-consignor record requirement, and the call-eligibility gate plus the trust-reconciliation ledger are the same deterministic "block the action until the credential clears / cross-check two records that must match" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an auction trade most small houses currently track on a bookkeeper's spreadsheet with no systematic cross-check between a specific auctioneer's license status and today's sale calendar.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the license/bond tracker with the call-eligibility gate, the trust-reconciliation ledger, and the settlement-deadline tracker; land the first 5-8 auction houses through National Auctioneers Association (NAA) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — NAA's member directory and state auctioneer association meetings, where small auction houses already juggle per-consignor trust reconciliation and statutory payout deadlines with no dedicated compliance software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249/mo base + $20/mo per active consignment sale per month tracked. Average client (6 sales/mo) = $249 + $120 = $369/mo. 25-32 auction houses × $369/mo = **$9,225-11,808/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every consignment sale opens its own trust-reconciliation and settlement-deadline event, and every auctioneer's license and bond renew on their own independent cycle — a continuous, per-sale compliance stream tied directly to the house's auction calendar.
**Biggest risk:** A commingled or unreconciled consignor trust account is exactly the fiduciary-breach finding a state audit is built to catch, and can strip the auction house's license outright — the trust-reconciliation ledger is the core protection.
**Growth path:** Side project → license/bond tracker, trust-reconciliation ledger, and settlement tracker deployed, first 5-8 auction-house retainers through NAA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 14-17 houses. Replaces-full-time-salary → 25-32 houses, pushing past $9.23-11.81K/mo.

### 3. Vehicle Towing & Storage-Lot Operator Non-Consent Tow Notification & Lien-Sale Compliance
**Problem:** Towing and vehicle-storage-facility operators handling non-consent (police-ordered, private-property, and abandoned-vehicle) tows operate under state towing/vehicle-storage-facility licensing — Texas TDLR's Vehicle Storage Facility license and California's CHP tow-rotation program among them — which require the operator to notify the registered owner and any lienholder of record of the tow within a strict statutory window, commonly 24-48 hours by mail or electronic lookup, and separately notify the state DMV within its own deadline; a notification sent late, or never sent, is exactly the finding a consumer complaint or state licensing-board audit is built to catch, and can force the operator to waive the entire storage-fee bill for that vehicle. Once a vehicle goes unclaimed past the state's minimum storage period — commonly 15-30 days depending on vehicle value and state — the operator must follow a statutory lien-sale process: public notice published in a newspaper of record, a certified-mail notice to the owner/lienholder, and a minimum waiting period before the vehicle can be sold at public auction to satisfy the storage lien, and a lien sale conducted without a documented, timely notice is exactly the procedural defect that voids the sale and exposes the operator to a conversion lawsuit from the vehicle's owner. A small tow operator running one or two lots and a rotating fleet of trucks manages per-tow notification-deadline tracking, per-vehicle minimum-storage-period countdowns, and lien-sale notice/publication sequencing with no dedicated compliance function beyond a paper impound log taped to the office wall.
**What to build:** A per-tow notification-deadline tracker computing the owner/lienholder and DMV notice due dates from the tow timestamp, with a "notice sent" confirmation field; a per-vehicle minimum-storage-period countdown flagging when a vehicle becomes lien-sale eligible; and a lien-sale notice/publication sequencer tracking the certified-mail and newspaper-publication steps required before an auction date can be set.
**Skill fit:** State towing and vehicle-storage-facility statutes are public, structured regulatory data with a well-defined per-tow, per-vehicle deadline requirement, and the notification tracker plus the lien-sale sequencer are the same deterministic "compute the deadline from the trigger event / require the documented step before the next stage proceeds" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a towing trade most small operators currently track on a paper impound log with no systematic cross-check between a specific vehicle's tow timestamp and its notification deadline.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the notification-deadline tracker, the storage-period countdown, and the lien-sale sequencer; land the first 5-8 operators through Towing and Recovery Association of America (TRAA) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — TRAA's member directory and state towing association meetings, where small tow operators already juggle per-tow notification deadlines and lien-sale sequencing with no dedicated compliance software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $5/mo per vehicle currently in storage tracked. Average client (40 vehicles in storage) = $199 + $200 = $399/mo. 24-30 operators × $399/mo = **$9,576-11,970/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every tow opens its own notification-deadline clock, every stored vehicle reopens its own minimum-storage countdown, and every lien sale requires its own notice/publication sequence — a continuous, per-vehicle compliance stream tied directly to the lot's daily tow volume.
**Biggest risk:** A lien sale conducted without a documented, timely owner/lienholder notice is exactly the procedural defect that voids the sale and exposes the operator to a conversion lawsuit — the notification tracker and the lien-sale sequencer are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → notification tracker, storage countdown, and lien-sale sequencer deployed, first 5-8 operator retainers through TRAA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 13-16 operators. Replaces-full-time-salary → 24-30 operators, pushing past $9.58-11.97K/mo.

### 4. Third-Party Debt Collection Agency State License & FDCPA/Regulation F Compliance
**Problem:** Independent third-party debt collection agencies operate under state collection-agency licensing statutes — most states require a license and surety bond, and larger agencies collecting across state lines must track a separate license, bond amount, and renewal date per state — layered with the federal Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (15 U.S.C. § 1692) and the CFPB's Regulation F, which since 2021 imposes a documented "7-in-7" call-frequency limit (no more than seven calls to a consumer about a specific debt within a seven-day period), a required validation-notice disclosure sent within five days of first contact, and an itemization-date disclosure that must accompany every notice; an agency that places an eighth call within the 7-day window on a specific account, or one that can't produce a documented validation notice for an account under CFPB inquiry, is exactly the finding a CFPB examination or a consumer FDCPA lawsuit is built to catch, and statutory damages plus attorneys' fees attach per violation regardless of actual harm. A small agency running a portfolio of thousands of active accounts across multiple states manages per-state license/bond renewal, per-account call-frequency compliance, and validation-notice/itemization-disclosure documentation with no dedicated compliance function beyond the collection software's own generic call log.
**What to build:** A per-state license and bond renewal tracker with a "cleared to collect" gate per state; a per-account rolling 7-day call-frequency counter blocking a call attempt once the account hits the federal cap; and a validation-notice/itemization-disclosure documentation tracker confirming the required notice was sent within the 5-day window for every new account.
**Skill fit:** State collection-agency licensing cycles and Regulation F's call-frequency and disclosure-timing rules are public, structured federal and state regulatory data with a well-defined per-account, per-state record requirement, and the call-frequency counter plus the collect-eligibility gate are the same deterministic "compute the rolling count against a threshold / block the action until the credential clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a collections trade most small agencies currently track through generic call-log software with no systematic cross-check between a specific account's call count and today's dialer activity.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the state license tracker, the 7-in-7 call-frequency counter, and the validation-notice tracker; land the first 5-8 agencies through ACA International member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — ACA International's (Association of Credit and Collection Professionals) member directory and state collection-agency association meetings, where small agencies already juggle Regulation F's call-frequency limits across thousands of accounts with no dedicated compliance software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $349/mo base + $0.15/mo per active account tracked. Average client (4,000 active accounts) = $349 + $600 = $949/mo. 10-13 agencies × $949/mo = **$9,490-12,337/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new account opens its own validation-notice clock and its own rolling 7-day call-frequency window that resets continuously, and every state license renews on its own independent cycle — a continuous, per-account compliance stream tied directly to the agency's active portfolio.
**Biggest risk:** An eighth call placed within a 7-day window on a specific account, or a missing validation notice, is exactly the finding a CFPB exam or an FDCPA lawsuit is built to catch — the call-frequency counter and the validation-notice tracker are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → state license tracker, call-frequency counter, and validation-notice tracker deployed, first 5-8 agency retainers through ACA International outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 6-8 agencies. Replaces-full-time-salary → 10-13 agencies, pushing past $9.49-12.34K/mo.

### 5. Process Serving Agency State Certification, Proof-of-Service Affidavit & Court Deadline Compliance
**Problem:** Independent process serving agencies operate under state and county certification/registration requirements — California Business & Professions Code §22350 et seq. requires registered process servers in counties over 40,000 population, Florida requires county-level certified process server appointment by the chief judge, and several states impose no certification at all but still enforce strict service-method and affidavit rules — renewed on their own cycle, often tied to a surety bond and, in California, fingerprinting through the county sheriff. Every completed service requires a proof-of-service affidavit detailing the date, time, location, method of service, and description of the person served, filed with the court within a statutory window that varies by case type and jurisdiction — commonly 5-20 days after service, and often tied to a hard trial or hearing date that can't move — and an affidavit filed late, or one missing a required detail, is exactly the finding that can get a case dismissed for improper service or force a costly re-service, landing squarely on the process server's reputation with the law firm that hired them. When a defendant can't be found for personal service, most states require a documented "due diligence" log of attempted service dates, times, and locations before a court will authorize substitute or publication service, and a due-diligence log missing even one attempt is exactly the gap a judge reviewing a motion for alternative service is built to reject. A small agency running a rotating pool of a dozen or more servers across multiple law-firm clients manages per-server certification/bond renewal, per-job proof-of-service affidavit deadlines tied to case-specific court dates, and due-diligence attempt logging with no dedicated compliance function beyond a paper job ticket per assignment.
**What to build:** A per-server state/county certification and bond renewal tracker with a "cleared to serve" gate; a per-job proof-of-service affidavit deadline tracker computing the court-filing due date from the case type and service date; and a due-diligence attempt log per job capturing every attempted-service date, time, and location for substitute/publication-service motions.
**Skill fit:** State and county process-server certification cycles and court affidavit-filing windows are public, structured regulatory and procedural data with a well-defined per-server, per-job record requirement, and the serve-eligibility gate plus the affidavit-deadline tracker are the same deterministic "block the action until the credential clears / compute the deadline from the trigger event" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a legal-support trade most small agencies currently track on a paper job ticket with no systematic cross-check between a specific server's certification status and today's assignment queue.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the certification/bond tracker with the serve-eligibility gate, the affidavit-deadline tracker, and the due-diligence log; land the first 5-8 agencies through National Association of Professional Process Servers (NAPPS) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — NAPPS's member directory and state process-server association meetings, where small agencies already juggle per-job affidavit-filing deadlines tied to hard court dates with no dedicated compliance software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $3/mo per completed service job tracked per month. Average client (250 jobs/mo) = $199 + $750 = $949/mo. 10-13 agencies × $949/mo = **$9,490-12,337/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new assignment opens its own proof-of-service affidavit deadline tied to that case's court date, every failed attempt reopens the due-diligence log, and every server's certification renews on its own independent cycle — a continuous, per-job compliance stream tied directly to the agency's assignment volume.
**Biggest risk:** A proof-of-service affidavit filed late, or a due-diligence log missing an attempt, is exactly the finding that can get a case dismissed for improper service — the affidavit-deadline tracker and the due-diligence log are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → certification/bond tracker, affidavit-deadline tracker, and due-diligence log deployed, first 5-8 agency retainers through NAPPS outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 6-8 agencies. Replaces-full-time-salary → 10-13 agencies, pushing past $9.49-12.34K/mo.
