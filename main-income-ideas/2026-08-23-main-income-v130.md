### 1. Life Settlement Provider & Broker Multi-State Licensing, NAIC Disclosure & Escrow-Release Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Life settlement providers and brokers who buy and sell in-force life insurance policies for a lump-sum payout to the policy owner operate under a state Life Settlement Act — adopted in some form by roughly 43 states, most based on the NAIC Life Settlements Model Act #697 — which requires a separate provider license and a separate broker license (distinct from an ordinary insurance producer license) in every state where the firm transacts business, each renewed on its own state-specific cycle. Before a transaction can close, the policy owner must receive a mandatory set of disclosures — the settlement's tax consequences, its possible effect on public-assistance eligibility, and available alternatives to selling the policy — and then has a statutory rescission window, commonly 15-30 days, during which they can void the transaction outright; a provider who releases purchase funds or completes the ownership transfer before that window has run has violated the statute's core consumer-protection mechanism, exactly the finding a state insurance department market-conduct exam is built to catch. Purchase funds must also be held in escrow and released to the policy owner only within a strict window — commonly 3 business days — after the issuing insurance carrier confirms the ownership and beneficiary change, and a fund release that jumps the gun before carrier confirmation, or a rescission-window violation, can void the transaction and trigger regulatory discipline against both the provider and broker license. A small life settlement brokerage transacting across a dozen or more states manages staggered provider/broker license renewals, per-transaction rescission-window tracking, and escrow-release timing against carrier confirmation on a mix of a case manager's spreadsheet and a filing cabinet of signed disclosure forms, with no systematic cross-check flagging which in-flight transactions are still inside their mandatory rescission period before funds can be released.

**What to build:** A per-state provider/broker license renewal tracker computing each state's specific renewal date; a per-transaction rescission-window tracker computing the exact deadline from the disclosure-delivery date and blocking fund release or ownership-transfer completion until the window has cleared; an escrow-release tracker gated on the insurance carrier's confirmed ownership/beneficiary change; and an annual state Department of Insurance reporting tracker for every state requiring settlement-volume disclosure.

**Skill fit:** State Life Settlement Acts and the NAIC Model Act's disclosure and rescission-window requirements are public, structured legal text with concrete day-count deadlines, and the rescission-window-block-release pattern plus the escrow-release gate are the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance clock clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a financial-services niche this repo has never addressed.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the multi-state license tracker, the per-transaction rescission-window/escrow-release gate, and the annual DOI reporting tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent life settlement brokerages.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Life Insurance Settlement Association (LISA) runs an active member directory and annual conference reaching exactly the small independent providers and brokers currently tracking staggered multi-state licensing and per-transaction rescission windows by hand.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per firm depending on multi-state license count and transaction volume. 25-32 firms × $429/mo avg = $10,725-13,728/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every state license renews independently on its own cycle, every transaction opens a fresh rescission-and-escrow-release clock regardless of the firm's overall volume, and every new state the firm expands into adds a fresh licensing obligation — a continuous, transaction-and-footprint-driven compliance stream tied directly to the firm's ability to keep closing settlements legally.

**Biggest risk:** A small brokerage transacting in only one or two states has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need a spreadsheet can still handle. Mitigate by qualifying leads for firms licensed and transacting across eight or more states, where the staggered renewal-and-rescission calendar genuinely outgrows manual tracking.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 firms ($5.1K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 22 firms ($9.4K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 25-32 firms plus a secondary-market policy-tracking add-on for firms that also purchase settled policies for portfolio resale.

### 2. Small-Batch Bottled Water & Water Bottling Plant Operator FDA 21 CFR Part 129 & State Health Department Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent bottled water plants — spring-water bottlers, purified/reverse-osmosis municipal-source bottlers, and private-label co-packers — operate under FDA's bottling-plant cGMP standard (21 CFR Part 129) and the bottled-water standard of identity (21 CFR Part 165), which together require an approved source (with annual re-testing for spring or well sources), a weekly coliform test on the finished product, and a periodic full contaminant panel mirroring EPA's National Primary Drinking Water Regulations, with the specific testing interval varying by contaminant class (some annual, some on a multi-year rotation) — a finished-product lot bottled and shipped without its current-cycle test results on file is exactly the finding an FDA or state inspection is built to catch, and a positive coliform result on an unreported lot is a recall-triggering event. Every plant must also maintain FDA Food Facility Registration, renewed on a biennial cycle under FSMA, and a written Preventive Controls for Human Food (HARPC) plan under 21 CFR Part 117, on top of a state health department bottling-plant permit subject to its own inspection cycle, and many retail chains additionally require third-party model-code certification through the International Bottled Water Association (IBWA) as a shelf-placement condition — a certification with its own audit and renewal cycle layered on top of the federal and state requirements. A small independent bottler running a single line or a handful of private-label SKUs manages source-water re-testing, weekly coliform logs, the staggered full-panel testing schedule by contaminant class, FDA registration renewal, and IBWA certification currency on a mix of a lab-report folder and a production spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a given production lot is actually cleared to ship before it leaves the plant.

**What to build:** A source-and-finished-product testing tracker computing each contaminant class's next-due test date and the weekly coliform cycle, with a "cleared to ship" gate blocking any lot from being marked shippable until its current test results are on file; an FDA Food Facility Registration biennial renewal tracker with the HARPC plan attached; a state bottling-plant permit renewal tracker; and an IBWA model-code certification tracker for plants supplying retail accounts that require it.

**Skill fit:** FDA's bottled-water testing intervals and EPA's contaminant-class schedule are public, structured federal regulatory data with concrete per-contaminant testing cadences, and the per-lot "cleared to ship" gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a packaged-beverage manufacturing niche distinct from every alcohol-production or third-party environmental-lab idea already in this repo.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the source/finished-product testing tracker with the ship-eligibility gate, the FDA registration/HARPC tracker, and the IBWA certification tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent bottled water plants and private-label co-packers.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the International Bottled Water Association (IBWA) runs an active member directory and annual convention reaching exactly the small independent bottlers currently reconciling weekly coliform logs and staggered full-panel testing against a production spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249-449/mo per plant depending on source count and SKU volume. 30-40 plants × $339/mo avg = $10,170-13,560/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** The weekly coliform cycle and staggered full-contaminant-panel testing recur continuously for the life of the plant, FDA facility registration renews on its own biennial federal clock, and every new source or private-label SKU the plant adds opens a fresh testing-and-registration obligation — a continuous, production-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the plant's ability to keep shipping product.

**Biggest risk:** A very small bottler running a single source and one SKU has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need a lab-report folder can still handle. Mitigate by qualifying leads for plants running multiple sources or private-label SKUs, where the staggered testing-and-registration calendar genuinely outgrows manual tracking.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 plants ($4.1K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 26 plants ($8.8K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 30-40 plants plus a retailer-facing IBWA certification-status export for plants selling into major grocery chains.

### 3. Wind Farm Operations & Maintenance (Wind Turbine Technician) Company GWO Safety Training & OSHA Confined-Space/Rescue Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Wind farm operations & maintenance companies whose technicians climb inside turbine towers and nacelles operate under the Global Wind Organisation's (GWO) Basic Safety Training (BST) — a five-module credential covering Working at Heights, First Aid, Manual Handling, Fire Awareness, and, for offshore work, Sea Survival — which every technician must renew on a mandatory 24-month cycle before they can be dispatched to climb, layered with OSHA's permit-required confined-space entry standard (29 CFR 1910.146) governing nacelle and tower access, which requires a documented rescue plan and a currently rescue-certified technician physically on site before any climb can begin; a technician dispatched on a lapsed GWO credential, or a climb started without a rescue-qualified technician present, is exactly the finding an OSHA inspection or a post-incident investigation is built to catch, and a rescue-readiness gap discovered mid-climb — hundreds of feet up a tower with no qualified rescuer on the ground — is one of the most severe possible failure modes in the trade. Every harness, lanyard, and piece of fall-protection equipment also carries a manufacturer-specified periodic inspection and service-life limit, and gear used past its inspection date or retirement point is a separate OSHA fall-protection citation waiting to happen. A small O&M company running several technician crews across multiple wind farm service contracts manages staggered per-technician GWO 24-month renewal dates, per-climb rescue-readiness confirmation, and per-harness inspection/retirement tracking on a mix of a dispatcher's spreadsheet and a filing cabinet of training certificates, with no systematic cross-check confirming a crew dispatched to a turbine today is actually cleared to climb.

**What to build:** A per-technician GWO BST/BTT 24-month renewal tracker with a "cleared to climb" dispatch gate; a per-climb rescue-plan and rescue-technician-on-site checker blocking a confined-space entry from being logged as authorized until a qualified rescuer is confirmed present; and a per-harness/lanyard inspection log tracking each item's manufacturer-specified service life and flagging any unit approaching retirement.

**Skill fit:** GWO's 24-month certification-renewal structure and OSHA's confined-space rescue-readiness requirement are published, structured standards-body and federal regulatory data, and the cleared-to-climb dispatch gate plus the per-climb rescue-readiness check are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a wind-energy service trade distinct from the residential/commercial solar installer idea already in this repo, whose NABCEP interconnection-permitting framework never touches a technician's own climbing certification or a confined-space rescue plan at all.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the GWO renewal tracker with the climb-eligibility gate, the per-climb rescue-readiness checker, and the harness inspection/retirement log. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent wind farm O&M companies.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the American Clean Power Association (ACP) runs an active member directory and annual conference reaching exactly the small independent O&M companies currently tracking staggered 24-month GWO renewals and per-climb rescue readiness across a growing technician roster without dedicated compliance software.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249-499/mo per company depending on technician headcount and turbine count serviced. 28-36 companies × $369/mo avg = $10,332-13,284/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every technician's GWO certification renews independently on its own 24-month clock, every turbine climb opens a fresh rescue-readiness check regardless of how routine the job is, and every new wind farm service contract the company wins adds a fresh technician-and-climb-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the company's ability to keep dispatching crews legally.

**Biggest risk:** A very small company running two or three technicians on a single wind farm contract has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads for companies servicing multiple wind farms or utility O&M contracts with a growing technician roster, where the staggered 24-month renewal calendar genuinely outgrows manual tracking.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 companies ($4.4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 24 companies ($8.9K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 28-36 companies plus a client utility-facing safety-audit-readiness export for companies renewing multi-farm service contracts.

### 4. Horse Racing Trainer & Stable Operator HISA Registration & State Racing Commission Medication/Licensing Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Horse racing trainers and stable operators must hold both a state racing commission trainer license — renewed on that state's own annual or biennial cycle — and, since 2022, a separate federal registration under the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Act (HISA), administered by the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Authority under FTC oversight; a horse entered to race under a trainer whose HISA registration or state license has lapsed is scratched or disqualified the moment the gap surfaces, exactly the finding a pre-race credential check is built to catch. Under HISA's Anti-Doping and Medication Control (ADMC) Program, every therapeutic substance administered to a horse carries its own specific detection and withdrawal time before that horse may be entered to race, and the treatment itself must be logged into HISA's tracking system within a strict reporting window — commonly 24-48 hours — of administration; a horse entered before its most recent treatment's withdrawal period has elapsed, or a treatment never logged within the reporting window, is exactly the fact pattern a pre-race inspection or a post-race sample is built to catch, and a positive test traced back to a missed withdrawal calculation can mean disqualification, purse forfeiture, and a suspension that shuts the trainer's whole stable out of racing. A small stable running a couple dozen horses across multiple tracks and states manages staggered state license renewals, HISA registration currency, and per-horse medication-withdrawal deadlines against every treatment logged for every animal — usually on a barn manager's paper treatment log and a memory of which horse is coming off a recent therapeutic dose — and a horse entered to race before its withdrawal window cleared, or a treatment never logged into HISA within the reporting deadline, is exactly the gap a stewards' inquiry or a HISA compliance review is built to catch.

**What to build:** A HISA federal registration and state racing commission trainer license renewal tracker with a "cleared to enter" gate; a per-horse medication and treatment log computing each administered substance's specific withdrawal deadline and blocking a race-entry from being logged until the window has cleared; and a treatment-reporting-window tracker flagging any administered substance not yet logged into HISA within the required 24-48 hour window.

**Skill fit:** HISA's ADMC substance list and withdrawal times, and state racing commission license-renewal cycles, are public, structured regulatory data with concrete numeric deadlines, and the per-horse "cleared to enter" gate is the same deterministic "verify the rule before the action proceeds" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a professional horse racing compliance surface entirely distinct from every recreational riding, farrier, or equine-dental idea already in this repo.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the HISA/state license tracker with the entry-eligibility gate, the per-horse medication-withdrawal tracker, and the treatment-reporting-window flag. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent racing stables.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the National Horsemen's Benevolent and Protective Association (National HBPA) runs active state-chapter networks reaching exactly the small independent stables currently tracking staggered HISA registration, state licensing, and per-horse withdrawal deadlines on a barn manager's paper log.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-399/mo per stable depending on horse count and multi-track footprint. 35-45 stables × $299/mo avg = $10,465-13,455/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every horse's medication log recurs with every fresh treatment regardless of stable size, every race entry re-triggers the withdrawal-and-registration eligibility check, and HISA's still-expanding federal registration and reporting requirements keep adding compliance surface as more states and tracks fall under its jurisdiction — a continuous, roster-and-entry-driven compliance stream tied directly to the stable's ability to keep racing legally.

**Biggest risk:** A very small stable racing two or three horses at a single home-state track has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads for stables running horses across multiple tracks and states, where the staggered per-horse withdrawal-and-entry calendar genuinely outgrows a paper barn log.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 stables ($4.5K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 30 stables ($9K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 35-45 stables plus a racing-secretary-facing entry-eligibility export trainers can hand over ahead of a race-day credential check.

### 5. Theatrical & Entertainment Rigging Company ETCP Certification & Venue Load-Test/Inspection Compliance Tracker

**What to build:** A per-rigger ETCP certification renewal tracker (five-year cycle with recertification-exam or CEU documentation) with a "cleared to sign the rigging plot" gate; a per-hardware-item (motor, shackle, chain hoist) proof-load-test and inspection tracker flagging any unit approaching its due date or retirement; and a per-show rigging-plot load-calculation log confirming the venue's certified point-load capacity wasn't exceeded, tied to the certified rigger of record.

**Skill fit:** ETCP's five-year certification-renewal structure and manufacturer-specified hardware inspection intervals are published, structured credential and equipment data, and the cleared-to-sign gate plus the per-hardware-item inspection tracker are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an entertainment-rigging trade distinct from the crane operator and rigging company idea already in this repo, whose NCCCO/OSHA Subpart CC framework governs a wholly different equipment class and hazard type.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — ETCP's own certified-rigger directory (administered through PLASA North America) and the USITT (United States Institute for Theatre Technology) member network reach exactly the small independent rigging companies currently tracking staggered five-year certification renewals and per-hardware proof-load tests across a growing equipment inventory without dedicated compliance software.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every rigger's ETCP certification renews independently on its own five-year clock, every piece of rigging hardware requires ongoing periodic inspection for the life of the equipment, and every new show or tour the company books opens a fresh load-calculation-and-sign-off record — a continuous, contract-and-inventory-driven compliance stream tied directly to the company's standing with the venues and touring productions that hire it.
