- **Time to first dollar < 90 days.** Long enterprise sales cycles are disqualifying by default.
- **Named distribution channel** for the first 10 customers *and* a credible path to the next 10x.

### 1. Cost Segregation Study Coordination for Real Estate Investors
**Problem:** Real estate investors who own commercial property or residential rentals with a cost basis above $500,000 are entitled to accelerate their depreciation deductions through a cost segregation study — an engineering analysis that reclassifies building components from 39-year (commercial) or 27.5-year (residential) depreciation to 5-, 7-, or 15-year schedules, generating large first-year deductions under bonus depreciation rules. A $1M commercial building typically produces $40,000-100,000 in additional first-year deductions, saving the investor $15,000-40,000 in taxes at a 37% marginal rate. Yet the majority of qualifying investors have never done a cost segregation study because they don't know they qualify, their CPA doesn't proactively surface it, and the process of engaging a cost segregation engineering firm and coordinating the study with the CPA feels opaque. A coordination service identifies qualifying properties, sources cost segregation engineers, manages the engagement, and delivers the completed study to the CPA — earning a contingency fee on the documented first-year tax benefit.
**What to build:** A deal-flow and coordination service — a property eligibility pre-screening tool (property type, purchase date, cost basis, and bonus depreciation eligibility under current tax law), a cost segregation engineering firm network (a vetted roster of cost segregation engineers priced by property type and square footage, with negotiated referral rates that allow a margin above what's passed to the client), an engagement management workflow (coordinating the site visit or document-based analysis between the engineer and the property owner, tracking deliverables and timelines), and a CPA handoff package (formatted study output ready for the CPA to incorporate into the tax return or amended return) — charged as a contingency fee of 20-25% of the first-year tax benefit delivered.
**Skill fit:** The pre-screening eligibility analysis and deal pipeline management are structured data problems (property characteristics → eligibility score → expected benefit range is a clean model to build). The builder doesn't need to perform the engineering analysis — the cost segregation engineer does that; the builder's value is knowing which properties qualify, sourcing the right engineers at competitive rates, and managing the coordination layer that currently gets lost between the investor and their CPA.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a property eligibility screener, a cost segregation engineer outreach template to build the initial vendor roster (20-30 firms in the US specialize in cost segregation; reaching 3-5 creates a working network), and a deal tracking and CPA handoff document template; the first 2-3 engagements are managed manually while the workflow is refined.
**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — distribute through CPA firms that specialize in real estate investors (they are the natural referral source: the CPA knows exactly which clients own qualifying property, and referring to a cost segregation coordination service is a value-add the CPA doesn't have to execute themselves), Real Estate Investor Association (REIA) chapters where property owners actively discuss tax strategy, and real estate syndicators who have multiple investor clients who could each benefit.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Contingency fee of 20-25% of first-year tax benefit. A qualifying property generating $50,000 in additional first-year deductions at a 37% marginal rate = $18,500 in tax savings; 22% contingency fee = **$4,070 per engagement**. At 3-4 engagements/month = **$12,000-16,000/mo**. The deal flow is naturally seasonal (tax planning peaks in Q4 and Q1), but a pipeline of 6-8 CPA referral relationships generating 1-2 referrals per quarter per firm sustains 3-4 monthly engagements year-round.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every CPA referral relationship generates compounding referral flow — the CPA's client base doesn't churn, so a CPA who sends one referral and sees a successful study delivered becomes a recurring source of annual referrals as new qualifying clients purchase properties. The engineering network quality improves with each engagement (tracking which engineers deliver fastest and most accurate studies for each property type), creating a quality moat over a general referral service with no institutional memory.
**Biggest risk:** Contingency fee collection depends on the client actually filing the amended return or incorporating the study into their current-year return — build a clear agreement upfront that the contingency fee is owed upon delivery of the completed study regardless of when the client files, to avoid collection delays that extend the cash cycle by 12+ months.
**Growth path:** Side project → first 2-3 property eligibility assessments through REIA contacts, first CPA referral relationship established, first cost segregation study coordinated and delivered. Replaces-part-time-income → 4-6 CPA referral sources active, 1-2 engagements/month, first repeat referrals from the same CPA (~$5-9K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 6-8 CPA relationships, 3-4 engagements/month, amended return pipeline from prior-year qualifying properties, pushing past $12-16K/mo.

### 2. Section 8 / HCV Housing Compliance Management for Independent Landlords
**Problem:** The Housing Choice Voucher (HCV/Section 8) program guarantees landlords a portion of each tenant's rent paid directly by the local Housing Authority — eliminating the risk of rent nonpayment. Yet the majority of independent landlords (who own 1-4 units) avoid the HCV program because the administrative burden is genuinely high: Housing Quality Standards (HQS) inspections require repairs within defined timelines or HAP (Housing Assistance Payments) are suspended, annual recertification generates 20-30 pages of paperwork per unit, HAP contract renewals require specific forms, and coordinating directly with underfunded Housing Authority offices means calls go unanswered and deadlines are missed. A compliance management service handles the inspection scheduling, repair-deadline tracking, HAP contract paperwork, and annual recertification process on the landlord's behalf — enabling small independent landlords to participate in the guaranteed-rent program without absorbing the administrative overhead that currently makes it not worth the effort.
**What to build:** A compliance management service — a property onboarding and Housing Authority intake workflow (collecting the HAP contract terms, inspection history, and assigned Housing Authority case worker contacts), an inspection deadline tracker (flagging HQS repair timelines and tracking contractor-completion confirmations so HAP payments are never suspended), a HAP contract and recertification paperwork service (completing and submitting the annual recertification forms and any required rent reasonableness documentation on the landlord's behalf), a Housing Authority communication log (maintaining a record of every phone call, email, and form submission to each Housing Authority office so disputes over missed deadlines have documented evidence), and a per-unit monthly retainer model.
**Skill fit:** The inspection deadline tracking, Housing Authority communication logging, and recertification paperwork coordination are structured workflow and document management problems — the builder's software and data skills build a far more reliable tracking system than the spreadsheet-and-reminder approach the average small landlord uses. The regulatory layer (HUD HQS standards and HAP contract terms) is a well-defined federal framework with published guidance that maps cleanly into a compliance checklist.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build an inspection deadline tracker, a Housing Authority contact database for target metro area Housing Authorities, and a recertification paperwork template library for the 3-5 most common HAP contract types; first 3-5 client landlords onboarded manually in one metro while the workflow is validated against real HQS inspection and recertification cycles.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through local landlord association meetings (nearly every metro has an active apartment owner or rental housing association where small landlords are the membership base), property management forums (BiggerPockets, Reddit's r/Landlord, local Facebook groups for landlords), and direct outreach to landlords who advertise HCV-accepting units on Craigslist, Zillow, and Apartments.com (these landlords are already participating and know the pain firsthand).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $45-80/unit/month retainer. Building a managed portfolio of 200 HCV units at $55/unit average = **$11,000/mo**; 250 units = **$13,750/mo**. Reaching 200 managed units in a single metro market requires 50-80 landlord clients averaging 3-5 HCV units each — achievable within 12-18 months of consistent landlord association presence and referral-from-landlord-to-landlord growth (the HCV landlord community is tight-knit and shares vendor referrals actively).
**Why this can go beyond side money:** HCV tenants have longer average tenancy than market-rate tenants (moving is expensive and a new unit must pass HQS inspection before the voucher transfers, creating strong tenure incentives) — managed units don't churn out of the portfolio at market-rate rates, so the recurring revenue base is stickier than typical property management. Each metro market has a Housing Authority whose voucher-holder count is public data, providing a direct measure of the addressable managed-unit market before a single sales call is made.
**Biggest risk:** Housing Authority responsiveness varies dramatically by office — some HAs are organized and process paperwork promptly; others are understaffed and slow, creating recertification delays that land on the landlord regardless of how well the compliance service performed. Set client expectations upfront: the service controls the submission quality and timing, not the HA's processing speed, and document every submission timestamp to demonstrate the service did its part if a payment delay occurs.
**Growth path:** Side project → first 5-10 HCV units managed in one metro, inspection deadline tracker validated against real HQS cycles, first recertification submission completed. Replaces-part-time-income → 80-100 managed units across 25-35 landlord clients, HA communication patterns for the target metro documented, referral growth from existing landlord clients (~$4-7K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 200-250 units, landlord association presence generating inbound inquiries without outreach, potentially expanding to a second metro market using the same HA contact-mapping playbook, pushing past $11-14K/mo.

### 3. Commercial Energy Procurement Brokering in Deregulated Markets
**Problem:** Businesses in deregulated electricity and natural gas markets (Texas, Pennsylvania, Ohio, Illinois, New York, Massachusetts, and most of New England) are legally permitted to choose their energy supplier rather than buying from the default utility at regulated tariff rates — but the majority of small commercial businesses ($2,000-20,000/year in electricity costs) never exercise this choice because comparing supplier offers requires understanding rate structures (fixed vs. index-linked vs. block-and-index), contract terms (renewal provisions, early termination penalties, capacity charges), and the nuances of each state's regulatory framework. An independent energy broker accesses the supplier market on the commercial customer's behalf, solicits competitive offers, explains the tradeoffs, and helps the customer select and contract with a supplier — earning a residual commission from the winning supplier for every unit of energy the customer consumes for the life of the contract.
**What to build:** An energy broker business — a commercial customer intake and energy usage analysis tool (collecting 12 months of utility bills to establish the customer's usage profile, peak demand patterns, and current effective rate for comparison against supplier offers), a supplier solicitation workflow (submitting the customer's usage profile to 5-10 competing suppliers in the customer's state, collecting and normalizing quotes for comparison), a contract terms analyzer (flagging renewal auto-extension clauses, capacity charge exposure, and early termination fee structures that create downstream risk for the customer), and a contract execution and renewal tracking calendar (ensuring customers renew proactively rather than rolling to month-to-month at unfavorable spot rates when fixed contracts expire) — earning 0.001-0.003/kWh residual commission from the winning supplier for the contract term.
**Skill fit:** The usage profile analysis, supplier quote normalization, and contract term risk-flagging are structured data problems that the builder solves faster and more accurately than a generalist energy broker operating from spreadsheets. The residual commission model means income compounds as more contracts are signed without prior contracts churning — a managed book of accounts generates income for 12-36 months per contract signed with no additional work once the contract is in place.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a utility bill analysis tool (extracting usage, demand, and effective rate from uploaded bill PDFs or manual entry), a supplier quote comparison template, and a contract renewal tracking calendar; broker licensing requirements vary by state (most deregulated states have a broker registration or supplier agent certification process that takes 2-4 weeks and costs $100-500), which can be completed in parallel with early prospecting.
**Time to first $:** 45-60 days (including broker registration) — distribute through commercial real estate property managers in deregulated states (who manage operating expenses for multiple commercial tenants and benefit from helping tenants control occupancy costs), small business accounting firms (whose clients are the exact commercial customers who need energy cost management), and small business associations in the highest-activity deregulated markets (Texas ERCOT market, PJM market covering PA/OH/IL/NJ).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Commission of $0.002/kWh for a commercial customer using 50,000 kWh/month = $100/month for a 24-month contract term. Building a book of 100 contracted commercial accounts at that usage level = **$10,000/mo** in fully residual commission income. Reaching 100 accounts in a market like Texas (where nearly all commercial customers are in the deregulated ERCOT market) requires contracting roughly 4-5 new customers per month for 20-24 months — achievable through property manager and accountant referral channels without any cold consumer outreach.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** The residual commission model is pure compounding — every new contract signed adds to a base that doesn't require re-selling to maintain. Customers with 2-3 year fixed contracts renew (and re-generate broker commission) when the contract expires, making the book of accounts a durable recurring income asset rather than a per-transaction revenue stream. The Texas ERCOT market alone has hundreds of thousands of commercial customers who have never been approached by an independent broker.
**Biggest risk:** Supplier commission structures and rates change when energy market conditions shift — in a period of high energy price volatility, suppliers tighten commission payouts or refuse to quote fixed-rate contracts, compressing the broker's income. Maintain supplier relationships with 8-10 competing suppliers so a period when one supplier exits the market or tightens its commission structure doesn't eliminate the ability to quote at all.
**Growth path:** Side project → broker registration completed, first 3-5 commercial accounts contracted through property manager referrals, first supplier commissions received. Replaces-part-time-income → 30-40 contracted accounts, property manager referral channel generating 2-3 new accounts/month, residual commission base covering ~$3-5K/mo. Replaces-full-time-salary → 100+ contracted accounts, renewal pipeline from 2021-2023 contracts generating re-contracting revenue, residual base pushing past $10-15K/mo with zero additional sales cost.

### 4. Process Serving Agency for Law Firms and Collection Attorneys
**Problem:** Civil litigation requires delivering legal documents — summonses, complaints, subpoenas, restraining orders, and judgment-enforcement writs — to defendants, witnesses, and third parties in person, with a sworn affidavit of service that becomes a court exhibit. Law firms and collection attorneys use independent process serving agencies for this work rather than staff, because service requires geographic coverage, scheduling flexibility (defendants must be served at home or work, not during business hours at a law office), and careful proof-of-service documentation. The process serving agency market is fragmented: most agencies are operated by one or two people using phone-tree dispatch for their server roster, with no real-time status tracking and inconsistent affidavit formatting that creates delays when courts require re-service. A technology-enabled agency with GPS-confirmed service attempts, standardized affidavit templates, and real-time status updates to the client law firm captures accounts from legacy agencies solely on operational reliability.
**What to build:** A process serving agency — a server roster management system (tracking each server's geographic coverage, license status by state, availability, and service success rate per address type), an order intake and dispatch tool (receiving service orders from law firm clients with defendant information, address, and documents, automatically assigning to the nearest available licensed server and confirming acceptance within minutes), a GPS-confirmed attempt logging system (servers log each service attempt in the app with GPS coordinates and timestamp, generating the location-verified attempt record that feeds directly into the standardized affidavit of service), and a client-facing status dashboard (showing law firm clients real-time attempt status for every active order, eliminating the "can you check on the status of that serve?" call that consumes attorney staff time) — earning the spread between the fee billed to the law firm and the fee paid to the field server.
**Skill fit:** The dispatch matching, GPS-confirmed attempt logging, and affidavit document generation are directly the builder's core skill set — this is a dispatch-and-document-management problem, not a legal expertise problem. The builder doesn't need to be a licensed process server to own and operate an agency (though being licensed in the initial state helps with quality control and backup service capacity); the servers are independent contractors who provide the field work while the agency provides the order pipeline and the operational infrastructure.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a server roster database, an order intake form with dispatch assignment and acceptance confirmation, and a GPS attempt log with affidavit PDF generator; initial server roster recruited from existing independent process servers who post in process serving communities (NAPPS — National Association of Professional Process Servers — member directory is the primary server recruiting source) before the first client order is placed.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through debt collection law firms (who file hundreds of collection suits monthly and need process service for every single one — highest volume buyer in the market), family law attorneys (divorce and custody filings require service on the opposing spouse and are filed daily in every county), and civil litigation boutiques who file commercial disputes and need service on out-of-state defendants requiring coordination with servers in multiple jurisdictions.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Billing rate to law firm: $75-150 per serve; server pay: $45-80 per serve; agency spread: $30-70 per serve. At 30 serves/day at $40 average spread = $1,200/day = **$25,000-30,000/mo**. Reaching 30 serves/day requires 4-6 active law firm accounts each placing 5-7 daily service orders — a realistic 9-12 month ramp given that collection attorneys in particular have predictable daily filing volumes and will consolidate their serving volume with one reliable agency rather than splitting across several.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Each law firm account has compounding volume — as the firm files more suits, service orders grow without any additional sales effort per order. Debt collection law firms in particular have monthly quotas of suits filed (driven by their collector clients' recovery targets), creating a predictable and growing order stream from each account relationship. The GPS-confirmed attempt logging creates an audit trail that protects the law firm in cases where defendants later contest service, making the agency genuinely superior to cash-only agencies with no location verification — a product quality argument that closes law firm accounts who've had contested service issues before.
**Biggest risk:** State-by-state licensing requirements for process servers vary — some states require process servers to be licensed or registered, and using an unlicensed server generates an affidavit of service that can be challenged as void, invalidating the lawsuit's service of process. Before accepting orders in any new state, verify the licensing requirements for servers in that state and confirm every roster server meets them — a single invalidated service that causes a lawsuit to be dismissed is the fastest way to lose a law firm account permanently.
**Growth path:** Side project → 3-5 server roster members in one metro, first 1-2 collection attorney clients placing daily orders, GPS attempt logging and affidavit generation validated in court submissions. Replaces-part-time-income → 10-15 serves/day, 2-3 attorney account relationships, affidavit quality reputation generating inbound law firm referrals (~$8-12K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 25-35 serves/day, 5-6 law firm accounts, server roster covering multiple counties and adjacent metro markets, pushing past $20-30K/mo.

### 5. Dental Practice Acquisition Due Diligence & Transition Coordination
**Problem:** Dentists buy and sell practices constantly — a graduating dentist's primary path to ownership is acquiring an existing practice, and a retiring dentist's primary liquidity event is selling theirs. A dental practice acquisition involves a complex multi-party coordination challenge: the buyer needs a collections analysis (3 years of production and collections data by procedure code and payer), a patient retention risk assessment (patient demographics, active patient count, referral source dependency), equipment condition and lease assignability review, and the synchronization of a dental CPA, a dental attorney, an equipment appraiser, a dental lender, and the selling dentist's transition plan — all on a 60-90 day closing timeline. Most buyers cobble this together themselves with minimal guidance, causing delays that kill deals, and most practice brokers are focused on marketing the practice (the seller's interest), not managing the buyer's due diligence (the buyer's interest). A due diligence coordination service manages the buyer's side of the process end-to-end.
**What to build:** A dental practice acquisition due diligence and transition coordination service — a data room setup and document collection workflow (requesting the standard due diligence package from the seller's broker: 3 years of tax returns, production/collections by procedure code, active patient count, equipment list, and lease documents), a collections and payer mix analysis tool (building the structured analysis of collections trend, payer concentration, and fee schedule comparison that the dental CPA and lender need), a vendor coordination workflow (managing the timeline and deliverable dependencies between the dental attorney, CPA, equipment appraiser, and lender), a lease assignment review checklist (flagging landlord consent requirements, co-tenancy clauses, and option terms that affect the practice's long-term viability), and a closing timeline tracker — charged as a flat project fee plus a success fee upon closing.
**Skill fit:** The collections analysis by procedure code and payer mix, patient retention risk modeling, and vendor coordination timeline management are directly the builder's data analysis and project coordination skills. Dental practice production and collections data is structured (exported from practice management software like Dentrix or Eaglesoft), making the analysis a data pipeline problem rather than requiring dental clinical expertise. The builder doesn't need to be a dentist; the value is in the data organization and process management that currently gets done inconsistently or not at all.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a due diligence document checklist, a collections analysis template (procedure code production/collections by year and payer category), and a vendor coordination timeline tracker; the first 2-3 engagements are managed manually while the analysis templates are refined against real practice data.
**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — distribute through dental CPA firms that specialize in practice transitions (every practice acquisition involves a CPA, and the CPA who represents the buyer is the natural referral source for a due diligence coordination service that makes their own analysis work easier), dental school alumni associations where recent graduates are actively seeking practice acquisition opportunities, and dental practice lenders (banks and specialty dental lenders like Live Oak Bank who finance acquisitions and want to see cleaner due diligence packages from borrowers).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $3,500-7,000 flat project fee per engagement (due diligence and coordination) + 0.5-1% success fee on purchase price at closing (average dental practice sells for $400,000-800,000 = $2,000-8,000 success fee). Total per deal: $5,500-15,000. At 1-2 closed transactions/month (closing rate varies; expect 50-60% of engaged due diligence projects to close) = **$8,000-20,000/mo** at steady state. The dental practice transaction market is large and consistent — 5,000-8,000 practices sell annually in the US, concentrated among a reachable set of dental CPA and lender referral partners.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Each dental CPA firm referral relationship generates multiple annual deal referrals — a CPA who advises 30-50 dentist clients will see 2-5 active acquisition transactions per year among that client base, making a single CPA relationship worth 2-5 engagements per year without additional prospecting. The dental practice transition market is counter-cyclical to general economic conditions (retiring dentists sell regardless of economic environment, and new dentists need practice ownership to generate income), providing transaction volume stability.
**Biggest risk:** Deal flow depends on closings — a pipeline of due diligence engagements that fail to close (seller backing out, lender declining to finance, buyer walking away after due diligence) generates analysis work but no success fee income. Price the flat project fee to cover the operational cost of a completed due diligence engagement regardless of whether the deal closes, and position the success fee as the upside rather than the base revenue, so the business remains viable even in a period of higher-than-normal deal mortality.
**Growth path:** Side project → first 2-3 due diligence engagements through dental CPA referrals, collections analysis template refined against real practice data, first deal closed and success fee received. Replaces-part-time-income → 1 closed deal/month, 2-3 CPA referral sources active, due diligence workflow systematized to reduce per-engagement time (~$6-12K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 1.5-2 closed deals/month, 4-6 referral sources (mix of dental CPAs, lenders, and alumni networks), analysis quality reputation generating direct inbound from dentist buyers, pushing past $10-20K/mo.

## This Session's Pick: #3 for Compounding Income; #4 for Fastest Ramp

**Best long-term income structure:** Idea #3 (commercial energy brokering) has the most durable income architecture of any idea in this session — every contract signed generates 12-36 months of passive residual commission with zero additional work, and the book of accounts compounds with every new contract without prior accounts churning. At 100 commercial accounts averaging 50,000 kWh/month, the monthly income is fully residual and requires only renewal management to sustain. The broker registration process (2-4 weeks, $100-500 in most deregulated states) is the only barrier to entry, and Texas's ERCOT market provides the largest single-state addressable market of commercial customers who have never been approached by an independent broker.

**Fastest path to replacing income:** Idea #4 (process serving agency) has the most compressed income ramp — 30-45 days to first dollar, 4-6 attorney accounts to a $20K+/mo spread income rate, and the highest per-unit economics in this session ($30-70 spread per serve vs. commission fractions or per-unit retainer amounts in the other ideas). Collection attorneys are the most accessible distribution channel in the series: there are thousands of them, they file suits daily, and they will switch agencies the moment a new provider demonstrates faster GPS-confirmed status updates and cleaner affidavit formatting than their current agency.

**Recommended pairing:** Launch #4 (process serving agency) as the primary income vehicle — the agency spread income reaches salary-replacement levels faster than any other idea in this session and the operational infrastructure is buildable in 2-3 weekends. In parallel, register as a commercial energy broker (#3) and begin placing commercial accounts, letting the residual commission base grow in the background while process serving covers immediate income needs. The two businesses require completely non-overlapping time and attention: serving agency work is front-loaded in the day (law firm dispatch and server coordination happen during business hours), while energy broker prospecting is relationship-driven and can be done in off-hours meetings with property managers and accountants.
