### 1. Veterinary Clinic DEA Registration, State CS Registration & Biennial Inventory Compliance
**Problem:** Veterinary practices — small animal clinics, equine practices, mixed-practice rural veterinaries, and specialty veterinary hospitals — that prescribe and dispense controlled substances face a multi-agency compliance framework involving DEA, state pharmacy boards, and state veterinary boards. DEA registration: every veterinarian who administers, dispenses, or prescribes controlled substances must hold a DEA Certificate of Registration specific to the practice location, with triennial renewal — a multi-veterinarian practice with multiple clinic locations must maintain separate DEA registrations for each practitioner and each location, and must update the registration when practitioners change or locations open. DEA Schedule II ordering compliance: Schedule II controlled substances (including ketamine, oxymorphone, and fentanyl patches commonly used in veterinary medicine) must be ordered only from DEA-registered distributors using DEA Form 222 (paper) or CSOS (DEA's electronic equivalent) — and those order records must be retained for 2 years and available for DEA inspection. Biennial controlled substance inventory: DEA regulations require every registered practitioner to conduct a complete physical inventory of all Schedule II-V controlled substances on hand every two years, with the inventory documented on the date it is taken and retained for 2 years — and a new inventory is required within 10 days of any change in DEA registration (new practitioner added, new location registered). State controlled substance registration: approximately 35 states maintain separate state controlled substance schedules and require practitioners to hold a state CS registration or state CS permit in addition to the federal DEA registration — with annual renewal and independent renewal dates from the DEA registration. Veterinary practices with 3-8 veterinarians maintaining individual DEA registrations, tracking biennial inventory due dates by practitioner, and managing state CS registrations across multiple locations fail to track expiration dates systemically — an expired DEA registration discovered during a DEA compliance inspection generates a DEA Show Cause proceeding, which can result in temporary suspension of controlled substance privileges for the entire practice.
**What to build:** A veterinary practice DEA and controlled substance compliance management service — a DEA registration portfolio (for each veterinarian and each registered location: DEA registration number, registration type, registered location address, triennial renewal date, and any pending change of information — practitioner name change, address change — with tiered renewal alerts beginning 6 months before expiration), a state CS registration tracker (for each practitioner in states requiring separate state CS registration: state CS registration number, annual renewal deadline, and renewal fee payment status — with tiered renewal alerts coordinated with the DEA renewal calendar to identify cases where the state CS and DEA renewals fall within the same quarter), a biennial CS inventory scheduler (for each DEA-registered practitioner and location: the date of the most recent biennial inventory, the next biennial inventory due date — calculated as 2 years from the most recent inventory date — the inventory record completion status, and any triggered inventory events — new practitioner addition, new location registration — within the last 10 days), a Schedule II DEA Form 222 / CSOS order log (for each Schedule II controlled substance order: order date, DEA order form number or CSOS transaction ID, substance ordered, quantity ordered, distributor name and DEA number, and the receipt confirmation with quantity received — maintaining a complete 2-year rolling Schedule II order record ready for DEA inspection), and a controlled substance loss and theft reporting tracker (DEA requires prompt reporting of any CS theft or significant CS loss on DEA Form 106 — for each reported or suspected loss: substance, quantity, date discovered, DEA Form 106 submission date and confirmation, and the local law enforcement report number — maintaining documentation that all loss events were properly reported within the required timeframe).
**Skill fit:** DEA registration requirements and biennial inventory rules are codified in 21 CFR Part 1301-1307 and documented in DEA's Practitioner's Manual for Veterinarians. State CS registration requirements are available from each state's pharmacy board or state controlled substance authority. Building the DEA registration portfolio, state CS tracker, biennial inventory scheduler, Form 222/CSOS order log, and loss reporting tracker are data management problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. AVMA (American Veterinary Medical Association) and state VMA member networks provide defined buyer communities.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a DEA registration portfolio with triennial renewal cycle management, a state CS registration tracker for the 35 states with separate CS registration requirements, a biennial inventory scheduler with triggered inventory detection, a Schedule II order log with 2-year retention and DEA-inspection-ready report, and a CS loss reporting tracker; first 5-8 veterinary practice clients through AVMA member outreach while the DEA registration portfolio and biennial inventory scheduler are validated against real practice CS compliance situations.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through AVMA (American Veterinary Medical Association) member network and state veterinary medical association outreach (AVMA's annual convention is the primary industry event; state VMAs have direct communication channels to practice owners and practice managers), veterinary practice management software referral networks (Impromed, AVImark, and ezyVet — the primary veterinary practice management systems — have partner ecosystems where compliance tools are promoted to their veterinary practice customer bases), and veterinary controlled substance distributor referral networks (Henry Schein Animal Health, MWI Animal Health, and other veterinary distributors who process Schedule II orders see DEA registration compliance gaps at every order and refer compliance management tools to practice managers in their distribution relationships).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per veterinary practice: $200-500/mo (depending on practitioner count, location count, and state CS registration footprint — a 3-veterinarian single-location small animal clinic: $200/mo; an 8-veterinarian multi-location mixed practice with state CS registrations in 2 states and active Schedule II ordering: $500/mo). Managing 30-50 practice retainers at $325/mo average = **$9,750-16,250/mo**. DEA registration renewal coordination packages (one-time $150-300 for practices managing first-time practitioner DEA registrations or new location DEA registrations — documentation preparation and submission guidance) generate supplemental revenue. Reaching 30-50 retainers requires engagement with 5-7 state VMA communities and 3-4 veterinary distributor referral relationships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Individual DEA registrations renew triennially but are staggered across all practitioners — a 6-veterinarian practice has 6 independent triennial renewal dates distributed across a 3-year window. Biennial CS inventories are due every 2 years per practitioner and per location — new hires trigger new biennial inventory cycles. State CS registrations renew annually and independently of DEA renewals. Schedule II order records must be maintained for every order throughout the year. New controlled substance distributor relationships require DEA order form reconciliation. Any practitioner departure requires DEA registration transfer or revocation within 30 days.
**Biggest risk:** A veterinary practice whose DEA registration lapses — because the triennial renewal date wasn't tracked — cannot legally administer, dispense, or prescribe any controlled substance until the registration is reinstated, creating an immediate patient care disruption for any patient requiring scheduled surgery, pain management, or sedation. DEA registration reinstatement after lapse requires submitting a new registration application with a processing time of 4-6 weeks. Build the DEA registration tracker with a 6-month advance alert — early enough to submit the renewal application well before any processing delays cause a lapse.
**Growth path:** Side project → DEA registration portfolio, state CS tracker, biennial inventory scheduler, and Schedule II order log deployed, first 5-8 practice retainers through AVMA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 15-20 practice retainers, DEA registration coordination packages generating supplemental revenue (~$4-6K/mo). ### 2. Nonprofit Multi-State Charitable Solicitation Registration, Form 990 Filing & State AG Reporting
**Problem:** Nonprofit organizations soliciting charitable contributions from donors across the country — through direct mail, online donation pages, email campaigns, and events — must register as charitable solicitation organizations in every state where they solicit, which currently means 41 states plus DC. Charitable solicitation registration: each state's charitable solicitation registration (administered by the state attorney general's office or secretary of state) requires an annual registration renewal, a filing fee (ranging from $0 to $400 depending on the state and the organization's annual revenue), financial disclosures (most states require an annual financial report or the most recent IRS Form 990 and audited financial statements above certain revenue thresholds), and in some states a paid solicitor or fundraising counsel registration if the nonprofit employs external fundraising firms. Failure to register in a required state before soliciting there is a state charitable solicitation law violation that can generate cease-and-desist orders, civil penalties, and in some states injunctions against future solicitation. IRS Form 990 annual filing: tax-exempt nonprofits must file IRS Form 990 (or 990-EZ or 990-N) annually by the 15th day of the 5th month after the organization's fiscal year end — for a June 30 fiscal year end, that is November 15. The organization may request an automatic 6-month extension (Form 8868), extending the deadline to May 15. Failure to file Form 990 for 3 consecutive years results in automatic revocation of tax-exempt status under IRC Section 6033(j). State attorney general and charity registration annual reports: beyond the charitable solicitation registration, many states require registered charities to file separate annual reports with the state attorney general's charity division — with a separate filing deadline, a separate filing fee, and in many states financial statements that must be attached. A mid-size nonprofit with $2-15M in annual revenue soliciting nationally manages 41+ state registrations with staggered annual renewal deadlines, Form 990 filing with extension management, and state AG annual reports — frequently with a single staff member or outside CPA managing all compliance without a systematic calendar.
**What to build:** A nonprofit multi-state charitable solicitation registration and Form 990 compliance management service — a multi-state charitable solicitation registration portfolio (for each state where the nonprofit is registered or is required to register: state name, registration number, annual renewal deadline, filing fee amount, required financial attachments — Form 990, audited financials, or reviewed financials depending on the state's revenue thresholds — and registration status — current, renewal due, or not yet registered — with tiered renewal alerts for each state, generating a unified annual renewal calendar across all 41+ states), a state revenue threshold tracker (for states with revenue-based filing requirements — states that require audited financials above $500,000 in revenue, reviewed financials above $250,000, or Form 990 only below $250,000 — tracking the organization's most recent annual revenue against each state's threshold and flagging states where a revenue increase has triggered a new financial statement requirement), an IRS Form 990 filing calendar (tracking the organization's fiscal year end, the Form 990 due date, the automatic extension deadline if Form 8868 is filed, and the Form 990 submission confirmation — with a 45-day pre-due-date alert to initiate the 990 preparation and an extension filing alert 14 days before the original due date if the return is not yet filed), a state attorney general annual report tracker (for states requiring a separate annual charity report: the state AG report due date, the required attachments, and the filing submission confirmation — coordinated with the charitable solicitation renewal calendar to combine filings where the state combines both requirements into a single submission), and a paid solicitor / fundraising counsel registration tracker (for nonprofits using external fundraising firms: the fundraising firm's state registration status in each state where the nonprofit is registered, since most states require both the charity and any paid solicitor to be registered before solicitation — confirming the fundraising firm's registration currency before any campaign launch in a new state).
**Skill fit:** Multi-state charitable solicitation registration requirements are documented in the Multi-State Filer Project's Unified Registration Statement (URS) and in each state's charitable solicitation law. IRS Form 990 filing requirements and extension procedures are documented in IRS Publication 4221-PC. Building the multi-state registration portfolio, revenue threshold tracker, Form 990 filing calendar, state AG report tracker, and paid solicitor registration tracker are data management problems the builder's engineering and data analytics skills address directly. Council of Nonprofits and AFP (Association of Fundraising Professionals) member networks provide defined buyer communities.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a multi-state charitable solicitation registration portfolio for all 41 registered states plus DC with state-specific revenue thresholds, filing fees, and financial attachment requirements, an IRS Form 990 filing calendar with extension management, a state attorney general annual report tracker, and a revenue threshold tracker; first 5-8 nonprofit clients through AFP member outreach while the multi-state registration portfolio and Form 990 calendar are validated against real nonprofit registration situations.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through AFP (Association of Fundraising Professionals) member network and Council of Nonprofits member outreach (the primary professional communities for nonprofit executive directors, development directors, and financial officers — AFP's International Fundraising Conference is the primary industry event), nonprofit CPA firm referral networks (CPA firms that prepare Form 990 for nonprofit clients see charitable solicitation registration compliance gaps when they request state compliance documentation during 990 preparation — they actively refer compliance management tools to their nonprofit clients who lack systematic state registration tracking), and nonprofit software platform referral networks (Bloomerang, Salesforce Nonprofit, and DonorPerfect — the primary donor management platforms — have partner ecosystems where compliance tools are promoted to their nonprofit customer bases).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per nonprofit: $250-700/mo (depending on state registration count, revenue tier driving financial attachment requirements, and Form 990 complexity — a $1M revenue nonprofit registered in 20 states with a simple 990-EZ: $250/mo; a $8M revenue nonprofit registered in all 41 states requiring audited financials in 28 states and a complex Form 990 with multiple schedules: $700/mo). Managing 20-40 nonprofit retainers at $425/mo average = **$8,500-17,000/mo**. State registration reinstatement packages (one-time $200-500 for nonprofits that have allowed state registrations to lapse and need reinstated registrations before a new fundraising campaign) generate event-driven supplemental revenue. Reaching 20-40 retainers requires engagement with AFP chapter communities in 5-6 major metro nonprofit markets.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Charitable solicitation registrations renew annually in every registered state — a nonprofit registered in all 41 states has 41 annual renewal events distributed throughout the year (states have varying renewal deadlines — some are fixed calendar dates, others are anniversary-of-registration dates, others are tied to the Form 990 due date). Form 990 is filed annually. State AG annual reports are filed annually independently of the solicitation registration in many states. Any revenue increase that pushes the organization across a state's financial statement threshold triggers a new audited or reviewed financials requirement. Geographic expansion into new states triggers new charitable solicitation registration applications. New fundraising campaigns in states where the nonprofit is not yet registered trigger new registration requirements before solicitation begins.

### 3. Compounding Pharmacy USP 797/800 Compliance Documentation, BOP Inspection Readiness & PCAB Accreditation
**Problem:** Compounding pharmacies that prepare sterile preparations — including IV admixtures, ophthalmic preparations, and intrathecal injections — must comply with USP Chapter 797 (Pharmaceutical Compounding — Sterile Preparations) standards and, if they compound with hazardous drugs, USP Chapter 800 (Pharmaceutical Compounding — Hazardous Drugs) standards. These standards impose detailed ongoing documentation requirements that small to mid-size compounding pharmacies (1-5 compounding pharmacists) routinely fail to maintain systematically. USP 797 documentation requirements: cleanroom ISO classification testing by a qualified certifier (every 6 months for ISO 5 primary engineering controls — laminar airflow workbenches, isolators — and ISO 7/8 rooms, plus after any major HVAC disruption), viable and non-viable environmental monitoring (surface and air sampling for microbial contamination at defined sample sites with documented results and out-of-spec action thresholds), garbing and hand hygiene competency assessments for each compounding technician and pharmacist (initial competency and annual re-assessment), media-fill testing (sterility simulation testing for each compounder — annually for low-risk and medium-risk preparations, semiannually for high-risk), and gloved fingertip and thumb sampling (every 6 months per compounder). USP 800 documentation requirements: a written hazardous drug (HD) list, a written assessment of risk document, containment segregated compounding area (C-SCA) or containment primary engineering control (C-PEC) certification documentation, closed system drug transfer device (CSTD) use records, and decontamination and cleaning log for the C-SCA and all HD surfaces. State board of pharmacy inspection readiness: state BOP inspectors who conduct pharmacy inspections for compounding pharmacies check USP 797/800 documentation directly — missing or outdated environmental monitoring logs, expired certifier reports, or undocumented personnel competency assessments generate inspection deficiencies and can result in compounding privileges suspension. PCAB accreditation: the Pharmacy Compounding Accreditation Board (PCAB, now operated by ACHC) provides voluntary accreditation for compounding pharmacies — a triennial accreditation survey assessing USP 797/800 compliance, quality management systems, and beyond-use dating practices. PCAB accreditation is increasingly required by hospital and clinic customers who outsource compounding to 503A pharmacies.
**What to build:** A compounding pharmacy USP 797/800 compliance documentation and inspection readiness service — a cleanroom certification schedule (for each primary and secondary engineering control: the ISO classification type — ISO 5 PEC, ISO 7 buffer room, ISO 8 ante room — the certifying company, the most recent certification date and certification report on file, the next required certification date — every 6 months — and any post-disruption recertification events with documentation — with tiered alerts beginning 30 days before each certification due date), an environmental monitoring log (for each monitoring sample site: the sample site location, monitoring type — viable air, non-viable particle count, surface contact — the sampling date, the result, the action level threshold for that site, and any out-of-spec result with the corrective action investigation record — maintaining a rolling 12-month EM log ready for BOP inspection), a personnel competency tracker (for each compounder — pharmacist or technician — who prepares sterile preparations: initial garbing competency assessment date, annual garbing re-assessment due date, media-fill test date and result, gloved fingertip/thumb sampling date and result, and any competency failure with remediation documentation — with alerts for each compounder's upcoming annual competency due dates), a USP 800 hazardous drug compliance document manager (for the pharmacy's USP 800 program: the current HD list with last review date and next required annual review, the written assessment of risk document with last update date, the C-SCA or C-PEC certification report, CSTD procurement records confirming CSTD use for each hazardous drug category, and the HD area decontamination log with documented cleaning events), and a BOP inspection readiness checklist (a pre-inspection checklist organized by the state BOP's inspection form categories — with each documentation element rated as current/on-file, approaching expiration, or missing — enabling the pharmacy director to identify and close documentation gaps before scheduling a state BOP inspection or receiving an unannounced inspection).
**Skill fit:** USP Chapter 797 and Chapter 800 standards are published by the United States Pharmacopeia — the 2023 revised USP 797 and 800 standards are the current operative versions. State BOP inspection checklists for compounding pharmacies are available from most state boards of pharmacy as public documents. PCAB accreditation standards are available from ACHC's PCAB accreditation program. Building the cleanroom certification schedule, EM log, personnel competency tracker, USP 800 document manager, and BOP inspection readiness checklist are data management problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. PCAB/ACHC accreditation contacts and IACP (International Academy of Compounding Pharmacists) member networks provide defined buyer communities.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a cleanroom certification schedule with 6-month interval tracking, an environmental monitoring log with action level tracking and out-of-spec corrective action management, a personnel competency tracker for all USP 797 competency elements, a USP 800 hazardous drug compliance document manager, and a BOP inspection readiness checklist aligned with the top 15 state BOP inspection forms; first 5-8 compounding pharmacy clients through IACP member outreach while the EM log and personnel competency tracker are validated against real pharmacy USP 797 documentation workflows.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through IACP (International Academy of Compounding Pharmacists) member network and PCAB/ACHC accreditation program outreach (IACP's annual Compounders on Capitol Hill conference and annual meeting are the primary industry events; PCAB/ACHC accreditation surveyors who conduct triennial accreditation surveys see USP 797/800 documentation gaps at every survey and refer compliance management tools to pharmacies preparing for accreditation), cleanroom certification company referral networks (HVAC/cleanroom testing companies that conduct the biannual ISO certification surveys see EM log and personnel competency gaps at every certification visit and actively refer documentation management tools to pharmacy directors in their client base), and compounding pharmacy liability insurance carrier referral networks (specialty pharmacy insurers who write compounding pharmacy liability coverage require documented USP 797/800 compliance and refer compliance management tools to pharmacies with informal documentation systems as a condition of underwriting).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per compounding pharmacy: $350-800/mo (depending on compounding volume, number of compounders, whether USP 800 HD compounding is performed, and PCAB accreditation status — a 2-compounder sterile-only 503A pharmacy with no HD compounding: $350/mo; a 6-compounder pharmacy performing both sterile and hazardous drug compounding with active PCAB accreditation and multiple ISO-classified rooms: $800/mo). Managing 18-30 pharmacy retainers at $550/mo average = **$9,900-16,500/mo**. PCAB accreditation preparation packages (one-time $600-1,200 for pharmacies preparing for a first-time PCAB triennial survey who need a full USP 797/800 documentation audit and gap closure plan) generate significant project-driven supplemental revenue. Reaching 18-30 retainers requires concentrated engagement with the IACP community and PCAB/ACHC accreditation client referral relationships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Cleanroom certifications are required every 6 months — a pharmacy with an ISO 5 laminar airflow workbench, an ISO 7 buffer room, and an ISO 8 ante room has 3 certification events per 6-month cycle. Environmental monitoring is performed monthly or quarterly. Compounder garbing competency assessments are annual for every compounder. Media-fill testing is annual or semiannual per compounder. Gloved fingertip sampling is semiannual per compounder. The HD list must be reviewed annually. The USP 800 assessment of risk must be updated when new hazardous drugs are added to the formulary. PCAB accreditation renewal is triennial. New compounder hires trigger new initial competency assessment and baseline sampling events.
**Biggest risk:** A compounding pharmacy whose state BOP inspector finds undocumented or out-of-date USP 797 environmental monitoring records during an unannounced inspection receives a pharmacy inspection deficiency — and if the deficiency is cited as a critical compounding practice violation, the state BOP has authority to immediately suspend the pharmacy's sterile compounding privileges pending remediation. A sterile compounding suspension for even 2-3 weeks eliminates revenue from the pharmacy's highest-margin product lines and creates patient continuity-of-care disruptions for patients on compounded medications. Build the BOP inspection readiness checklist as a monthly self-assessment tool — reviewed by the pharmacist-in-charge every 30 days to confirm all documentation elements are current before the possibility of an unannounced inspection.
**Growth path:** Side project → cleanroom certification schedule, EM log, personnel competency tracker, USP 800 document manager, and BOP inspection readiness checklist deployed, first 5-8 pharmacy retainers through IACP outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 9-14 pharmacy retainers, PCAB accreditation preparation packages generating project-driven revenue (~$5-8K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 18-30 pharmacy retainers, cleanroom certifier and PCAB/ACHC referral pipelines generating consistent new clients, pushing past $9-16K/mo.

### 4. Multi-State Specialty Contractor License Portfolio, Surety Bond Renewal & CE Compliance
**Problem:** Electrical, plumbing, and HVAC/mechanical contractors performing licensed trade work across multiple states — including national commercial contractors, regional construction companies with multi-state project footprints, and utility infrastructure contractors — must manage a layered compliance framework at both the company level and the individual license level in each operating state. State contractor licensing: electrical, plumbing, and HVAC contractor licenses are issued by state licensing boards (or in some states by local jurisdictions such as city or county electrical boards) with annual or biennial renewal, surety bond requirements, and financial responsibility thresholds. Most states require a separate contractor license examination, and licenses are not transferable between states — a national electrical contractor working in 12 states must hold 12 independent state electrical contractor licenses. Individual journeyman and master licenses: beyond the company contractor license, most states require each individual electrician, plumber, or HVAC mechanic to hold a state-issued journeyman or master license for the work they perform — a 30-person electrical contractor with journeymen working in 6 states must track up to 180 individual-state license combinations, each with independent renewal dates and CE requirements. Surety bond management: most state contractor licenses require a surety bond in a state-specified amount (typically $10,000-$50,000) from a state-accepted surety carrier, with annual renewal independent of the license renewal date. CE requirements: licensed electrical, plumbing, and HVAC contractors and individual licensees must complete state-mandated CE hours per renewal cycle — typically 8-24 hours for master license renewal — with state-specific topic requirements such as National Electrical Code (NEC) updates for electricians.
**What to build:** A specialty contractor multi-state license and compliance management service — a company contractor license portfolio (for each operating state: state contractor license number, license type — electrical, plumbing, HVAC/mechanical — annual or biennial renewal deadline, qualifying individual designation and their license currency in that state, surety bond carrier and bond amount and expiration date, and any state-specific financial responsibility documentation — with tiered renewal alerts for the company license and the bond), an individual license tracker (for each licensed employee: journeyman or master license number and classification, all states in which the individual holds a current license with expiration dates and CE requirements per renewal cycle, CE hours completed to date with provider and course name, and projected CE completion status — with alerts when CE is projected to fall short before renewal), a surety bond renewal calendar (for every company and individual surety bond across all licensed states: bond carrier, bond number, bond amount, bond expiration date, and premium renewal payment status — with 90-day and 30-day pre-expiration alerts, since bond expirations are frequently independent of license renewal dates), a NEC / code update CE tracker (for electrical contractors: tracking the current adopted National Electrical Code edition in each operating state — which varies, as states adopt new NEC editions on independent schedules — and the CE requirement for NEC update training at each code cycle change, ensuring licensed electricians complete the NEC update CE before working under the newly adopted code), and a project state deployment check (before any licensed technician is dispatched to a project in a specific state: confirming the technician holds a current, valid individual license in that state and in the required license category — preventing unlicensed trade work which is a state licensing board violation and a general contractor contract compliance issue).
**Skill fit:** State electrical, plumbing, and HVAC contractor licensing requirements and individual journeyman/master license requirements are available from each state's contractor licensing board. NEC adoption status by state is published by NECA (National Electrical Contractors Association). Building the company license portfolio, individual license tracker, surety bond renewal calendar, NEC code update CE tracker, and project state deployment check are data management problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. NECA (National Electrical Contractors Association), PHCC (Plumbing-Heating-Cooling Contractors National Association), and ACCA (Air Conditioning Contractors of America) member networks provide defined buyer communities.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a company contractor license portfolio for the 30 highest-construction-activity states, an individual journeyman and master license tracker with state-specific CE requirements for electrical, plumbing, and HVAC license types, a surety bond renewal calendar, a NEC edition adoption tracker by state, and a project state deployment check; first 5-8 specialty contractor clients through NECA or PHCC member outreach while the individual license tracker and project deployment check are validated against real contractor employee license rosters.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through NECA (National Electrical Contractors Association) chapter network and PHCC member outreach (NECA's annual convention and PHCC's CONNECT conference are the primary industry events for electrical and plumbing contractor owners respectively), construction industry surety bond agent referral networks (surety agents who write contractor license bonds have direct insight into multi-state bond management complexity for their contractor clients and refer compliance management tools to contractors with large multi-state bond portfolios as part of their bonding relationship management), and general contractor subcontractor prequalification referral networks (large general contractors who manage subcontractor prequalification programs require current state license verification for all trade subcontractors — GCs whose subcontractor management teams see license tracking gaps in specialty contractor prequalification packets refer compliance management tools to their specialty contractor subs).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per contractor: $300-750/mo (depending on licensed employee count, state footprint, and trade type complexity — a 15-person electrical company licensed in 3 states: $300/mo; a 50-person multi-trade contractor with electricians, plumbers, and HVAC mechanics licensed in 8 states requiring individual license tracking for 40+ individual licenses: $750/mo). ### 5. Employer Immigration Compliance — Form I-9 Audit Readiness, E-Verify & H-1B LCA Filing Management
**Problem:** Employers who hire non-citizen workers and/or sponsor foreign national employees on work visas face a multi-layer immigration compliance framework administered by DHS (USCIS and ICE), DOL, and in some states by state employment verification laws — and the consequences of compliance failures are among the most operationally disruptive of any employer regulatory obligation. Form I-9 employment eligibility verification: every U.S. employer must complete a Form I-9 for every new hire — regardless of citizenship status — within 3 business days of the first day of employment. The completed I-9 must be retained for the longer of 3 years after the date of hire or 1 year after the date of employment termination, and must be stored in a form ready for ICE inspection. ICE conducts I-9 audits (Notices of Inspection) with a 3-business-day production window — employers who cannot produce complete, compliant I-9 records for all current and recently terminated employees face civil penalties of $272-$2,701 per substantive violation and $578-$23,331 per knowingly hiring violation. E-Verify compliance: federal contractors and subcontractors above certain contract thresholds are required to use E-Verify for all new hires. 22+ states have enacted state E-Verify mandates for employers above employee count thresholds. Non-compliant federal contractors risk contract termination; non-compliant employers in state-mandate states face state labor department enforcement. H-1B visa and Labor Condition Application (LCA) management: employers sponsoring H-1B workers must file a Labor Condition Application with DOL certifying the prevailing wage for the sponsored position before each H-1B petition — LCAs expire after 3 years, and a new LCA must be filed before the H-1B petition renewal (extension). H-1B petition extensions must be filed with USCIS before the current H-1B status expires (premium processing is available for a fee to expedite decisions). LCA public access file compliance: employers must maintain a public access file for each LCA containing the LCA, a notice of filing, wage rate documentation, and benefit records — available for public inspection within 1 business day of request. Mid-size employers (50-500 employees) with mixed citizenship workforces, 10-50 H-1B sponsored employees across departments, and federal contracting E-Verify obligations manage these overlapping requirements without a dedicated immigration compliance coordinator.
**What to build:** An employer immigration compliance management service — a Form I-9 compliance audit tool (a systematic I-9 self-audit system: for each current employee, the I-9 completion status — Section 1 and 2 completed within the required timeframe — document type and document expiration dates for employees who presented time-limited documents such as EADs and work authorization documents, re-verification due dates for expiring documents, and a retention schedule confirming each terminated employee's I-9 will be retained until the retention deadline — with a daily alert list for re-verification deadlines within the next 30 days and a weekly I-9 completeness check confirming no new hires are missing completed I-9s), an E-Verify compliance tracker (for employers subject to E-Verify mandates: the employer's E-Verify MOU status, E-Verify case creation log — for each new hire, the E-Verify case number, case creation date within the required 3-business-day window, TNC (Tentative Nonconfirmation) status if applicable and the employee notification and resolution timeline, and E-Verify final case result — maintaining an auditable E-Verify case history for all new hires), an H-1B sponsored employee status dashboard (for each H-1B sponsored worker: employee name, USCIS petition receipt number, H-1B approval notice expiration date, the H-1B extension filing window — the earlier of 6 months before expiration for premium processing or the regular processing window allowing sufficient time before expiration — LCA certification expiration date, and the extension filing status — with tiered alerts beginning 9 months before H-1B approval expiration to initiate the extension process), an LCA public access file manager (for each certified LCA: the LCA case number, position title, prevailing wage rate, LCA effective and expiration dates, notice of filing documentation confirming the required 10-day posting, the wage rate documentation, and a public access file completeness certification — with alerts when LCA expiration approaches within 6 months), and an I-9 retention and purge calendar (for terminated employees: the I-9 retention deadline — 3 years from hire date or 1 year from termination date, whichever is later — and a scheduled purge date after the retention period expires, with a purge confirmation record — maintaining clean retention records and avoiding over-retention of I-9s for employees whose retention period has passed, which is itself a minor ICE audit vulnerability).
**Skill fit:** Form I-9 completion and retention requirements are documented in the USCIS I-9 Handbook for Employers (M-274). E-Verify program requirements are documented in USCIS E-Verify program manuals and state E-Verify mandate laws. H-1B LCA requirements are documented in DOL's LCA Handbook and 20 CFR Part 655. Building the I-9 audit tool, E-Verify compliance tracker, H-1B status dashboard, LCA public access file manager, and I-9 retention calendar are data management and workflow problems the builder's engineering and data analytics skills address directly. SHRM (Society for Human Resource Management) and AILA (American Immigration Lawyers Association) employer member networks provide defined buyer communities.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a Form I-9 compliance audit tool with re-verification tracking and daily expiring document alerts, an E-Verify case creation log with TNC management, an H-1B sponsored employee status dashboard with extension filing window alerts for up to 50 sponsored employees, an LCA public access file manager, and an I-9 retention and purge calendar; first 5-8 employer clients through SHRM member outreach while the I-9 audit tool and H-1B dashboard are validated against real employer employee rosters.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through SHRM (Society for Human Resource Management) chapter network and AILA employer member outreach (SHRM's annual conference is the primary event for HR directors and compliance managers — the primary buyer at mid-size employers; AILA's employer-side immigration attorney network refers compliance management tools to employer clients as part of their immigration law practice), federal contractor compliance consulting firm referral networks (consulting firms that help companies meet federal contractor E-Verify and affirmative action requirements see I-9 and H-1B compliance gaps at every compliance assessment and refer compliance management tools to their employer clients), and employer immigration law firm referral networks (immigration attorneys who file H-1B petitions for employer clients see LCA public access file compliance failures at every audit and actively refer compliance management tools to employers who lack systematic LCA documentation).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per employer: $400-1,000/mo (depending on total employee count, H-1B sponsored employee count, and E-Verify mandate status — a 100-employee employer with 8 H-1B sponsored employees and a federal contractor E-Verify mandate: $400/mo; a 350-employee employer with 35 H-1B sponsored employees across 5 departments requiring full LCA public access file management and active I-9 re-verification management: $1,000/mo). Reaching 15-25 retainers requires engagement with SHRM chapter communities and 3-4 immigration law firm referral relationships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Form I-9 re-verification is required for every employee who presented a time-limited work authorization document — EADs (Employment Authorization Documents) are typically issued in 1-2 year increments, generating continuous re-verification events. H-1B extensions must be filed before each H-1B status expiration — typically every 3 years — for every sponsored employee. **Biggest risk:** An employer who receives an ICE Notice of Inspection and cannot produce complete, compliant I-9 records within the 3-business-day production window — because I-9s were completed on paper in different offices without systematic storage — faces substantive violation penalties on every I-9 that is missing, incomplete, or has expired document re-verification that wasn't tracked. For a 200-employee employer, even a 20% I-9 deficiency rate (40 violations at $1,000 average penalty) generates $40,000 in civil penalties from a single ICE audit. **Growth path:** Side project → I-9 audit tool, E-Verify compliance tracker, H-1B status dashboard, LCA public access file manager, and I-9 retention calendar deployed, first 5-8 employer retainers through SHRM outreach.
