## Ideas

### 1. PFAS Environmental Assessment & Remediation Contractor CERCLA/SDWA Sampling & Reporting Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Firms performing environmental site assessment, groundwater monitoring, and remediation-system operation-and-maintenance work at PFAS-impacted sites — drinking water wells, former firefighting-foam training areas, industrial dischargers — face two newly finalized federal triggers: EPA's 2024 rule designating PFOA and PFOS as CERCLA hazardous substances (89 Fed. Reg. 39124), which imposes a potentially-responsible-party (PRP) notification obligation on any site where a release is identified, and EPA's 2024 Safe Drinking Water Act PFAS rule (40 CFR Part 141), which sets enforceable maximum contaminant levels (MCLs) for six PFAS compounds and requires water systems and remediating parties to sample using EPA Method 1633 (wastewater/solids) or Method 533/537.1 (drinking water) through a NELAP-accredited laboratory on a state- or utility-mandated recurring schedule. Once a site enters active remediation, the remediation system's operation-and-maintenance monitoring must be reported on a quarterly or semi-annual filing schedule to the overseeing state agency, and a missed sampling event, a lapsed lab accreditation, or a late O&M report is exactly the finding a state environmental agency audit or a CERCLA PRP dispute is built to catch — with real financial exposure, since PFAS remediation liability at a single site can run into the millions. A small environmental consulting or remediation firm running dozens of PFAS-impacted sites at once currently tracks sample due dates, lab accreditation currency, and O&M reporting deadlines across a shared spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific site's next required sampling event and its filing deadline are both on track before the deadline actually arrives.

**What to build:** A per-site PFAS sampling-schedule tracker mapped to EPA Method 1633/533/537.1 and the applicable state or utility monitoring frequency; a NELAP-lab-accreditation currency tracker per contracted lab; a remediation-system O&M monitoring and quarterly/semi-annual reporting-deadline tracker; and a CERCLA PRP-notification and correspondence log per site.

**Skill fit:** EPA's 2024 PFAS drinking-water rule and CERCLA hazardous-substance designation are recently finalized, highly public regulatory actions with a concrete per-site sampling and filing trigger, and the deadline-tracking-plus-lab-accreditation cross-check is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a hazardous-substance niche entirely distinct from the ESA and building-material-hazard ideas already in this repo, none of which involve a CERCLA PRP mechanic or a recurring post-designation drinking-water MCL sampling requirement of any kind.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the sampling-schedule tracker, the lab-accreditation tracker, and the O&M reporting-deadline tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small environmental consulting and remediation firms already managing PFAS-impacted sites.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — the National Ground Water Association (NGWA) and state drinking-water-administrator association contact lists reach exactly the small firms currently tracking PFAS sampling deadlines on a shared spreadsheet.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per firm depending on active-site count. 25-35 firms × $420/mo avg = $10,500-14,700/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every site's sampling cycle recurs on its own EPA/state-mandated schedule regardless of remediation progress, and every newly PFAS-designated site — a rapidly growing list as more states adopt their own PFAS MCLs — adds a fresh, permanent monitoring obligation.

**Biggest risk:** Some small environmental firms are still building out PFAS-specific service lines and may not yet see this as distinct from general environmental consulting. Mitigate by targeting firms already advertising PFAS site-assessment or remediation services, where the sampling-deadline burden is already real and being managed by hand.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 firms ($5K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 22 firms ($9.2K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 25-35 firms plus a state-agency-ready compliance export a firm can produce the moment an auditor asks.

### 2. Community Health Worker (CHW) Employer State Certification & CMS Billing-Eligibility Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Community Health Workers — non-clinical frontline workers who provide care coordination, health education, and patient navigation, primarily for Medicaid populations — are certified under state programs such as the Texas DSHS Community Health Worker Certification (Texas Health & Safety Code Chapter 48) and comparable registries in Ohio, Indiana, Oregon, and Minnesota, typically requiring a state-approved core-competency training curriculum, a certifying exam, and a renewal cycle (commonly two years) with continuing-education hours split across specific competency domains — communication, care coordination, capacity-building, and advocacy. Since CMS added Community Health Integration billing codes (G0019, G0022) to the Medicare Physician Fee Schedule, a CHW's services are only billable when the CHW works under a documented supervising-practitioner care plan and the encounter meets the code's specific minimum-service-time threshold — a claim filed without that care-plan documentation, or against a lapsed certification, is exactly the finding a state Medicaid program-integrity audit or a CMS claims audit is built to catch, and a systemic finding can trigger clawback across every claim billed during the lapse. An organization employing or contracting with 10-40 CHWs across multiple clinics or community organizations currently tracks certification renewal, CE-hour accumulation by domain, and per-encounter time documentation on a program coordinator's spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific CHW's certification is current and a specific encounter meets the billing code's time threshold before a claim goes out.

**What to build:** A per-CHW state certification and CE-hour tracker split by required competency domain; a supervising-practitioner care-plan and billing-code eligibility gate per client; a per-encounter service-time log matching CMS's G0019/G0022 minimum-time thresholds; and a program-wide compliance-readiness report for a state Medicaid audit.

**Skill fit:** State CHW certification-renewal cycles and CMS's newly published CHW billing-code time-threshold rules are public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-worker and per-encounter trigger, and the billing-eligibility gate is the same deterministic "block the claim until the record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a community-health workforce niche entirely distinct from every other healthcare-credentialing idea already in this repo, none of which pair a non-clinical care-coordination certification with a brand-new CMS billing-code time-documentation requirement.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the certification/CE tracker, the billing-eligibility gate, and the encounter time-log. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to community health centers and CHW employer organizations.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — the National Association of Community Health Workers (NACHW) and state CHW association member directories reach exactly the program coordinators currently tracking this on a spreadsheet.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-399/mo per employing organization depending on CHW headcount. 35-45 organizations × $290/mo avg = $10,150-13,050/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every CHW's certification renews on its own two-year clock, every encounter reopens the billing-code time-documentation requirement, and a growing organization onboarding new CHWs compounds the certification-and-billing-eligibility surface it has to track.

**Biggest risk:** Adoption of the new CMS CHW billing codes is still ramping and varies by state Medicaid program. Mitigate by leading with the certification-renewal tracker as the core value proposition and treating the billing-eligibility gate as an add-on whose value grows as adoption increases.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 orgs ($4.4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 28 orgs ($8.1K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 35-45 organizations plus a Medicaid-audit-ready export.

### 3. Certified Peer Recovery Support Specialist Employer State Certification, Supervision-Log & Medicaid Billing Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Certified peer recovery support specialists — individuals with lived substance-use or mental-health recovery experience who provide peer support services — hold a state-specific credential (NAADAC's National Certified Peer Recovery Support Specialist designation, or a state board such as the Pennsylvania Certification Board or Texas HHSC's peer-support certification) required before billing Medicaid under HCPCS code H0038, renewed on a two-year cycle with CE-hour requirements split between recovery-specific and ethics/supervision categories, and requiring an ongoing clinical-supervision relationship documented per state rule — a licensed clinician must sign off on the specialist's supervision log at a set interval before that specialist's services remain billable. A peer-support employer — a recovery community organization, an opioid treatment program, or a behavioral health agency — running 10-30 certified specialists across multiple sites must confirm every specialist's certification and supervision log are current before a specific service is billed, and a lapsed certification or a missing supervision-log entry discovered during a state Medicaid program-integrity audit can trigger clawback of every claim billed under that specialist during the lapse. A small recovery organization currently tracks certification renewal, CE-hour accumulation, and supervision-log currency on a program director's spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific specialist is currently certified and supervised before their service is billed.

**What to build:** A per-specialist state certification and CE-hour tracker split by required category; a clinical-supervision-log currency tracker with a billing-eligibility gate tied to H0038 claims; a per-site roster showing exactly which specialists are currently billable; and a program-wide audit-readiness export.

**Skill fit:** State peer-support certification-renewal cycles and Medicaid's H0038 supervision requirements are public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-specialist trigger, and the billing-eligibility gate is the same deterministic "block the claim until the record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a behavioral-health peer-support niche entirely distinct from the CHW idea in this same session — a CHW's brand-new billing codes carry no clinical-supervision-sign-off requirement at all, while a peer specialist's long-established H0038 code requires a documented clinical-supervisor sign-off on a recurring interval, a supervision-log mechanic unique to this credential.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the certification/CE tracker, the supervision-log tracker, and the billing-eligibility gate. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to recovery community organizations and opioid treatment programs.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — NAADAC's certified-member directory and state certification-board affiliates, active in nearly every state, reach exactly the small recovery organizations currently reconciling certification and supervision-log currency on a spreadsheet.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249-499/mo per organization depending on specialist headcount. 30-40 organizations × $360/mo avg = $10,800-14,400/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every specialist's certification recurs on its own two-year renewal clock, every supervision-log entry reopens on its own required interval, and a growing recovery organization onboarding new specialists compounds the certification-and-supervision surface it has to track.

**Biggest risk:** Some peer-support programs are grant-funded rather than Medicaid-billing, reducing the billing-eligibility gate's urgency. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward organizations actively billing Medicaid for peer support services, where clawback risk is real and immediate.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 orgs ($4.3K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 24 orgs ($8.6K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 30-40 organizations plus a state-audit-ready supervision-log export.

### 4. Dental Therapist Multi-Site Practice Supervision-Agreement Scope & CE Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Dental therapists — a mid-level provider category distinct from dental hygienists, authorized to perform a limited scope of restorative procedures such as routine fillings, simple extractions, and pulpotomies on primary teeth — practice under state Dental Therapy Practice Acts now enacted in Minnesota (Minn. Stat. §150A.105), Vermont, Maine, Arizona, Michigan, New Mexico, Idaho, Washington, Connecticut, Nevada, and Ohio, plus the Alaska Dental Health Aide Therapist program operating under the Indian Health Care Improvement Act. Every state's framework requires a biennial license renewal with CE hours, and — critically — a current, specific written collaborative management or supervision agreement on file with a supervising dentist that enumerates exactly which procedures the therapist is authorized to perform at that practice, since a dental therapist's real-world scope varies not just by state but by the specific delegation terms negotiated into each practice's agreement; performing a procedure outside the agreement's current enumerated scope is a licensing-board finding distinct from, and in addition to, ordinary malpractice exposure. A multi-site practice or tribal health program employing several dental therapists across clinics must also track each therapist's Medicaid dental credentialing separately, since most dental therapy programs exist specifically to expand Medicaid access in underserved areas. A practice running 5-15 dental therapists across several sites currently tracks license renewal, CE hours, and the current supervision agreement's specific procedure list on a practice manager's spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific therapist at a specific site today is authorized to perform the specific procedure about to be scheduled.

**What to build:** A per-therapist state license and CE-hour tracker with biennial renewal countdown; a per-practice-site collaborative/supervision-agreement tracker enumerating the current authorized-procedure list; a scheduling-eligibility gate cross-checking a scheduled procedure against the therapist's current agreement scope before the appointment is confirmed; and a per-therapist Medicaid dental-credentialing tracker.

**Skill fit:** State Dental Therapy Practice Acts and their supervision-agreement requirements are public, structured statutory data with a concrete per-therapist and per-procedure trigger, and the scheduling-eligibility gate is the same deterministic "block the action until the authorization is current" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a mid-level dental-provider niche entirely distinct from the Independent Dental Hygienist Direct-Access idea already in this repo, whose fixed preventive-only scope has no restorative-procedure authorization or per-practice negotiated-scope mechanic of any kind.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the license/CE tracker, the supervision-agreement/procedure-scope tracker, and the scheduling-eligibility gate. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to dental therapy-employing practices and tribal health programs.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — the American Dental Therapy Association's member/employer directory and the National Indian Health Board's tribal-health-program contacts reach exactly the small practices and programs currently tracking supervision-agreement scope on a spreadsheet.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per practice/program depending on therapist headcount. 20-28 practices × $420/mo avg = $8,400-11,760/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every therapist's license and CE cycle renews on its own biennial clock, every new site or renegotiated supervision agreement reopens the authorized-procedure-list tracking obligation, and a growing dental-therapy workforce — the practice model is actively expanding into new states — compounds the number of practices needing this.

**Biggest risk:** Dental therapy is legal in a still-limited number of states, capping the total addressable practice count today. Mitigate by pricing to reflect the real per-therapist compliance stakes and expanding into newly authorizing states as the workforce grows.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10 practices ($4.2K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 18 practices ($7.6K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 20-28 practices plus a licensing-board-ready supervision-agreement export.

### 5. Industrial Facility Stormwater Multi-Sector General Permit (MSGP) Benchmark-Monitoring & Corrective-Action Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Industrial facilities in roughly 29 EPA-regulated sectors — scrap recycling, metal fabrication, chemical manufacturing, mining, hazardous waste treatment, and others — that discharge stormwater must obtain coverage under the Clean Water Act's NPDES Multi-Sector General Permit (MSGP) under 40 CFR §122.26(b)(14), or a state-issued equivalent in delegated states, by filing a Notice of Intent (NOI), then conducting sector-specific quarterly visual monitoring and benchmark analytical monitoring comparing sampled pollutant concentrations against sector-specific benchmark values, an annual comprehensive facility compliance evaluation, and an annual Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan (SWPPP) review and update, filing a Notice of Termination (NOT) when coverage ends. Two consecutive benchmark exceedances at the same outfall trigger a mandatory corrective-action response with its own documentation and timeline — a facility that misses a quarterly monitoring event, fails to document a required corrective action, or lets its SWPPP go stale is exactly the finding an EPA Region or state environmental agency inspection is built to catch, and MSGP violations carry per-day, per-violation civil penalty exposure. A facility managing this in-house, or a small environmental consulting firm serving several small industrial clients, currently tracks quarterly monitoring due dates, benchmark-exceedance history per outfall, and SWPPP review dates on a shared spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific outfall's monitoring is current and its exceedance history hasn't crossed the corrective-action trigger.

**What to build:** A per-facility, per-outfall quarterly monitoring and benchmark-exceedance tracker with an automatic corrective-action trigger after two consecutive exceedances; a SWPPP annual-review and update-due tracker; an NOI/NOT filing tracker; and a compliance-readiness report for an EPA or state inspection.

**Skill fit:** The MSGP's published sector-specific benchmark tables and monitoring schedule are public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-outfall, per-quarter trigger, and the corrective-action-trigger gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a permanent-industrial-facility niche entirely distinct from the Construction Stormwater SWPPP & NPDES Construction General Permit idea already in this repo, whose project-based, soil-disturbance-duration obligations have no permanent-facility benchmark-monitoring or two-strike corrective-action mechanic of any kind.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the outfall-monitoring tracker, the SWPPP-review tracker, and the corrective-action trigger. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small industrial facilities in MSGP-covered sectors and the environmental consultants who serve them.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Institute of Scrap Recycling Industries (ISRI) member directory and state environmental-consultant association contacts reach exactly the small facilities and consultants currently tracking quarterly benchmark monitoring on a spreadsheet.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-399/mo per facility depending on outfall count. 40-50 facilities × $290/mo avg = $11,600-14,500/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every outfall's quarterly monitoring cycle recurs for as long as the facility operates, the SWPPP review reopens annually regardless of exceedance history, and a facility adding a production line or new outfall compounds the number of monitoring points it has to track.

**Biggest risk:** Many facilities rely on an outside environmental consultant for the technical monitoring work itself. Mitigate by selling to both the facility and the consultant serving it — the consultant can white-label the tracker across their whole client book.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 facilities ($4.4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 30 facilities ($8.7K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 40-50 facilities plus an inspection-ready compliance export.
