# Main Income Ideas - 2026-11-16 (Session 209)

## Who this is for
A 29-year-old software engineer with data analytics skills who wants to replace a full-time salary ($8-15K+/mo) within 12-24 months by building solo, in evenings and weekends, with no outside funding. Every idea below must have a realistic path to first revenue inside 90 days and a named, reachable distribution channel.

## Lens for this session: Alex Hormozi
Built from the $100M Offers / $100M Leads frameworks:
- **Value equation:** Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood of Success) / (Time Delay x Effort & Sacrifice). This session sells to trades and technical service firms, where perceived likelihood is raised by showing finished work and where the buyer's biggest cost is time.
- **Grand Slam Offer:** performance-priced or guarantee-backed.
- **Name the offer and stack the value:** Hormozi's advice to give the package a clear name and to stack bonuses. Each idea turns a vague service into a named package with a price.

Per standing feedback, none of these are compliance or regulatory tools. Each solves a revenue-leakage or growth problem.

## Ideas

### 1. Lead-to-Install Engine for Home EV-Charger Installers

**Problem:** An electrician who installs home EV chargers gets leads from car owners who know nothing about panels, permits or costs. Quotes require a site visit, many leads go cold and the installer does not sell add-ons like a second charger or a panel upgrade.

**What to build:** A homeowner intake with car model, panel photo and garage location, an instant ballpark price range and a three-tier package (Basic, Standard with surge protection, Premium with a second outlet), a booking link for the site visit and a follow-up sequence. Show lead-to-quote and quote-to-job rates and package mix.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Book 10 more installs in 90 days or the first quarter is free." Fee: $119/mo plus $25 per install.

**Skill fit:** Intake forms, price ranges, tiered quotes and follow-up flows.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-60 days through electrician and installer groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** An installer doing 8 installs a month pays $119 + $200 = about $320. 40 installers x $320 = **$12,800/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Each install is a $1,000-3,000 job, and package mix increases it.

**Biggest risk:** Incentive programs and demand can change. Keep the tool useful for general electrical quotes.

**Growth path:** EV-charger installers, then solar, battery, generator and heat-pump installers.

### 2. Proposal-and-Maintenance Engine for Smart-Home and Home-Theater Integrators

**Problem:** An integrator sells a $10-50K system, then disappears. Quotes are long spreadsheets that confuse homeowners, and support calls are free favors with no maintenance plan.

**What to build:** A visual proposal builder with Good, Better, Best packages and room-by-room options, a deposit link, a post-install handoff page with videos and a paid annual service plan with check-in reminders. Show proposal-to-job rate and plan attach rate.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Close 15% more proposals or the first quarter is free." Fee: $129/mo.

**Skill fit:** Proposal builder, handoff pages and plan billing.

**MVP scope:** 3-4 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 45-75 days through integrator communities.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $129/mo. 95 integrators = **$12,255/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Large jobs make one extra close valuable, and service plans add recurring revenue.

**Biggest risk:** A small market. Add security, networking and audio-visual installers.

**Growth path:** Smart-home integrators, then security, audio-visual, networking and lighting installers.

### 3. Contract-Renewal and Supply Engine for Independent Copier and Managed-Print Dealers

**Problem:** A small copier dealer lives on service contracts and toner, but renewals are discovered when a customer cancels. Toner reorders come by phone, and meter readings are sent by hand.

**What to build:** A customer portal with automatic meter submission, a toner-low reorder link, a renewal reminder at 90 days with an upgrade offer and a simple quote for replacement machines. Show renewal rate and supply revenue.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Raise contract renewals 10 points or the first quarter is free." Fee: $119/mo.

**Skill fit:** Customer portals, reminder logic and quote generation.

**MVP scope:** 3-4 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 45-75 days through dealer groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $119/mo. 100 dealers = **$11,900/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Contracts are recurring, and a missed renewal loses years of revenue.

**Biggest risk:** Integration with machine data. Start with manual meter entry and customer-submitted photos.

**Growth path:** Copier dealers, then IT service firms, phone-system vendors and office-supply dealers.

### 4. Preventive-Maintenance and Downtime Engine for Small Diesel and Truck-Repair Shops

**Problem:** A diesel repair shop sees fleet and owner-operator trucks only when they break down. Trucks that come in on a schedule would earn the shop more, but the shop does not track mileage or service intervals.

**What to build:** A vehicle and mileage log per customer, a service-interval reminder by text, a priority booking slot for plan customers, a downtime estimate with each quote and a monthly fleet summary. Show plan customers and repeat revenue per truck.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Add 20 fleet trucks on maintenance plans in 90 days or the first quarter is free." Fee: $99/mo plus $3 per truck on a plan.

**Skill fit:** Vehicle logs, reminders and booking slots.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-60 days through shop-owner groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** A shop with 60 plan trucks pays $99 + $180 = about $280. 45 shops x $280 = **$12,600/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Scheduled work is steadier than breakdowns, and fleets are high value.

**Biggest risk:** Incumbent shop-management software. Differentiate on fleet reminders and plans.

**Growth path:** Diesel and truck shops, then trailer, bus, equipment and farm-machinery repair.

### 5. Route-Density and Renewal Engine for Commercial Window-Washing Companies

**Problem:** A window-washing company serves storefronts and offices on a rolling schedule, but routes are inefficient, quotes are based on guesses and customers lapse quietly. Adding a neighbor on the same street costs little, but no one sells it.

**What to build:** A route map showing customers by street and schedule, a "next-door neighbor" quote link when a job is booked, a recurring schedule with a one-tap renewal, a quote tool by pane count and a lapsed-customer win-back. Show jobs per route hour and renewals.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Add 20% more jobs on the same routes or the first quarter is free." Fee: $79/mo.

**Skill fit:** Route maps, quote tools and renewal flows.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 21-45 days through owner groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $79/mo. 150 companies = **$11,850/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Route density raises profit without more drive time, and recurring contracts renew.

**Biggest risk:** Competition from field-service tools. Differentiate on neighbor quotes and route-density reports.

**Growth path:** Window washing, then pressure washing, janitorial, gutter and lawn services.
