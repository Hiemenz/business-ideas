### 1. Independent Respiratory Therapist Home Oxygen & Ventilator Practice Multi-Facility Licensure & CMN Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Respiratory therapists (RTs) who provide home oxygen setup, CPAP/BiPAP titration, home ventilator management, and apnea monitoring — either independently or on a per-diem basis across multiple hospitals, home health agencies, and DME suppliers — must hold a state respiratory care license under the applicable state Respiratory Care Practice Act (e.g., California Business & Professions Code §3700 et seq.), maintain their NBRC credential (CRT/RRT), and complete state-mandated continuing education (typically 12-30 hours per renewal cycle) tracked against specific category requirements. Each facility they contract with requires a separate credentialing packet (license copy, malpractice insurance certificate, TB test, immunization record, background check) with its own expiration clock. When billing Medicare for home oxygen, CMS's home oxygen Local Coverage Determination (42 CFR §410.38) requires a face-to-face physician encounter, qualifying oxygen saturation test, and a Certificate of Medical Necessity (CMN) that must be renewed every 12 months per patient — a lapsed CMN means the equipment stops being reimbursable and the therapist's ordering relationship is flagged. RTs also need annual OSHA respirator (N95) fit-test and bloodborne pathogen recertification. Independent RTs juggling 3-6 facility relationships and dozens of home-oxygen patients have no single place tracking which license, CE bucket, facility credential, or patient CMN is about to lapse.
**What to build:** License/credential portfolio (state RT license, NBRC CRT/RRT, ACLS/PALS/NRP) with renewal countdowns; per-facility credentialing-packet tracker showing each hospital/DME/home-health client's required documents and expiration dates; CE-hour accumulator split by state board category; per-patient CMN 12-month renewal calendar with physician face-to-face encounter reminder; OSHA fit-test/TB-test renewal alerts; a document vault for uploading proof so nothing is re-collected from scratch at each facility.
**Skill fit:** State respiratory-care board license-verification databases and NBRC credential status are public, structured data a developer can pull and diff against; CMS's LCD/CMN rules are published regulatory text that maps cleanly into a rules engine with date-driven alerts — a natural fit for someone with data-pipeline experience.
**MVP scope:** Build the license/CE tracker and CMN renewal calendar in 2-3 weekends; recruit the first 5-8 independent or per-diem RTs directly from state respiratory care society membership rosters, offering free onboarding in exchange for feedback.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribution through the American Association for Respiratory Care (AARC)'s state chapter newsletters and its "AARC Connect" member community, plus outreach in per-diem RT staffing Facebook groups.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $79/mo per independent RT × 100 users = $7,900/mo; add a shared multi-therapist credentialing dashboard for small home-oxygen DME suppliers and home health agencies at $249/mo × 20 agencies = $4,980/mo. Combined realistic ceiling: ~$12,900/mo at 100 individual + 20 agency accounts.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every home-oxygen patient forces a new CMN renewal every 12 months, and RT licenses/CE renew on fixed cycles — these are recurring, non-optional triggers that keep the therapist logging in monthly rather than setting the tool up once and forgetting it.
**Biggest risk:** RTs may keep tracking this informally on a spreadsheet with low urgency to switch until they get burned by a CMS audit or a lapsed facility credential that blocks a shift; counter this by shipping a "revenue-at-risk" dashboard on first login that flags any patient whose CMN is already overdue, making the cost of not switching visible immediately.
**Growth path:** Side project → 40 paying RTs (~$3,160/mo) → add DME-supplier/agency tier → 100 individuals + 20 agencies (~$12,900/mo) → replaces full-time salary.

### 2. Oral & Maxillofacial Surgery Practice Sedation/Anesthesia Permit, DEA & Facility-Inspection Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Oral and maxillofacial surgery (OMS) practices performing IV sedation or general anesthesia in-office must hold a state dental board sedation/anesthesia permit (e.g., California's Oral/Parenteral Conscious Sedation and General Anesthesia permits under Business & Professions Code §1646-1646.9), which requires a biennial unannounced on-site facility inspection covering emergency drug kit contents, crash cart medication expiration dates, and monitoring equipment calibration. Permit holders must keep the surgeon and clinical staff current on ACLS/PALS/ATLS certification, document quarterly or semi-annual emergency mock drills (required in most states as a permit condition), and separately maintain DEA registration (renewed every 3 years) with a controlled-substance log for the Schedule II opioids used peri-operatively. A single expired crash-cart medication or a missed mock drill discovered during a random board inspection can suspend the anesthesia permit — shutting down the practice's ability to perform same-day extractions and implant surgery, its core revenue.
**What to build:** Sedation/anesthesia permit renewal and facility-inspection countdown; DEA registration and state controlled-substance license tracker with per-transaction log; emergency drug kit/crash cart medication expiration log with auto-generated reorder alerts; ACLS/PALS/ATLS staff certification tracker across the whole clinical team; mock-drill scheduling with documentation upload for board audit; a one-click "board inspection readiness" report.
**Skill fit:** State dental board sedation-permit rules and inspection checklists are published regulatory text; tracking expiration dates across drugs, certifications, and permits with tiered alerting is a data-modeling and pipeline problem well matched to an engineering background.
**MVP scope:** Build the permit/DEA/certification tracker and crash-cart expiration log in 2-3 weekends; sign the first 5-8 solo or small-group OMS practices sourced from a state oral surgery society directory.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribution through state components of the American Association of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgeons (AAOMS) and state dental board CE-provider mailing lists that OMS practices already subscribe to.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $399/mo per OMS practice (priced above generic dental tools given the anesthesia-permit stakes) × 35 practices = $13,965/mo.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** The biennial facility re-inspection, the recurring mock-drill schedule, and the 3-year DEA renewal are hard regulatory deadlines with practice-closure consequences if missed, which keeps the practice manager logging in continuously rather than treating the tool as a one-time setup.
**Biggest risk:** OMS is a narrow specialty with a smaller total practice count than general dentistry, capping near-term growth; mitigate by expanding into the larger pool of general dentists who separately hold IV sedation permits once the core OMS product and pricing are validated.
**Growth path:** Side project → 15 OMS practices (~$5,985/mo) → expand to sedation-permit general dentists → 35 total practices (~$13,965/mo) → replaces full-time salary.

### 3. Roller & Ice Skating Rink Operator Indoor Air-Quality, Fire-Code & Staff-Certification Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Indoor ice rinks that use fossil-fuel ice resurfacers (Zamboni-type machines) are subject to state and local indoor air-quality rules requiring documented, recurring carbon monoxide and nitrogen dioxide testing — several states (Minnesota Rule 4620.3400, Massachusetts 105 CMR 675, and similar rules in New York, Michigan, and Rhode Island) mandate specific testing frequency and maximum exposure thresholds following a string of CO-poisoning incidents at rinks nationwide. Both ice and roller rinks separately need annual fire marshal inspections tied to their assembly-occupancy permit, ADA/egress compliance documentation, and staff certifications for skate guards (CPR/AED) and resurfacer operators (equipment safety training). A rink that skips a testing interval or lets its assembly permit lapse risks closure by the local fire marshal or health department — and a documented CO incident is both a life-safety and liability event.
**What to build:** Air-quality test log (CO/NO2 readings by time and rink zone) with automatic threshold-breach alerts and testing-interval reminders; ice resurfacer maintenance and emissions-tuning log; fire marshal/assembly-occupancy inspection countdown; staff CPR/AED and resurfacer-operator certification tracker; skate-rental sanitation checklist logging where required by local health code.
**Skill fit:** State air-quality testing thresholds and frequency rules are published agency text that converts directly into a rules engine; time-series sensor-reading logs with threshold alerting are a natural fit for someone with a data-engineering background.
**MVP scope:** Build the air-quality log and inspection calendar in 2-3 weekends; recruit the first 5-8 rinks (ice and roller) through direct outreach and a free pilot offer.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribution through the U.S. Ice Rink Association (USIRA) member directory and state youth-hockey association rink-facility contact lists.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo per rink × 70 rinks = $13,930/mo, reachable across the roughly 1,800 ice rinks and 3,000 roller rinks operating in the U.S.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** CO/NO2 testing is required on a recurring schedule tied directly to insurance underwriting and liability exposure, making this a routine, high-frequency-use tool rather than an annual set-and-forget compliance form.
**Biggest risk:** The standalone-rink market is finite; mitigate by pricing per-facility high enough to be profitable at modest scale and by expanding into multi-rink regional operators and municipal parks-and-recreation departments running several facilities under one account.
**Growth path:** Side project → 20 rinks (~$3,980/mo) → add municipal multi-rink accounts → 70 rinks (~$13,930/mo) → replaces full-time salary.

### 4. Independent Dental Hygienist Direct-Access Practice Permit, CE & Collaborative-Agreement Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Over 40 states now permit some form of dental hygiene "direct access," letting registered dental hygienists (RDHs) deliver preventive and therapeutic care — cleanings, sealants, fluoride, screenings — in schools, nursing homes, and mobile clinics without an on-site supervising dentist. Examples include California's Registered Dental Hygienist in Alternative Practice (RDHAP) license and Colorado's independent-practice hygienist statute. These direct-access permits require a separate state application and renewal on top of the base RDH license, CE hours specifically allocated to the direct-access scope (distinct from general CE requirements), a filed collaborative or consultation agreement with a supervising dentist for cases needing referral, and per-facility access agreements plus background checks for each school district, nursing home, or mobile-clinic contract. Many states also require a distinct business license or fictitious-business-name filing for the independent practice and a state-set minimum liability insurance policy. An independent hygienist juggling five facility contracts and a collaborating dentist relationship has no consolidated view of which permit, agreement, or CE bucket is about to expire.
**What to build:** Direct-access/alternative-practice permit renewal tracker; CE-hour accumulator split between general and direct-access-specific requirements; collaborative/supervising-dentist agreement expiration and referral-log tracker; per-facility (school district, nursing home, mobile clinic) access-agreement and background-check renewal tracker; infection-control/OSHA certification renewal alerts.
**Skill fit:** State dental hygiene board direct-access statutes are public regulatory text with clear renewal cycles; tracking multiple concurrent facility agreements against one practitioner's calendar is a structured data-modeling problem suited to an engineer.
**MVP scope:** Build the permit/CE/agreement tracker in 2-3 weekends; recruit the first 5-8 independent hygienists through direct-access practice sections of state dental hygienists' associations.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribution through the American Dental Hygienists' Association (ADHA) state constituent societies and their independent/direct-access-practice special interest groups.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $69/mo per independent hygienist × 150 hygienists = $10,350/mo, achievable given the fast-growing direct-access hygienist population in expansion states.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Collaborative agreements and direct-access permits require periodic re-filing (commonly annual) and CE renews on a fixed state-mandated cycle — recurring compliance events rather than a one-time account setup.
**Biggest risk:** Direct-access rules vary enormously by state, so a single rules engine will misfire if applied broadly too soon; mitigate by launching first in the five states with the clearest, most mature direct-access frameworks (California, Colorado, Washington, Oregon, Minnesota) before expanding the rules engine state by state.
**Growth path:** Side project → 50 hygienists across 3 states (~$3,450/mo) → expand states → 150 hygienists (~$10,350/mo) → replaces full-time salary.

### 5. Self-Storage Facility Operator Lien-Notice, Auction & Inspection Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Small independent self-storage operators (1-5 locations, outside the large REIT chains that build their own software) must comply with state Self-Service Storage Facility Acts — adopted in nearly every state, such as California Business & Professions Code §21700-21716 and Texas Property Code Chapter 59 — which dictate exact required notice language, statutory notice windows (typically a default notice after a set delinquency period followed by a published auction notice with its own separate lead time), and lien-sale/auction procedures before a delinquent tenant's unit can legally be auctioned. Getting any part of the sequence wrong — wrong notice period, missing publication, wrong recipient address — voids the lien and exposes the owner to a conversion lawsuit from the tenant. Facilities separately need to track fire-code and sprinkler-system inspections tied to their occupancy permit, tenant-insurance certificate requirements, and local business-license renewals, plus hazardous-material storage-ban compliance in many jurisdictions.
**What to build:** Delinquent-tenant lien-timeline tracker that auto-calculates each state's required notice windows and generates compliant notice letters and auction listings from state-specific templates; auction publication-deadline tracker; fire/sprinkler inspection countdown tied to the occupancy permit; tenant-insurance certificate expiration tracker; local business-license renewal calendar.
**Skill fit:** State self-storage lien statutes are public text with explicit day-count rules that map directly onto a deadline-calculation engine — a strong match for someone comfortable turning regulatory text into structured logic.
**MVP scope:** Build the lien-notice calculator and auction calendar in 2-3 weekends; sign the first 5-8 independent facility owners through state self-storage association chapters.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribution through the Self Storage Association (SSA) state chapters and independent-operator Facebook and LinkedIn groups, a segment distinct from the large REIT operators who already run enterprise software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $139/mo per facility × 90 independently-owned facilities = $12,510/mo.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every newly delinquent unit triggers a fresh statutory notice/auction timeline, and fire and insurance inspections renew annually — continuous, non-optional events across a facility's tenant roster keep the account in active weekly use, not just an annual login.
**Biggest risk:** A defective lien notice creates real legal liability for the operator; mitigate by positioning the tool strictly as a documentation and calendar aid (not legal advice), building per-state notice templates from primary statute text, and having an attorney review the template library before launch.
**Growth path:** Side project → 25 facilities (~$3,475/mo) → add multi-location independent operators → 90 facilities (~$12,510/mo) → replaces full-time salary.
