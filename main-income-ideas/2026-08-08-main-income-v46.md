### 1. Scrap Metal & Secondhand Dealer State License, Daily Transaction Reporting & EPA Universal Waste Compliance
**Problem:** Scrap metal recyclers, secondhand dealers, and precious metal/jewelry buyers must hold a state scrap metal dealer or secondhand dealer license (administered by the state police, department of revenue, or a dedicated regulatory board depending on the state), renewed annually and typically requiring a surety bond. Layered on top, most cities and counties require dealers to record every transaction in detail — seller ID, item description, serial numbers where present, and in many jurisdictions a thumbprint or photo — and to report that transaction daily to a police-linked stolen-property database (LeadsOnline or BusinessWatch International are the two dominant platforms most local ordinances point to), often paired with a mandatory holding period (commonly 3-15 days) before the purchased item can be melted, processed, or resold, specifically to give victims of theft a window to recover stolen property. Missing a daily reporting deadline or violating a hold period is a licensing violation and, if the item turns out to be stolen, can expose the dealer to a receiving-stolen-property investigation. Separately, EPA classifies several waste streams scrap yards routinely handle — used lead-acid batteries pulled from scrapped vehicles, used oil drained during dismantling, and refrigerant recovered from air conditioners and refrigerators under 40 CFR Part 82 (EPA Section 608) — as universal waste or regulated refrigerant, each with specific accumulation time limits and handling requirements. A small scrap yard or secondhand dealer (1-5 locations) running daily police-database reporting, per-item hold-period tracking, and universal waste accumulation clocks without dedicated compliance staff risks both license suspension and environmental enforcement.
**What to build:** A state scrap/secondhand dealer license and bond portfolio (per location: license number, renewal deadline, bond carrier and amount — with tiered renewal alerts); a daily transaction reporting compliance tracker that confirms every day's purchases were submitted to the applicable local police reporting system (LeadsOnline/BusinessWatch or a municipal equivalent) by the required deadline, typically the next business day; a per-item hold-period release gate that blocks any purchased item from being melted, processed, or resold until the jurisdiction's mandated hold period has elapsed, calculated per item from its purchase date; a universal waste and refrigerant handling log (item type, accumulation start date, the applicable 180-day/270-day/365-day accumulation limit depending on generator category, and disposition to a permitted recycler); and an EPA Section 608 refrigerant recovery technician certification tracker confirming any staff who drain refrigerant before scrapping appliances or vehicle A/C systems hold current certification.
**Skill fit:** State secondhand/scrap dealer statutes and municipal transaction-reporting ordinances are public regulatory sources, as are EPA universal waste and Section 608 refrigerant rules. Building the daily reporting compliance tracker, the per-item hold-period release gate, and the waste accumulation clock are exactly the kind of deadline-and-record data management problems a software engineer's background is suited to. The Institute of Scrap Recycling Industries (ISRI) and its state chapters provide a concentrated, identifiable buyer community.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the state license/bond portfolio, a daily transaction reporting compliance tracker wired to the two dominant reporting platforms' submission formats, a per-item hold-period release gate, and a universal waste accumulation log; get the first 5-8 clients through ISRI state chapter outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — Institute of Scrap Recycling Industries (ISRI) state chapter member outreach, plus referral relationships with LeadsOnline and BusinessWatch International reseller/implementation partners who see yards with informal, error-prone daily reporting processes.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per dealer: $250-600/mo (a single-location secondhand dealer with light EPA exposure: $250/mo; a multi-location scrap yard handling universal waste and refrigerant recovery: $600/mo). Managing 25-35 clients at $425/mo average = **$10,625-14,875/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Daily transaction reporting is triggered by every purchase every operating day, per-item hold periods recur on every transaction, and universal waste accumulation clocks run continuously — this is a daily-use operational tool, not an annual-renewal checkbox.
**Biggest risk:** Reselling or melting a purchased item before its local hold period expires — because the hold period wasn't tracked per item — creates receiving-stolen-property exposure if the item is later identified as stolen. The per-item release gate blocking any item from being processed until its hold period elapses is the core protection.
**Growth path:** Side project → license/bond tracker, daily reporting compliance tracker, and hold-period release gate deployed, first 5-8 dealer retainers through ISRI outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-15 dealer retainers, LeadsOnline/BusinessWatch referral relationship established. Replaces-full-time-salary → 25-35 dealer retainers, pushing past $10.6-14.9K/mo.

### 2. Non-Bank Consumer Installment Lender State Licensing & Rate-Cap Compliance
**Problem:** Non-bank consumer installment lenders — storefront and online small-dollar and installment loan companies — must hold a state consumer lending or installment lender license in every state where they originate loans, most processed through NMLS (the same licensing system used by mortgage loan originators and money transmitters, but under the state's separate Consumer Finance/Installment Lending license category), each state specifying its own maximum APR or fee structure, permissible loan terms, and disclosure requirements layered on top of federal TILA (Truth in Lending Act, Regulation Z) APR disclosure requirements. Licensing requires annual renewal, a surety bond sized to loan volume, minimum net worth, and background-cleared responsible individuals named with the state. Because state rate caps vary enormously — some states cap installment loan APR near 36%, others use tiered rate structures that allow substantially higher rates on smaller loan amounts — a lender operating across 10-20 states must apply the correct state's rate cap and fee structure to every single loan it originates. A rate calculation error is a usury violation that can render the loan unenforceable and trigger consumer restitution obligations. Small lenders originating 5,000-50,000 loans a year across a multi-state footprint manage this rate-cap complexity on spreadsheets without a systematic per-state validation step before funding.
**What to build:** A multi-state consumer lending license portfolio (per state: license number, renewal deadline, bond carrier/amount, and net worth compliance status — with tiered renewal alerts); a state rate-cap and fee-structure reference engine (maximum APR, permissible fee types and amounts, and minimum/maximum loan term by state and loan-amount tier); a pre-origination compliance check that validates every proposed loan's APR and fee structure against the borrower's state before the loan is funded, blocking origination if the terms exceed the applicable cap; a TILA/Regulation Z disclosure documentation tracker confirming each loan file has the required federal disclosures on file; and a state regulatory change monitor tracking rate-cap and fee-structure statutory amendments by state.
**Skill fit:** State consumer lending statutes and rate caps are public regulatory sources (NMLS Consumer Access, state banking/financial regulator sites). Building the multi-state license portfolio, the rate-cap reference engine, and the pre-origination compliance check are exactly the kind of rules-engine and deadline-tracking data problems a software engineer's background is suited to. American Financial Services Association (AFSA) and the Online Lenders Alliance (OLA) provide a concentrated, identifiable buyer community.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a multi-state consumer lending license portfolio, a rate-cap and fee-structure reference database for the 20 highest-volume installment lending states, and a pre-origination compliance check; get the first 5-8 clients through AFSA/OLA member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — American Financial Services Association (AFSA) and Online Lenders Alliance (OLA) member directory outreach, and referral relationships with state consumer lending compliance attorneys who see rate-cap validation gaps at every multi-state expansion engagement.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per lender: $500-1,200/mo (a small single-state storefront installment lender: $500/mo; a multi-state online lender licensed in 15+ states: $1,200/mo). Managing 15-20 clients at $800/mo average = **$12,000-16,000/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every loan origination is a compliance event requiring rate-cap validation, state rate caps and fee structures are amended periodically requiring re-validation of the reference engine, and license renewals recur annually across every licensed state.
**Biggest risk:** Originating a loan whose APR or fee structure exceeds a state's rate cap — because the cap wasn't validated against the borrower's specific state and loan-amount tier — is a usury violation risking loan unenforceability and consumer restitution liability. The pre-origination compliance check blocking any loan that would exceed the applicable cap is the core protection.
**Growth path:** Side project → multi-state license portfolio and pre-origination compliance check deployed for 20 states, first 5-8 lender retainers through AFSA/OLA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 10-12 lender retainers, compliance attorney referral relationships established. Replaces-full-time-salary → 15-20 lender retainers, pushing past $12-16K/mo.

### 3. Medical Spa & Aesthetic Practice State Medical Board Delegation & Laser/Injectable Compliance
**Problem:** Medical spas offering laser hair removal, IPL, laser skin resurfacing, and injectables (Botox, dermal fillers) operate under state medical board physician-delegation and supervision rules that vary significantly by state: most states require a designated supervising or medical director physician (in some states an NP or PA with prescriptive authority may serve this role) who is responsible for approving written standing orders or protocols for each procedure type performed by non-physician staff, and many states impose specific on-site or reasonably-available proximity requirements for the supervising physician when energy-device procedures are performed by delegated staff — aestheticians, laser technicians, or RNs. Non-physician staff performing laser procedures must hold any state-required laser technician certification or registration (a growing number of states now require this), and the practice must maintain FDA 510(k) clearance documentation and device-specific operator training records for every laser or energy device on-site. A medical spa operating delegated non-physician staff without documented, current standing orders and staff certification is operating outside the state's supervision framework — an unlicensed-practice-of-medicine exposure that reaches the practice, the medical director, and the technician performing the procedure.
**What to build:** A medical director designation and standing order documentation system (per procedure type: the current standing order/protocol on file, medical director signature and date, and the state-required periodic review cadence — with review-due alerts); a non-physician staff credential tracker (laser technician certification/registration by state, RN or aesthetician license currency, and device-specific training completion per device on-site — with expiration alerts); a device compliance registry (FDA 510(k) clearance documentation per laser/energy device, device-specific operator training log, and maintenance/calibration schedule); and a procedure eligibility gate that, before any energy-device or injectable procedure is scheduled with a non-physician provider, confirms the provider holds current device training and the applicable standing order is on file and current.
**Skill fit:** State medical board delegation and supervision rules are public regulatory sources, compiled state-by-state by the American Med Spa Association's supervision matrix, and FDA device clearance data is public. Building the standing order documentation system, staff credential tracker, device registry, and eligibility gate are data and document management problems well suited to the builder's engineering background. AmSpa (American Med Spa Association) provides a concentrated, identifiable buyer community.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the medical director standing order system, non-physician staff credential tracker, device compliance registry, and procedure eligibility gate; get the first 5-8 clients through AmSpa outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — AmSpa (American Med Spa Association) member directory outreach and networking at The Medical Spa Show, the industry's primary annual gathering of med spa owners and medical directors.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per practice: $300-750/mo (a single-location spa with one medical director: $300/mo; a multi-location spa group with 3-5 locations and several non-physician providers: $750/mo). Managing 20-30 clients at $500/mo average = **$10,000-15,000/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Standing orders require periodic (typically annual) review, staff certifications renew on a rolling schedule, every new hire triggers an onboarding credential event, every new device triggers new clearance/training documentation, and every procedure booking runs through the eligibility gate — continuous daily usage, not an annual checkbox.
**Biggest risk:** A non-physician staff member performing an energy-device or injectable procedure without a current standing order or device certification — because the credential lapse wasn't caught before scheduling — creates unlicensed-practice-of-medicine exposure for the practice and medical director. The procedure eligibility gate blocking the booking until both are confirmed current is the core protection.
**Growth path:** Side project → standing order system, staff credential tracker, and procedure eligibility gate deployed, first 5-8 practice retainers through AmSpa outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 10-15 practice retainers, Medical Spa Show relationships established. Replaces-full-time-salary → 20-30 practice retainers, pushing past $10-15K/mo.

### 4. Propane/LP Gas Dealer State License, NFPA 58 & DOT Cylinder Compliance
**Problem:** Propane (LP gas) retailers delivering to residential, agricultural, and commercial tanks must hold a state LP gas dealer license — typically administered by the state fire marshal or department of agriculture — renewed annually, and in most states must ensure delivery drivers and service technicians hold specific employee training credentials, most commonly the National Propane Gas Association's Certified Employee Training Program (CETP) modules covering bobtail delivery, cylinder filling, and tank installation, each with its own state-mandated renewal interval. Compliance with NFPA 58 (the Liquefied Petroleum Gas Code, adopted by reference in most state fire codes) governs tank installation clearances, requires a visual leak check at every delivery plus more thorough periodic system inspections at defined intervals, and mandates odorization verification. Layered on top, DOT cylinder regulations require exchange cylinders to be requalified on a 5-12 year cycle depending on cylinder type, with the requalification date stamped on the cylinder, and delivery drivers must maintain current hazmat endorsement and CDL compliance. A propane dealer running 3-15 delivery trucks and thousands of residential and agricultural tank accounts, tracking driver CETP module currency, periodic tank inspection schedules, and cylinder requalification dates on paper, faces both state fire marshal enforcement exposure and genuine NFPA 58 safety liability.
**What to build:** A state dealer license and bond tracker (license number, renewal deadline, bond documentation — with tiered alerts); a driver/technician CETP module certification tracker (per employee: module completed, completion date, and the state-required renewal interval — with expiration alerts); a tank account periodic inspection calendar (per customer tank: last periodic inspection date, next-due date calculated from the applicable NFPA 58 interval, and leak-check documentation logged at every delivery); and a DOT cylinder requalification tracker (per exchange cylinder in the dealer's fleet: manufacture or last-requalification date, next-due date by cylinder type, and an out-of-service flag for any cylinder past its requalification window).
**Skill fit:** NFPA 58 and state fire marshal LP gas dealer rules are public regulatory sources, as are NPGA CETP module requirements and DOT cylinder requalification cycles. Building the license tracker, CETP certification tracker, tank inspection calendar, and cylinder requalification tracker are data management and scheduling problems well suited to the builder's engineering background. The National Propane Gas Association (NPGA) and its state affiliates provide a concentrated, identifiable buyer community.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the state dealer license tracker, a driver/technician CETP certification tracker, a tank account periodic inspection calendar, and a DOT cylinder requalification tracker with fleet-wide out-of-service flagging; get the first 5-8 clients through NPGA state affiliate outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — National Propane Gas Association (NPGA) state affiliate member outreach, and referral relationships with propane dealer commercial insurance carriers who require documented periodic tank inspection and CETP certification currency at policy renewal.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per dealer: $300-700/mo (a small dealer with 3 trucks: $300/mo; a larger dealer with 12 trucks and an active cylinder exchange program: $700/mo). Managing 25-35 clients at $475/mo average = **$11,875-16,625/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Periodic tank inspections recur on NFPA 58 intervals across thousands of accounts, cylinder requalification recurs per individual cylinder in the fleet, CETP module renewal recurs per employee, and delivery-time leak checks are logged daily — a continuous stream of non-optional compliance events.
**Biggest risk:** Delivering to a customer tank that is overdue for its periodic NFPA 58 inspection — where an undetected leak subsequently causes a fire or explosion — is a catastrophic liability exposure. The tank inspection calendar with a hard block on scheduling delivery to any overdue tank is the core protection.
**Growth path:** Side project → license tracker, CETP tracker, and tank inspection calendar deployed, first 5-8 dealer retainers through NPGA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-15 dealer retainers, insurance carrier referral relationship established. Replaces-full-time-salary → 25-35 dealer retainers, pushing past $11.9-16.6K/mo.

### 5. Crane & Rigging Company OSHA Subpart CC, NCCCO Certification & State License Compliance
**Problem:** Companies operating mobile cranes, tower cranes, and rigging services for construction and industrial projects must comply with OSHA's crane standard (29 CFR 1926 Subpart CC), which requires every crane operator to hold current certification from an accredited testing organization — most commonly NCCCO, the National Commission for the Certification of Crane Operators — in the specific crane type and capacity class operated, with certification renewing on a 5-year cycle requiring either a recertification exam or documented continuing education. A subset of states and major cities (New York City and Chicago among them) layer an additional state or local crane operator license on top of NCCCO certification. Separately, Subpart CC requires an annual inspection by a qualified person for every crane, more frequent monthly inspections documented in writing, and daily pre-shift inspection checklists, plus qualification documentation for riggers (who must meet specific competency criteria) and signal persons (who must pass a qualification test). A crane and rigging company running 10-40 cranes across multiple jurisdictions, tracking operator certification expirations, annual and monthly inspection documentation, and rigger/signal-person qualification records without dedicated safety staff, faces OSHA citation exposure — crane-related violations draw some of the agency's highest per-incident penalties — and is managing one of the leading causes of preventable construction fatalities.
**What to build:** An operator certification portfolio (per operator: NCCCO certification type and capacity class, expiration date, any state or local license required for specific jurisdictions, and 5-year recertification/CE tracking — with tiered renewal alerts); a crane inspection compliance log (per crane: annual qualified-person inspection date and report on file, monthly inspection log, and a daily pre-shift inspection checklist with deficiency flagging that places the crane out-of-service until the deficiency is corrected and documented); a rigger and signal-person qualification tracker (per employee: qualification basis and documentation, with any periodic requalification tracked); and a job-assignment eligibility gate that, before an operator is assigned to a specific crane and job, confirms current NCCCO certification for that exact crane type and capacity class and any state or local license required for the job's jurisdiction.
**Skill fit:** OSHA 29 CFR 1926 Subpart CC and NCCCO certification requirements are public regulatory sources, as are state and municipal crane operator licensing ordinances. Building the operator certification portfolio, inspection compliance log, rigger/signal-person tracker, and job-assignment eligibility gate are data management and scheduling problems well suited to the builder's engineering background. The Specialized Carriers & Rigging Association (SC&RA) provides a concentrated, identifiable buyer community.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the operator certification portfolio, a crane inspection compliance log covering annual/monthly/pre-shift requirements, a rigger and signal-person qualification tracker, and a job-assignment eligibility gate; get the first 5-8 clients through SC&RA outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — Specialized Carriers & Rigging Association (SC&RA) member directory outreach, and referral relationships with crane and rigging commercial insurance carriers who require documented operator certification and inspection currency at policy underwriting.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per company: $400-900/mo (a small 8-crane operation: $400/mo; a larger 30-crane multi-state operation with NYC or Chicago local licensing requirements: $900/mo). Managing 20-25 clients at $650/mo average = **$13,000-16,250/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Annual and monthly crane inspections recur continuously across the fleet, operator 5-year recertification cycles stagger across a company's full operator roster throughout the year, and every new job assignment runs through the eligibility gate — a continuous, operationally load-bearing compliance stream.
**Biggest risk:** An operator assigned to a crane or capacity class outside their current NCCCO certification, or a crane operated past its annual inspection due date, is both an OSHA citation and a credible catastrophic-failure risk. The job-assignment eligibility gate, which blocks any assignment until the operator's certification and the crane's inspection status are both confirmed current, is the core protection.
**Growth path:** Side project → operator certification portfolio, inspection compliance log, and job-assignment eligibility gate deployed, first 5-8 company retainers through SC&RA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 10-12 company retainers, insurance carrier referral relationship established. Replaces-full-time-salary → 20-25 company retainers, pushing past $13-16.25K/mo.

## This Session's Pick: #5 for Highest Ceiling and Safety Enforcement Urgency; #3 for Fastest-Growing Buyer Pool

**Highest ceiling and safety enforcement urgency:** Idea #5 (crane and rigging) combines the highest per-client retainer in this session with the sharpest catastrophic-failure risk of any idea here — crane incidents draw some of OSHA's largest per-violation penalties, and the job-assignment eligibility gate is a uniquely defensible product because it prevents the single most common root cause of a crane fatality (an under-certified operator on an over-capacity machine) rather than just documenting after the fact.

**Fastest-growing buyer pool:** Idea #3 (medical spa) sits in one of the fastest-growing segments of outpatient healthcare services, with new locations opening constantly and a genuinely under-served compliance gap — most med spa owners are estheticians or business operators rather than clinicians, and the medical-director delegation framework is exactly the kind of paperwork-heavy, high-stakes-if-wrong requirement that gets deprioritized until a state board inquiry forces the issue.

Idea #2 (installment lender) has the cleanest, most mechanical value proposition of the session — a rate-cap validation engine is unambiguous and easy to demo — but the buyer pool is smaller and more concentrated than the others, making the ramp to 15-20 clients slower than #3 or #5.
