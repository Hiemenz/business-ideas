### 1. Home Energy Rater & Weatherization Contractor RESNET HERS & DOE Weatherization Assistance Program Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Home energy raters who produce the HERS (Home Energy Rating System) Index Score builders rely on for the 2021 IECC's Energy Rating Index compliance path must hold current RESNET HERS Rater certification, and their rating provider is required to field-review a percentage of every rater's completed ratings as an ongoing quality-assurance (QA) sampling obligation — a rater whose certification lapses, or whose QA sampling rate falls below the provider's threshold, can have their ratings suspended mid-project, invalidating the energy-code compliance path a builder relied on for their certificate of occupancy. Weatherization contractors performing DOE Weatherization Assistance Program (WAP) work through a state weatherization agency operate under a parallel but separate framework: technicians must hold BPI (Building Performance Institute) Building Analyst or Envelope Professional certification, every completed unit must be tested against DOE's Standard Work Specifications (SWS) with a documented post-work blower-door and duct-leakage test, and a state-agency Quality Control Inspector (QCI) must sign off on the completed unit before the contractor can submit for reimbursement — a unit invoiced without a QCI sign-off on file, or a technician dispatched past their BPI certification's renewal window, is exactly the finding a state WAP monitoring visit is built to catch, and can suspend the contractor's entire WAP funding stream. A small rating/weatherization firm running several raters and technicians across a growing pipeline of new-construction ratings and WAP units manages per-rater RESNET certification and QA-sampling status, per-unit SWS test documentation, and QCI sign-off tracking on a mix of the rating software's own project file and a paper WAP job binder, with no systematic cross-check between which units are actually cleared to invoice and which are still awaiting QCI review.

**What to build:** A per-rater RESNET certification renewal and QA-sampling-rate tracker; a per-WAP-unit Standard Work Specification checklist with the required blower-door/duct-leakage test result logged against the specific unit; a per-technician BPI certification renewal tracker with a "cleared to weatherize" dispatch gate; and a QCI final sign-off tracker blocking a unit from being marked ready to invoice until sign-off is on file.

**Skill fit:** RESNET's QA-sampling requirements and DOE's Standard Work Specifications are published, structured standards-body and federal program data with concrete per-unit test requirements, and the per-unit "cleared to invoice" gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a residential energy-efficiency trade this repo has never addressed.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the RESNET/QA tracker, the WAP SWS checklist with test-result logging, and the QCI sign-off gate. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent rating firms and WAP subcontractors.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Building Performance Association (formerly Efficiency First) runs an active member directory, and RESNET's own rating-provider network reaches exactly the small independent raters and weatherization contractors currently tracking QA sampling and per-unit SWS testing without dedicated compliance software.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249-449/mo per firm depending on rater/technician headcount and WAP contract volume. 30-40 firms × $359/mo avg = $10,770-14,360/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every rater's QA-sampling status recurs continuously against every rating filed, every WAP unit generates its own SWS-test-and-QCI-sign-off event before it can be invoiced, and every new state WAP contract a firm wins adds a fresh unit-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the firm's ability to keep collecting reimbursement.

**Biggest risk:** A firm doing only a handful of new-construction ratings with no WAP contract has a comparatively lower-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by leading with the RESNET QA tracker as the core offer for every rating firm, with the WAP SWS/QCI tracker as a differentiator for firms holding a state weatherization contract.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 firms ($4.3K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 26 firms ($9.3K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 30-40 firms plus a builder-facing compliance-certificate export for firms serving multiple production homebuilders.

### 2. Wetland Delineation & Clean Water Act Section 404 Permitting Consultant Jurisdictional Determination & Mitigation-Monitoring Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Wetland delineation consultants who perform field delineations under the Army Corps of Engineers' 1987 Wetland Delineation Manual and its regional supplements, and who shepherd a developer's or landowner's project through the Army Corps' Section 404 permitting process, manage two independent regulatory clocks a missed deadline can quietly invalidate: an Approved Jurisdictional Determination (JD) is only valid for five years, and a project relying on an expired JD that hasn't been reverified can find its permit basis challenged mid-construction; and most small projects proceed under a Nationwide Permit (NWP) the Corps reissues — and can substantively revise — on its own roughly five-year cycle (most recently 2021), meaning a project authorized under a since-superseded NWP may require re-verification before work can lawfully continue. Every field delineation also generates its own routine/atypical/problem-area data sheet under the 1987 manual's methodology, and permits carrying a compensatory mitigation condition — permittee-responsible mitigation or a purchased mitigation-bank credit — typically require five to ten years of documented vegetation and hydrology monitoring against defined success criteria before the mitigation obligation is considered discharged. A small consulting firm running a dozen or more active projects across staggered JD-expiration dates, NWP-verification cycles, and multi-year mitigation-monitoring obligations manages this on a project manager's spreadsheet and a filing cabinet of field data sheets, and a project that proceeds on an expired JD, or a mitigation site that misses a required monitoring-year report, is exactly the finding a Corps compliance review or a citizen-suit challenge is built to catch.

**What to build:** A per-project Approved JD expiration tracker computing the five-year due date from the JD's approval date; a per-project Nationwide Permit verification tracker flagging any authorization relying on a since-superseded NWP; a field-data-sheet archive tying each site visit's routine/atypical/problem-area delineation record to the specific project; and a mitigation-monitoring tracker computing each required monitoring-year report's due date against the permit's success-criteria schedule.

**Skill fit:** The Army Corps' JD-validity period and NWP reissuance cycle are public, structured federal regulatory data with concrete numeric deadlines, and the per-project expiration-and-reverification computation is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an environmental-consulting niche distinct from the Phase I ESA coordination service already in this repo, whose contamination-screening framework never touches wetland jurisdiction at all.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the JD expiration tracker, the NWP verification tracker, and the mitigation-monitoring tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent wetland consulting firms.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Society of Wetland Scientists (SWS) and the Association of State Wetland Managers (ASWM) both run active member directories and chapter meetings reaching exactly the small independent consultants juggling staggered JD-expiration and mitigation-monitoring deadlines across a growing project portfolio.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per firm depending on active-project count and mitigation-monitoring obligations. 25-32 firms × $429/mo avg = $10,725-13,728/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every project's JD recurs on its own five-year clock, every mitigation site under permit generates a fresh monitoring-year report obligation for five to ten years, and every new project the firm takes on adds a fresh delineation-and-permitting record — a continuous, project-portfolio-driven compliance stream tied directly to the firm's ongoing exposure on every permit it has ever secured.

**Biggest risk:** A firm doing only one-off Preliminary JDs with no compensatory mitigation exposure has a comparatively lower-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads for firms managing several concurrent Approved JDs and mitigation-monitoring obligations, where the staggered multi-year calendar genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10 firms ($4.3K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 20 firms ($8.6K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 25-32 firms plus a mitigation-bank-credit tracking add-on for firms brokering credits on behalf of developer clients.

### 3. Building Commissioning Agent ASHRAE Guideline 0, Title 24 NRCC-CX & Functional Performance Testing Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Building commissioning agents who verify that a new commercial building's mechanical, electrical, plumbing, and control systems actually perform as designed operate under ASHRAE Guideline 0's multi-phase commissioning process — an Owner's Project Requirements/Basis of Design review, a design-phase review, a written commissioning plan, functional performance testing (FPT) of every commissioned system with a documented issue log, and a final commissioning report — a process most state energy codes now require for larger nonresidential buildings before occupancy, and one California's Title 24 Part 6 makes explicit by requiring a signed NRCC-CX compliance certificate filed with the local enforcement agency before certain building types can receive their certificate of occupancy. Every functional test failure must be logged, resolved, and re-tested before the system can be marked commissioned, and a building that reaches final inspection with an incomplete FPT report on file — or in California, with no NRCC-CX certificate filed — is exactly the finding a building department's final review is built to catch, a delay whose cost lands squarely on the commissioning agent's relationship with the general contractor and owner who hired them to prevent exactly that outcome. A small commissioning firm running a CCP-credentialed principal and several field engineers across a dozen or more concurrent projects manages per-project Cx-phase milestones, functional-test issue logs, and jurisdiction-specific filing requirements on a mix of spreadsheets and a shared drive of test reports, with no systematic cross-check between which projects have a complete, filed Cx record and which are still carrying open issues as their occupancy date approaches.

**What to build:** A per-project commissioning-phase tracker (OPR/BOD review, design review, Cx plan, FPT, final report) with milestones tied to the construction schedule; a functional-test issue log linking each failed test to its resolution and re-test before the system is marked commissioned; a jurisdiction-specific commissioning-requirement lookup flagging which projects require a filed compliance certificate (Title 24 NRCC-CX, ASHRAE 90.1 Section 4.2.5, or a local energy-code amendment); and a CxA/CCP credential renewal tracker for every commissioning agent on staff.

**Skill fit:** ASHRAE Guideline 0's phase structure and Title 24's NRCC-CX filing requirement are published, structured standards-body and state regulatory data, and the per-project phase-milestone tracker plus the issue-log-to-resolution gate are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a building-systems verification trade distinct from the structural special-inspection agency idea already in this repo, whose IBC Chapter 17 framework never touches mechanical or electrical functional performance at all.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the Cx-phase milestone tracker, the functional-test issue log, and the jurisdiction-requirement lookup with the credential tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent commissioning firms.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Building Commissioning Association (BCxA) runs an active member directory and regional chapter meetings reaching exactly the small independent commissioning firms currently tracking multi-phase Cx milestones and jurisdiction-specific filing requirements across a growing project load without dedicated compliance software.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $349-699/mo per firm depending on concurrent project count and credentialed-agent headcount. 22-28 firms × $499/mo avg = $10,978-13,972/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every project runs its own independent multi-phase Cx clock from OPR review through final report, every functional test failure generates a fresh issue-to-resolution record, and every new project the firm wins adds a fresh jurisdiction-requirement determination — a continuous, project-portfolio-driven compliance stream tied directly to the firm's billable project count.

**Biggest risk:** A firm working only in jurisdictions with no mandatory commissioning filing has a comparatively lower-stakes compliance surface. Mitigate by leading with the Cx-phase milestone tracker and issue log as the core offer for every firm, with the jurisdiction-filing lookup as a differentiator for firms working in California and other mandatory-Cx-filing states.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10 firms ($5K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 18 firms ($9K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 22-28 firms plus a multi-jurisdiction filing-requirement expansion tier for firms taking on projects in new states.

### 4. Central Sterile Processing / Endoscope Reprocessing Compliance for Ambulatory Surgery Centers & Specialty Clinics CRCST/CER, AAMI ST79 & Biological-Indicator Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** The sterile processing department (SPD) technicians who reprocess surgical instruments and flexible endoscopes for ambulatory surgery centers, endoscopy suites, and specialty procedure clinics operate under a technician-credential-and-per-load recordkeeping structure entirely separate from the facility's own CMS survey and state licensure clock: every technician handling sterilization or high-level disinfection must hold current CBSPD or HSPA (Healthcare Sterile Processing Association) CRCST/CER certification, renewed on its own continuing-education cycle, and AAMI ST79 requires that every sterilizer load containing an implant carry a documented negative biological indicator (BI) spore-test result before the implant can be released for use in a patient — an implant released on an unconfirmed or positive BI result is exactly the finding a state health-department survey or an accreditation-body inspection is built to catch, and one of the most severe possible sterile-processing failures given the direct patient-safety consequence. Flexible endoscopes carry their own parallel obligation under AAMI ST91: a per-scope reprocessing log — pre-cleaning, leak test, manual clean, high-level disinfection cycle, drying, and storage — with a lot-control number traceable back to the specific procedure the scope was used in, since a reprocessing failure discovered after the fact requires the facility to reconstruct exactly which patients were exposed to that scope. A small ASC or specialty clinic running several SPD technicians and a growing endoscope inventory manages per-technician CRCST/CER currency, per-load BI spore-test results, and per-scope reprocessing logs on a mix of the sterilizer's own printout log and a paper endoscope binder, with no systematic cross-check between a specific implant-containing load and whether its BI result actually cleared before the implant went into a patient.

**What to build:** A per-technician CRCST/CER certification renewal tracker with a "cleared to release implant loads" gate; a per-sterilizer-load biological indicator result log placing an automatic release hold on any implant-containing load until its BI result is confirmed negative; a per-endoscope reprocessing log with lot-control traceability tied to the specific procedure each scope was used in; and a recall-trace report that can pull every patient exposed to a specific scope or sterilizer load if a positive BI or a reprocessing failure surfaces after the fact.

**Skill fit:** AAMI ST79's implant-release BI requirement and AAMI ST91's per-scope reprocessing log are published, structured standards-body data with a concrete release-gate condition, and the per-load release-hold plus the per-scope traceability log are the same deterministic "block the action until the record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a sterile-processing compliance surface distinct from the ASC facility-licensure survey idea already in this repo, whose administrator-level licensing clock never touches a technician's own certification or a single load's BI result at all.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the technician certification tracker, the BI release-hold log, and the per-scope reprocessing/traceability log. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent ambulatory surgery centers and specialty endoscopy clinics.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Healthcare Sterile Processing Association (HSPA) runs an active member directory and regional chapter meetings, supplemented by Ambulatory Surgery Center Association (ASCA) vendor-outreach channels, reaching exactly the small facilities currently tracking BI results and scope reprocessing logs on paper with no systematic release-hold cross-check.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-549/mo per facility depending on SPD technician headcount and endoscope inventory size. 28-36 facilities × $409/mo avg = $11,452-14,724/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every technician's CRCST/CER certification renews on its own CE cycle, every sterilizer load generates a fresh BI-result-and-release-hold event, and every endoscope procedure adds a new reprocessing-log entry to that scope's traceability chain — a continuous, procedure-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the facility's ability to keep releasing instruments and scopes for patient use.

**Biggest risk:** A very small clinic doing only a handful of procedures a week with a single SPD technician has a comparatively lower-stakes tracking need a paper log can still handle. Mitigate by qualifying leads for ASCs and specialty clinics running several technicians and a growing endoscope inventory, where the per-load and per-scope volume genuinely outgrows manual tracking.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 facilities ($4.9K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 22 facilities ($9K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 28-36 facilities plus a state health-department survey-readiness export facilities can hand an inspector on request.

### 5. Wildfire Mitigation & Home-Hardening Contractor Defensible-Space Zone & IBHS Wildfire Prepared Home Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Contractors who perform defensible-space vegetation clearance and structural home-hardening retrofits in wildfire-urban-interface territory operate under state statutes like California's PRC 4291, which requires a documented clearance across three zones around a structure — 0-5 feet (ember-resistant, largely noncombustible), 5-30 feet (lean, clean, and green), and 30-100 feet (reduced fuel load) — with an inspection or self-certification a growing number of jurisdictions require before a property can obtain or renew wildfire-zone insurance coverage. Contractors performing the structural side of the work — Class A roofing, ember-resistant vents, enclosed eaves, and a noncombustible zone-0 perimeter — pursue certification against IBHS's (Insurance Institute for Business & Home Safety) Wildfire Prepared Home standard, a designation a growing number of insurers now tie directly to wildfire-zone policy renewal eligibility or a FAIR Plan mitigation credit, meaning a retrofit completed without the specific IBHS-required components documented can leave a homeowner short of the designation they paid for the work expecting to receive. Because defensible-space clearance is vegetation work, it must be redone every season as brush and grass regrow, while the structural retrofit is a one-time job — two very different recurrence patterns a contractor running a route of properties across a fire season has to track simultaneously. A small contractor running crews across dozens of properties currently tracks each property's zone-by-zone clearance status, its structural retrofit checklist progress toward IBHS designation eligibility, and the annual re-clearance due date on a paper route sheet and a phone's photo roll, and a property whose zone-0 clearance was never redocumented for the current season, or a retrofit submitted for IBHS designation missing one required component, is exactly the gap a jurisdiction's compliance inspection or an insurer's mitigation audit is built to catch.

**What to build:** A per-property defensible-space zone assessment tracker (0-5/5-30/30-100 ft) with a photo-documented pre/post clearance record and a jurisdiction-specific compliance certificate generator; an IBHS Wildfire Prepared Home retrofit checklist (roof class, vent type, eave enclosure, zone-0 material) gating whether a completed job is actually eligible for designation submission; a recurring annual re-clearance scheduler tied to each property's defensible-space obligation; and an insurer-facing compliance-certificate export homeowners can submit for a wildfire-zone policy renewal or FAIR Plan mitigation credit.

**Skill fit:** PRC 4291's zone-by-zone clearance requirements and IBHS's Wildfire Prepared Home component checklist are published, structured statutory and standards-body data, and the per-property annual re-clearance scheduler plus the retrofit-eligibility gate are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a wildfire-mitigation trade this repo has never addressed.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the defensible-space zone tracker with the certificate generator, the IBHS retrofit checklist, and the annual re-clearance scheduler. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent wildfire mitigation and home-hardening contractors.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Firewise USA community network and IBHS's own Wildfire Prepared Home contractor directory, supplemented by state Fire Safe Council chapters, reach exactly the small independent contractors currently tracking zone clearance and retrofit-component checklists on a paper route sheet across a growing wildfire-urban-interface client base.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-399/mo per contractor depending on crew size and property volume in wildfire-urban-interface territory. 40-55 contractors × $289/mo avg = $11,560-15,895/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every property's defensible-space clearance must be redone every fire season for as long as the contractor services it, every structural retrofit generates its own one-time IBHS-eligibility documentation event, and insurer demand for both keeps expanding as more western-state insurers tie coverage to mitigation status — a continuous, property-portfolio-and-season-driven compliance stream tied directly to homeowners' ability to keep their wildfire-zone insurance in force.

**Biggest risk:** A contractor working only outside any wildfire-urban-interface zone has essentially no market for this offer. Mitigate by qualifying leads for contractors already operating in California, Colorado, Oregon, and other states with active PRC-4291-style statutes and a large IBHS-participating insurer base.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 contractors ($4.3K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 35 contractors ($10.1K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 40-55 contractors plus a multi-property portfolio dashboard for contractors serving HOAs and community wildfire-protection-plan territories.
