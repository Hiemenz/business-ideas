### 1. Secondary-Market Ticket Resale Broker Multi-State Reseller Registration & All-In Pricing Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Professional ticket resale brokers — running a real multi-state operation across sports, concert, and theater inventory rather than occasional personal reselling — operate under a patchwork of state ticket-reseller statutes: New York's Arts and Cultural Affairs Law §25.07 requires broker registration with the Secretary of State and bans bot-assisted bulk purchasing (paralleling the federal BOTS Act of 2016), Illinois's Ticket Sale and Resale Act (815 ILCS 414) requires total-price disclosure before a buyer completes checkout and bans undisclosed "speculative ticketing" (selling a ticket the broker doesn't yet possess), and several states require a broker to hold a specific dollar amount in bond or trust to cover consumer refund claims. The FTC's 2024 Junk Fees Rule now layers a federal all-in-pricing requirement for live-event tickets on top of whatever a given state already mandates, and a listing that shows a base price with fees revealed only at the final checkout step — even briefly, even by an automated feed pushed to a marketplace — is exactly the finding a state attorney general's consumer-protection sweep or an FTC complaint is built to catch. A broker selling into a dozen or more states through their own site and multiple marketplace feeds currently tracks which states they hold active registration or bond coverage in, whether every live listing actually displays all-in pricing before checkout, and what refund-guarantee language applies to each sale on a shared spreadsheet, with a lapsed state registration or a marketplace feed that reverts to base-price display discovered only when a state cease-and-desist letter or a wave of chargebacks arrives.

**What to build:** A multi-state reseller-registration and bond-coverage currency tracker flagging any state the broker actively sells into without current registration or bond coverage on file; an all-in-pricing compliance checklist verifying every live listing displays total price (ticket plus all fees) before an ad is published or pushed to a marketplace feed; and a per-sale refund-guarantee documentation log tied to event-cancellation and ticket-invalidity claims.

**Skill fit:** Multi-state ticket-reseller registration requirements and each state's all-in-pricing mandate are public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-state, per-listing compliance trigger, and the "block the listing until pricing/registration checks clear" gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a live-event ticket resale niche that does not exist anywhere else in this repo.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the multi-state registration/bond tracker, the all-in-pricing listing checklist, and the refund-guarantee documentation log. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to professional resale brokerages.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the National Association of Ticket Brokers' (NATB) member directory reaches exactly the professional multi-state resale operations currently reconciling registration status and pricing-disclosure compliance on a spreadsheet with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149-249/mo per brokerage depending on the number of states sold into. 40-55 brokerages × $200/mo avg = $8,000-11,000/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new state a broker expands sales into reopens the registration-and-bond question, every event listed reopens the all-in-pricing check before it goes live, and every marketplace feed added multiplies the number of listings that need the same pricing-compliance verification — a continuous, expansion-driven compliance stream tied directly to the broker's own sales growth.

**Biggest risk:** A broker selling exclusively through a single marketplace (like StubHub) that already enforces all-in pricing platform-wide has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward brokers selling directly through their own site and multiple marketplace feeds across several states, where the registration-and-pricing compliance load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 brokerages ($3K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 28 brokerages ($5.6K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 40-55 brokerages plus a state-AG-inquiry-ready registration-and-pricing-compliance export a broker can produce the moment a consumer-protection complaint arrives.

### 2. Household Goods Moving Broker FMCSA Part 371 Trust/Bond & Carrier-Verification Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Household goods moving brokers — who arrange a consumer's move but never operate the truck themselves — register under an entirely separate FMCSA framework from the motor carriers they dispatch shipments to: 49 CFR Part 371's broker rules require a distinct broker operating authority, a $75,000 trust fund or surety bond (BMC-84 or BMC-85) sized and renewed independently of any carrier's cargo insurance, and mandatory delivery of FMCSA's "Ready to Move" brochure and "Your Rights and Responsibilities When You Move" consumer booklet at the time of every written estimate under 49 CFR 375.213. FMCSA has aggressively revoked broker operating authority since its 2019 "rogue mover" enforcement crackdown for exactly two failures: a lapsed trust/bond, and a broker misrepresenting itself as a carrier or failing to clearly disclose broker status to the consumer — either one is exactly the finding an FMCSA compliance review is built to catch, and losing broker authority shuts down the business overnight. Every shipment a broker dispatches also has to go to a carrier holding current, verified FMCSA operating authority and insurance — dispatching to a carrier whose authority has lapsed exposes the broker to liability and its own FMCSA sanction the moment that shipment is inspected or a claim is filed. A small brokerage running dozens of monthly move referrals across several carrier partners currently tracks its own trust/bond currency, per-quote booklet-delivery compliance, and each carrier partner's authority/insurance status on a spreadsheet and manual SAFER-database lookups, with a bond lapse or an unverified carrier referral discovered only during an FMCSA audit or after a shipment already went out.

**What to build:** A trust fund/surety bond coverage currency tracker with renewal alerts well ahead of expiration; a per-quote consumer-booklet delivery compliance log confirming the required FMCSA disclosures were sent before an estimate is finalized; and a per-referral carrier-authority verification tool cross-checking each carrier's FMCSA registration and insurance status against the public SAFER database before a shipment is dispatched.

**Skill fit:** FMCSA broker-registration requirements and the public SAFER carrier-lookup database are structured, machine-checkable regulatory data supporting a concrete per-referral verification gate — the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly — applied to a broker-specific compliance surface (trust/bond currency, consumer-booklet delivery, carrier-authority verification) entirely distinct from the Interstate Household Goods Moving Company idea already in this repo (Session 52), whose data model instead tracks a licensed motor carrier's own driver qualification files, cargo insurance, and DOT vehicle inspections — fields a broker, who never operates a truck, has no use for at all.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the bond-currency tracker, the booklet-delivery compliance log, and the carrier-authority verification tool. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to newly FMCSA-registered household goods brokers via FMCSA's public broker-registration database.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — FMCSA's public broker-registration filings surface exactly the newly licensed brokerages who haven't yet built a system for tracking bond currency and carrier verification, reachable through direct outreach and moving-industry operator communities.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249-399/mo per brokerage depending on monthly referral volume. 28-35 brokerages × $320/mo avg = $8,960-11,200/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every shipment referred reopens the carrier-authority verification check, the trust/bond renewal recurs on its own annual clock for as long as the brokerage operates, and every new carrier partner added to the referral network compounds the number of authority statuses being tracked simultaneously — a continuous, referral-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the brokerage's own growth.

**Biggest risk:** A very small brokerage referring a handful of moves a month to one long-trusted carrier has a comparatively low-stakes verification need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward brokerages dispatching across multiple carrier networks and states, where the carrier-verification load genuinely outgrows manual SAFER lookups.

**Growth path:** Side project → 14 brokerages ($4.5K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 22 brokerages ($7K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 28-35 brokerages plus an FMCSA-audit-ready bond-and-carrier-verification export a brokerage can produce the moment a compliance review notice arrives.

### 3. Equine Boarding & Riding Stable Operator Liability-Act Sign, Contract & Coggins/EIA Entry-Gate Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Boarding and riding stable operators depend on their state's Equine Activity Liability Act (adopted in roughly 47 states) for limited immunity from injury lawsuits arising from the inherent risks of being around horses — but that immunity applies only if the stable posts the state's exact statutorily-required warning-sign language at every entrance, arena, and boarding area, and includes that same statutory language verbatim in every boarding agreement and lesson contract; a sign that's outdated, missing, or worded even slightly off the statute, or a contract that omits the required language, voids the stable's liability protection entirely the moment a boarder, visitor, or lesson student is injured, exposing the owner to a full negligence suit the immunity exists specifically to prevent. Separately, nearly every state requires a current negative Coggins test (an Equine Infectious Anemia blood test, documented on USDA VS Form 10-11 and typically valid 6-12 months) on file for any horse boarded, competing, or crossing state lines, and any horse leaving the property temporarily for a show, sale, or veterinary visit needs a current Certificate of Veterinary Inspection (CVI) for interstate travel — a stable that lets an untested horse comingle with the herd risks a disease outbreak and a state agriculture department violation, and a horse sent to a show without a current CVI can be turned away at the gate. A stable running dozens of boarded horses with regular seasonal turnover currently tracks Coggins expiration dates, CVI issuance for horses coming and going, and liability-sign/contract-language currency on a barn office clipboard and a filing cabinet, with a lapsed Coggins triggering a herd quarantine risk, or a missing sign voiding the stable's entire legal defense, discovered only after an incident has already happened.

**What to build:** A per-horse Coggins/EIA test expiration tracker with an entry gate blocking any new or returning horse from being logged as boarded until a current test is on file; a per-departure CVI/health-certificate tracker flagging any horse leaving the property without current interstate-travel documentation; and a liability-sign-and-contract-language currency checklist matched to the stable's specific state's statutory text, flagging any sign or contract template that predates a law update.

**Skill fit:** State Equine Activity Liability Act statutory sign language and each state's Coggins-test interval are public, structured legal and veterinary-regulatory data with a concrete per-horse entry trigger, and the "block boarding until the test clears" gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a boarding-stable liability-and-disease-testing compliance surface entirely distinct from the Horse Racing Trainer & Stable Operator idea already in this repo (Session 130), whose HISA medication-withdrawal clock and racing-commission license govern horses actively entered to race with no boarding-liability-sign or entry-testing concept at all, and from the recreational Equine Riding Instructor and independent Farrier/Equine Dental Technician side-income ideas already in this repo, none of which tracks a liability-statute sign requirement or a disease-test entry gate of any kind.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the Coggins/EIA entry-gate tracker, the CVI departure tracker, and the liability-sign/contract-language checklist. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to commercial boarding stables with active lesson programs.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the American Horse Council's state-chapter network (individual state Horse Councils) reaches exactly the commercial boarding and riding stables currently reconciling liability-sign currency and per-horse Coggins status on a barn clipboard with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $129-249/mo per stable depending on boarded-horse count. 50-65 stables × $180/mo avg = $9,000-11,700/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new boarder reopens the Coggins-entry-gate question, every horse leaving and returning to the property reopens CVI tracking, and any update to the stable's state liability statute or contract template reopens the sign-and-contract-language compliance check — a continuous, boarder-turnover-driven compliance stream tied directly to the stable's own occupancy.

**Biggest risk:** A very small hobby stable boarding only the owner's own horses with no outside boarders or lesson students has a comparatively low-stakes liability exposure. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward commercial boarding stables with active boarder turnover and lesson programs, where the liability-and-disease-testing load genuinely outgrows a barn clipboard.

**Growth path:** Side project → 20 stables ($3.6K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 38 stables ($6.8K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 50-65 stables plus a liability-defense-ready sign-and-contract-language export a stable owner can hand their insurer or attorney the moment an incident is reported.

### 4. Kombucha & Fermented Beverage Producer Per-Batch ABV Threshold & Shelf-Life Retest Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Kombucha and other live-culture fermented beverages keep fermenting after bottling — residual sugar and yeast activity, accelerated by warm shipping or storage conditions, can push a batch's alcohol content upward over weeks on a distributor's truck or a retailer's cooler shelf — and any batch that drifts over the 0.5% ABV threshold under the Federal Alcohol Administration Act instantly becomes a federally regulated "alcohol beverage" requiring a TTB Brewer's Notice or Basic Permit, COLA label approval, and excise-tax compliance the producer never obtained, exactly the finding that has forced major retailers to pull national kombucha brands from shelves after independent lab testing found products exceeding the legal threshold. A producer selling as non-alcoholic has to maintain rigorous batch-level ABV testing at multiple checkpoints — immediately post-primary-fermentation, again post-bottling, and periodically on shelf-life samples pulled back from actual distribution — since a single untested batch that drifts over 0.5% and reaches a retail cooler is exactly the fact pattern a state ABC or TTB spot inspection is built to catch, with consequences ranging from a mandatory recall to retroactive TTB permitting obligations for product already sold. A small producer running multiple SKUs and flavor batches per week currently tracks batch ABV lab results, shelf-life retest scheduling, and fermentation-start dates on a shared spreadsheet and a folder of emailed lab reports, with no systematic cross-check confirming every batch currently sitting on a retail shelf actually has a current, passing ABV test on file.

**What to build:** A per-batch ABV test-result tracker gating a batch's release to distribution until a passing lab result (under 0.5%) is logged at each required checkpoint; a shelf-life retest scheduler flagging any batch still in market approaching its next required retest date; and a recall-readiness lot-to-retailer traceability log tying each batch code to every distribution destination it shipped to.

**Skill fit:** The 0.5% ABV threshold and TTB's kombucha testing guidance are a single, numerically precise, publicly documented compliance trigger, and the batch-release gate plus shelf-life retest scheduler are the same deterministic "block the action until the test clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a live-fermentation beverage category entirely distinct from every craft brewery, winery, distillery, and cidery idea already in this repo — all TTB-permitted intentional-alcohol producers from day one of production with no ABV-drift detection problem of any kind — and from the Small-Batch Bottled Water & Water Bottling Plant Operator idea already in this repo (Session 130), an FDA-regulated non-fermented packaged product with no fermentation, no ABV drift, and no TTB relationship whatsoever.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the per-batch ABV gate, the shelf-life retest scheduler, and the lot-to-retailer traceability log. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small kombucha and fermented-beverage producers distributing through retail coolers.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — Kombucha Brewers International's (KBI) member directory reaches exactly the small producers currently reconciling batch ABV test results and shelf-life retest schedules on a spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-349/mo per producer depending on SKU count and weekly batch volume. 40-50 producers × $270/mo avg = $10,800-13,500/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every batch brewed reopens the ABV-test gate before it can ship, every new SKU added compounds the shelf-life retest schedule, and a growing retail distribution footprint increases the recall-traceability surface that has to stay current — a continuous, batch-and-distribution-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the producer's own growth.

**Biggest risk:** A very small home-based producer selling only fresh at a single weekly farmers market with no shelf-stored retail inventory has a comparatively low-stakes drift-testing need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward producers distributing through retail coolers and multiple SKUs, where the shelf-life retest and traceability load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 18 producers ($4.9K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 32 producers ($8.6K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 40-50 producers plus a recall-ready lot-traceability export a producer can generate the moment a shelf-life retest comes back over threshold.

### 5. Property Preservation & REO Field Services Contractor Investor-Timeline & Vacant-Property-Registration Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Property preservation contractors — subcontractors hired by national field-service companies on behalf of mortgage servicers, HUD, Fannie Mae, and Freddie Mac to secure, maintain, and winterize vacant and foreclosed properties — operate under two independent, overlapping deadline regimes that never appear on the same calendar. The investor side sets rigid response and recurrence timelines: HUD's Single Family Housing Policy Handbook 4000.1 property preservation requirements commonly mandate an initial inspection within 24-48 hours of a vacancy determination, initial securing (boarding, re-keying) within a similarly tight window, and then recurring maintenance — lawn cuts, debris removal, seasonal winterization — on a fixed cycle (commonly every 20-30 days) for as long as the property stays in inventory, with Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac servicing guides imposing their own similar but not identical cycle lengths and per-visit photo-documentation requirements. The municipal side is entirely separate: hundreds of individual cities and counties require a vacant or foreclosed property to be registered with code enforcement within a specific number of days of a vacancy determination or a recorded notice of default, with an annual renewal fee and escalating daily fines for a late or missing filing — fines that get billed straight back to whichever contractor is on record maintaining the property. A missed investor maintenance cycle risks contract termination from the field-service company — the contractor's entire revenue source — while a missed municipal registration deadline in any one of the dozens of cities a contractor services generates a fine passed directly to the contractor. A small contractor running work orders across several counties currently tracks per-property inspection/securing/maintenance-cycle deadlines and city-by-city registration deadlines on a spreadsheet and a paper work-order stack from the field-service company's dispatch portal, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific property's municipal registration is current before a fine posts.

**What to build:** A per-property investor-deadline tracker computing each work order's inspection, securing, and recurring-maintenance due dates from its vacancy-determination date and the specific investor's cycle length; a city-by-city vacant-property-registration deadline and annual-renewal tracker matched to each property's exact jurisdiction; and a photo-documentation compliance checklist confirming every required before/after photo is captured and logged before a visit is submitted for invoicing.

**Skill fit:** HUD/Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac property preservation timelines and municipal vacant-property-registration ordinances are public, structured, jurisdiction-specific regulatory data with concrete per-property deadlines, and the "compute the deadline, gate the invoice" pattern is the same one this series builds repeatedly, applied to a physical-property-maintenance compliance surface entirely distinct from the Mortgage Servicer idea already in this repo (Session 37), whose CFPB Regulation X compliance core governs a still-performing or delinquent loan's payment processing and loss-mitigation communication timeline for a servicer that never itself dispatches a crew to a property at all.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the investor-deadline tracker, the municipal registration tracker, and the photo-documentation compliance checklist. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent property preservation subcontractors.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the National Association of Mortgage Field Services' (NAMFS) member and vendor directory reaches exactly the small subcontractors currently reconciling investor cycles and municipal registration deadlines on a spreadsheet with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-349/mo per contractor depending on active-property-portfolio size across jurisdictions. 32-42 contractors × $270/mo avg = $8,640-11,340/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new work order reopens the investor-deadline clock, every property located in a new city reopens the municipal-registration question, and a growing multi-investor, multi-jurisdiction portfolio compounds the number of deadlines tracked simultaneously — a continuous, portfolio-driven compliance stream tied directly to the contractor's own work volume.

**Biggest risk:** A contractor working only a handful of properties for a single investor in one county has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward contractors servicing multiple field-service companies across several counties or states, where the investor-and-municipal-deadline load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 16 contractors ($4.3K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 26 contractors ($7K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 32-42 contractors plus a field-service-audit-ready investor-and-municipal-compliance export a contractor can produce the moment a vendor-performance review arrives.
