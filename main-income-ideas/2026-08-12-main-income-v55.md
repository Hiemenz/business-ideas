### 1. Deferred Service Recovery for Auto Repair Shops
**Problem:** A customer brings their car in for an oil change. The technician inspects it and finds that the brake pads are at 20% life, the cabin air filter is clogged, and the front CV axle boots are cracked. The service advisor writes up the recommendations, quotes them, and the customer says: "Just the oil change today, I'll come back for the rest." The service advisor accepts that, completes the oil change, and the three deferred recommendations disappear into the shop management software as declined items. Three months later, the customer is back for another oil change — and there is a 35-40% chance that no one in the shop reminds them about the brake pads, now at 10% life and overdue. The industry calls this "deferred work" and it represents an estimated $47 billion in annually unrecaptured service revenue across the US auto repair market. An independent shop or small regional chain running $800K-$2.5M in annual service revenue has, at any given time, $200,000-600,000 in deferred work sitting in completed repair orders — visible in the shop management software if someone runs a report, but invisible in daily operations because no one is systematically following up. The same operational problem as veterinary declined estimates (Session 54) but a different buyer (auto shop owner vs. practice manager), a different software ecosystem (Tekmetric, Mitchell1, Shop-Ware vs. ezyVet), and a different reactivation logic: auto service has a mileage and time-interval component that makes the follow-up timing precise — a brake job deferred at 45,000 miles is urgently due by 55,000 miles, not just "whenever the customer comes back."
**What to build:** A deferred service recovery tool that integrates with the shop's management software via API — Tekmetric has a well-documented REST API; Mitchell1 and Shop-Ware both have integration frameworks — pulling declined line items from closed repair orders with the vehicle's mileage at time of service. Core output: a deferred work queue showing every customer with open declined items, sorted by recency and estimated urgency (brake-related items sorted above cabin filters, since the safety implication drives more urgent reactivation). A mileage-based urgency calculator: for deferred items with a recommended service interval (brakes typically at 12 months or 12,000 miles, whichever comes first), the tool estimates the customer's current mileage from their service history mileage intervals and flags when they're likely approaching the point where the deferred service is overdue. An automated outreach sequence: 30 days after a deferred item, a personalized SMS ("Hi [name], your [year] [make model] had some services recommended at your last visit — your brake pads were at 20% and are likely due now. Want to schedule?") with a one-click booking link integrated with the shop's scheduling system. A monthly deferred work revenue report for the shop owner: total value of deferred work outstanding, total value recovered in the last 30 days, recovery rate by service category (brakes vs. engine services vs. maintenance vs. tires), and a list of the top 20 highest-value open deferred items ready for personal outreach.
**Skill fit:** Tekmetric API integration, repair order and declined line item data models, mileage interval estimation from historical service records, SMS automation via Twilio, and a dashboard with a revenue pipeline view. The mileage-based urgency model is the most interesting engineering piece — it requires estimating current mileage from the customer's last 2-3 service records and applying service interval logic per item type. No ML required — a simple extrapolation model (average miles/month from history × months since last service) gets to 80% accuracy.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — Tekmetric API integration pulling declined line items from closed repair orders in the last 90 days, a deferred work queue ranked by value and recency, and the 30-day automated SMS outreach sequence. A shop owner who sees $280,000 in open deferred work for the first time — and recovers $18,000 of it in the first month — has validated the product in 30 days.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — auto repair shop owners are reachable through the Automotive Service Excellence (ASE) community, NAPA AutoCare center owner network (6,000+ member shops), the Tekmetric user community and conference, AutoLeap and Shop-Ware user groups, and auto repair shop owner Facebook groups (Auto Shop Success, Shop Owner Mastery). A Tekmetric integration listed in their app marketplace generates organic discovery from the 4,000+ shops using the platform.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-499/month per shop depending on repair order volume. 35 shops at $325/month = **$11,375/month**. Upsell: a declined quote re-engagement module ($99/month add-on) — for customers who were given an estimate but never approved it (not just deferred items in closed orders, but full estimates sent to the customer that were never responded to), a structured follow-up sequence with a 10% discount offer at the 7-day mark and a final urgency message at 14 days.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every repair order generates new deferred items. The outreach sequences fire continuously without human intervention. The deferred work queue is always active because customers decline services at every visit across every vehicle type. Switching means abandoning the deferred work history and mileage model built on 90+ days of repair order data — something a shop owner doesn't accept once they're seeing recovery revenue from the tool.
**Biggest risk:** Tekmetric API changes or rate limiting on high-volume shops. Mitigation: build a CSV import fallback for shops not on Tekmetric, cache all repair order data locally after the initial pull, and build incremental sync to minimize API calls after the backfill.
**Growth path:** Side project → Tekmetric integration, deferred work queue, 30-day outreach sequence, and monthly recovery report live, first 5-8 shop clients. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-18 shops, add Mitchell1 and Shop-Ware integrations, add mileage-based urgency calculator, add declined quote re-engagement module. Replaces-full-time-salary → 35+ shops, add fleet account deferred service tracking (commercial fleets with 10-50 vehicles are a shop's highest-value accounts and have structured PM intervals that make the urgency model very precise), expand to tire shops and dealership service departments with identical deferred work dynamics.

### 2. Driver Pay Settlement Transparency for Small Trucking Companies
**Problem:** A small trucking company with 8-25 drivers pays each driver on a cents-per-mile basis with a layered structure: a base rate per loaded mile, a fuel surcharge component, accessorial pay (detention time when a shipper holds the driver waiting, layover pay when the driver can't get a load home), performance bonuses, and deductions for violations, cargo claims, or advance repayments. The weekly settlement — the driver's pay statement — is calculated manually by the dispatcher or owner every Friday, using data from the transportation management system (TMS), the ELD device (electronic logging device, which tracks miles), and a pile of paper receipts for detention and layover claims. The process takes 3-4 hours per week, errors are common, and when a driver receives their settlement and disputes a line item ("I drove 1,840 miles this week, not 1,720" or "You didn't pay my detention at the Riverside terminal"), the process of investigating and resolving the dispute takes another hour and involves hunting through email threads, load board paperwork, and ELD logs. The result: drivers feel they're being underpaid even in weeks when the math is correct, because they can't verify the settlement against their own records. This distrust is a significant contributor to trucking's astronomical driver turnover rate — industry-wide around 90-100% annually at large carriers, but still 40-60% at small companies — with each driver departure costing the company $5,000-15,000 in rehiring and training costs.
**What to build:** A driver pay settlement transparency tool — integrating with the TMS (Axele, Tailwind TMS, McLeod) and ELD provider (Samsara, KeepTruckin/Motive, ELD Mandate) via API to pull miles driven per load, load details, and hours-of-service data. The settlement calculation engine: configurable pay rules per driver (base CPM, fuel surcharge formula, accessorial rates), applied against the week's load data to produce a line-item settlement — every mile, every load, every accessorial, every deduction shown as a separate line with the source data that generated it. A driver-facing settlement review app: each Friday, the driver receives an SMS with a link to their settlement statement on a mobile-optimized page showing every line item with the source (the load number, the ELD mileage record, the detention time stamp from the shipper). A dispute submission button on any line item: the driver taps "Dispute" on the line they think is wrong, enters their counter-claim, and submits. The owner receives the dispute with both the company's calculation and the driver's counter-claim side by side. A settlement history: the driver can see every prior settlement, compare weekly trends, and understand how their pay has changed over time.
**Skill fit:** TMS and ELD API integrations — Samsara and Motive have strong APIs; Axele and Tailwind both have developer documentation. The settlement calculation engine is the core product: a configurable rules engine that maps load data to pay calculations. The driver-facing app is a read-only mobile web view — no native app required. The dispute workflow is a simple state machine (submitted → under review → resolved). Strong alignment with data engineering: aggregating data from two or three APIs (TMS + ELD + optionally fuel card data from Comdata or EFS) into a coherent settlement statement is exactly the kind of data pipeline problem the builder can build fast.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — Samsara ELD API integration pulling miles per driver per week, a configurable CPM pay rule, a line-item settlement calculation, and a mobile-optimized settlement review page that the driver can access via SMS link. An owner who goes from spending 4 hours on Friday calculating settlements to 30 minutes reviewing an auto-generated draft — and whose drivers stop calling to dispute their pay — has validated the product in week 1.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — small trucking company owners are reachable through the Owner-Operator Independent Drivers Association (OOIDA) community, the FreightWaves and Trucking Info news communities, trucking-focused Facebook groups (Small Trucking Company Owners, Trucking Business and Beyond — 50,000+ members), and the Samsara and Motive user communities. Trucking dispatch software companies (Axele, Tailwind) have app marketplaces and integration partner programs that generate organic discovery.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-549/month per company depending on driver count. 30 companies at $350/month = **$10,500/month**. Upsell: a driver performance and scorecard module ($99/month add-on) — a weekly driver scorecard showing miles driven, fuel efficiency, on-time delivery rate, and safety events (hard braking, speeding from ELD data), giving the owner visibility into driver performance beyond just mileage and reducing the subjective disputes ("I'm working hard but you're not paying me what I'm worth") with objective data.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Driver pay is calculated every single week. The settlement engine runs every Friday across every driver. The ELD and TMS integrations are live data connections that refresh continuously. Switching means rebuilding the configurable pay rules and losing the settlement history — which drivers reference when disputing current settlements based on prior-week precedents.
**Biggest risk:** TMS fragmentation — small trucking companies use dozens of different TMS platforms. Mitigation: start with the Samsara ELD integration (covering 15,000+ trucking companies) for mileage data and a CSV import from any TMS for load details. The ELD mileage data is the most dispute-prone component; load-level detail is secondary and can be manually entered in V1.
**Growth path:** Side project → Samsara integration, CPM settlement calculation, mobile driver review page, and dispute workflow live, first 5-8 trucking company clients. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-18 companies, add Motive (KeepTruckin) ELD integration, add Axele and Tailwind TMS integrations, add driver scorecard module. Replaces-full-time-salary → 30+ companies, add fuel card integration (Comdata API) for fuel surcharge auto-calculation, expand to regional carriers with 25-75 drivers, add owner's financial dashboard (revenue per mile by lane, driver cost per mile) — transforming from a driver pay tool into a full driver cost analytics platform.

### 3. SaaS Spend and Utilization Visibility for Mid-Market Companies
**Problem:** A company with 75-250 employees is paying for 45-80 SaaS tools every month — Slack, Zoom, Salesforce, HubSpot, GitHub, Figma, Notion, Loom, Calendly, Canva, Dropbox, DocuSign, and dozens more. Finance sees recurring charges on the corporate card and in bank statements but doesn't know which tool each charge is for, which department owns it, or whether anyone is actually using it. HR doesn't know that 40% of the GitHub seats provisioned to the product team haven't been accessed in 90 days. The head of engineering doesn't know that the company is paying for both Figma and Sketch — acquired through separate team purchases — and that only 3 people in the entire company use Sketch. IT doesn't know that a software subscription is auto-renewing next month at a rate that was negotiated when the company had 60 employees and the vendor charges by seat count based on the original contract, not actual usage. Gartner estimates that organizations waste 25-40% of their SaaS spend on unused or redundant tools. For a 150-person company spending $180,000/year on SaaS, that's $45,000-72,000 in waste. The existing tools in this space — Vendr, Zluri, Zylo — target enterprise companies ($500M+ revenue) with 6-figure contract values and deployment complexity that puts them out of reach for a 150-person company that just needs to know what they're paying for and who is actually using it.
**What to build:** A SaaS spend and utilization visibility tool for mid-market companies — a data aggregation platform that connects to the company's credit card and bank feeds via Plaid (or a manual CSV upload for companies not ready to connect bank credentials), identifies recurring SaaS charges using a vendor intelligence database (a curated list of SaaS vendor names, domains, and billing descriptors — Stripe's billing descriptor for Notion, the banking name for Salesforce charges, etc.), and enriches each charge with category (CRM, project management, development tools, communication), vendor website, pricing model, and a link to the vendor's usage API documentation. The utilization layer: for tools with accessible usage APIs — Slack (workspace analytics API), GitHub (admin API), Zoom (dashboard API), Google Workspace (admin SDK) — pull seat count vs. active user count automatically. For tools without public usage APIs, a lightweight browser extension that tracks page visits per tool per domain (with explicit employee consent) as a utilization proxy. Core output: a monthly SaaS inventory with total spend, cost per seat, active users vs. provisioned seats for connected tools, and a flagged list of anomalies — tools with zero active users in the last 30 days, tools with redundant functionality overlap (two project management tools, two video conferencing tools), subscriptions auto-renewing within 30 days. A renewal calendar that surfaces every subscription renewal date (extracted from receipt emails via a Gmail or Outlook integration, or manually entered) so finance knows about renewals 60-90 days in advance rather than seeing the charge appear on a statement.
**Skill fit:** Plaid API for financial data (or bank statement CSV parsing), SaaS vendor classification engine (a curated JSON database of billing descriptors → vendor name/category that the builder maintains), Slack and GitHub admin API integrations for utilization data, Gmail API for receipt parsing to extract renewal dates. The vendor intelligence database is a competitive moat built over time — the ability to classify 95%+ of recurring charges is what separates a useful tool from a partial picture. This is a data engineering and classification problem with a clean React dashboard front-end — well within the builder's skill set.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — bank statement CSV upload, a billing descriptor → vendor classification engine covering the top 200 SaaS tools, a spend inventory dashboard, and a Slack API integration showing active users vs. provisioned seats. A head of finance who uploads 3 months of bank statements and sees $14,000/month in SaaS spend classified by tool and category — including 8 tools they didn't know the company was paying for — has justified the subscription in the first 20 minutes.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — mid-market finance, IT, and operations leaders are reachable through CFO Connect (a community of CFOs at growth-stage companies), the Controller community on LinkedIn and Slack, and product-led growth communities where Ops leaders discuss software spend. A "we found $40K in SaaS waste in our first week" case study shared in a finance Slack community generates 30-50 inbound inquiries. YC-backed startups scaling from 50 to 200 employees are a particularly hot segment — they add SaaS tools rapidly during growth phases and have no visibility into what they've accumulated.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-899/month per company depending on employee count and SaaS tool volume. 25 companies at $550/month = **$13,750/month**. Upsell: a contract negotiation intelligence module ($199/month add-on) — benchmarks for what companies of similar size typically pay for each tool (derived from aggregated, anonymized customer data across the tool's user base), so the finance leader can walk into a Salesforce or HubSpot renewal negotiation knowing that comparable companies pay 20% less per seat than the current rate.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** SaaS tools are added continuously as the company grows. The spend inventory is never "done" — new tools appear every month, renewal dates roll forward, and utilization data needs refreshing. The renewal calendar has high switching cost because it contains manually enriched renewal dates and negotiation history that finance built over time. The vendor intelligence database becomes more accurate the more companies use it — a moat that strengthens with scale.
**Biggest risk:** Plaid bank feed connectivity issues — some corporate card providers (Amex, certain bank-issued corporate cards) have limited or unreliable Plaid connectivity. Mitigation: the CSV upload path (exported from the bank's web interface) works universally and is the default for V1. Plaid connectivity is added as an enhancement once the core classification engine is validated.
**Growth path:** Side project → bank statement CSV upload, top-200 SaaS vendor classification, spend inventory dashboard, and Slack utilization integration live, first 5-8 company clients. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-18 companies, add Plaid integration, add GitHub and Zoom utilization APIs, add Gmail receipt parsing for renewal date extraction. Replaces-full-time-salary → 25+ companies, add contract negotiation benchmarking module, expand vendor classification database to 1,000+ tools, offer a managed optimization service (fractional SaaS procurement consultant backed by the platform's data).

### 4. Warranty Claim Portal and Subcontractor Routing for Home Builders
**Problem:** A regional home builder completing 60-200 homes per year has a warranty obligation on every home — typically 1 year for workmanship defects, 2 years for mechanical systems (plumbing, HVAC, electrical), and 10 years for structural defects under most state builder warranties and Magnuson-Moss warranty law. When a homeowner discovers a defect — a window that leaks in heavy rain, a door that doesn't close flush, an HVAC system that short-cycles — they need to submit a warranty claim. The builder's warranty coordinator receives the claim (via email, phone, or a generic web form), determines which subcontractor is responsible (the window installer, the finish carpenter, the HVAC contractor), contacts the sub to schedule the repair, and follows up with the homeowner when the repair is complete. Across a portfolio of 150 homes under active warranty, this generates 30-60 warranty service calls per month. The coordination is done in a shared Excel spreadsheet that everyone's afraid to edit, and the communication to both the homeowner and the subcontractor is done entirely by email and phone. When a claim sits unaddressed for 2 weeks, the homeowner posts a 1-star Google review ("Terrible warranty service — no one responds") — and that review damages the builder's reputation in the exact market where they need referrals. Purpose-built builder CRM software (BuilderTrend, CoConstruct) has basic warranty modules, but the claim routing to specific subcontractors based on which sub did the work in that specific home requires data that's usually not consistently recorded in those systems.
**What to build:** A warranty claim portal and subcontractor routing system for home builders — a homeowner-facing portal where the homeowner submits warranty claims with a photo and description, selects the affected system (roof, windows, doors, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, finishes, appliances), and receives immediate confirmation that the claim was received. The builder's back end: a claim management dashboard showing all open claims by status (submitted, under review, assigned to sub, sub confirmed, repair scheduled, repair completed, closed), warranty zone (is this claim within the workmanship warranty period or only the structural period?), and a claim history per address. Sub routing logic: the builder configures which subcontractor did which work in each home (or imports this from BuilderTrend's job costing data) — when a plumbing claim comes in on Lot 47, the system knows it was Smith Plumbing who roughed in that home and automatically notifies Smith Plumbing with the claim details and a link to schedule the repair. The sub receives an SMS and email, confirms receipt with one click, and enters their repair date — which is immediately reflected in the homeowner's portal view. Automated homeowner updates at each status change. A warranty dashboard for the builder's warranty manager: average time-to-close by system type, claims per home (identifying homes with repeat issues), and subcontractors with the highest claim volumes (quality feedback for future project selection).
**Skill fit:** A clean data model: homes, warranty periods, claims, subcontractors, assignment history, and status state machine. The portal is a public-facing form with photo upload (S3) and a status tracker. The sub routing logic is a configurable lookup table (home → trades → subcontractors) with SMS and email notification via Twilio and SendGrid. The dashboard is standard aggregation. Optional BuilderTrend API integration for importing sub assignments from job costing data. No complex algorithms — clean workflow software where the value is in orchestrating a multi-party coordination that currently happens over disconnected email threads.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — homeowner claim submission form with photo upload, claim management dashboard with status workflow, manual sub assignment (the builder selects which sub to notify for each claim), and SMS/email notification to the sub and homeowner at each status change. A warranty coordinator who goes from managing 45 open claims in a spreadsheet to seeing them in a dashboard with automated sub notifications — and who stops getting calls from homeowners asking "what's the status of my claim?" — has validated the product in week 1.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — regional home builder owners and warranty managers are reachable through the National Association of Home Builders (NAHB) member community and local HBA (Home Builders Association) chapters, BuilderTrend and CoConstruct user communities, and construction industry conferences. Home builder warranty consultants — who work with builders to reduce warranty costs and improve homeowner satisfaction — are a high-leverage referral channel, the same role as veterinary practice consultants in the Session 54 vet idea.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-599/month per builder depending on annual home completion volume. 30 builders at $375/month = **$11,250/month**. Upsell: a warranty analytics and subcontractor scorecard module ($99/month add-on) — a quarterly report showing claim rates by subcontractor, repair time by sub, and repeat-claim rates by home and system type. A builder who sees that their drywall sub generates 3x more warranty claims than the industry average has the data to have a performance conversation or switch subs — and the tool's analytics are the justification.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Active warranty claims flow in continuously from recently completed homes. The homeowner portal is a permanent feature of each home sale — builders share the portal link at closing and it serves as a credibility signal (professional warranty process, not a builder who dodges calls). The sub routing configuration is built over time as the builder adds more completed projects — switching means reconfiguring every sub assignment from scratch for every active warranty home.
**Biggest risk:** Adoption friction with subcontractors — requiring subs to use a new system adds friction. Mitigation: the sub interaction is 100% SMS-based in V1. The sub receives a text, taps a link, confirms receipt and enters a date on a mobile-optimized page. No app download, no account creation for the sub.
**Growth path:** Side project → homeowner claim portal, claim management dashboard, SMS-based sub notification, and status update automation live, first 5-8 builder clients. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-18 builders, add BuilderTrend API integration for automatic sub assignment from job costing, add warranty analytics and sub scorecard module, add homeowner satisfaction survey (sent at claim close). Replaces-full-time-salary → 30+ builders, add warranty reserve forecasting (statistical model of expected warranty costs per home based on historical claim rates by market and home type), expand to commercial contractors with punch list and warranty obligation management post-completion.

### 5. Route Efficiency and Contract Retention for Pest Control Companies
**Problem:** A pest control company with 500-3,000 residential accounts provides quarterly or monthly service — a technician visits each home, treats the perimeter and interior as needed, and the customer pays $50-150/month on a recurring contract. Revenue leakage comes from three distinct operational failures that compound on each other. First, contract cancellations that go undetected: a customer cancels their recurring service (or simply stops responding to scheduling calls), and the cancellation doesn't surface to the branch manager for 4-6 weeks because the scheduling system marks the account as "pending contact" rather than "cancelled" — by then, the customer has found a competitor and signed up. Second, route inefficiency: the routing for the company's technicians is set up when the company first builds the route and is rarely re-optimized as the account geography shifts. A technician might drive 35 minutes between consecutive stops that could be sequenced in 15 minutes with a short route restructure — across a 6-stop route, that's an extra hour of drive time per day per tech, translating to 2-3 fewer service calls per day and a significant cost drag. Third, pricing stagnation: customers on recurring annual contracts signed 3-5 years ago are paying rates that haven't kept pace with technician wages, chemical costs, and fuel. A company that locked 800 accounts at $75/month in 2021 is now losing margin on those accounts while new accounts are being sold at $95/month — a $20/month per-account gap across 800 accounts is $192,000/year in unrealized revenue.
**What to build:** A three-part operational intelligence tool for pest control companies — integrating with the company's field service software (ServiceTitan, Jobber, FieldRoutes, PestPac) via API. Module 1 (contract retention alert): a daily monitoring sweep that flags any account that missed a scheduled service call without a rescheduled appointment — surfacing cancelled and at-risk accounts to the branch manager within 24 hours rather than 4-6 weeks. An automated reactivation sequence for accounts that cancel: a 3-touch SMS sequence over 14 days with a return offer ($25 off the first service back). Module 2 (route efficiency analyzer): a weekly route report for each technician showing their current route sequence, the total drive time under the current sequence, and the drive time under the optimally re-sequenced route — using the Google Maps Distance Matrix API to compute stop-to-stop drive times and a nearest-neighbor optimization. Not a turn-by-turn routing app (competitors like Route4Me handle that) but an analytics view showing the manager how much time is being wasted on poorly sequenced routes and which specific stops should be reassigned between technicians. Module 3 (pricing gap report): a monthly analysis of every recurring contract, comparing the current rate to the company's current new-account rate for the same service type and frequency. Flagging accounts that are more than 15% below current rate as "repricing candidates" — with a configurable threshold and a template for the price increase notification letter.
**Skill fit:** ServiceTitan and Jobber have developer APIs; PestPac has an integration framework. The cancellation monitoring is a simple status-change detection query run daily. The route efficiency calculation uses the Google Maps Distance Matrix API (straightforward HTTP calls) with a nearest-neighbor optimizer (a standard algorithm, implementable in an afternoon). The pricing gap report is a SQL-style aggregation comparing contract rates to a configured current rate table. All three modules are data engineering problems with clean dashboard outputs — no exotic algorithms.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — Jobber or ServiceTitan API integration, the cancellation alert daily monitoring with SMS reactivation sequence, and the pricing gap report. The route efficiency module is V2. A branch manager who stops losing accounts for 3-4 weeks before discovering the cancellation — and who identifies $12,000/month in underpriced accounts ready for repricing — has covered the subscription cost in the first billing cycle.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — pest control company owners and branch managers are reachable through the National Pest Management Association (NPMA) community and PestWorld conference, the Pest Control Technology magazine readership, ServiceTitan and Jobber user communities and partner marketplaces, and the Pest Control Business Owners group on Facebook (25,000+ members). A single case study — "recovered 8 cancelled accounts in month 1 and identified $14,000/month in underpriced contracts" — posted in that Facebook group generates 20-30 inbound inquiries from branch managers with the exact same problem.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-499/month per company depending on account count. 35 companies at $325/month = **$11,375/month**. Upsell: a technician performance dashboard ($99/month add-on) — tracking service calls completed per day per technician, chemical usage per service call (identifying technicians who over-apply product, which is both a cost problem and a liability risk), and callback rate (the percentage of services that result in a customer callback reporting the pest problem wasn't resolved) — giving the branch manager objective performance data for 1-on-1s and compensation decisions.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Service calls happen every day. Cancellations surface daily. Route sequences change every time the account geography shifts (new accounts added, cancelled accounts removed). The pricing gap report runs every month as time passes and the rate gap between legacy accounts and current pricing widens. Switching means rebuilding the cancellation monitoring rules and losing the pricing gap history — which the branch manager references when timing repricing waves to avoid spooking customers by raising all prices simultaneously.
**Biggest risk:** Field service software fragmentation — pest control companies use PestPac, ServSuite, and numerous regional tools alongside the mainstream platforms. Mitigation: Jobber and ServiceTitan together cover a large plurality of the SMB pest control market. Start with Jobber (fastest-growing, best API) and expand based on customer demand.
**Growth path:** Side project → Jobber integration, cancellation alert with reactivation sequence, and pricing gap report live, first 5-8 company clients. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-18 companies, add ServiceTitan and PestPac integrations, add route efficiency analyzer using Google Maps Distance Matrix, add technician performance dashboard. Replaces-full-time-salary → 35+ companies, add a customer satisfaction score (a 1-question SMS survey after each service completion), expand to lawn care and pool service companies with identical recurring account + route + pricing stagnation dynamics.
