# Main Income Ideas - 2026-08-22 (Session 123)

## Who this is for
You're 29, you write software and know your way around data — pipelines, scrapers, structured reference data, rules engines. You want to replace a full-time salary ($8-15K+/mo) within 12-24 months with something you build solo in evenings and weekends, no outside funding, no co-founder, and you want the first paying customer inside 90 days. The wedge in this series is always the same: small operators in a regulated industry who have non-optional government or accreditation-body obligations — licenses, permits, certifications, filings, inspections — and no compliance department to track them. You sell a monthly-retainer SaaS that turns their compliance binder into a living calendar with alerts and hard gates. The regulator (or accrediting body) creates the deadline; you sell the peace of mind of never missing it.

### 1. Industrial & Protective Coatings / Bridge & Tank Painting Contractor SSPC/AMPP Certification & OSHA Lead-in-Construction Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Industrial coatings contractors who blast, prep, and repaint steel bridges, elevated water tanks, petroleum storage tanks, and structural steel operate under SSPC/AMPP's QP1 (Painting/Coating Application) and QP2 (Removal of Hazardous Coatings) contractor certification programs — credentials most state DOTs and municipal water authorities now require as a bid condition, renewed on a recurring audit cycle — and every crew member performing surface prep or spray application typically needs an individual SSPC or NACE (now AMPP) certified craftsman/inspector credential kept current. Because most older bridges and tanks were originally coated with lead-based paint, any abrasive blasting or removal work triggers OSHA's lead-in-construction standard (29 CFR 1926.62), requiring a written compliance program, initial and periodic air monitoring, and blood-lead-level medical surveillance for every exposed employee on a fixed testing interval — a worker whose blood-lead test lapses past its required interval is barred from that scope of work. The spent abrasive blast media, laden with lead residue, must be tested and, if it fails the hazardous-waste characteristic, manifested and disposed of under RCRA Subtitle C — a disposal record the contractor must produce on demand. A small contracting firm running several blast-and-paint crews across concurrent bridge and tank projects manages staggered SSPC/AMPP certification renewals, per-employee blood-lead testing intervals gating who can work a lead-abatement scope, and per-project hazardous-waste manifest documentation on a project manager's spreadsheet and a filing cabinet of lab results, and a crew member dispatched past their blood-lead testing interval, or a project closed out without a complete hazardous-waste manifest on file, is exactly the finding an OSHA inspection or a state DOT contract compliance audit is built to catch.

**What to build:** A per-employee blood-lead-testing interval tracker with a "cleared to work lead-abatement scope" dispatch gate; a firm and per-technician SSPC/AMPP QP1/QP2 and individual certification renewal tracker; a per-project hazardous-waste blast-media manifest log; and a state DOT/municipal bid-qualification document vault assembling current certifications for submission with each new bid.

**Skill fit:** SSPC/AMPP's certification renewal cycles and OSHA's lead-in-construction blood-lead testing intervals are published, structured compliance data with concrete numeric thresholds, and the dispatch-eligibility gate blocking lead-abatement work until a technician's blood-lead test clears is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a heavy-industrial coatings trade this repo has never addressed.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the blood-lead testing tracker with the dispatch gate, the SSPC/AMPP certification tracker, and the hazardous-waste manifest log. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent industrial coatings contractors.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — SSPC/AMPP's own certified-contractor directory and member network reaches exactly the small firms already tracking QP1/QP2 renewal and blood-lead testing by hand, since certification currency is a condition of every bid they submit.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249-449/mo per contracting firm depending on crew count and project mix. 35-45 firms × $329/mo avg = $11,515-14,805/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every crew member's blood-lead test recurs on its own fixed interval for as long as they perform abatement work, every project generates a fresh hazardous-waste manifest, and SSPC/AMPP certifications renew on their own audit cycle — a continuous, crew-and-project-driven compliance stream tied directly to the firm's eligibility to keep bidding public infrastructure work.

**Biggest risk:** A firm doing only new-construction coating work with no lead-abatement scope has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads for firms actively bidding bridge and tank recoat/rehabilitation contracts specifically, where lead exposure is unavoidable, and by positioning the SSPC/AMPP certification tracker alone as a lower-tier entry offer.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 firms ($3.9K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 28 firms ($9.2K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 35-45 firms plus a state-DOT bid-qualification document-vault upsell for firms bidding across multiple states.

### 2. Precious Metals Refiner & Bullion Dealer BSA Dealer-in-Precious-Metals AML Program & Chain-of-Custody Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Small precious metals refiners and wholesale bullion dealers — businesses that receive scrap gold, silver, and platinum-group material from jewelers, dental practices, pawnshops, and industrial scrap generators, smelt and assay it, and resell refined bullion to mints, manufacturers, and investors — trigger the Bank Secrecy Act's dedicated AML program requirement for "dealers in precious metals, precious stones, or jewels" (31 CFR Part 1027) once their annual purchases or sales of covered goods from any one person exceed $50,000, requiring a written, risk-based AML program with a designated compliance officer, documented customer due diligence and identity verification above the threshold, ongoing employee AML training, and ready-for-inspection recordkeeping — a program structure entirely separate from a money-transmitter's FinCEN MSB registration or a pawnbroker's state police-reporting statute, and one FinCEN has specifically targeted for enforcement in the precious metals trade given its historical use in money laundering. Every incoming lot also requires a documented chain-of-custody record — source, weight, assayed purity, and settlement price — since a refiner that cannot reconstruct a lot's origin on request is exactly the finding a FinCEN examination or a state assayer's-license audit is built to catch, and many states additionally require the refiner itself to hold a state secondhand-precious-metals or refiner registration separate from any pawnbroker license. A small refining operation processing dozens of incoming lots a week manages per-customer $50,000-threshold tracking across a rolling twelve months, per-lot chain-of-custody and assay documentation, and AML program review currency with no dedicated compliance officer — and a customer relationship that crossed the threshold without triggering enhanced due diligence, or a lot with no reconstructable chain of custody, is exactly the gap a FinCEN exam is built to catch.

**What to build:** A per-customer rolling twelve-month purchase/sale total tracker flagging any relationship approaching or past the $50,000 31 CFR 1027 threshold before enhanced due diligence is triggered; a per-lot chain-of-custody log (source, intake weight, assayed purity, settlement price, and disposition); an AML program review and employee-training-currency tracker; and a state refiner/secondhand-precious-metals registration renewal tracker.

**Skill fit:** 31 CFR Part 1027's threshold-triggered AML program requirement is public, structured federal regulatory data with a concrete dollar threshold, and the rolling twelve-month per-customer total that gates enhanced due diligence is the same deterministic "compute the trigger, flag the threshold breach" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a wholesale bullion-refining trade distinct from every walk-in consumer-facing pawnbroker or scrap-metal-dealer idea already in this repo.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the per-customer threshold tracker, the per-lot chain-of-custody log, and the AML program review tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent refiners and bullion dealers.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the International Precious Metals Institute (IPMI) runs an active member directory and annual conference reaching exactly the small independent refiners and bullion dealers navigating 31 CFR 1027's threshold tracking without a dedicated compliance officer.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $349-699/mo per refiner depending on lot volume and customer-relationship count. 20-28 refiners × $499/mo avg = $9,980-13,972/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new customer relationship opens its own rolling twelve-month threshold clock, every incoming lot generates a fresh chain-of-custody record, and the AML program itself requires periodic independent review for the life of the business — a continuous, customer-and-lot-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the refiner's standing with FinCEN.

**Biggest risk:** A very small refiner buying almost exclusively from a handful of long-standing, well-below-threshold customers has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads for refiners with a broad, churning customer base of pawnshops and scrap generators, where the per-customer threshold genuinely requires systematic tracking rather than memory.

**Growth path:** Side project → 8-10 refiners ($4-5K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 16-18 refiners ($8-9K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 20-28 refiners plus a multi-state refiner-registration expansion tier.

### 3. Manufactured/Mobile Home Installer & Retailer HUD Code, Installer License & Set-Up Permit Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Manufactured/mobile home installers and retailers sell and site-install new HUD-code manufactured housing under the National Manufactured Housing Construction and Safety Standards Act's federal construction standard (24 CFR Part 3280) and the Model Manufactured Home Installation Standards (24 CFR Part 3285), which govern the foundation type, support-pier spacing, and anchoring/tie-down system required for the home's specific wind zone and site soil conditions — installation work enforced through a state manufactured-housing commission's individual installer licensing (renewed on its own cycle, often with continuing education) and a per-home set-up permit requiring a state or third-party inspector's sign-off before the home can be occupied. Retailers separately hold a state manufactured-home dealer license with its own bond and displayed-inventory rules, and every home sold carries manufacturer warranty obligations the dealer must track against a state-mandated warranty-service-response window. A small dealer/installer running several simultaneous home set-ups across a rural or exurban territory manages per-installer license and CE-hour renewal, per-home foundation/anchoring-system documentation and set-up-permit inspection status, and per-sale warranty-response-deadline tracking on a paper job folder and a filing cabinet, and a home occupied before its set-up permit cleared inspection, or an installer dispatched past their license renewal, is exactly the finding a state manufactured-housing commission audit is built to catch — with consequences ranging from a stop-work order to installer license suspension.

**What to build:** A per-installer state license and CE-hour renewal tracker with a "cleared to install" dispatch gate; a per-home set-up permit and foundation/anchoring-system documentation tracker computing the wind-zone-specific requirement and flagging any home occupied before inspection sign-off; a dealer license and bond renewal tracker; and a per-sale manufacturer warranty-claim and state-mandated response-window tracker.

**Skill fit:** HUD's Part 3280/3285 standards and state manufactured-housing commission installer/dealer licensing rules are public, structured federal and state regulatory data, and the per-home "cleared to occupy" inspection gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a manufactured-housing category distinct from the RV dealer and house-mover ideas already in this repo.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the installer license/dispatch-gate tracker, the per-home set-up permit tracker, and the warranty-response tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent manufactured home dealers/installers.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Manufactured Housing Institute (MHI) and state manufactured-housing associations run active member directories reaching exactly the small independent dealer/installer businesses navigating installer licensing and set-up-permit compliance without dedicated staff.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-349/mo per dealer/installer depending on installer headcount and home volume. 45-60 dealers × $269/mo avg = $12,105-16,140/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every home sold generates its own set-up permit and warranty-response clock, and every installer's license and CE cycle renews independently — a continuous, sales-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the dealer's ability to keep installing and occupying homes legally.

**Biggest risk:** Large multi-lot manufactured-housing retailers often run in-house compliance staff. Mitigate by explicitly targeting independent single- or few-lot dealers managing installer licensing and set-up permits themselves.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 dealers ($4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 35 dealers ($9.4K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 45-60 dealers plus a multi-state installer-licensing expansion tier.

### 4. Water Treatment Equipment Dealer & Installer WQA Certification, State License & NSF/ANSI Device Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Water treatment equipment dealers who sell and install residential and light-commercial water softeners, reverse-osmosis systems, and whole-house filtration equipment pursue Water Quality Association (WQA) Certified Water Specialist and Master Water Specialist installer certification — a credential most manufacturers require to authorize a dealer's warranty-backed installation — renewed through WQA's continuing-education cycle, and, for point-of-entry system installation involving any plumbing modification, must hold a state well-contractor or plumbing-contractor license. Every device model sold must meet the specific NSF/ANSI standard applicable to its function (NSF/ANSI 44 for cation exchange softeners, 53/58 for health-effects filtration and reverse osmosis, 62 for distillation) and the dealer must be able to produce that certification on request during a state health-department or consumer complaint inquiry. A fast-growing number of jurisdictions — several California counties under regional water-quality control board rules, and parts of Texas and other states — now restrict or outright ban self-regenerating water-softener discharge into the sanitary sewer system, meaning a softener sold into a restricted jurisdiction without a compliant discharge configuration (or a demand-initiated regeneration control) exposes both the dealer and the homeowner to a code violation. A small dealer running several installers across a multi-county territory manages per-installer WQA certification and state license renewal, per-device NSF/ANSI certification documentation, and per-jurisdiction softener-discharge-restriction compliance on a spreadsheet and a folder of manufacturer spec sheets, and a softener installed in a restricted county without a compliant configuration, or an installer dispatched past their license renewal, is exactly the finding a local code-enforcement complaint or a state licensing board inquiry is built to catch.

**What to build:** A per-installer WQA certification and state license renewal tracker with a "cleared to install" dispatch gate; a per-device NSF/ANSI certification reference library matched to each product line sold; a per-jurisdiction softener-discharge-restriction lookup flagging any installation address in a restricted county before the job is scheduled; and a warranty-registration tracker tying each installed unit's serial number to its manufacturer warranty terms.

**Skill fit:** WQA's certification structure, NSF/ANSI's device standards, and the growing list of local softener-discharge ordinances are published, structured reference and regulatory data, and the per-address discharge-restriction lookup gate before a job is scheduled is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a water-treatment-equipment trade distinct from the backflow-tester and irrigation-contractor ideas already in this repo.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the installer certification/dispatch-gate tracker, the NSF/ANSI reference library, and the per-jurisdiction discharge-restriction lookup. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent water treatment dealers.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Water Quality Association (WQA) runs an active member directory, certified-dealer locator, and annual conference reaching exactly the small independent dealers navigating installer certification and the growing patchwork of local discharge ordinances.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149-299/mo per dealer depending on installer headcount and territory. 55-70 dealers × $219/mo avg = $12,045-15,330/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every installer's WQA certification and state license renews on its own cycle, every device sold generates a fresh NSF/ANSI documentation and warranty-registration event, and the list of restricted jurisdictions keeps expanding — a continuous, installer-and-sales-volume-driven compliance stream with a structurally growing compliance surface.

**Biggest risk:** A dealer operating entirely outside any softener-discharge-restricted jurisdiction has a comparatively lower-stakes tracking need for that specific feature. Mitigate by leading with the installer-certification/dispatch-gate tracker as the core offer for every dealer, with the discharge-restriction lookup as a differentiator for dealers in California, Texas, and other affected states.

**Growth path:** Side project → 18 dealers ($3.9K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 40 dealers ($8.8K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 55-70 dealers plus a manufacturer-authorized-dealer compliance-status export upsell.

### 5. Structural Masonry, Tuckpointing & Building Waterproofing Restoration Contractor ICRI Certification, OSHA Scaffold & Lead-Safe Work Practice Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Masonry restoration contractors who perform tuckpointing, spalling-concrete repair, and building waterproofing/sealant application on existing commercial and residential structures typically work from swing-stage scaffolds or suspended platforms, requiring compliance with OSHA's scaffolding standard (29 CFR 1926 Subpart E) — a documented competent-person inspection before each shift, fall-protection tie-off, and a rigging/suspension-system inspection certificate for every swing stage in use — and individual technicians increasingly hold International Concrete Repair Institute (ICRI) certified-technician credentials specific to the repair-material system being applied (structural concrete repair, sealants, waterproofing membranes), renewed on ICRI's continuing-education cycle. Any job disturbing lead-based paint or coatings on a pre-1978 building — a frequent occurrence on older masonry facades — additionally requires the firm and every disturbing worker to hold current EPA RRP lead-safe certification, with documented containment and cleanup-verification records for the specific unit or structure. A small restoration contractor running several concurrent swing-stage or scaffold jobs across a metro area tracks per-scaffold daily inspection and rigging-certificate currency, per-technician ICRI certification renewal matched to the repair system used on each job, and per-job RRP lead-safe documentation on a paper job-site binder and a shoebox of inspection tags, and a swing stage operated past its rigging-inspection interval, or a lead-disturbing job started without documented RRP containment, is exactly the finding an OSHA inspection or an EPA RRP compliance sweep is built to catch.

**What to build:** A per-scaffold/swing-stage daily competent-person inspection log with a rigging-certificate renewal tracker; a per-technician ICRI certification tracker matched to the repair-material system used on each active job; an EPA RRP firm/renovator certification and per-job lead-safe-containment documentation tracker for pre-1978 structures; and a job-site OSHA-inspection-readiness document export.

**Skill fit:** OSHA's scaffolding standard, ICRI's certification structure, and EPA's RRP rule are all published, structured compliance and credentialing-body data, and the per-scaffold daily inspection log plus the per-job lead-safe documentation gate are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a structural-restoration trade distinct from the rope-access and roofing contractor ideas already in this repo.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the scaffold inspection/rigging-certificate tracker, the per-technician ICRI certification tracker, and the RRP lead-safe documentation tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent masonry restoration contractors.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the International Concrete Repair Institute (ICRI) runs an active member and certified-technician directory, chapter meetings, and an annual conference reaching exactly the small independent restoration contractors navigating scaffold inspection and lead-safe documentation without dedicated safety staff.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-399/mo per contractor depending on crew count and swing-stage inventory. 40-55 contractors × $289/mo avg = $11,560-15,895/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every scaffold requires a fresh daily inspection log for the duration of each job, every technician's ICRI certification recurs on its own renewal cycle, and every pre-1978 building job generates its own RRP lead-safe documentation event — a continuous, job-and-crew-driven compliance stream tied directly to the contractor's ability to keep working at height legally.

**Biggest risk:** A small contractor doing only ground-level tuckpointing with no swing-stage or lead-paint exposure has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads for contractors regularly working multi-story facades and pre-1978 buildings specifically, and by positioning the ICRI certification tracker alone as a lower-tier entry offer.

**Growth path:** Side project → 14 contractors ($4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 30 contractors ($8.7K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 40-55 contractors plus a multi-crew swing-stage fleet inspection add-on for larger firms.
