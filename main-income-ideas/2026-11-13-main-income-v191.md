# Main Income Ideas - 2026-11-13 (Session 191)

## Who this is for
A 29-year-old software engineer with data analytics skills who wants to replace a full-time salary ($8-15K+/mo) within 12-24 months by building solo, in evenings and weekends, with no outside funding. Every idea below must have a realistic path to first revenue inside 90 days and a named, reachable distribution channel.

## Lens for this session: Alex Hormozi
Built from the $100M Offers / $100M Leads frameworks:
- **Value equation:** Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood of Success) / (Time Delay x Effort & Sacrifice).
- **Grand Slam Offer:** performance-priced or guarantee-backed.
- **Launch and promotion plays:** a grand-opening presale, a seasonal promotion calendar and lead prioritization, all aimed at making demand arrive on a date the owner chooses.

Per standing feedback, none of these are compliance or regulatory tools. Each solves a revenue-leakage or growth problem.

## Ideas

### 1. Grand-Opening Presale Campaign Engine for New Gyms, Studios and Clinics

**Problem:** A new studio or gym signs a lease and spends months of rent before it has members. Founding-member presales fill the space on opening day and fund the build-out, but most first-time owners do not know how to run one: they post on social media and hope.

**What to build:** A launch kit with a presale landing page (founding-member tier with a locked-in rate and a cap on spots), a countdown, a referral bonus for each friend brought in and an email/SMS sequence for the 6 weeks before opening. Track presale revenue, spots left and referral sources, and show a plan for hitting a member target by the opening date.

**Grand Slam angle:** "100 founding members before opening day, or you do not pay." The fee is 5% of presale revenue.

**Skill fit:** Landing pages, payment links, referral tracking and campaign automation.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-60 days, through new-business owner groups, commercial real-estate leasing contacts and franchise-lite communities.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** A launch collecting $40,000 of presale revenue pays a 5% fee of $2,000. Run 6 launches a month = $12,000/mo. Add a $99/mo post-launch retention add-on for another 30 owners = $2,970/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every launch produces a case study, and owners who succeed buy the retention tools afterward.

**Biggest risk:** Launch-driven revenue is lumpy and depends on a steady flow of new openings. Build a pipeline through landlords and small-business lenders.

**Growth path:** Gyms, then studios, salons, clinics, restaurants and coworking spaces.

### 2. Done-For-You Holiday Promotion Calendar for Independent Retailers

**Problem:** Independent shops earn 20-40% of annual revenue between Halloween and New Year, yet most decide the promotions the week before. Customers receive generic emails or nothing, and the owner is too busy to plan.

**What to build:** A calendar of promotions (early-bird, gift guide, last-minute shipping cutoff, post-holiday gift-card push) for each retail category, with ready-to-send email and SMS copy, signage files and an ad template. Import the shop's product list so the gift guide fills itself. Show campaign revenue against last year.

**Grand Slam angle:** "A full holiday plan in 24 hours. If you do not beat last year's holiday revenue, the fee is refunded."

**Skill fit:** Template library, product import and campaign scheduling.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 14-30 days, since this is seasonal and sells quickly to owners in October and November.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $399 per holiday season, plus $79/mo year-round for other seasonal plans (Mother's Day, back-to-school). 60 shops x $399 = $23,940 per season, about $6,000/mo averaged across the year, plus 50 shops x $79 = $3,950/mo. About **$10,000/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Each season is a repeat purchase, and a calendar of five seasons turns a one-off sale into recurring revenue.

**Biggest risk:** Strong seasonality. Sell annual bundles covering all five retail seasons.

**Growth path:** Boutiques and gift shops, then toy stores, bookstores, garden centers and specialty food shops.

### 3. Subcontractor Bid-Leveling Tool for Small General Contractors

**Problem:** A small GC collects quotes from 3-4 subs per trade, each in a different PDF format, and compares them by hand: who included permits, who excluded demolition, who priced the cheaper material. Mistakes in comparison turn into change orders and lost margin.

**What to build:** Upload sub quotes (PDF or email). Extract line items and exclusions with an LLM, normalize them into a side-by-side comparison and flag scope gaps ("Sub B excludes demo and haul-away"). Produce a recommended pick with the real price after adding the missing scope.

**Grand Slam angle:** "We find at least one scope gap per project that would have cost you more than the subscription, or the first month is free."

**Skill fit:** Document parsing, LLM extraction and comparison tables.

**MVP scope:** 3-4 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days through GC and builder owner groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149/mo. 80 contractors = **$11,920/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Each bid cycle needs the tool, and the quote database becomes a local price benchmark.

**Biggest risk:** Extraction accuracy on messy PDFs. Keep a human review step and flag low-confidence fields.

**Growth path:** Small GCs, then remodelers, property managers with capital projects and owner-builders.

### 4. Inbound-Lead Prioritizer for Small B2B Vendors

**Problem:** A small B2B vendor (software, equipment, commercial services) gets 100 inbound leads a month and calls them in the order they arrived. Large, ready buyers wait behind tiny ones, and good leads go cold while the rep chases a student with a free-trial login.

**What to build:** Enrich each inbound lead (company size, industry, website signals, email domain) and score it against the vendor's best past customers. Rank the queue, mark likely junk and alert a rep within minutes when a top-tier lead arrives. Report win rate by score band.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Your reps talk to the best leads first. A 45-day pilot with a win-rate comparison, or it's free."

**Skill fit:** Data enrichment APIs, scoring models and routing.

**MVP scope:** 3-4 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 45-75 days through B2B founder and sales-lead communities.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $179/mo. 65 vendors = **$11,635/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** The scoring model learns from each vendor's closed deals, and faster handling of top leads shows up in revenue.

**Biggest risk:** Enrichment data costs and accuracy. Pass enrichment cost through to higher tiers and start with domain-based signals.

**Growth path:** Small B2B vendors, then agencies, equipment dealers and franchise sales teams.

### 5. Supplier-ETA and Job-Site Delivery Coordinator for Small Contractors

**Problem:** A crew shows up Monday and the cabinets or windows are not there. Suppliers promise "sometime this week," the office checks by phone and the crew sits idle. A day of idle labor costs $600-1,500 and customers lose confidence.

**What to build:** A materials tracker per job: each order with supplier, promised date and a confirmation step 48 hours before. Send the supplier an automated "still on track?" text, update the job schedule when a delivery slips and alert the project lead to reschedule the crew. Show idle-crew days avoided.

**Grand Slam angle:** "Cut idle crew days in half in 90 days, or the first quarter is free."

**Skill fit:** Order tracking, messaging and schedule updates.

**MVP scope:** 3 weekends.

**Time to first $:** 30-60 days through contractor owner groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $119/mo. 95 contractors = **$11,305/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** It ties into every job's schedule, and idle-day savings are simple to prove.

**Biggest risk:** Suppliers who do not reply to texts. Build the tool to work from the contractor's side, with a supplier-contact log and escalation prompts.

**Growth path:** Remodelers and builders, then roofers, solar installers, landscapers and sign companies.
