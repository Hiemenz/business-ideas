## Ideas

### 1. HVAC & Refrigeration Contractor EPA Section 608 Technician Certification & State License Compliance
**Problem:** Every technician who services equipment containing refrigerant must hold an EPA Section 608 certification (Type I, II, III, or Universal) under 40 CFR Part 82 Subpart F — and the company itself typically needs a state or local mechanical/HVAC contractor license, plus in many states a separate refrigerant-handling or EPA-recognized certification on file. For equipment with a refrigerant charge of 50+ lbs, EPA requires leak-rate calculation, repair-within-30-days recordkeeping, and retained service records for 3 years (40 CFR 82.157). A 15-tech HVAC company is juggling 15 individual 608 cards (no expiration, but must be verifiable and on file), state contractor license renewal (varies 1-3 years), local mechanical permits per job, and EPA leak inspection logs — usually tracked in a shared spreadsheet the office manager half-updates. When a state board audits or a customer's insurance requires proof, missing paperwork means fines (up to $48,762/day per EPA violation under the Clean Air Act) or lost commercial contracts that require certified-tech proof before dispatch.
**What to build:** A technician roster with 608 cert type/verification-number storage, state contractor license + local permit tracking with renewal alerts, an equipment leak-rate log (auto-flags equipment needing repair within the 30-day EPA window), a "can this tech legally work on this job" eligibility gate before dispatch software assigns a ticket, and a one-click compliance binder export for commercial customers or state board audits.
**Skill fit:** EPA's certified-technician lookup and state contractor license databases are public/scrapable for verification cross-checks; the leak-rate math and 30-day-repair-window tracking is a straightforward data-engineering problem (ingest equipment charge size + refrigerant readings, compute thresholds, trigger alerts) — exactly the kind of deterministic rules engine a backend engineer builds quickly.
**MVP scope:** Two to three weekends: tech roster + cert expiration/verification tracker, state license renewal calendar, and a leak-rate/repair-deadline log with email alerts. Get first 5-8 clients from local HVACR contractor associations and RSES chapter meetings, offering a free compliance audit of their current tech roster as the hook.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — post in ACCA (Air Conditioning Contractors of America) local chapter forums and RSES (Refrigeration Service Engineers Society) chapter meetings, where multi-tech shop owners already discuss compliance headaches.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $99/mo base + $10/mo per active technician. Average client (12 techs) = $219/mo. 55 clients × $219/mo = $12,045/mo.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Techs churn (new hires need cert verification, departures need removal), refrigerant leak events recur seasonally, and state license renewals hit on fixed 1-3 year cycles — all of it generates ongoing logins and alerts rather than a one-time setup.
**Biggest risk:** Shops without a designated office admin never log in after onboarding. Mitigate with automated weekly digest emails ("2 licenses expire in 30 days, 1 tech's cert is unverified") that require zero login to deliver value.
**Growth path:** Side project → 20 HVAC clients ($4,400/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 55 clients ($12K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary via expansion into commercial refrigeration and plumbing contractors with similar license-tracking needs.

### 2. DMEPOS Home Medical Equipment Supplier Medicare Accreditation, Surety Bond & Multi-State License Compliance
**Problem:** Any business supplying durable medical equipment, prosthetics, orthotics, or supplies (DMEPOS) that bills Medicare must be accredited by a CMS-approved accreditation organization (ACHC, CHAP, BOC, etc.) under 42 CFR 424.57, hold a $50,000 surety bond per National Supplier Clearinghouse (NSC) enrollment location, and re-enroll/revalidate with the NSC on a set cycle. If the supplier ships products to patients in other states, it typically needs a separate HME/DME dealer license in each destination state (roughly 30+ states require one), each with its own renewal cycle and often a state-specific surety bond. A 3-location DME supplier shipping into 10 states is tracking one CMS accreditation renewal, one NSC revalidation, one federal bond, and up to 10 state licenses with 10 different bonds and renewal dates — miss one and Medicare deactivates the billing privileges for that location, which for most small DME suppliers is 60-80% of revenue.
**What to build:** A location-and-license portfolio dashboard mapping every ship-to state to its licensure requirement, bond amount, and renewal date; an accreditation-cycle tracker with document checklist (policies, procedures, site-visit prep materials) tied to the CMS accreditation standards; NSC revalidation deadline alerts; and a "can we ship here" eligibility check before a new patient order is fulfilled in an unlicensed state.
**Skill fit:** State HME/DME licensing requirements and CMS's NSC supplier standards are published and structured enough to build a rules database from; the multi-state shipping-eligibility gate is a lookup-table problem well suited to a developer who can model 50-state regulatory variance cleanly, something most DME software (built for billing, not compliance) ignores entirely.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends: the 50-state license/bond requirement database, a location tracker mapped against it, and a revalidation/accreditation countdown calendar. Land first 5-8 clients by offering free "which states are you exposed in" audits to small multi-state DME suppliers.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — via AAHomecare (American Association for Homecare) member forums and state HME associations (many states have one, e.g. Texas Association of Home Care & Hospice's DME section), where compliance is a constant discussion topic.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $349/mo base per legal entity + $40/mo per additional ship-to state tracked. Average client ships to 8 states = $349 + $280 = $629/mo. 20 clients × $629/mo = $12,580/mo.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Accreditation renews every 3 years, NSC revalidation is continuous, state licenses renew on staggered annual/biennial cycles, and adding any new ship-to state requires new licensure — the tracking need never goes to zero for a growing supplier.
**Biggest risk:** Small single-location DME suppliers may not feel the multi-state pain — target multi-state and growing suppliers specifically, not single-storefront oxygen suppliers with one Medicaid ID.
**Growth path:** Side project → 8 DME clients ($5K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 20 clients ($12.5K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding pharmacy licensing modules for suppliers that also dispense.

### 3. Vape & Tobacco Retailer PACT Act Federal Registration, State Excise & Age-Verification Compliance
**Problem:** Any retailer selling vapor products or cigarettes that ships product (even locally via delivery apps) must register with the ATF and every state/local tax administrator under the federal PACT Act (15 U.S.C. §§375-378, expanded to vapor products by the 2021 amendment), file monthly reports of all interstate sales to each destination state's tax administrator, and separately hold state tobacco/vapor retail licenses with excise tax stamp compliance that varies wildly by state (some tax vapor by volume, some by wholesale price, some not at all — and rates change routinely). On top of that, most states now require documented age-verification procedures and mystery-shopper compliance logs to avoid FDA Tobacco Control Act retailer warning letters. A 3-store vape/tobacco retailer selling online is tracking ATF PACT Act registration renewal, up to 40+ state monthly PACT reports (even for zero sales, many states require a nil report), state retail licenses per location, and age-verification incident logs — almost always managed reactively after a state notice-of-violation arrives.
**What to build:** A PACT Act monthly report generator that ingests sales data and produces the state-by-state filing package (including nil reports for states with zero shipments), a state license/excise-registration tracker per location, an age-verification compliance log with incident documentation, and renewal alerts for ATF registration and state licenses.
**Skill fit:** The state-by-state PACT Act reporting requirements and excise tax rate tables are published (if scattered) public data — exactly the kind of "50-state matrix nobody has assembled cleanly" data-engineering problem that's fast for a developer to build and hard for a non-technical compliance person to maintain manually.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends: sales-data-to-PACT-report generator (start with CSV upload from POS export), state license/renewal tracker, and nil-report automation for non-shipping states. First 5-8 clients from vape retailer associations offering free PACT Act filing for the first month.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Vapor Technology Association and state-level vape retailer associations (many states have one post-PMTA-crackdown) actively discuss PACT Act compliance burden; also NATO (National Association of Tobacco Outlets) local chapters for combo tobacco/vape stores.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149/mo base + $15/mo per state actively reported to. Average client reports to 8 states = $149 + $120 = $269/mo. 45 clients × $269/mo = $12,105/mo.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** PACT Act reports are due monthly without exception (including nil reports), and state excise rates change often enough that the eligibility/rate table needs continuous maintenance — this is filing software with a hard monthly deadline, not a set-and-forget tool.
**Biggest risk:** Vape regulation is politically volatile (FDA PMTA enforcement, potential federal flavor bans) and some states restrict online vapor sales entirely — build the product to also serve traditional tobacco-only retailers so a vapor-specific regulatory shock doesn't erase the client base.
**Growth path:** Side project → 15 clients ($4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 45 clients ($12K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding alcohol excise tracking for combo liquor/tobacco stores.

### 4. Auto Body & Collision Repair Shop EPA 6H NESHAP, State BAR License & OEM Certification Compliance
**Problem:** Auto body shops that spray paint or use certain surface coatings are subject to the EPA's Auto Body Refinishing NESHAP (40 CFR Part 63 Subpart HHHHHH, "6H"), which requires an initial notification to EPA, use of a compliant spray booth/gun-cleaning system, and retained records proving compliant equipment use — inspected during EPA or state environmental agency audits. Separately, most states require a repair-shop license through their DMV or Bureau of Automotive Repair (California's BAR being the best-known model many states mirror), with periodic renewal and sometimes a separate hazardous-waste generator registration for solvents and used paint. On the revenue side, insurance Direct Repair Program (DRP) contracts increasingly require current I-CAR Gold Class shop certification and OEM-specific certifications (Ford, GM, Tesla, etc.) per technician — losing certification can mean losing DRP referral volume, which is 40-70% of many shops' business. A shop owner is juggling EPA 6H recordkeeping, state BAR license renewal, hazardous waste registration, and a rotating roster of I-CAR/OEM certifications per tech tied directly to which insurers will refer work.
**What to build:** An EPA 6H compliance binder (equipment compliance records, initial notification tracking), state BAR/repair-license renewal calendar, hazardous waste generator registration tracker, and — the revenue-protecting feature — an I-CAR/OEM certification-per-technician tracker mapped to which DRP contracts require which certifications, with alerts before a lapse could jeopardize an insurer referral relationship.
**Skill fit:** EPA 6H requirements and state BAR license rules are public regulatory text suited to a structured checklist/database; the DRP-certification-to-insurer mapping is a data problem (which certs which insurers require) that a technically-minded builder can source directly from shop owners in the first few sales calls and encode once.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends: EPA 6H compliance checklist/binder, state license renewal tracker, and I-CAR/OEM cert-per-tech tracker with insurer-requirement mapping. First 5-8 clients from local ASA (Automotive Service Association) or SCRS (Society of Collision Repair Specialists) chapter meetings, pitched as "protect your DRP contracts."
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — SCRS and ASA state affiliate meetings are where independent shop owners already compare notes on losing DRP eligibility over expired certifications.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $12/mo per technician tracked. Average client (10 techs) = $199 + $120 = $319/mo. 40 clients × $319/mo = $12,760/mo.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Technician certifications expire on rolling annual cycles, EPA and state records must be maintained continuously, and DRP insurer requirements change often enough (new OEM cert requirements as vehicle tech evolves) that the tracker needs regular updates — this isn't a one-time compliance checkbox.
**Biggest risk:** Shop owners may not connect "compliance tracking" to "revenue protection" — lead every sales conversation with the DRP-contract-loss angle, not the EPA angle, since that's what actually motivates a $300+/mo purchase.
**Growth path:** Side project → 15 shops ($4.8K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 40 shops ($12.8K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by expanding into paint/coating supplier partnerships that refer shops needing 6H compliance documentation.

### 5. Notary Signing Service State Commission, E&O Bond & Background Screening Compliance
**Problem:** Companies that dispatch notary signing agents for mortgage closings and title work must ensure every notary on their roster holds a current state notary commission (renewal cycles range 4-10 years by state), an E&O insurance policy (title companies typically require $25K-100K minimum), a surety bond where the state requires one, and — per ALTA (American Land Title Association) best practices that most title companies and lenders now contractually require — an annual criminal background screening through an approved vendor (commonly the NNA's Background Screening service). Many states are also rolling out Remote Online Notarization (RON) authorization as a separate credential notaries must hold and renew if the service offers virtual closings. A signing service with 40 notaries on its roster is tracking 40 commission expiration dates across however many states those notaries are commissioned in, 40 E&O policy renewal dates, 40 annual background screening renewals, and a subset of RON authorizations — and if a title company audits the roster and finds even one notary with a lapsed background screening, it can suspend the entire vendor relationship, not just that notary's assignments.
**What to build:** A notary roster with commission/E&O/bond/background-screening expiration tracking per notary, an eligibility gate that blocks assignment of a notary whose credentials have lapsed before a closing is scheduled, RON-authorization tracking by state, and a one-click audit-ready roster export for title company or underwriter compliance reviews.
**Skill fit:** State notary commission renewal rules and RON authorization status are published per-state data (a well-known 50-state matrix problem); the assignment-eligibility gate is a straightforward rules engine — exactly the "block the action if the underlying credential data says no" pattern common across every idea in this series, which a developer can build once and reuse the architecture for.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends: notary roster with credential expiration tracking, an audit-export report, and a basic assignment-eligibility check. First 5-8 clients from signing service operator communities, pitched around "never get audited and lose a title company relationship over a lapsed background check again."
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the NNA (National Notary Association) signing agent community and active Snapdocs/Notary Cafe operator forums are where signing service owners already discuss losing title company contracts over compliance gaps.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $8/mo per notary on roster. Average client (35 notaries) = $199 + $280 = $479/mo. 25 clients × $479/mo = $11,975/mo.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Notary rosters churn constantly (new signers added, credentials lapsing on staggered cycles), background screenings must renew annually per ALTA standards regardless of commission length, and RON authorization is a newly-added recurring compliance layer most signing services haven't automated at all yet.
**Biggest risk:** Smaller signing services with under 10 notaries may not see enough value to pay — focus initial sales on services with 25+ notary rosters, where a single audit failure has real revenue consequences.
**Growth path:** Side project → 10 signing services ($4.8K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 25 clients ($12K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by expanding into title agency-side notary vendor management (title companies managing their own approved-vendor rosters directly).

## This Session's Pick: #4 (Auto Body 6H & BAR License) for the clearest revenue-protection hook; #2 (DMEPOS Multi-State Compliance) for the highest per-client price ceiling
Auto body shops are the standout because the sales pitch writes itself around something owners already fear losing — DRP insurer referral volume — rather than an abstract EPA fine risk. That makes the value proposition concrete and the $300+/mo price point easy to justify from the first sales call, and SCRS/ASA chapter meetings are an unusually dense, easy-to-reach distribution channel for a niche this specific.

DMEPOS suppliers are the pick for ceiling because the stakes (losing Medicare billing privileges) and complexity (50-state licensure plus federal accreditation) support the highest per-client price in this session at just 20 clients. It's a smaller total addressable market than auto body shops, but each client is worth roughly double, which shortens the path to $12K/mo once the first handful of suppliers are onboarded through AAHomecare's network.
