### 1. Chimney Sweep & Solid-Fuel Heating Appliance Service Company CSIA/NFI Certification & NFPA 211 Inspection-Level Compliance
**Problem:** Chimney sweep and solid-fuel heating appliance service companies depend on every technician holding current certification through the Chimney Safety Institute of America (CSIA) or the National Fireplace Institute (NFI), renewed on a multi-year cycle through continuing education, since most homeowners' insurance carriers and virtually every real-estate transaction now require proof of a CSIA-certified inspection before binding or renewing coverage on a home with a wood stove, fireplace, or pellet appliance — a job completed by a technician whose certification has lapsed produces an inspection report an insurer or title company won't accept, forcing an expensive re-inspection and putting the company's referral relationship with local insurance agents and real-estate agents at risk. Every job must also be documented at the correct NFPA 211 inspection level — Level 1 for a routine annual sweep of an unchanged system, Level 2 (requiring a video camera scan of the full flue) for a real-estate transaction, insurance-mandated inspection, or after any appliance/fuel-type change, and Level 3 (requiring removal of permanent components) when Level 1 or 2 reveals a hidden hazard — and a company that documents a real-estate-transaction inspection at Level 1 when the client actually needed a Level 2 camera scan has produced a report a home inspector or the buyer's attorney can reject outright, a gap that surfaces at the worst possible moment: mid-closing. A small chimney company running several technicians across a heavy fall/winter booking season and dozens of insurance-driven and real-estate-driven jobs manages per-technician CSIA/NFI certification currency, per-job NFPA 211 inspection-level assignment, and insurance/real-estate-compliant report generation with no dedicated compliance function.
**What to build:** A per-technician CSIA/NFI certification currency tracker with a "cleared to inspect" gate blocking a job from being assigned to a technician whose certification has lapsed; a per-job NFPA 211 inspection-level selector that flags the correct level from the job type (routine, insurance, real-estate, post-appliance-change) before the appointment is scheduled; and an insurance/real-estate-ready report generator producing the documentation format carriers and title companies expect.
**Skill fit:** CSIA/NFI's certification-renewal cycle and NFPA 211's inspection-level criteria are public, structured standards with a well-defined per-technician and per-job record requirement, and the inspect-eligibility gate plus the inspection-level selector are the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance record clears / compute the requirement from the job type" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a seasonal home-services trade most small chimney companies currently track on a paper appointment book with no systematic cross-check between a technician's certification status and the inspection level a specific job actually requires.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the certification tracker with the inspect-eligibility gate, the NFPA 211 level selector, and the report generator; land the first 5-8 companies through National Chimney Sweep Guild (NCSG) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — National Chimney Sweep Guild (NCSG) member directory and CSIA's own certified-sweep locator, where small chimney companies already navigate certification renewal and insurance-driven Level 2 inspection documentation with no dedicated compliance staff.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $179/mo base + $10/mo per certified technician tracked. Average client (4 technicians) = $179 + $40 = $219/mo. 41-52 companies × $219/mo = **$8,979-11,388/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every technician's CSIA/NFI certification renews on its own independent multi-year cycle, every job opens its own NFPA 211 inspection-level determination, and the fall/winter booking surge generates a concentrated wave of insurance- and real-estate-driven Level 2 inspections every single season — a continuous, per-technician and per-job compliance stream tied directly to the company's booking volume.
**Biggest risk:** A job completed by a technician whose CSIA/NFI certification has lapsed, or a real-estate/insurance job documented at the wrong NFPA 211 level, is exactly the gap that gets an inspection report rejected by a carrier or title company at the worst possible moment — the inspect-eligibility gate and the inspection-level selector are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → certification tracker, inspection-level selector, and report generator deployed, first 5-8 company retainers through NCSG outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 22-26 companies. Replaces-full-time-salary → 41-52 companies, pushing past $8.98-11.39K/mo.

### 2. Farm & Agricultural Equipment Dealer State Franchise-Protection, EPA Emissions-Label & ROPS Compliance
**Problem:** Farm and agricultural equipment dealers operate under a state Equipment Dealer Protection Act or Farm Equipment Dealership Act — a distinct body of franchise law, separate from motor-vehicle dealer statutes, found in states including Minnesota, Wisconsin, Iowa, and Illinois, that gives a dealer specific inventory- and parts-repurchase rights and a "day in court" venue protection if a manufacturer terminates the dealership agreement — and a dealer who lets its own compliance documentation (warranty-claim records, inventory reconciliation) lapse weakens its negotiating position and its legal footing the moment a manufacturer relationship sours, since the statute's repurchase and venue protections typically hinge on the dealer being able to document its own performance under the agreement. Every new tractor, combine, or self-propelled implement sold must carry a verifiable EPA emissions-certification label matching its engine tier, and a dealer selling a unit whose label doesn't match its actual engine — common with gray-market or improperly re-manifolded imports — risks EPA enforcement independent of any state dealer-licensing issue. Every used tractor taken in on trade or resold must have its Rollover Protective Structure (ROPS) verified against OSHA and ANSI/ASAE S318 performance standards before resale, since a ROPS that was removed, damaged, or never properly reinstalled is one of the most common findings behind a fatal tractor-rollover investigation, and a dealer who resells a unit with a compromised ROPS carries direct liability exposure. Manufacturer warranty-reimbursement claims are separately audited against the manufacturer's own flat-rate labor schedule, and a claim that doesn't match the flat-rate time allowed risks a post-payment clawback months later. A small dealership running a rotating equipment inventory and multiple manufacturer relationships manages franchise-protection documentation, per-unit EPA emissions-label verification, per-used-unit ROPS verification, and warranty-claim flat-rate matching with no dedicated compliance function.
**What to build:** A franchise-protection documentation log tracking the dealer's own performance records (inventory turns, warranty response times) that the state Equipment Dealership Act's repurchase and venue protections depend on; a per-unit EPA emissions-label verification tracker confirming the label matches the engine before a new unit is marked sale-ready; a per-used-unit ROPS verification checklist gating resale until confirmed compliant; and a warranty-reimbursement claim tracker matching each claim against the manufacturer's flat-rate schedule before submission.
**Skill fit:** State Equipment Dealership Act provisions, EPA engine-emissions tier data, and manufacturer flat-rate labor schedules are structured, lookup-table-friendly regulatory and commercial data well suited to a developer who can model state-by-state and manufacturer-by-manufacturer variance cleanly, and the ROPS-verification gate plus the flat-rate-matching check are the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance record clears / flag the mismatch before submission" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a dealer trade most small equipment dealers currently track on a shared spreadsheet with no systematic per-unit ROPS or flat-rate cross-check.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the franchise-protection documentation log, the EPA emissions-label tracker, the ROPS verification checklist, and the warranty flat-rate matcher; land the first 5-8 dealers through North American Equipment Dealers Association (NAEDA) outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — North American Equipment Dealers Association (NAEDA) member directory and state farm equipment dealers association meetings, where independent dealership owners already navigate manufacturer warranty-audit letters and ROPS liability exposure with no dedicated compliance staff.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299/mo base + $10/mo per unit in active warranty-tracking inventory. Average client (15 units) = $299 + $150 = $449/mo. 20-25 dealers × $449/mo = **$8,980-11,225/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every used unit taken on trade opens its own ROPS-verification event, every new unit sold opens its own emissions-label check, and every warranty repair opens its own flat-rate-matching event — a continuous, per-unit compliance stream tied directly to the dealer's inventory turnover and service volume.
**Biggest risk:** A used tractor resold with a compromised ROPS is exactly the fact pattern behind a fatal rollover liability claim, and a warranty claim that doesn't match the manufacturer's flat-rate schedule risks a clawback discovered months after payment — the ROPS-verification gate and the flat-rate-matching tracker are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → franchise-protection log, emissions-label tracker, ROPS checklist, and flat-rate matcher deployed, first 5-8 dealer retainers through NAEDA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-15 dealers. Replaces-full-time-salary → 20-25 dealers, pushing past $8.98-11.23K/mo.

### 3. Direct Primary Care (DPC) Medical Practice State DPC Statute & Insurance-Exemption Compliance
**Problem:** Direct primary care (DPC) physicians charge patients a flat monthly or annual membership fee for unlimited primary-care access instead of billing insurance per visit, an arrangement that depends entirely on the practice's membership agreement matching the specific statutory language required by the physician's state DPC-enabling statute — now on the books in more than 30 states — to qualify for the statute's exemption from state Department of Insurance licensure; an agreement missing the required "this is not insurance" disclosure, a mandated fee-schedule or fee-cap disclosure, or a statutory right-to-cancel provision risks the state insurance commissioner classifying the practice as an unauthorized health insurer, an exposure carrying far steeper penalties than any ordinary medical-board licensing issue and one that has nothing to do with whether the physician's own state medical license is in good standing. Several state DPC statutes additionally require the practice to file a notice or registration with the state insurance commissioner even though the arrangement is exempt from full insurance licensure, and a practice that never files the required notice — a step easy to miss since it isn't part of the physician's own medical-board renewal — is exactly the gap a state insurance department's periodic review of self-reported DPC arrangements is built to catch. A small DPC practice running one or more physicians across a growing membership panel manages per-provider medical-license currency, statutory membership-agreement-language compliance, and state insurance-commissioner notice filing with no dedicated compliance function — and a membership agreement missing a single required disclosure clause, discovered during a state insurance department inquiry, can force the practice to refund every member's fees collected under the deficient agreement.
**What to build:** A membership-agreement compliance checker validating each state's required DPC statutory disclosure language (insurance-exemption notice, fee-cap disclosure, right-to-cancel provision) against the practice's actual agreement template before it's used with a new member; a state insurance-commissioner notice/registration tracker flagging any required filing that hasn't been submitted; and a per-provider state medical license currency tracker.
**Skill fit:** State DPC-enabling statutes' required disclosure language is public, structured, lookup-table-friendly legal text well suited to a developer who can model the 30-plus state variance cleanly, and the agreement-compliance checker plus the notice-filing tracker are the same deterministic "match the record against the rule / flag the missing filing" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a fast-growing primary-care model most small DPC practices currently launch using a generic downloaded template with no systematic per-state statutory cross-check.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the membership-agreement compliance checker, the insurance-commissioner notice tracker, and the provider license tracker; land the first 5-8 practices through DPC Alliance member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — DPC Alliance (formerly DPC Coalition) member directory and DPC-focused regional physician meetups, where independent DPC physicians launching or expanding a practice already navigate state-specific statutory agreement language with no dedicated regulatory-affairs staff.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $20/mo per provider tracked. Average client (2 providers) = $199 + $40 = $239/mo. 38-47 practices × $239/mo = **$9,082-11,233/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new member signed opens its own agreement-compliance check against the current statutory language, every new provider added carries its own license-currency clock, and a state legislature amending its DPC statute (a genuinely recurring event as more states adopt or update these laws) triggers a practice-wide agreement-template review — a compliance stream tied directly to the practice's membership growth and its state's own legislative activity.
**Biggest risk:** A membership agreement missing a single required statutory disclosure clause, discovered during a state insurance department inquiry, can force the practice to refund every member enrolled under the deficient agreement and expose it to unauthorized-insurance penalties — the agreement-compliance checker is the core protection.
**Growth path:** Side project → agreement-compliance checker, notice tracker, and provider license tracker deployed, first 5-8 practice retainers through DPC Alliance outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 18-22 practices. Replaces-full-time-salary → 38-47 practices, pushing past $9.08-11.23K/mo.

### 4. Pet Cemetery & Pet Cremation State Pet Cemetery Act & Perpetual-Care Trust Compliance
**Problem:** Pet cemeteries and pet cremation businesses operate under a state Pet Cemetery Act — on the books in states including California (administered by the Cemetery and Funeral Bureau), New York, and Louisiana — requiring a dedicated interment or cremation record for every animal (owner information, species, date and method of disposition, and plot or niche assignment for a burial) and licensure or registration with the state cemetery-regulating agency, distinct from any human funeral or crematory license and carrying its own renewal cycle; a facility that lets its state Pet Cemetery Act registration lapse, or can't produce a complete interment record when a grieving pet owner or a state investigator requests one, is exactly the finding a state cemetery-board complaint investigation is built to catch. The statute's core consumer protection is the perpetual-care trust fund: a fixed percentage of every plot sale or interment fee must be deposited into an irrevocable trust dedicated to funding the grounds' maintenance indefinitely, even after the business itself closes, and a facility that fails to deposit the required percentage — or that commingles trust-fund contributions with its own operating account — is committing a trust-fund violation a state cemetery-board audit targets aggressively, since the entire statutory purpose is preventing an abandoned pet cemetery from falling into disrepair once its owner stops operating. A small pet cemetery and cremation operation running a physical burial ground alongside individual and communal cremation services manages per-interment record completeness, perpetual-care trust-fund deposit reconciliation against required percentages, and state registration currency with no dedicated compliance function.
**What to build:** A per-interment/cremation record tracker (owner, species, disposition method, plot/niche assignment) with a completeness gate before a service is marked closed; a perpetual-care trust-fund deposit reconciliation tracker computing the required percentage from each sale and flagging any shortfall against actual deposits; and a state registration renewal tracker.
**Skill fit:** State Pet Cemetery Act trust-fund percentage requirements and registration-renewal cycles are public, structured regulatory data with a well-defined per-interment record requirement, and the trust-fund reconciliation tracker plus the registration renewal tracker are the same deterministic "reconcile the ledger against the required percentage / compute the deadline from the cycle" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a small, close-knit death-care niche most independent pet cemetery operators currently track on a paper ledger with no systematic per-sale trust-fund cross-check.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the interment/cremation record tracker, the perpetual-care trust-fund reconciliation tracker, and the state registration tracker; land the first 5-8 facilities through International Association of Pet Cemeteries and Crematories (IAOPCC) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — International Association of Pet Cemeteries and Crematories (IAOPCC) member directory, where independent pet cemetery and cremation operators already navigate perpetual-care trust-fund deposit tracking and state registration renewal with no dedicated compliance staff.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249/mo base + $15/mo per additional acre of interment grounds actively tracked. Average client (2 additional acres) = $249 + $30 = $279/mo. 32-40 facilities × $279/mo = **$8,928-11,160/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every interment or cremation opens its own record-completeness event, every sale triggers its own perpetual-care trust-fund deposit reconciliation, and the state registration renews on its own independent cycle regardless of service volume — a continuous, per-service compliance stream tied directly to the facility's interment and cremation volume.
**Biggest risk:** A perpetual-care trust-fund shortfall, or a missing interment record discovered during a state cemetery-board investigation, is exactly the finding that can revoke the facility's registration and leave its grounds without funded long-term maintenance — the trust-fund reconciliation tracker and the interment-record completeness gate are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → interment/cremation record tracker, trust-fund reconciliation tracker, and registration tracker deployed, first 5-8 facility retainers through IAOPCC outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 16-20 facilities. Replaces-full-time-salary → 32-40 facilities, pushing past $8.93-11.16K/mo.

### 5. Powersports (ATV/UTV/Motorcycle) Dealer State License, CPSC ATV Action Plan & EPA/CARB Emissions Compliance
**Problem:** Powersports dealers selling ATVs, UTVs, and motorcycles hold a state powersports or off-highway-vehicle dealer license distinct from a general motor-vehicle dealer license, renewed annually and commonly bonded — a dealer operating past a lapsed powersports license risks a state licensing complaint independent of any manufacturer relationship. Every dealer selling adult-size ATVs must operate under the CPSC's mandatory ATV safety standard (16 CFR Part 1420), which requires the dealership to follow an approved action plan: offering free hands-on rider training to every purchaser, displaying required age/engine-size warning labels matching each model's engine displacement, and refusing sale of an adult-size ATV for a child's use — a dealer that sells an adult ATV without documenting the training offer, or without the correct engine-size warning label displayed, is exactly the finding a CPSC compliance investigation targets first, and CPSC actively investigates ATV dealers following any youth-injury incident report. Every recreational off-highway engine sold must carry a verifiable EPA (and, for California sales, CARB) emissions-certification label under 40 CFR Part 1051, and a dealer selling a "gray market" or improperly imported unit without a valid certification label faces EPA enforcement exposure the dealership rarely discovers until an inspector flags it. Every unit must also be checked against open NHTSA and manufacturer safety-recall campaigns before delivery, since selling a motorcycle or ATV with an open, uncompleted recall is one of the most common findings a post-incident product-liability investigation targets. A small powersports dealership running several sales staff across a rotating new and used inventory manages state license currency, per-sale CPSC action-plan documentation, per-unit EPA/CARB emissions-label verification, and per-unit recall clearance with no dedicated compliance function.
**What to build:** A per-sale CPSC action-plan documentation tracker confirming the training offer and correct age/engine-size warning label were provided before a sale is marked closed; a per-unit EPA/CARB emissions-label verification tracker gating a new unit from inventory-ready status until confirmed; and a per-unit NHTSA/manufacturer recall-clearance check blocking delivery until confirmed recall-free or repaired.
**Skill fit:** The CPSC's ATV action-plan requirements, EPA's Part 1051 emissions-certification framework, and NHTSA's public recall database are structured, checkable federal regulatory and safety data with a well-defined per-sale and per-unit record requirement, and the action-plan documentation tracker plus the recall-clearance check are the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a youth-safety-sensitive dealer trade most small powersports dealerships currently track on a paper sales folder with no systematic per-sale CPSC or recall cross-check.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the CPSC action-plan tracker, the EPA/CARB emissions-label verification tracker, and the recall-clearance check; land the first 5-8 dealers through Motorcycle Industry Council (MIC) and Specialty Vehicle Institute of America (SVIA) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — Motorcycle Industry Council (MIC) dealer network and Specialty Vehicle Institute of America (SVIA) member directory, where independent powersports dealers already navigate CPSC action-plan documentation and per-unit recall clearance with no dedicated compliance staff.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $12/mo per sales staff tracked. Average client (4 staff) = $199 + $48 = $247/mo. 37-45 dealers × $247/mo = **$9,139-11,115/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every ATV sale opens its own CPSC action-plan documentation event, every new unit received opens its own emissions-label verification check, and every unit sold requires its own fresh recall-clearance check against a constantly updating NHTSA and manufacturer database — a continuous, per-sale and per-unit compliance stream tied directly to the dealer's sales volume.
**Biggest risk:** An adult-size ATV sold without the CPSC-required training offer and warning label, or a unit delivered with an open, uncompleted safety recall, is exactly the finding a CPSC investigation or a post-incident product-liability claim is built to catch — the action-plan tracker and the recall-clearance check are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → CPSC action-plan tracker, emissions-label verification tracker, and recall-clearance check deployed, first 5-8 dealer retainers through MIC/SVIA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 20-24 dealers. Replaces-full-time-salary → 37-45 dealers, pushing past $9.14-11.12K/mo.
