### 1. Quote Follow-Up Automation for Home Improvement Contractors
**Problem:** A roofing, remodeling, or painting contractor sends 30-50 quotes a month and actively follows up on maybe a third of them. The rest go cold — not because the customer chose a competitor, but because nobody followed up and the customer's urgency faded. The contractor's CRM (or more likely their inbox) has no systematic follow-up logic: a quote goes out, the customer says "I'll think about it," and the next touchpoint happens only if the contractor remembers to call. Industry data from contractors using Jobber puts the average quote-to-job conversion rate at 30-40%; contractors who follow up systematically run 50-65%. The gap is captured revenue the contractor is leaving behind on every single quote they send, without any additional marketing spend.
**What to build:** A quote follow-up automation tool that integrates with Jobber and Housecall Pro via their APIs, detects when a quote is sent but not accepted, and fires a timed outreach sequence: a personalized SMS at day 3 ("Hi [name], just wanted to check if you had any questions about the estimate for your [job type]"), a follow-up at day 7, and a last-touch at day 14 with a soft urgency note ("Our schedule fills up quickly — happy to hold a spot if you'd like to move forward"). The sequence stops automatically the moment the customer accepts, declines, or books. A dashboard for the contractor: open quotes by age, conversion rate before vs. after the sequence, and total recovered revenue from quotes that closed after follow-up outreach.
**Skill fit:** Jobber and Housecall Pro both have well-documented REST APIs for quote and job data. SMS delivery via Twilio. The sequence logic is a simple state machine per quote: sent → day 3 → day 7 → day 14 → closed. The conversion rate comparison (before tool vs. after tool) is a straightforward cohort analysis — exactly the kind of analytics work the builder can produce fast.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — Jobber API integration pulling open quotes, the 3-touch SMS sequence with a stop-on-close trigger, and a basic dashboard showing open quote pipeline and sequence status per quote. A contractor who closes 3 additional jobs in the first month from quotes they would have let go cold has paid for the tool many times over.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — home improvement contractors are reachable through the Jobber partner marketplace (Jobber lists integration partners to its 200,000+ user base), Housecall Pro's app ecosystem, and contractor Facebook groups (Contractor Fight, Home Service Millionaire — 30,000-80,000 members each). A case study showing "recovered $14,000 in closed quotes in month 1" posted in those communities generates 20-40 inbound inquiries.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149-299/month per contractor depending on quote volume. Average client at $220/month. 40 contractors × $220/month = **$8,800/month**. Upsell: a declined quote reactivation module ($49/month add-on) — for quotes the customer explicitly declined, a 30-day re-engagement sequence triggered when material prices drop or the contractor has a schedule gap.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every quote the contractor sends restarts a new follow-up sequence. The tool runs continuously without any human intervention on the contractor's end. Once a contractor sees their conversion rate climb from 32% to 51%, the tool is permanently justified — and the historical quote and conversion data builds a switching cost that grows with every new quote sent.
**Biggest risk:** Contractor buy-in on SMS outreach tone — some contractors worry automated texts feel impersonal. Mitigation: the sequences are fully customizable per contractor (their name, their voice, their job types), and the stop-on-response trigger ensures no customer ever gets a follow-up after they've already replied.
**Growth path:** Side project → Jobber integration, 3-touch SMS sequence, and conversion dashboard live, first 5-8 contractor clients through Jobber marketplace. Replaces-part-time-income → 14-18 contractors, add Housecall Pro integration, add email channel alongside SMS, add declined quote reactivation module. Replaces-full-time-salary → 40+ contractors, add ServiceTitan integration for larger contractors, add a quote analytics benchmarking report (your conversion rate vs. contractors in your trade and region).

### 2. Win-Back Campaigns for Gyms and Fitness Studios
**Problem:** A gym or fitness studio loses 3-6% of its member base every month to cancellations — a combination of relocation, financial pressure, schedule changes, and dissatisfaction. Mindbody and Pike13, the dominant studio management platforms, store every cancellation with its date, reason code, and membership type. Neither platform does anything systematic with that data after the cancellation is recorded. The studio's response is typically a manual email from the front desk manager, if anything, sent within a day or two of the cancellation — and then nothing. But the research on customer win-back is consistent: the probability of reactivating a cancelled member is highest at 30-60 days post-cancellation, when their old habits have faded but their guilt and nostalgia are still present. A studio losing 40 members per month and reactivating even 8 of them at a $79/month membership is recovering $638/month in recurring revenue — more than enough to justify a purpose-built win-back tool.
**What to build:** A win-back campaign tool that integrates with Mindbody and Pike13 via their APIs, pulls every cancellation daily, and enrolls each cancelled member in a 3-touch re-engagement sequence: a personal-feeling SMS at day 14 ("We miss you at [Studio Name] — if life got busy, we get it. We'd love to have you back"), a targeted offer at day 30 ("Come back this month and your first week is on us"), and a final low-pressure check-in at day 60. The sequence stops if the member re-books or explicitly opts out. A dashboard for the studio owner: cancellations by month, reactivation rate by outreach touch, recovered revenue from win-back campaigns, and a breakdown of cancellation reason codes (so they can see if "too expensive" is spiking, signaling a pricing issue).
**Skill fit:** Mindbody has a well-documented REST API with webhook support for membership events; Pike13 has a similar API surface. The sequence logic is the same timed state machine as the quote follow-up tool — cancelled → day 14 → day 30 → day 60 → reactivated or closed. The cancellation reason code analysis is a simple aggregation. SMS via Twilio. The dashboard is a standard retention analytics view — cohort analysis by cancel month, reactivation rate by touch.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — Mindbody API integration pulling cancellations, the 3-touch SMS win-back sequence, and a basic dashboard showing reactivations and recovered revenue. A studio owner who sees 6 cancelled members come back in month 1 and can attribute $474/month in recovered revenue directly to the tool has validated the product before the first billing cycle ends.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — gym and fitness studio owners are reachable through the Mindbody partner marketplace (Mindbody has 58,000+ businesses on the platform and an active partner program), the IHRSA (International Health, Racquet & Sportsclub Association) member community, and studio owner Facebook groups (Fitness Business Podcast community, Studio Growth community). A "recovered $6,200 in cancelled memberships in 90 days" case study shared in those communities generates immediate inbound.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-399/month per studio depending on membership base size. Average client at $280/month. 35 studios × $280/month = **$9,800/month**. Upsell: a churn prediction module ($79/month add-on) — flagging active members who are showing early churn signals (attendance dropping below their historical average for 3+ consecutive weeks) before they cancel, so the studio can intervene proactively.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Cancellations arrive every day, automatically enrolling new members in the win-back sequence without any studio staff involvement. The reactivation attribution data — which exact message at which timing brought a member back — builds a proprietary optimization model that improves with scale. A studio that's been running the tool for 6 months has a win-back history that would take months to rebuild elsewhere.
**Biggest risk:** Mindbody API access tiers — Mindbody gates some API functionality behind partnership agreements that take time to establish. Mitigation: start with the Mindbody public API (sufficient for membership and cancellation data) and apply for the partner program early; Pike13 has a more open API surface as a fallback for the first customers.
**Growth path:** Side project → Mindbody integration, 3-touch win-back sequence, and reactivation dashboard live, first 5-8 studio clients through Mindbody marketplace. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-16 studios, add Pike13 integration, add the churn prediction module, add email channel alongside SMS. Replaces-full-time-salary → 35+ studios, add martial arts schools and yoga studios (identical membership dynamics), add a membership health score dashboard giving the owner a single number that summarizes their retention risk.

### 3. Influencer Campaign ROI Tracking for Small DTC Brands
**Problem:** A direct-to-consumer brand doing $500K-$2M on Shopify works with 10-30 micro-influencers per month — paying flat fees of $200-800 per post or 10-15% commissions — and has essentially no idea which creator actually drove sales. UTM links get shared incorrectly (a creator posts a clean URL without the tracking parameter), coupon codes get leaked to deal-sharing sites (RetailMeNot, Honey), and Instagram's last-touch attribution model takes credit for conversions that originated on TikTok three days earlier. The brand's founder manually checks each creator's referral link in Shopify's analytics, sees wildly inconsistent numbers, and makes next month's influencer budget decisions based on gut feel and follower count. An influencer who drove $8,000 in revenue last month gets cut because their follower count looks low; a creator with 200K followers and zero trackable conversions gets a bigger budget. The problem compounds: bad attribution → bad budget decisions → rising cost per acquisition → shrinking margins.
**What to build:** An influencer campaign ROI tracking tool that connects to Shopify via the Shopify Admin API, issues each influencer a unique discount code and UTM-tagged link, tracks both pathways to conversion (code at checkout + UTM-tagged session → order), and applies a 3-day attribution window to capture delayed conversions from social content (a viewer sees the TikTok, thinks about it, and buys 2 days later). Core output: a per-influencer dashboard showing clicks, orders, revenue, average order value, ROAS (revenue ÷ influencer cost), and a 30-day trend. A campaign-level view: total influencer spend, total attributed revenue, blended ROAS, and a ranked creator list from highest to lowest ROAS so the brand knows exactly who to pay more and who to cut. An automated weekly report emailed to the founder every Monday — no login required for the weekly summary.
**Skill fit:** Shopify Admin API is well-documented with strong webhook support for orders and discount code usage. UTM parameter parsing from Shopify's referrer data is straightforward. The 3-day attribution window is a configurable lookback query over session and order timestamps. The creator ROAS ranking is a simple aggregation — total order revenue attributed ÷ creator cost entered manually (or via a simple cost-entry field per creator). No ML, no complex statistics — clean data engineering with a clear output.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — Shopify API integration, unique discount code generation per creator, order attribution by code and UTM, and a per-creator performance dashboard with the weekly email digest. A brand that discovers their top-performing micro-influencer (50K followers, $400/post) drove 3x more revenue than their biggest-name creator (400K followers, $1,200/post) has permanently changed how they allocate their influencer budget.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — DTC brand founders are reachable through DTC-specific Slack communities (DTC Slack, the Operators community), Twitter/X DTC founder circles, Shopify's partner ecosystem and app store, and newsletters like DTC Newsletter and Nik Sharma's content. A "we found our real top performer and it wasn't who we thought" case study resonates immediately in those communities because every DTC founder has the same attribution problem.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/month per brand depending on creator count and order volume. Average client at $400/month. 25 brands × $400/month = **$10,000/month**. Upsell: a creator discovery module ($99/month add-on) — a searchable database of micro-influencers in the brand's niche filtered by estimated engagement rate and past brand partnership history, so the founder can find new creators to test without paying a talent agency.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new influencer campaign adds new creators, new tracking links, and new attribution data to the tool. The historical ROAS database — which creators performed across which campaigns at which spend levels — is a proprietary asset the brand has built inside the tool and wouldn't recreate from scratch. Monthly influencer spend decisions are permanently tied to the tool's output once it's become the source of truth for attribution.
**Biggest risk:** Coupon code leakage — if a creator's unique discount code gets posted to a deal site, all those conversions attribute to that creator even though they weren't driven by the creator's content. Mitigation: flag any discount code with an unusually high redemption velocity (10+ uses in an hour) as a potential leak, and build a single-use or limited-use code option for brands that want tight attribution at the cost of conversion friction.
**Growth path:** Side project → Shopify integration, unique code and UTM tracking, per-creator ROAS dashboard, and weekly email digest live, first 5-8 brand clients through DTC Slack and Shopify app store. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-16 brands, add WooCommerce integration, add the creator discovery module, add a campaign budget optimizer (suggesting reallocation across creators based on trailing ROAS). Replaces-full-time-salary → 25+ brands, add TikTok and Instagram story link tracking, expand to Amazon sellers running influencer programs through Amazon's affiliate API.

### 4. Cash Flow Forecasting for Small Contractors
**Problem:** A general contractor or specialty trade contractor with 6-15 active jobs knows their total backlog — $1.2M in signed contracts — but has no visibility into when the cash from those contracts actually arrives. Each job has its own payment schedule: a deposit at signing, a draw at framing, a draw at rough-in, a final payment at completion. Those milestones land at unpredictable times depending on weather, inspections, and client responsiveness. Meanwhile, payroll runs every two weeks regardless of when the next draw comes in. The result: the contractor has $1.2M in backlog and $18,000 in the bank on a Thursday when payroll is $42,000 on Friday. Not because the business is failing — because the cash timing is invisible until it's a crisis. Contractors describe this as the most stressful part of running their business, and it's entirely a data problem: the contract values and milestone schedules exist in their agreements, but nobody has mapped them to a calendar.
**What to build:** A 13-week rolling cash flow forecast for small contractors — a tool where the contractor enters each active job's remaining payment milestones (amount, estimated date, and milestone trigger), and the tool produces a week-by-week cash position view: expected cash in from draws, expected cash out (payroll, materials, sub payments pulled from QBO via API), and the resulting cash balance. A red/yellow/green indicator for each week: green means comfortable, yellow means watch it, red means a gap is coming and the contractor needs to act now (accelerate a draw request, delay a material order, or draw on their line of credit). An alert system: when a milestone slides past its expected date (the contractor marks it as delayed), the forecast automatically updates and re-flags any newly created cash gaps.
**Skill fit:** QuickBooks Online API for pulling recurring expenses, payroll data, and outstanding bills into the cash-out side of the forecast. The cash-in side is milestone data entered manually per job — the contractor knows their payment schedule, they just don't have a tool to aggregate it. The 13-week rolling window is a straightforward date-bucketed aggregation. The red/yellow/green logic is a configurable minimum cash balance threshold. No complex modeling — the value is entirely in the aggregation and visualization of data the contractor already has but can't see in one place.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — manual milestone entry per job, QBO API integration for expense data, week-by-week cash position view, and the red/yellow/green gap alert. A contractor who sees a cash gap coming in week 6 — with enough time to request a draw early and avoid a crisis — has gotten more value from the tool in the first week than the annual subscription cost.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — small contractor owners are reachable through the Associated General Contractors (AGC) local chapter communities, the National Association of the Remodeling Industry (NARI) member network, contractor-focused Facebook groups (The Contractor Fight — 50,000+ members), and QBO ProAdvisor referrals (bookkeepers who serve contractor clients are a high-leverage referral channel because cash flow is a recurring conversation in every bookkeeper-contractor relationship).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-449/month per contractor depending on job volume. Average client at $300/month. 35 contractors × $300/month = **$10,500/month**. Upsell: a draw request automation module ($79/month add-on) — when a milestone is due, the tool generates the AIA G702/G703 pay application form pre-filled with the contract data, so the contractor can send the draw request to the GC or owner in two clicks instead of filling it out manually.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Active jobs are always being added, and each new job adds new milestones to the rolling forecast. The historical cash flow data — which milestone types reliably slip, which clients pay on time — becomes a predictive layer the contractor trusts. Switching means re-entering all active job milestones from scratch, a task no contractor wants to do mid-project.
**Biggest risk:** Manual milestone entry friction — contractors are not known for careful data entry, and if the milestone dates are stale, the forecast is wrong. Mitigation: the alert system for missed milestones (the contractor marks a payment as "delayed" with a new expected date) makes maintenance quick, and the red/yellow/green indicator creates a strong incentive to keep the data current because the output is directly useful.
**Growth path:** Side project → manual milestone entry, QBO integration, 13-week forecast, and gap alerts live, first 5-8 contractor clients through AGC chapter outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-18 contractors, add draw request automation module, add integration with Buildertrend and CoConstruct to pull milestone schedules directly from the job management software. Replaces-full-time-salary → 35+ contractors, add a subcontractor payment scheduling view (when do I owe my subs vs. when am I receiving draws), expand to commercial specialty trades (electrical, plumbing, mechanical) with longer milestone windows and more complex payment structures.

### 5. Subcontractor Document Collection for General Contractors
**Problem:** Every new subcontractor a general contractor brings onto a job must provide a certificate of insurance (COI) naming the GC as additionally insured, a signed W-9, a signed subcontract agreement, and in many cases OSHA 10 or OSHA 30 certification for their crew leads. The GC's project manager sends an email requesting these documents, the sub responds piecemeal over 3-5 business days (W-9 today, COI tomorrow, subcontract next week), and the PM manually checks each document as it arrives — verifying the COI expiration date, confirming the additional insured endorsement is present, checking that the subcontract version matches the current template. Until everything is in order, the sub legally cannot start on site. A GC running 15-25 active projects with 3-6 new subs onboarding per project is spending 4-6 hours per week on document collection and verification across their project management team — and still letting subs start work before their COI is confirmed because the job schedule won't wait.
**What to build:** A subcontractor onboarding and document collection tool — the GC configures a checklist of required documents per sub type (framing sub requires COI + W-9 + subcontract; electrical sub additionally requires state contractor license), then sends each new sub a single link. The sub opens the link on their phone, uploads each required document, and e-signs the subcontract via DocuSign API. The tool automatically validates the COI: it parses the expiration date and confirms the GC is listed as additionally insured (using a simple PDF field parser for standard ACORD 25 form fields). The GC's dashboard shows every sub across all active projects in one view — green (all docs received and valid), yellow (docs pending), red (COI expired or additional insured missing) — and pings the GC and the sub when a document is missing or when a COI is within 30 days of expiration. A "cleared to work" status per sub, auto-generated when all documents are in order.
**Skill fit:** DocuSign API for subcontract e-signing. PDF parsing for ACORD 25 COI fields (expiration date and additional insured text are in standardized positions on the standard form — parseable with pdfplumber or a similar library). S3 for document storage. The dashboard state machine (pending → complete → expired) is straightforward. The ACORD 25 parsing is the most interesting technical piece and also the strongest moat — a tool that auto-validates the COI instead of requiring the PM to check it manually saves significant time and eliminates human error on the most consequential check.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — sub-facing document upload link, DocuSign integration for subcontract e-signing, manual COI expiration date entry with expiration alert, and the GC dashboard with per-project sub status. The ACORD 25 auto-parser is V2. A PM who goes from chasing COI documents across 12 email threads to seeing a single dashboard with every sub's status across all projects has justified the tool in the first week.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — general contractors and project managers are reachable through Associated Builders and Contractors (ABC) chapter communities, the Associated General Contractors (AGC) member network, Procore's app marketplace (Procore is the dominant construction project management platform and lists integration partners to its user base), and construction industry LinkedIn groups. A sub compliance consultant — the same high-leverage referral channel as a bookkeeper for the cash flow tool — can refer 5-10 GC clients from a single relationship.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149-349/month per GC depending on project volume and sub count. Average client at $225/month. 40 GCs × $225/month = **$9,000/month**. Upsell: a COI monitoring service ($49/month add-on) — ongoing monitoring of every sub's COI expiration across all their projects, with automatic re-request emails sent to the sub 45 and 15 days before expiration so the GC never has an expired COI on an active job.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new project adds new subs, each requiring a new onboarding sequence. The document history per sub builds over time — a GC who has worked with a sub before can see their prior documents in the system and issue a new subcontract with one click against their existing W-9 and COI (if not yet expired). That convenience compounds the switching cost with every project completed.
**Biggest risk:** Sub adoption friction — requiring the sub to upload documents to a new link instead of emailing them might create resistance. Mitigation: the link is completely frictionless for the sub (no account creation, mobile-optimized, one-page upload form), and the GC controls the mandate — if the GC requires the link, the sub uses the link. Framing it as "you'll get your payment faster because we'll process your paperwork faster" is usually enough.
**Growth path:** Side project → document upload link, DocuSign integration, manual COI validation, and GC dashboard live, first 5-8 GC clients through ABC chapter outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-18 GCs, add ACORD 25 auto-parser for COI validation, add Procore integration for syncing sub list from active projects, add COI monitoring add-on. Replaces-full-time-salary → 40+ GCs, add state contractor license verification (scraping state licensing board lookup pages for the sub's license number and expiration), expand to commercial real estate property managers who have the same sub onboarding problem for maintenance and renovation contractors.
