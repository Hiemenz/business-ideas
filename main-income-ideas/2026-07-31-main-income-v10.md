# Main Income Ideas - 2026-07-31 (Session 10)

## Who this is for
Builder profile: 29, software engineering + data analytics skills. Same bar as Sessions 1-9: not "$500-3K/mo of side money" but a solo-buildable path with a credible route to **$8-15K+/mo** — full-time-salary-replacement income — within 12-24 months.

## Ground rules for every idea in this series
- **Solo-buildable start.** One person, evenings/weekends, no funding, no cofounder required to get the first version live.
- **Realistic path to $8-15K+/mo**, not a side-money ceiling — every idea states the math (client count x price point, or transaction count x margin) plainly, no TAM hand-waving.
- **Time to first dollar < 90 days.** Long enterprise sales cycles are disqualifying by default.
- **Named distribution channel** for the first 10 customers *and* a credible path to the next 10x.
- **Boring is fine.** Low-glamour, real-pain problems beat trendy ones.
- **No repeats.** Nothing below overlaps a concept already covered across Sessions 1-9 of this series, `side-income-ideas/`, or `discovered-problems/`. This session introduces five genuinely new sectors: healthcare provider credentialing (distinct from ICHRA benefit administration in Session 9 and pharmacy reimbursement in Session 4), oversize/overweight load permitting (distinct from DOT compliance management in Session 9 and freight brokerage in Session 8), Professional Employer Organization brokering (distinct from equipment leasing/finance brokerage, blue-collar placement, and senior living placement covered earlier), commercial lease administration (distinct from the STR compliance data API in Session 1), and specialty tax credit identification and contingency-fee recovery (a contingency model applied to an entirely new sector — R&D and WOTC credits — not covered in any prior session).

### 1. Medical & Dental Practice Credentialing Service
**Problem:** Every healthcare provider (physician, therapist, dentist, nurse practitioner) who wants to accept insurance must complete "credentialing" — a paperwork-intensive enrollment process — with each insurance network separately. Credentialing requires gathering and submitting licenses, DEA registrations, malpractice insurance certificates, education verification, and signed attestations to each payer, and then waiting 60-180 days for approval. Solo practitioners and small practices either do this themselves (hours of tedious data entry into each payer's portal) or pay a medical billing company that treats it as a side task and does it poorly. A missed credentialing deadline or an error in an application can delay a provider's ability to bill for months, costing thousands of dollars in lost revenue.
**What to build:** A credentialing management service — a provider document repository (licenses, certifications, malpractice certs, all tracked with expiration dates), a payer-application tracking tool (which networks are pending, approved, or need follow-up, with deadline alerts), and a document-submission workflow that handles the per-payer application process on behalf of the provider — charging a flat fee per payer application ($150-300 per application) plus an optional monthly maintenance retainer for re-credentialing and CAQH profile maintenance ($75-150/mo per provider).
**Skill fit:** Direct on the document-tracking and deadline-management tooling (credentialing is a structured-data problem: provider attributes → payer-specific application requirements → submission status tracking, the same pattern used across several prior sessions); the payer application process is procedural and learnable, not a licensed healthcare profession.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends for a provider document repository and payer-application status tracker; the first 5-10 payer application workflows are manual, refined into a repeatable checklist before any further automation.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through healthcare staffing agencies (who place locum tenens and temp providers who need rapid credentialing), medical billing companies (who often refer credentialing out rather than handle it), and healthcare attorney firms that advise new practice setups.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $150-300 per payer application; a new solo provider typically needs to credential with 8-12 payer networks = $1,200-3,600 per new provider onboarded; plus $75-150/mo maintenance per active provider client. 10-15 new providers/mo plus 80-100 providers on monthly maintenance = **$12-25K/mo** at steady state, with the maintenance base compounding as new providers are added each month without dropping off.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** The initial credentialing project creates a long-term maintenance relationship (re-credentialing happens every 2-3 years per payer; CAQH profiles require quarterly attestation) — each new provider added to the maintenance roster increases monthly recurring revenue permanently, creating a compounding annuity without the client churn that plagues one-time service businesses.
**Biggest risk:** Credentialing errors or missed deadlines that cause a provider to lose billing eligibility expose the service to real financial liability from an upset client — document every action taken, every submission made, and every payer communication with timestamps; provide clients with a clear written scope covering what the service does and does not guarantee (the payer's processing time is outside the service's control).
**Growth path:** Side project → first 3-5 provider credentialing projects through healthcare staffing agency referrals, payer-application workflow mapped for the most common networks (BCBS, Aetna, Cigna, UHC). Replaces-part-time-income → 20-30 providers on monthly maintenance + 5-8 new provider projects/mo, billing company referral partners active (~$6-10K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 80-100 providers on monthly maintenance base (compounding without churn), 10-15 new projects/mo, maintenance revenue alone approaching $8-15K/mo before any new-project fees.

### 2. Oversize/Overweight Load Permit Management
**Problem:** Any truck hauling cargo that exceeds standard legal size or weight limits (excavators, wind turbine components, manufactured homes, large transformers, prefabricated bridge sections) must obtain a permit from every state and, in some states, every county it traverses — a multi-jurisdiction paperwork exercise that requires route surveys, axle-weight calculations, travel restrictions (time of day, pilot car requirements), and sequential submissions to each jurisdiction's permitting authority. Heavy haul trucking companies, crane rental fleets, and equipment dealers arranging delivery moves deal with this on every single load and still largely handle it through phone calls and fax submissions to state DOT offices, since most state portals are not interconnected.
**What to build:** A permit management service — a route-analysis tool that maps a planned move, identifies each jurisdiction requiring a permit, and calculates the applicable size/weight rules and fees; a multi-jurisdiction submission workflow that prepares and submits permit applications (electronically where available, by fax or phone where not); and a permit-status tracking system that monitors approvals and flags any travel restriction conditions before the move date — charging a flat fee per permit per jurisdiction plus a per-move coordination fee.
**Skill fit:** Direct on the route-analysis and multi-jurisdiction tracking tooling (this is a geographic routing and rules-engine problem: route polyline → list of state/county jurisdictions with their specific permit rules, a data-engineering problem the builder's skillset handles well); the permit submission itself is procedural work with each state's DOT system, learnable without specialized licensing.
**MVP scope:** 3-4 weekends for a route-to-jurisdiction mapper and permit-status tracker, starting with the 10-15 most common permit states in a target corridor (e.g., the Southeast or Midwest, where heavy equipment movement is high); state permit rules are publicly documented in each state's DOT oversize/overweight manual.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through heavy haul trucking companies (findable via FMCSA carrier search filtering for flatbed/oversize carriers), crane rental companies (regional directories of crane contractors), and equipment dealers who arrange machine deliveries and deal with permit requirements on every sale.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $50-150 per permit per jurisdiction plus a $100-250 per-move coordination fee; a 5-state move generates $350-1,000 in permit fees plus coordination; handling 20-30 moves/mo across a client base of 5-8 repeat heavy haul operators = **$10-20K/mo**, with volume that scales predictably as each client account grows and refers other carriers.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every oversize load move requires permits — there is no workaround, no "do it internally without a permit service" option for a compliant carrier — creating demand that is legally mandatory and recurring on every job the carrier books. Repeat clients (a carrier running 10-15 oversize moves/mo) generate predictable monthly revenue without any sales effort after the initial relationship is established.
**Biggest risk:** A permit application submitted with incorrect axle weights or dimensions that results in a citation, accident, or bridge damage liability for the carrier is a serious error — build the route-analysis and weight-calculation tools carefully and have the carrier verify all vehicle specifications before submission; consider carrying professional liability insurance from the start given the real consequences of a mis-permitted load.
**Growth path:** Side project → first 3-5 moves permitted for 1-2 repeat heavy haul operators, permit workflows mapped for 8-10 states. Replaces-part-time-income → 10-15 moves/mo across 3-5 carrier clients, route-analysis tool handling the jurisdiction identification automatically, manual submission refined into a repeatable process (~$5-10K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 25-35 moves/mo, portal integrations with the highest-volume state permit systems reducing per-move submission time, 8-12 repeat carrier clients generating consistent monthly volume, pushing past $12-20K/mo.

### 3. PEO Brokering for Small Businesses
**Problem:** Professional Employer Organizations (PEOs) co-employ a small business's workforce, giving the business access to large-group health insurance rates, streamlined payroll processing, comprehensive HR software, and better workers' compensation pricing — tangible cost savings that most businesses with 5-50 employees would benefit from but rarely pursue because the PEO market is confusing (dozens of competing providers with opaque pricing), the sales process is driven entirely by PEO direct reps who have conflicts of interest, and most small business owners have never heard of co-employment. An independent PEO broker represents the employer's interests, shops their workforce across multiple PEOs, compares total cost of employment, and places them with the best fit — earning a commission from the winning PEO.
**What to build:** A PEO comparison and placement service — a client intake and workforce census tool (employee count, current benefits, workers' comp class codes, payroll run history), a multi-PEO RFP submission and response-comparison framework that normalizes each PEO's proposal into a consistent cost-comparison format, and an ongoing account management relationship with placed clients — earning a monthly commission of $15-50/employee/mo paid by the PEO for the life of the client relationship.
**Skill fit:** Direct on the data side (normalizing and comparing PEO proposals across different pricing structures — administrative fee percentages vs. flat per-employee rates vs. bundled vs. unbundled health costs — is exactly the kind of multi-variable cost comparison that a data analytics background handles well); the PEO relationship and client advisory work is learnable, and PEO broker certification programs (NAPEO offers training resources) exist specifically for independent brokers entering this space.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends for a workforce-census intake tool and a PEO proposal comparison template; establishing appointment/commission agreements with 4-6 major PEOs (ADP TotalSource, Insperity, TriNet, Paychex PEO, Oasis, Justworks) is the primary setup work, accomplished by registering as an independent broker with each PEO's channel partner program.
**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — distribute through small business accountants (who see the payroll and benefit costs and can identify which clients are overpaying), commercial insurance agents (who hold the workers' comp relationship and can identify clients whose comp rates would improve under a PEO), and small business attorneys who advise on entity and employment structure.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Commission of $15-50/employee/mo; an average placed client has 15 employees, generating $225-750/mo ongoing commission. Placing 2-3 clients/mo over 24 months builds a book of 50-60 active client relationships (assuming modest churn) × 15 employees avg × $30/employee/mo average = **$22-27K/mo** in ongoing passive commission — the ceiling is genuinely high because commission compounds as new clients are placed on top of existing ones without the old ones dropping off proportionally.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Unlike fee-per-transaction services where income only flows when a new transaction closes, PEO broker commissions are perpetual — a client placed three years ago still generates monthly income as long as they stay with the PEO. The book of business functions like a recurring revenue portfolio that grows with each new placement and largely holds value through client tenure, making this one of the few ideas in this series with a compounding-annuity structure that genuinely accelerates over time rather than plateauing.
**Biggest risk:** Commission rates and structures vary by PEO and can change at contract renewal — do not build financial projections on the highest available commission rate, and diversify across 4-6 PEO relationships rather than concentrating on a single PEO's program, since a single PEO reducing its broker commission has outsized impact if they represent 80% of your placed book.
**Growth path:** Side project → first PEO channel partner agreements signed, first 2-3 client placements through accountant referrals (these first placements generate the case studies needed to unlock more referral relationships). Replaces-part-time-income → 15-20 active client relationships, 150-200 employees under management, accountant and insurance agent referral partners generating 2-3 new client opportunities/mo (~$5-8K/mo and building). Replaces-full-time-salary → 40-60 active client relationships, 600-900 employees generating ongoing commission, new placements each month adding to an already-substantial base, pushing past $12-18K/mo with the compounding base still growing.

### 4. Commercial Lease Administration Service
**Problem:** Small-to-mid-size commercial tenants (businesses occupying leased office, retail, or industrial space) sign multi-year leases with dozens of financial and operational obligations they stop tracking the moment the lease is signed: rent escalation dates, CAM (common area maintenance) charge reconciliation deadlines, option exercise windows (renew or expand options that expire if not exercised by a specific date), tenant improvement allowance reimbursement triggers, and co-tenancy clauses. Missing a lease option deadline forfeits negotiating leverage or forces a disruptive relocation; failing to audit a CAM reconciliation typically means paying overcharges the landlord won't proactively refund. Most tenants have no system for this and most small business attorneys don't manage it actively after the lease is signed.
**What to build:** A lease administration service — a lease-abstract tool that reads a commercial lease and extracts the key dates, obligations, and financial terms into a structured format (rent schedule, escalation triggers, CAM cap terms, option windows, landlord notice requirements); a deadline-alert system that surfaces critical action dates 90/60/30 days in advance; and a CAM audit workflow that reviews the landlord's annual CAM reconciliation statement against the lease's allowable expense definitions, identifying overcharges — charging a per-lease setup fee plus a monthly monitoring retainer, with an optional contingency fee on CAM overcharges recovered.
**Skill fit:** Direct on the lease-abstraction and CAM-audit tooling (extracting structured data from lease documents and comparing landlord expense allocations against contractual limits is a data-extraction and rules-comparison problem squarely in the builder's skillset); commercial lease terms follow well-established patterns, and the CAM audit methodology is documented in commercial real estate industry resources without requiring a real estate license.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends for a lease-abstract template and deadline-tracking system, plus a CAM reconciliation comparison tool; the first 5-10 leases are abstracted manually to validate the extraction fields before any document-parsing automation is added.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through commercial tenant-rep brokers (who place tenants in leases and want to offer post-placement value), commercial real estate attorneys (who review leases but often don't provide ongoing administration), and small business accountants (who handle rent payments but not lease compliance).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $200-400 per-lease setup (abstracting and onboarding a new lease) + $75-200/lease/mo monitoring retainer; 50-80 leases under active administration = **$5-16K/mo** in monitoring retainer alone, supplemented by CAM audit fees ($300-800 per annual reconciliation reviewed) and option-exercise facilitation fees. The book grows steadily as new lease clients are added and existing clients sign new leases.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Commercial leases run 3-10 years, so a client onboarded today generates retainer revenue for the full lease term without re-acquisition effort — and the CAM audit work arrives annually whether or not any new clients are added, providing a recurring revenue spike each spring when landlords issue their prior-year CAM reconciliations. The steady drip of missed lease events (options, escalations, CAM deadlines) means clients have a visceral financial reason to stay subscribed rather than cancel to save the monthly fee.
**Biggest risk:** Missing a critical lease deadline (like an option exercise window) that causes a client to forfeit significant lease rights is a real liability risk — maintain a triple-redundant reminder system (tool alert, email, and a personal calendar confirmation for every option deadline), and be explicit in client agreements that the client is ultimately responsible for reviewing and acting on deadline notices sent by the service.
**Growth path:** Side project → first 5-8 leases abstracted and monitored through tenant-rep broker referrals, CAM audit workflow validated on first annual reconciliation season. Replaces-part-time-income → 20-30 leases under management, 2-3 tenant-rep and accounting referral partners active, first CAM audit recoveries generating contingency fee income (~$4-7K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 60-80 leases across a mix of office, retail, and industrial tenants, monitoring retainer base stable, annual CAM audit season providing a revenue surge each spring, pushing past $10-16K/mo.

### 5. Specialty Tax Credit Identification & Contingency-Fee Recovery (R&D, WOTC)
**Problem:** Two federal tax credits — the Research & Development (R&D) tax credit and the Work Opportunity Tax Credit (WOTC) — leave tens of thousands of small and mid-size businesses with unclaimed money each year, not because they don't qualify but because their generalist CPA doesn't specialize in credit documentation. The R&D credit is available to any business doing qualified research activities (software development, engineering, product testing, process improvement) and can be worth $10,000-500,000+ for a small manufacturer, software firm, or engineering shop. WOTC pays employers $2,400-9,600 per qualifying new hire from a defined target group (veterans, long-term unemployed, SNAP recipients). Most qualifying businesses have no idea they're eligible, and a contingency-fee specialist who only gets paid when they find real credits has an obvious value proposition.
**What to build:** A tax credit qualification and documentation service — a qualification-screening tool (structured questionnaire that maps a business's activities and hiring practices to R&D and WOTC eligibility criteria), an R&D credit documentation workflow (gathering contemporaneous evidence of qualified research activities: project descriptions, time allocations, qualified wage calculations — the documentation the IRS requires to defend a claim), and a WOTC screening and pre-certification workflow (screening new hires for target group membership, submitting IRS Form 8850 to the state workforce agency within the required 28-day window) — all delivered on a contingency fee basis (15-25% of the credit value identified and claimed, paid only when the client's CPA files the return).
**Skill fit:** Direct on the documentation and data-gathering tooling (structured-data collection from businesses — mapping employee time, project activities, and hiring records to IRS qualification categories — is a data-analytics problem); the R&D credit methodology (the four-part test, qualified research expenses calculation, the simplified credit election) is a well-defined technical framework documented in IRS regulations and case law, learnable without a CPA license since the service provides documentation rather than filing tax returns.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends for a qualification-screening questionnaire and an R&D project-documentation template; the WOTC workflow is simpler (a hire-screening form and IRS Form 8850 submission to the state agency), deployable in the same timeframe.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through CPAs and tax professionals (who can refer clients where they don't specialize but still want to offer value; the contingency structure means no out-of-pocket cost to the CPA's client if no credits are found), manufacturing trade associations (where R&D credit eligibility is highest among members), and HR consulting firms (for the WOTC component, since screening must happen at time of hire).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** R&D credit contingency: 20% of a $75,000 average credit = $15,000 per client; working 5-7 R&D clients simultaneously through a documentation cycle (each takes 4-8 weeks of iterative document gathering) = **$10-20K per documentation cycle**. WOTC screening at $50-100/qualifying hire screened adds recurring income per client. A pipeline of 10-15 active R&D documentation projects at any given time — spread across manufacturing, software, engineering, and agriculture clients — generates $15-25K/mo once the referral pipeline is running.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** R&D credits are claimable retroactively for 3 prior open tax years, meaning a new client can generate multiple years of contingency fees from a single engagement rather than one year's worth — the first client who qualifies for $75K/yr in credits for 3 prior years generates $45K in contingency fees from a single documentation project. Credits can also be claimed in future years, creating a recurring annual relationship with each client once the first credit is established.
**Biggest risk:** The IRS scrutinizes R&D credit claims and has a specific examination process — documentation must genuinely support the claimed activities, and overstating credits (claiming development activities that don't pass the four-part test) creates audit risk for the client. Stay strictly within defensible territory: document only activities that clearly qualify, and make the contingency fee structure conditional on the client's CPA reviewing and approving the credit claim before filing, so the CPA's professional judgment serves as a quality gate.
**Growth path:** Side project → first 2-3 R&D documentation projects through CPA referrals (offer to do one gratis to establish a case study and trust the CPA can review), credit methodology validated against IRS guidance. Replaces-part-time-income → 5-8 active projects across manufacturing and software clients, 2-3 CPA referral partners active, first completed projects generating contingency fee income (~$6-12K/mo depending on credit size). Replaces-full-time-salary → 12-15 active projects at any time, WOTC screening retainers generating predictable monthly base income alongside the larger R&D contingency fees, pushing past $15-25K/mo.

## This Session's Pick: Start With #1, Track #3 as the Highest-Compounding Parallel Bet

**Clearest ramp with the best compounding structure:** Idea #1 (medical credentialing) has the most direct path from first project to a growing recurring base. Every provider credentialed becomes a monthly maintenance client (CAQH attestation, re-credentialing cycles) — so income doesn't reset to zero after each project but accumulates. The distribution channel (healthcare staffing agencies, medical billing companies) is a warm referral relationship rather than cold outreach to random businesses, and the skill fit on the document-tracking and deadline-management tooling is direct.

**Highest-compounding parallel bet:** Idea #3 (PEO brokering) has the most genuinely annuity-like income model in all ten sessions of this series — every placed client generates monthly commission indefinitely, and commissions from clients placed two years ago stack under commissions from clients placed last month without the old ones churning out proportionally. The setup work (channel partner agreements with 4-6 PEOs) is a few weeks of paperwork, after which the distribution channel (accountants and commercial insurance agents) overlaps with the same referral network useful for credentialing. Building #3 alongside #1 shares a referral-partner development effort rather than splitting it.

Running #1 as the primary build (fastest to first dollar, strong recurring base) while setting up PEO channel partner agreements and placing the first 2-3 clients for #3 continues this series' pattern of pairing a faster-ramping, per-project idea with a slower-building, perpetual-commission model running alongside it — the combination of the two creates an income structure that both compounds and is defensible against single-client churn.
