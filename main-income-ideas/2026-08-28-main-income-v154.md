### 1. Spray Fireproofing & Firestopping Contractor UL System, Special-Inspection & Firestop Documentation Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Contractors who spray fire-resistive material (SFRM) onto structural steel or install firestop systems that seal penetrations in fire-rated walls and floors must document that the exact field condition matches a UL-classified or FM-approved system number. IBC Chapter 17 requires special inspections of both SFRM application (thickness and density/cohesion/adhesion verified per ASTM E605 pull tests) and firestop installations (per ASTM E2174/E2393), and this documentation has to be captured before drywall or ceiling tile covers the work — once it's closed in, verifying it means expensive demolition. Firestop technicians also need manufacturer certification (3M, Hilti, STI programs) that expires and requires refresher training, and many jurisdictions now require periodic firestop reinspection (ASTM E2393 recommends a 3-year cycle for life-safety-critical penetrations). A firm that can't produce matched UL-system documentation on demand fails inspection, triggers rework, and risks liability if a fire later exposes an undocumented penetration.
**What to build:** A UL/FM system-number library keyed to wall and floor assembly types with a per-job checklist that forces photo documentation (before cover) tied to the specific system number used; an installer-certification expiration tracker across manufacturer programs; a pull-test/thickness-verification log for SFRM jobs; batch/lot tracking for fireproofing material; and a special-inspection report generator formatted for AHJ submission.
**Skill fit:** This is a structured-data and document-generation problem — matching field conditions to UL Product iQ system numbers, generating standardized inspection reports, and tracking certification expirations, all against public regulatory and manufacturer data sources. No proprietary data access is needed.
**MVP scope:** Build the system-number lookup, photo-documentation checklist, and certification tracker over 2-3 weekends. Get the first 5-8 clients by joining the Firestop Contractors International Association (FCIA) member directory outreach list and attending regional AWCI (Association of the Wall and Ceiling Industry) chapter meetings where firestop subcontractors gather.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — direct outreach to FCIA member firms and AWCI regional chapter attendees, offering a free first job's documentation as a demo.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $250-400/mo per contractor firm for job-documentation and certification tracking × 35-45 firms = $9,000-16,000/mo.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new job requires fresh, job-specific documentation before the work gets covered — this isn't a one-time setup fee, it's recurring per-project usage for the life of the contractor relationship, plus ongoing certification-renewal tracking across every installer on staff.
**Biggest risk:** Small firms have survived for years on paper checklists and phone-camera photos and may not see the value until they fail an inspection. Mitigate by leading the sales pitch with the cost of a single failed special inspection (drywall demo, re-inspection fees, schedule delay penalties) rather than the software itself.
**Growth path:** Side project → 10 firms ($3-4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 30 firms ($9-12K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary.

### 2. Youth Travel Sports Club SafeSport Background-Check, Training-Renewal & MAAPP Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Under the Protecting Young Victims from Sexual Abuse and Safe Sport Authorization Act of 2017, every club affiliated with a National Governing Body (USA Swimming, USA Gymnastics, USA Volleyball, and similar bodies for other Olympic-track sports) must ensure that every adult with frequent contact with minor athletes completes SafeSport Trained certification (renewed annually) and passes a background check (renewed every two years), and the club must maintain a Minor Athlete Abuse Prevention Policy (MAAPP) covering travel supervision, locker-room protocols, one-on-one interaction rules, and electronic-communication limits. NGBs conduct compliance audits and can suspend a club's membership — ending its ability to enter sanctioned competitions — over a single lapsed certification. Most small travel clubs are run by a volunteer parent board or a single part-time administrator managing 20-150 coaches and staff across multiple teams with staggered renewal dates, and tracking this by spreadsheet routinely lets certifications lapse unnoticed until an audit or, worse, an incident.
**What to build:** A roster dashboard tracking each staff member's SafeSport Trained expiration date, background-check renewal date, and MAAPP-policy acknowledgment signature; automated email reminders at 60/30/14 days before any expiration; NGB-specific rule sets since USA Swimming, USA Gymnastics, and others have different specifics; and an audit-ready export formatted for NGB compliance reviews.
**Skill fit:** A roster and date-tracking data problem structurally identical to license-renewal tracking, built against publicly published NGB safe-sport policy documents — no proprietary or gated data required.
**MVP scope:** Build the roster tracker and reminder engine in 2-3 weekends. Find the first clients through public USA Swimming Local Swim Committee (LSC) administrator listservs, the USA Gymnastics club directory, and club-business-manager Facebook groups where compliance deadlines are a constant topic of complaint.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — direct outreach to club business managers identified in public NGB club directories, offering a free compliance audit of their current roster as the hook.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $79-149/mo per club × 80-120 clubs = $9,000-14,000/mo.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** SafeSport certification (annual) and background checks (biennial) never stop expiring across a staff roster that also turns over — this is a permanent, recurring tracking need, and a lapsed cert that costs a club its ability to compete is an existential risk administrators will pay to avoid.
**Biggest risk:** USA Swimming's own Deck Pass system already includes some built-in tracking for that one NGB. Mitigate by targeting multi-sport clubs (which need a cross-NGB roll-up no single NGB portal provides) and smaller NGBs that have no native tooling at all.
**Growth path:** Side project → 20 clubs ($2-3K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 70 clubs ($8-10K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary.

### 3. Fixed Base Operator (FBO) Airport Minimum-Standards, NFPA 407 Fuel-Farm & TSA Access Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** FBOs providing fueling, hangar, and ground-service operations at general-aviation airports operate under an airport-authority "minimum standards" agreement (insurance minimums, equipment requirements, operating-hours commitments that vary airport by airport) and must maintain NFPA 407 (Aircraft Fuel Servicing) compliance for their fuel-farm operations, including annual refresher training for line-service staff who fuel aircraft. Fuel storage triggers EPA Spill Prevention, Control, and Countermeasure (SPCC) plan requirements with periodic review and update obligations, and fuel quality must be tested per shift (per ASTM D1655 for jet fuel, D975 for diesel) with logged results. At airports with scheduled or charter service, FBO employees with ramp access need TSA-issued badges under 49 CFR 1542/1544, and badge lists must stay current. Airport authorities audit FBOs periodically and can revoke the operating agreement for lapses, and insurance carriers require documented NFPA 407 training records as a condition of coverage.
**What to build:** An NFPA 407 line-staff training and certification tracker with annual-refresher alerts; an SPCC plan review-date and site-inspection log; an airport minimum-standards renewal checklist (insurance certificate-of-insurance expiration, required-equipment inspection dates); a per-shift fuel-quality test log; and a TSA badge-access roster for badged employees at Part 1542 airports.
**Skill fit:** A structured recordkeeping and scheduling problem against public regulatory sources (FAA Advisory Circulars, the NFPA 407 standard, EPA's SPCC rule) — a strong fit for a data-tracking-oriented developer, with no proprietary aviation data needed.
**MVP scope:** Build the tracker in 2-3 weekends. Get the first clients through the National Air Transportation Association (NATA) FBO member directory and regional airport-manager-association conferences where FBO operators and airport staff mix.
**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — the FBO market is small and relationship-driven, so conversion through NATA outreach and conference introductions runs slightly longer than a pure cold-email channel.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $300-500/mo per FBO × 25-35 FBOs = $9,000-15,000/mo (NATA estimates roughly 3,000 FBOs nationally, the large majority independently owned and small).
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Fuel-farm staff training and SPCC plan review are recurring annual and periodic obligations tied directly to insurance coverage and the airport lease itself — neither can lapse without real operational consequences.
**Biggest risk:** The addressable market per niche is small relative to how price-sensitive small FBOs can be. Mitigate with higher per-seat pricing justified by the insurance and liability stakes, and by being one of very few tools built specifically for this niche rather than competing with generic compliance software.
**Growth path:** Side project → 10 FBOs ($3-4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 28 FBOs ($9-12K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary.

### 4. Dermatology Practice iPLEDGE REMS, In-Office Lab CLIA & Mohs Documentation Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Dermatology practices that prescribe isotretinoin (Accutane and generics) must comply with the FDA's iPLEDGE Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS) program: monthly prescriber and patient attestations through the REMS portal, mandatory pregnancy-testing windows for patients who can become pregnant (a 19-day lockout with two negative tests before the first prescription, then a monthly testing-and-attestation cycle for the duration of therapy), and periodic prescriber and pharmacy REMS re-certification. Missing an attestation window locks a patient out of therapy for a full cycle and can trigger a REMS program compliance review. Practices running an in-house Mohs surgery suite or dermatopathology lab must separately maintain CLIA certification and, for complex testing, CAP or COLA accreditation with proficiency testing three times a year. Practices billing Medicare for Mohs procedures also have to track global-period documentation and modifier use correctly.
**What to build:** A patient-level iPLEDGE REMS attestation calendar tracking each patient's testing window and portal-deadline, with countdown alerts to MA and nursing staff; a CLIA/CAP proficiency-testing cycle tracker for the in-office lab; a prescriber REMS-certification renewal tracker; and a Mohs-surgery documentation checklist built around Medicare global-period billing rules.
**Skill fit:** A deadline and countdown-tracking problem layered on a public regulatory calendar (iPLEDGE program rules are published by the REMS program administrator) — a straightforward data-and-alerts build.
**MVP scope:** Build the patient-level REMS countdown tracker in 2-3 weekends. Reach the first 5-8 clients through the American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) practice-management email list and state dermatology society meetings, and by direct outreach to independent practices listed in the AAD's public "Find a Dermatologist" directory.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — direct outreach to independent, non-hospital-owned dermatology practices, offering a free audit of their current isotretinoin patient panel's attestation status as the opening hook.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $300-500/mo per practice × 30-40 practices = $9,000-16,000/mo.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** iPLEDGE attestation is a monthly, per-patient, ongoing obligation for the entire duration of every isotretinoin course — continuous recurring usage directly tied to the practice's continued ability to prescribe, since a REMS violation can trigger prescriber decertification.
**Biggest risk:** Larger practices may lean on the REMS portal's own per-patient reminders. Mitigate by aggregating status across the practice's entire patient panel in one dashboard (which the REMS portal doesn't offer) and bundling in lab-accreditation tracking the REMS portal has nothing to do with.
**Growth path:** Side project → 12 practices ($3.5-6K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 32 practices ($9.5-16K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary.

### 5. Municipal Advisor Firm SEC Form MA, MSRB Rule G-42 & Series 50 Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Since the SEC's 2014 Municipal Advisor Rule (implementing Dodd-Frank), any firm or individual advising municipalities — cities, school districts, water and utility authorities — on bond issuance or the investment of bond proceeds must register with both the SEC (Form MA) and the MSRB, file an annual Form MA update within 90 days of fiscal year end, complete MSRB continuing education under Rule G-3, and document compliance with the fiduciary duty and fee-disclosure obligations of MSRB Rule G-42 for every engagement. Individuals acting as municipal advisor representatives must pass the Series 50 qualification exam and track their own CE cycle separately from the firm's. Small municipal-advisory shops — often one to three people spun out of a larger bank's public-finance desk or a bond law firm — juggle these deadlines by memory, and a missed annual Form MA update lets the firm's registration lapse, barring it from advising on any new bond deal until it's cured.
**What to build:** A Form MA annual-update deadline tracker calculated from each client firm's fiscal year end; a Series 50 exam-status and MSRB CE-cycle tracker per registered representative; a per-engagement G-42 fiduciary-disclosure document generator and archive; and a fee-disclosure documentation checklist tied to each bond engagement.
**Skill fit:** A deadline and document-tracking problem built against publicly available SEC and MSRB rule text, with the public MSRB EMMA and Form MA filing systems available as reference data — no proprietary access needed.
**MVP scope:** Build the tracker in 2-3 weekends. Reach the first clients through the National Association of Municipal Advisors (NAMA) member directory and by vendor outreach at state Government Finance Officers Association (GFOA) chapter events, where municipal advisors regularly attend alongside their government clients.
**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — this is a small, specialized, relationship-driven market (NAMA counts roughly 600 member firms, most of them tiny), so conversion through association outreach and referral takes longer than a volume cold-email channel.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $200-350/mo per firm × 30-45 firms = $9,000-16,000/mo (a meaningful but realistic share of NAMA's roughly 600-firm membership, most of which are small shops without in-house compliance staff).
**Why this can go beyond side money:** The annual Form MA renewal and per-engagement G-42 documentation are non-optional and existential — a lapsed registration bars the firm from any new bond business — which creates durable, forced recurring usage.
**Biggest risk:** The niche is small and advisor-relationship-driven with a slow sales cycle. Mitigate by pricing higher per seat than a mass-market compliance tool and prioritizing a NAMA annual-conference presence for credibility with a skeptical, close-knit professional community.
**Growth path:** Side project → 12 firms ($2.5-4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 35 firms ($9-12K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary.

## This Session's Pick: #4 (Dermatology Practice iPLEDGE Compliance) for the highest income ceiling with the fastest, most direct distribution channel; #2 (Youth Travel Sports Club SafeSport Compliance) for the largest and most underserved client pool.

Dermatology stands out because isotretinoin prescribing is common (a large share of general dermatology practices run an active iPLEDGE panel) and the AAD's public "Find a Dermatologist" directory makes cold outreach mechanical — every independent practice in a metro area is one directory search away, and the REMS attestation deadline is monthly and per-patient, which means usage never goes idle. Youth travel sports clubs are the volume play: there are thousands of NGB-affiliated clubs nationally, most run by volunteers with zero budget for enterprise compliance software, and the "your club could get suspended from competition" framing makes the sale itself nearly write itself once a prospect sees their own roster's expiration dates.

Both ideas share the pattern that's worked across this whole series: pick a regulatory deadline that's genuinely existential to the client's ability to operate, then build the smallest possible tool that turns "I hope someone remembers" into "the system tells us." Neither idea needs anything beyond a Postgres table of people/certifications/dates and a cron job for reminder emails — the hard part is finding the first 10 clients, not the code.
