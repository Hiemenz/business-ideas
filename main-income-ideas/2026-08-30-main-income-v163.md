### 1. Bulk/Hauled Potable Water Delivery Service State Hauler Permit, Tank-Sanitization & Bacteriological Sample Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Companies that deliver potable water by tanker truck to homes on private wells that have run dry, cisterns, construction sites, and disaster-relief situations must be certified or permitted as water haulers by the state health department — most states regulate bulk water hauling under their Safe Drinking Water Act/public health code, requiring NSF/ANSI Standard 61-certified food-grade tank and hose materials, a fixed tank-sanitization (chlorination) schedule with documented contact time and chlorine-residual test results, recurring bacteriological (total coliform/E. coli) sample testing of both the fill source and the tank at a state-certified lab, and cross-connection control at every fill station and delivery point to prevent backflow contamination. A hauler that misses a scheduled tank sanitization or a required bacteriological sample, or that fills from an uncertified source, is exactly the finding a state health department inspection or a customer's own follow-up well test is built to catch, and a documented waterborne illness traced to a hauler's tank is both a public-health event and a business-ending liability claim. A small hauler running several trucks and dozens of recurring residential and commercial delivery routes currently tracks tank sanitization dates, bacteriological sample results, and source-fill certification on a driver's paper log and a folder of lab reports, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific truck's water is actually certified safe before it's dispatched to today's route.

**What to build:** A per-truck/tank sanitization schedule tracker with chlorine-residual test-result logging and a "cleared to fill" gate; a bacteriological sample tracker matching each truck and route to its most recent lab result with an overdue-sample alert; a source-fill-station certification log confirming every fill point is an approved, cross-connection-controlled source before a truck can log a fill; and a customer-facing water-quality certificate generator a hauler can hand a customer or a state inspector on request.

**Skill fit:** State water-hauler permitting rules and bacteriological sample-interval requirements are public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-truck and per-route trigger, and the "cleared to fill/deliver" gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a mobile-delivery niche entirely distinct from the fixed community water system and backflow-prevention-tester ideas already in this repo, neither of which involves a mobile tank's own sanitization cycle or a per-route bacteriological sample of any kind.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the tank-sanitization tracker, the bacteriological-sample tracker, and the source-fill certification log. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent water-hauling companies.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — state rural water association member directories (many haulers are also members of their state's rural water association alongside municipal system operators) and independent water-hauler Facebook groups reach exactly the small operators currently tracking sanitization and sample dates on a paper log with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149-299/mo per hauler depending on fleet size and delivery volume. 40-55 haulers × $220/mo avg = $8,800-12,100/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every truck's tank-sanitization cycle recurs on its own fixed interval, every route's bacteriological sample reopens on its own schedule, and every new truck a growing hauler adds compounds the number of simultaneously tracked compliance records — a continuous, fleet-and-route-driven compliance stream tied directly to the hauler's own delivery volume.

**Biggest risk:** A hauler serving only non-potable uses (construction-site dust control, livestock watering) has comparatively low stakes exposure. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward haulers serving residential potable-water customers, where a documented contamination event carries real liability and state-inspection scrutiny.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 haulers ($3.3K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 30 haulers ($6.6K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 40-55 haulers plus an inspection-ready water-quality certificate export a hauler can produce the moment a state health inspector asks.

### 2. Extended Vehicle Service Contract (VSC) Administrator/Obligor Multi-State Registration & Contractual-Liability-Insurance Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Companies that administer or financially "obligate" extended vehicle service contracts sold through dealerships must, in most states, register or hold a license as a service contract provider/administrator, and — critically — every contract sold must be backed by either a contractual liability insurance policy (CLIP) from an admitted insurer or a funded reimbursement insurance policy, with the backing policy's coverage amount tracked against the administrator's total outstanding contract liability in that state. States including California, Florida, and Texas require annual or biennial registration renewal, a financial-responsibility filing, and in several states an annual complaint-ratio and outstanding-liability report to the state Department of Insurance. An administrator whose CLIP lapses, or whose registration renewal is missed in even one of the dozens of states it sells contracts into, exposes every contract sold in that state to being deemed unauthorized insurance — a violation that can trigger a cease-and-desist order and leave the administrator on the hook for claims with no reinsurance backing at all. A small regional VSC administrator selling through 50-150 dealer partners across several states currently tracks per-state registration renewal, CLIP/reimbursement-policy currency, and outstanding-liability reporting on a compliance manager's spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific state's registration and backing insurance are both current before new contracts are sold there.

**What to build:** A per-state registration/license renewal tracker with financial-responsibility filing deadlines; a CLIP/reimbursement-insurance-policy currency tracker cross-referenced against total outstanding contract liability by state; a dealer-partner appointment and contract-volume log; and a state-launch readiness gate blocking new contract sales in a given state until registration and backing insurance are both confirmed current.

**Skill fit:** State service-contract-provider statutes and financial-responsibility requirements are public, structured regulatory text with a concrete per-state trigger, and the state-launch readiness gate is the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a service-contract-backing niche entirely distinct from the dealership-side F&I office and used-car-dealer licensing ideas already in this repo, neither of which involves an administrator's own multi-state registration or backing-insurance currency.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the state registration tracker, the CLIP/reimbursement-policy currency tracker, and the state-launch readiness gate. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small regional VSC administrators.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — the Vehicle Protection Association's (VPA) member directory reaches exactly the small administrators currently reconciling multi-state registration and backing-insurance currency on a spreadsheet with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $399-799/mo per administrator given the direct unauthorized-insurance stakes and multi-state scope. 20-28 administrators × $550/mo avg = $11,000-15,400/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every state registration recurs on its own independent renewal clock, the backing insurance policy has to stay sized against a constantly growing outstanding-liability balance as more contracts are sold, and every new dealer partner or state a growing administrator adds compounds the compliance surface it has to track.

**Biggest risk:** Established VSC administration platforms (PCMI, CIC) already serve large national administrators; differentiate by targeting the long tail of small regional administrators these enterprise platforms price out.

**Growth path:** Side project serving 5-6 small administrators → VPA channel presence → 20-28 clients replaces-full-time-salary.

### 3. Marine Surveyor (Vessel Condition & Valuation) SAMS/NAMS Accreditation & Survey-Report Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent marine surveyors who inspect boats and yachts for insurance underwriting, pre-purchase financing, and damage/valuation claims hold accreditation through the Society of Accredited Marine Surveyors (SAMS) or the National Association of Marine Surveyors (NAMS), each requiring a multi-year recertification cycle with continuing-education hours and, for SAMS, a periodic peer review of submitted survey reports to maintain "Accredited Marine Surveyor" status. Every survey report a surveyor issues has to be built to the standard an insurance underwriter or lender actually relies on — referencing the applicable American Boat and Yacht Council (ABYC) standards for the systems inspected and USCG requirements where applicable — and a surveyor whose report is later found deficient in a claim dispute risks both a professional-liability claim against their own E&O policy and a SAMS/NAMS ethics review that can suspend accreditation entirely. A surveyor running several surveys a week across multiple insurance and brokerage referral relationships currently tracks accreditation CE-hour accumulation, E&O policy renewal, and the completeness of each issued report's standards-reference documentation on a personal spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific survey report actually references every required standard before it's delivered to the client and the insurer relying on it.

**What to build:** A SAMS/NAMS accreditation and CE-hour tracker with peer-review submission scheduling; an E&O insurance policy renewal tracker; a per-survey report-completeness checklist confirming required ABYC-standard references and photo documentation are included before a report is marked ready to deliver; and a report archive supporting a surveyor's own liability defense if a claim dispute surfaces months or years later.

**Skill fit:** SAMS/NAMS published accreditation-renewal and CE requirements, plus ABYC's published standards library, are public, structured professional data with a concrete per-surveyor and per-report trigger, and the report-completeness gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a marine-survey niche entirely distinct from every vessel-operator idea already in this repo (charter fishing, commercial fishing, towing vessel, parasailing), none of which involve inspecting and valuing a third party's vessel at all.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the accreditation/CE tracker, the E&O renewal tracker, and the per-survey report-completeness checklist. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent SAMS/NAMS-accredited surveyors.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Society of Accredited Marine Surveyors' (SAMS) member directory reaches exactly the small independent surveyors currently tracking CE hours and report completeness on a personal spreadsheet with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $99-199/mo per surveyor depending on survey volume. 60-80 surveyors × $145/mo avg = $8,700-11,600/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every survey issued reopens its own report-completeness check, the accreditation CE cycle recurs on its own independent renewal clock, and the E&O policy renews annually against a growing body of past reports still exposed to a future claim dispute.

**Biggest risk:** A surveyor doing only a handful of surveys a year has comparatively low urgency. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward surveyors performing 100+ surveys/year for insurance and brokerage referral partners, where report-completeness stakes and E&O exposure are real and recurring.

**Growth path:** Side project → 25 surveyors ($3.6K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 45 surveyors ($6.5K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 60-80 surveyors plus a claim-defense-ready report archive export.

### 4. Indoor Climbing Gym Belay Certification, PCIA Staff Credential & Wall-Inspection Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Indoor climbing gyms that let members climb independently, rather than only under a supervising guide, must verify and document every member's belay competency through a facility belay test before granting independent-climbing access — a member climbing or belaying without a passed, current belay-test record on file is exactly the liability gap a gym's own insurer and a post-accident lawsuit are built to find, since a fall caused by an unqualified belayer is among the most common serious-injury incidents in the industry. Gym staff who supervise, instruct, or administer belay tests must themselves hold current certification through the Professional Climbing Instructors Association (PCIA) or the American Mountain Guides Association (AMGA), renewed on its own cycle with required continuing education, and the climbing walls, holds, and anchor hardware must be inspected on a documented schedule against ASTM F1918 and the Climbing Wall Association's (CWA) inspection guidelines, with several states additionally classifying climbing walls under a state amusement-device statute requiring its own periodic state inspection and permit. A gym running a membership base of hundreds to thousands of climbers and a staff of a dozen or more instructors currently tracks member belay-test results, staff PCIA/AMGA certification currency, and wall/hold/anchor inspection logs on a front-desk binder and a spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific member badged in today actually has a current, passed belay test on file before they're allowed on the wall unsupervised.

**What to build:** A per-member belay-test result tracker tied to the gym's access-control system, gating independent-climbing privileges on a current passed test; a per-staff PCIA/AMGA certification and CE-hour tracker; a wall, hold, and anchor-hardware inspection log matched to ASTM F1918/CWA-recommended intervals plus any state amusement-device inspection cycle; and a facility compliance-readiness report for an insurer's underwriting renewal or a post-incident review.

**Skill fit:** PCIA/AMGA published certification-renewal cycles and CWA's published inspection guidelines are public, structured data with a concrete per-member and per-staff trigger, and the belay-access gate is the same deterministic "block the access until the record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a climbing-gym niche entirely distinct from the trampoline park, zipline/aerial-adventure course, and paintball-field ideas already in this repo, none of which involve a per-member belay-competency credentialing gate of any kind.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the belay-test/access-gate tracker, the staff certification tracker, and the wall/hardware inspection log. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent (non-chain) climbing gyms.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Climbing Wall Association's (CWA) member gym directory reaches exactly the small independent gyms currently tracking belay tests and staff certification on a front-desk binder with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-399/mo per gym depending on membership size and staff headcount. 35-45 gyms × $290/mo avg = $10,150-13,050/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new member requires a fresh belay-test record before independent access, every staff certification recurs on its own renewal clock, and the wall/hardware inspection schedule runs continuously for as long as the facility operates — a continuous, membership-and-staff-driven compliance stream tied directly to the gym's own growth.

**Biggest risk:** A small gym offering only guided/instructor-led climbing with no independent-access privileges has comparatively low stakes. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward gyms offering member belay certification and independent access, where the access-gate liability is real.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 gyms ($4.4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 28 gyms ($8.1K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 35-45 gyms plus an insurer-ready compliance export a gym can produce ahead of its annual liability-policy renewal.

### 5. Post-Disaster Structural Safety Assessment Firm ATC-20/ATC-45 Evaluator Certification & Deployment-Readiness Tracker

**Problem:** Firms and independent structural engineers or architects who perform rapid post-disaster building safety evaluations — tagging a damaged building green (safe to occupy), yellow (restricted use), or red (unsafe) after an earthquake, hurricane, or flood — do so as certified evaluators under the Applied Technology Council's ATC-20 (post-earthquake) and ATC-45 (post-wind/flood) rapid-evaluation programs, and in most states must also hold a state-specific Safety Assessment Program (SAP) registration administered by the state's Office of Emergency Services or equivalent, renewed on its own multi-year cycle with required refresher training. A firm on a state or municipal on-call roster for post-disaster deployment has to be able to prove, the moment a disaster is declared, that every evaluator it plans to deploy has a current ATC-20/45 certification and SAP registration — an expired credential discovered mid-deployment doesn't just disqualify that one evaluator, it can get the whole firm removed from the roster the state relies on for the next disaster. Firms also have to track each evaluator's professional liability (E&O) insurance currency, since a red/yellow/green tag issued in the field is exactly the kind of determination that draws a lawsuit if a later-discovered structural failure contradicts it. A firm maintaining a roster of 10-25 credentialed evaluators across multiple states currently tracks certification/registration expiration and E&O currency on a principal's spreadsheet updated sporadically between disasters, with no systematic way to instantly confirm, the moment a deployment call comes in, exactly which evaluators on the roster are currently eligible to deploy.

**What to build:** A per-evaluator ATC-20/ATC-45 certification and state SAP registration tracker with refresher-training due dates; an E&O insurance currency tracker per evaluator; a deployment-readiness dashboard showing, at a glance, exactly which roster evaluators are currently eligible to deploy to a given state; and a per-deployment tag-documentation log (building ID, tag color, evaluator, date) supporting the firm's own liability defense and the state's post-disaster reporting requirements.

**Skill fit:** ATC-20/45's published certification-renewal cycles and each state's SAP registration requirements are public, structured data with a concrete per-evaluator trigger, and the deployment-readiness dashboard is the same deterministic "instantly confirm who's currently eligible" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a disaster-response niche that does not exist anywhere else in this repo.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the certification/registration tracker, the E&O currency tracker, and the deployment-readiness dashboard. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to structural engineering firms that maintain post-disaster evaluator rosters.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — state Structural Engineers Association chapters (SEAOC and its sister-state affiliates) and the Applied Technology Council's own registered-evaluator program contacts reach exactly the small firms currently tracking evaluator eligibility on a spreadsheet between disasters.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per firm depending on roster size and number of states registered in. 25-35 firms × $420/mo avg = $10,500-14,700/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every evaluator's ATC-20/45 certification and state SAP registration recurs on its own independent renewal clock regardless of whether a disaster has occurred recently, and every new state a growing firm registers its roster in adds a fresh registration-tracking obligation — a continuous, roster-and-footprint-driven compliance stream that keeps the account in active use between disasters, not just during them.

**Biggest risk:** Demand for the underlying evaluation service itself is disaster-driven and irregular, but the compliance-tracking need is constant since the certification and registration clocks keep running whether or not a disaster has struck recently. Mitigate by pricing as a flat monthly readiness retainer rather than a per-deployment fee, so the product's value doesn't depend on disaster frequency.

**Growth path:** Side project serving 5-6 small structural firms → SEAOC/ATC channel presence → 25-35 firms replaces-full-time-salary.
