### 1. Professional Special Needs Trust (SNT) & Pooled Trust Trustee Medicaid/SSI Distribution & Court-Accounting Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Professional trustees administering first-party self-settled "(d)(4)(A)" special needs trusts, third-party SNTs, and nonprofit "(d)(4)(C)" pooled trusts exist for one purpose: preserving a beneficiary's Medicaid and SSI eligibility while still improving their quality of life, and every single distribution has to be tested against Social Security's own resource and income rules (POMS SI 01120.199-203) before it's paid — a distribution for the wrong category of expense (paying a beneficiary's rent or utilities directly, for instance, can count as "in-kind support and maintenance" that reduces their SSI check dollar-for-dollar) isn't a bookkeeping error, it's exactly the finding an SSA redetermination or a state Medicaid eligibility review is built to catch, and getting it wrong can retroactively disqualify the beneficiary from the benefits the trust exists to protect. Every trust also carries its own court-accounting or annual-report obligation to the appointing probate court or, for pooled trusts, to the sponsoring nonprofit's own oversight board, and a first-party trust carries a mandatory Medicaid-payback provision that must be satisfied from remaining trust assets the moment the beneficiary dies — a payback calculation a trustee has to get right against years of accumulated state Medicaid expenditure or face personal liability for shortfall. A trustee running a caseload of dozens of trusts for different beneficiaries, each with its own distribution history, resource-limit exposure, and accounting deadline, currently tracks distribution categorization, ABLE-account coordination, and court-accounting due dates on a spreadsheet and a filing cabinet of receipts, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific distribution won't push a beneficiary's countable resources over the SSI limit before the check is cut.

**What to build:** A per-distribution eligibility-impact classifier flagging any requested distribution that risks counting as an available resource or in-kind support under SSA's own POMS categories before it's approved; a per-trust court-accounting/annual-report deadline tracker computing each trust's next-due filing from its appointment or last-filing date; and a Medicaid-payback estimate tracker for first-party trusts, running an ongoing projected-payback balance against trust assets so a trustee never approves a discretionary distribution that could leave the payback obligation unfunded.

**Skill fit:** SSA's POMS distribution-category rules and each state's Medicaid trust-payback formula are public, structured federal and state regulatory data with a concrete per-distribution eligibility test, and the distribution-classifier gate is the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a fiduciary niche entirely distinct from the Professional Fiduciary Guardian & Conservator idea already in this repo, which accounts for a ward's entire probate estate under a completely different legal standard with no Medicaid/SSI eligibility test or payback-provision calculation of any kind.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the distribution eligibility classifier, the court-accounting deadline tracker, and the Medicaid-payback estimator. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent professional trustees and pooled-trust program administrators.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Special Needs Alliance's (SNA) member attorney network and the Academy of Special Needs Planners (ASNP) reach exactly the small trustee practices currently reconciling distribution eligibility and payback exposure on a spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249/mo base + $10/mo per active trust tracked. Average client (15 trusts) = $249 + $150 = $399/mo. 25-32 trustee practices × $399/mo avg = $9,975-12,768/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new distribution request reopens its own eligibility-classification question regardless of how routine the expense, every trust's court-accounting cycle recurs on its own independent clock for as long as the trustee holds the appointment, and every new trust added to the caseload compounds the number of simultaneously tracked payback projections — a continuous, caseload-driven compliance stream tied directly to the trustee's own practice growth.

**Biggest risk:** A trustee holding only a handful of small, low-activity trusts has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward trustees managing larger caseloads with active first-party trusts carrying payback exposure, where the distribution-and-accounting load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 trustee practices ($4.8K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 20 practices ($8K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 25-32 practices plus a redetermination-ready distribution-history export a trustee can hand SSA or a state Medicaid examiner on request.

### 2. Independent Escrow Company State Escrow Law Licensure, Trust-Account Reconciliation & Bulk-Sale Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent escrow companies — licensed under a state's standalone Escrow Law rather than as an arm of a title insurer or real estate broker, with California's Escrow Law (Financial Code Division 6, administered by the Department of Financial Protection and Innovation) the leading model — hold a license category that legally forbids affiliation with the real estate or title-insurance industries, and instead handle escrow for the transactions a title company never touches: bulk-sale escrows protecting a business's creditors when substantially all its assets are sold under UCC Article 6, boat and aircraft sale escrows, franchise-purchase escrows, and general stakeholder escrows for litigation settlements. Every escrow file requires the company's trust account to reconcile exactly to the sum of every open file's held balance, and a trust account that's out of balance — even by a small, explainable timing difference — is exactly the finding a state examiner's periodic escrow audit is built to catch, with consequences ranging from a corrective-action order to license revocation for a pattern of shortfalls. Licensed independent escrow companies must also file audited annual financial statements meeting the regulator's minimum net-worth and fidelity-bond requirements, and a bulk-sale escrow specifically requires the escrow holder to publish or record a notice to the seller's creditors and hold funds through a statutory waiting period before disbursing to the seller — a disbursement released before that waiting period clears is exactly the exposure the bulk-sale-escrow mechanism exists to prevent. A small independent escrow company running dozens of concurrent files across business-sale, vehicle-title, and stakeholder escrows currently tracks trust-account reconciliation, bulk-sale notice-and-waiting-period status, and bond/net-worth compliance on a bookkeeper's spreadsheet and a filing cabinet of signed instructions, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific file's held balance actually reconciles and its statutory waiting period has actually cleared before funds are released.

**What to build:** A per-file trust-account reconciliation tool cross-checking every open escrow's held balance against the company's actual bank balance daily, flagging any discrepancy before it compounds into an audit finding; a bulk-sale escrow notice-and-waiting-period tracker computing each file's statutory disbursement-eligible date from its notice-publication date, with a release-block gate; and a bond-coverage and audited-net-worth tracker cross-checked against the regulator's minimum thresholds.

**Skill fit:** State Escrow Law trust-accounting and bulk-sale notice requirements are public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-file reconciliation and waiting-period trigger, and the disbursement-release gate is the same deterministic "block the action until the record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a standalone-escrow niche entirely distinct from the Title Insurance Agency escrow ideas already in this repo, both of which regulate an escrow function tied to a real estate closing under an insurance department's title framework that an independent, non-affiliated escrow company's Escrow Law license has no relationship to at all.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the trust-account reconciliation tool, the bulk-sale notice/waiting-period tracker, and the bond/net-worth compliance tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent, non-title-affiliated escrow companies.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the American Escrow Association's (AEA) member directory and the Escrow Institute of California's chapter meetings reach exactly the small independent escrow companies currently reconciling trust accounts and bulk-sale waiting periods on a spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-349/mo per escrow company depending on concurrent-file volume. 30-40 companies × $270/mo avg = $8,100-10,800/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every open file generates its own daily reconciliation event for as long as it stays open, every bulk-sale escrow opens its own independent notice-and-waiting-period clock, and every new file type the company takes on reopens the trust-account exposure question — a continuous, file-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the company's own transaction growth.

### 3. Non-Judicial Foreclosure Trustee Notice-Sequence, Statutory-Timeline & Trustee's-Deed Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** The trustee named under a deed of trust — the party a mortgage servicer refers a defaulted loan to for a non-judicial foreclosure in the roughly 30 states that use this framework — is legally responsible for executing a rigid, statutorily defined notice-and-sale sequence where a single missed day voids the entire sale: a Notice of Default must be recorded and mailed to the borrower, triggering a mandatory state-defined reinstatement period (commonly 90 days) during which the borrower can cure and stop the process; once that period expires, a Notice of Trustee's Sale must be recorded, posted at the property, published in a qualifying newspaper for a specific number of consecutive weeks, and mailed to the borrower and any junior lienholders, all completed a minimum number of days before the sale date under a sequence like California Civil Code §2924 et seq. — and a sale conducted even one day short of that minimum publication-and-mailing window is void, exposing the trustee and the foreclosing lender to a wrongful-foreclosure claim. Every postponement or cancellation the borrower or lender requests has to be announced and re-recorded following its own sub-sequence of rules, and after a sale actually occurs, the trustee must execute and record a Trustee's Deed Upon Sale within a state-mandated window, along with proper disbursement of any sale-proceeds surplus back to junior lienholders and the former borrower in strict statutory priority order — a surplus disbursed out of order is a separate, independently litigable claim on top of the sale itself. A small independent trustee company running dozens to hundreds of loan files at different stages of the notice sequence currently tracks reinstatement-period expiration, publication-week counts, and mailing deadlines on a paper tickler file and a shared spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific file's full notice sequence actually completed before that sale is calendared.

**What to build:** A per-loan notice-sequence tracker computing each file's reinstatement-period expiration, required publication-week count, and minimum-notice-before-sale date from its Notice of Default recording date, with a hard "not sale-ready" flag until every statutory step is confirmed complete; a postponement/cancellation sub-sequence log re-computing the notice requirements every time a sale date moves; and a post-sale Trustee's Deed recording and surplus-disbursement tracker enforcing the state's statutory lienholder priority order.

**Skill fit:** State non-judicial foreclosure statutes' notice-timeline and publication requirements are public, numerically precise, structured legal data with a concrete per-file sequencing trigger, and the "not sale-ready" gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a foreclosure-trustee niche entirely distinct from the Mortgage Servicer idea already in this repo, whose CFPB Regulation X compliance core governs a still-performing or newly delinquent loan's payment processing and loss-mitigation timeline rather than the statutory notice-and-sale sequence a trustee executes only after a loan is referred to foreclosure.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the notice-sequence tracker with the sale-readiness gate, the postponement/cancellation re-sequencing log, and the post-sale deed/surplus-disbursement tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent, non-servicer-affiliated foreclosure trustee companies.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the American Legal & Financial Network's (ALFN) member directory reaches exactly the small independent trustee companies currently reconciling notice-sequence deadlines on a paper tickler file with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per trustee company depending on concurrent active-file volume. 24-30 companies × $429/mo avg = $10,296-12,870/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every loan referred to foreclosure opens its own independent notice-sequence clock, every postponement request reopens the sequencing question for that specific file, and every completed sale generates its own post-sale deed-and-surplus-disbursement event — a continuous, referral-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the trustee company's own file count.

**Biggest risk:** A very small trustee company handling only a handful of files in a single, stable jurisdiction has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward companies running a larger multi-county or multi-state file book, where the notice-sequencing load genuinely outgrows a paper tickler file.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 companies ($5.1K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 20 companies ($8.6K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 24-30 companies plus a litigation-ready notice-sequence export a company can produce the moment a wrongful-foreclosure claim is filed.

### 4. Independent Utilization Review Organization (URO) State Certification, Reviewer-Licensure & Statutory-Decision-Clock Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent utilization review organizations making medical-necessity determinations for workers' comp insurers and health plans operate under state URO certification statutes — Texas Labor Code §408.0231's Division of Workers' Compensation Utilization Review Agent certification and California Labor Code §4610 among them — that require every reviewing physician or nurse on the roster to hold a current license in good standing, matched to the *specific clinical specialty* of the treatment being reviewed (an orthopedic surgery request reviewed by a reviewer outside orthopedics is exactly the finding a state URO audit is built to catch), and renewed on the URO's own certification cycle separate from any individual reviewer's own license renewal. The core operational risk is the statutory decision clock: state law sets a hard timeframe — commonly measured in business days from receipt of a complete request — within which the URO must issue its determination, and in several states a request that isn't decided within that window is automatically deemed approved by operation of law, meaning a URO that simply runs out the clock on a complex case has just approved a treatment it never actually reviewed. Every denial also triggers a state-mandated appeal path — an independent medical review (IMR) or independent review organization (IRO) referral — with its own separate statutory deadline for forwarding the case file, and a URO that misses that forwarding deadline faces its own compliance finding independent of the original review's merits. A small URO running a rotating panel of contracted physician reviewers against a caseload of hundreds of active review requests currently tracks reviewer license/specialty-match currency, per-case decision-clock countdowns, and IMR/IRO forwarding deadlines on a case manager's spreadsheet and a shared calendar, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific case was actually assigned to a specialty-matched, currently licensed reviewer before the clock on it starts running.

**What to build:** A per-reviewer license and specialty-credential currency tracker with a "cleared to review" gate blocking any case assignment to a reviewer whose license or specialty match isn't current; a per-case statutory decision-clock tracker computing each state's specific deadline from the request's receipt date and flagging any case approaching its deemed-approval threshold; and an IMR/IRO forwarding-deadline tracker triggered automatically the moment a case is marked denied.

**Skill fit:** State URO certification statutes' decision-timeframe rules and reviewer-credentialing requirements are public, numerically precise, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-case trigger, and the decision-clock tracker plus the "cleared to review" gate are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a medical-necessity-review niche entirely distinct from the Health Plan TPA, Workers' Compensation TPA, and COBRA Administrator ideas already in this repo, none of which makes a clinical determination or tracks a reviewer-license-to-specialty match or a statutory deemed-approval clock at all.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the reviewer credentialing gate, the per-case decision-clock tracker, and the IMR/IRO forwarding-deadline tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent, non-insurer-owned utilization review organizations.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the National Association of Independent Review Organizations' (NAIRO) member directory reaches exactly the small UROs currently reconciling reviewer credentials and statutory decision clocks on a shared calendar with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $349-699/mo per URO depending on reviewer-roster size and monthly case volume. 20-26 organizations × $499/mo avg = $9,980-12,974/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every incoming case opens its own independent decision-clock the moment it's received, every reviewer added to the panel reopens the specialty-match credentialing question, and every denial generates its own IMR/IRO forwarding-deadline event — a continuous, case-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the URO's own caseload growth.

**Biggest risk:** A very small URO handling a narrow specialty with a stable, small reviewer panel has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward UROs running a larger multi-specialty reviewer panel across several states' differing decision-clock rules, where the tracking load genuinely outgrows a shared calendar.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10 organizations ($5K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 16 organizations ($8K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 20-26 organizations plus a state-audit-ready reviewer-credential-and-decision-clock export a URO can produce the moment a certification renewal review arrives.

### 5. Court-Appointed Receiver Bonding, Court-Accounting & Asset-Custody Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent professionals appointed by a state or federal court as receivers — to take custody of and operate a distressed business, a foreclosed commercial property, or the frozen assets of a Ponzi-scheme unwind — operate under an appointment order that defines a specific, asset-limited scope of powers and a bonding requirement sized to the value of the assets under the receiver's control, and a receiver who acts outside the appointment order's specific authorization (selling an asset the order never granted authority over, for instance) has acted outside their appointment entirely, exposing them to personal liability independent of whether the action itself was reasonable. Every receivership requires periodic accounting to the appointing court — for a federal equity receivership, under 28 U.S.C. §754/§959 and the local district's own receivership rules, or for a state receivership, under that state's receivership statute — itemizing every receipt and disbursement of estate assets, and an accounting filed late, or one whose ledger doesn't reconcile to the receivership's actual bank and asset records, is exactly the finding the appointing court's periodic review or an objecting creditor is built to catch, with consequences ranging from a show-cause order to removal and personal surcharge liability for any unexplained shortfall. A receiver also has to maintain a complete asset inventory and chain-of-custody record for every piece of property taken into the receivership estate, since a receivership's core value to the court is confidence that every dollar and every asset is accounted for from the day of appointment to the day of discharge. A receiver running several concurrent receivership matters across different courts currently tracks bond-coverage currency, per-matter accounting deadlines, and asset-custody records on a spreadsheet and a filing cabinet of bank statements, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific matter's accounting actually reconciles to its asset ledger before the filing goes to the court.

**What to build:** A per-matter court-accounting deadline tracker computing each receivership's next-due filing from its appointment date and the appointing court's own reporting cadence; a receipts-and-disbursements ledger reconciliation tool flagging any matter's book balance that doesn't match its actual bank/asset records before an accounting is filed; and a bond-coverage tracker cross-checked against the current value of assets under management in each active matter, with an alert on any shortfall as an estate's asset value grows.

**Skill fit:** State and federal receivership accounting-cycle rules and bonding requirements are public, structured legal data with a well-defined per-matter record requirement, and the accounting-deadline tracker plus the ledger-reconciliation tool are the same deterministic "compute the deadline from the appointment date / cross-check two records that must match before the filing goes out" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a civil/commercial court-supervised niche entirely distinct from the Professional Fiduciary Guardian & Conservator idea already in this repo, whose probate-court appointment governs a *person's* entire estate under incapacity law rather than a court-defined, asset-specific scope of powers over a *business or property* under commercial litigation.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the court-accounting deadline tracker, the ledger reconciliation tool, and the bond-coverage tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent receivers handling business and property receiverships.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the National Association of Federal Equity Receivers' (NAFER) member directory reaches exactly the small independent receivers currently reconciling per-matter accounting and bond coverage on a spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per receivership management company depending on concurrent active-matter caseload. 22-28 companies × $429/mo avg = $9,438-12,012/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every active receivership recurs its own court-accounting cycle for as long as the appointment stands, every new matter accepted reopens the bond-coverage and asset-custody question, and a growing caseload compounds the number of simultaneously tracked accounting deadlines across different courts — a continuous, caseload-driven compliance stream tied directly to the receiver's own practice growth.

**Biggest risk:** A receiver holding only one or two small, short-duration matters has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward receivers running a larger multi-matter caseload across several courts, where the accounting-and-bond-coverage load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 companies ($5.1K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 18 companies ($7.7K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 22-28 companies plus a court-ready accounting-and-asset-custody export a receiver can produce the moment an objecting creditor or the court itself requests one.
