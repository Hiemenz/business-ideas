### 1. Wildlife/Bird Abatement Falconry Service Operator Federal/State Permit, Raptor-Record & Site-Contract Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Companies that fly trained raptors under contract to disperse nuisance birds from airports, vineyards, landfills, and golf courses operate under a dual-license structure most small operators track from memory: a state falconry license (apprentice, general, or master class, each with its own sponsorship and raptor-possession-count rules, renewed on the state's own cycle) paired with a federal falconry permit under 50 CFR 21.29 requiring an annual report and a current per-raptor possession-and-banding record — a falconer flying a raptor without a matching, current band-number record on file is exactly the finding a USFWS or state wildlife-agency inspection is built to catch, and a lapsed license discovered mid-contract can end the relationship with a client whose entire wildlife-hazard program depends on that operator's authority to fly. When a contract authorizes live-trapping or lethal take of a genuinely depredating bird rather than pure non-lethal dispersal, the operator must also hold a USDA Wildlife Services or state depredation permit specific to that action and species — a dispersal-only falconer who traps or takes a bird without that additional permit exposes both the falconer and the client airport or vineyard to a federal Migratory Bird Treaty Act violation. Airport contracts additionally expect the falconer to log every bird-activity observation and any wildlife strike into the site's own wildlife-hazard management records feeding the FAA's National Wildlife Strike Database, and a small operator running contracts across several sites currently tracks license/permit currency, per-raptor banding records, and per-site activity logs on a paper falconry journal and a filing folder, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific raptor is currently licensed and permitted before it's flown at today's site.

**What to build:** A per-falconer state license-class and federal 50 CFR 21.29 permit renewal tracker with a per-raptor possession-and-banding record; a depredation-permit tracker flagging which contracted sites and species are covered for live-trapping or lethal take versus dispersal-only; and a per-site visit log capturing bird-activity counts and any strike incidents, exportable as a client- or FAA-facing report.

**Skill fit:** USFWS falconry permit rules and state wildlife-agency licensing cycles are public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-falconer and per-raptor trigger, and the license-currency gate plus the per-site activity-log export are the same deterministic recordkeeping pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a wildlife-hazard-management niche this repo has never addressed.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the license/permit tracker with the per-raptor banding record, the depredation-permit flag, and the per-site activity log. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent falconry abatement operators.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — the North American Falconers Association (NAFA) member directory and state falconry association rosters reach exactly the small operators currently tracking license renewal and raptor records on a paper falconry journal with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $20/mo per active contract site tracked. Average client (8 sites) = $199 + $160 = $359/mo. 28-35 operators × $359/mo = **$10,052-12,565/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new contract site adds a fresh per-site activity-log obligation, every raptor added to an operator's mews reopens the banding-record requirement, and state license renewal recurs independently of any single contract — a continuous, site-and-raptor-count-driven compliance stream.

**Biggest risk:** The total number of professional abatement falconers nationally is genuinely small. Mitigate by expanding the product to cover combined non-lethal bird-abatement programs (falconry paired with pyrotechnic and bio-acoustic deterrents) that many of the same small operators already run side by side.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 operators ($4.3K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 28-35 operators ($10-12.6K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding an airport-facing wildlife-strike-report export built directly from the per-site activity log.

### 2. Licensed Hunting & Fishing Outfitter/Guide State License, Federal Land-Agency Permit & Client Tag-Eligibility Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent hunting and fishing outfitters — guiding clients on big-game hunts and backcountry fishing trips, distinct from a boat-based charter captain — hold a state Fish & Wildlife-issued outfitter and per-guide license renewed on the state's own cycle, and when operating on federal land, a U.S. Forest Service or Bureau of Land Management Special Use Permit that allocates a fixed number of client-service-days per management unit per season — an outfitter that exceeds its allocated service-days, or guides on a unit without a permit on file for that specific season, is exactly the finding a ranger field check or an end-of-season agency use-report audit is built to catch, and repeated violations are grounds for permit non-renewal that ends the outfitter's access to that unit entirely. The single obligation with the sharpest legal exposure: before every hunt or fishing trip, the outfitter must confirm each client holds a currently valid state hunting or fishing license and, for big game, the specific species tag or draw-permit for that unit and season — guiding a client who turns out to be untagged or unlicensed is a poaching-adjacent violation that can cost the outfitter its own license regardless of the client's intent, and a state game-warden field check is the moment that gap surfaces. A small outfitter running several guides across multiple hunting units and fishing waters currently tracks per-guide license renewal, per-unit federal permit service-day allocation, and per-client tag/license verification on a lodge manager's paper logbook, with no systematic gate confirming a specific client is actually tagged and licensed before today's hunt departs.

**What to build:** A per-guide state license and federal Special Use Permit service-day allocation tracker computing remaining days per unit in real time; a per-client tag/license eligibility gate blocking a hunt or trip from being logged as booked until the client's state license and species tag/draw-permit are confirmed on file; and an end-of-season use-report generator reconciling actual client-days against each unit's allocation for the permitting agency.

**Skill fit:** State outfitter-licensing rules and federal land-agency Special Use Permit allocation structures are public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-guide and per-client trigger, and the client tag-eligibility gate is the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a land-based guiding niche entirely distinct from the Charter Fishing Vessel USCG Captain's License idea and the Whitewater Rafting & River Outfitter idea already in this repo, neither of which involves verifying a client's own hunting or fishing credential before departure.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the guide license/permit allocation tracker, the client tag-eligibility gate, and the end-of-season use-report generator. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent hunting and fishing outfitters.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — state Outfitters and Guides Associations (Montana Outfitters and Guides Association, Colorado Outfitters Association, Alaska Professional Hunters Association) reach exactly the small outfitters currently reconciling federal service-day allocations and client tag verification on a lodge logbook with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $15/mo per guide tracked. Average client (8 guides) = $199 + $120 = $319/mo. 30-40 outfitters × $319/mo = **$9,570-12,760/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every client booked reopens the tag-and-license eligibility check, every federal permit's service-day allocation resets on its own seasonal clock, and every new guide a growing outfitter hires compounds the number of simultaneously tracked state licenses — a continuous, booking-and-guide-headcount-driven compliance stream.

**Biggest risk:** A very small outfitter running one or two guides on a single state-land unit with no federal permit has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward outfitters running multiple guides across several federal-permitted units, where a service-day overage or an untagged client is a real license-ending risk.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 outfitters ($4.8K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 30-40 outfitters ($9.6-12.8K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding a state-game-warden-ready client-eligibility export an outfitter can produce the moment a field check occurs.

### 3. Independent Medical Review Officer (MRO) Private Practice 49 CFR Part 40 Verification-Interview & Result-Reporting Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent Medical Review Officers — physicians certified through the Medical Review Officer Certification Council (MROCC) or a comparable body, distinct from the collection site that draws the specimen — review every non-negative drug-test result a laboratory reports under 49 CFR Part 40 before that result can be reported to the employer, requiring a documented verification interview with the tested employee within a fixed regulatory window, a determination of whether a legitimate medical explanation exists for the result, and a final verified-result report sent back to the employer or C-TPA within its own separate deadline — an MRO who misses the interview window, or whose verification file can't show the specific medical records reviewed for a claimed legitimate explanation, exposes the employer to a result that a labor arbitrator or DOT modal-agency audit can throw out entirely, undoing a termination or safety-sensitive removal the employer already acted on. MRO certification itself renews on a five-year cycle with a continuing-education requirement, and for any verified positive result involving a CDL holder, the MRO's practice must also complete the required query or report to the FMCSA Drug & Alcohol Clearinghouse within its own deadline. A small independent MRO practice serving dozens of C-TPAs and direct employer accounts currently tracks per-case verification-interview deadlines, per-account service agreements, its own MROCC recertification, and Clearinghouse-reporting completion on a shared spreadsheet and a stack of case files, with no systematic alert before a specific case's interview window or reporting deadline passes unmet.

**What to build:** A per-case verification-interview deadline tracker computing the countdown from lab-result receipt to interview completion and final report, with an overdue alert; an MROCC certification and CE-hour renewal tracker; and a per-account (C-TPA or direct employer) service log paired with an FMCSA Clearinghouse reporting-completion tracker for CDL-holder cases.

**Skill fit:** 49 CFR Part 40 Subpart G's verification-interview and reporting-deadline structure is public, structured federal regulatory data with a concrete per-case trigger, and the deadline-countdown tracker is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a post-laboratory drug-test-review niche entirely distinct from the Mobile DOT/Non-DOT Drug & Alcohol Testing Collection Site Operator idea already in this repo (that idea's compliance core is the specimen-collection side of Part 40 — collector refresher training and chain-of-custody form completeness — with no verification-interview or result-reporting concept at all) and from the Occupational Health Clinic & DOT Medical Examiner NRCME idea, whose physical-exam registry never touches a drug-test result's legal verification.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the per-case deadline tracker, the MROCC certification tracker, and the per-account/Clearinghouse reporting log. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent MRO practices.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Medical Review Officer Certification Council (MROCC) directory and the American Association of Medical Review Officers (AAMRO) member network reach exactly the small practices currently reconciling verification-interview deadlines on a shared spreadsheet with no dedicated system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299/mo base + $25/mo per contracted C-TPA/employer account. Average client (10 accounts) = $299 + $250 = $549/mo. 20-26 practices × $549/mo = **$10,980-14,274/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every non-negative lab result reopens the verification-interview deadline clock, every new C-TPA or employer account adds a fresh service relationship to track, and MROCC recertification recurs on its own five-year cycle independent of case volume — a continuous, case-and-account-driven compliance stream.

**Biggest risk:** A solo MRO handling only a handful of cases a month for one or two employer accounts has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward MRO practices serving multiple C-TPAs with real monthly case volume, where a missed verification window is a genuine legal-exposure event for the employer relying on it.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10 practices ($5.5K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 20-26 practices ($11-14.3K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding an employer-facing verified-result audit export an MRO practice can produce the moment a labor dispute or DOT audit arises.

### 4. Agritourism Operator (Corn Maze, Pumpkin Patch & U-Pick Farm) Liability-Statute Signage & Registration Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Farms operating corn mazes, pumpkin patches, hayrides, and u-pick fields rely on their state's Agritourism Liability Limitation Act to cap or eliminate ordinary-negligence liability for a visitor injury — but that statutory protection is conditional: over 40 states require the operator to post specifically worded warning signage (exact statutory language and minimum lettering size vary by state) at defined locations such as the entrance, ticket booth, and parking area, and several states additionally require an annual agritourism registration or exemption filing with the state Department of Agriculture to qualify at all. A farm with missing, outdated, or non-compliant signage forfeits the statute's protection entirely — a hayride tip-over or a corn-maze trip-and-fall claim that would have been capped under the statute instead proceeds as an ordinary, unlimited negligence suit, the exact outcome the signage requirement exists to prevent. Farms running a compressed multi-week fall season (or a longer u-pick season spanning berries through pumpkins) also have to track any on-site attraction beyond the core farm activity — a hay-wagon ride, a rope-and-net "jump pad," or a zipline add-on — that can trigger a separate state amusement-device inspection requirement layered on top of the agritourism statute, plus health-department permits for any on-site cider or cocoa concession stand. A small farm running a seasonal attraction currently tracks its posted-signage compliance, annual registration filing, and any attraction-specific inspection or food-permit requirement on the farm owner's own memory and a folder of old signage printouts, with no systematic record confirming every required sign is actually posted and current before opening day.

**What to build:** A state-specific signage-compliance checklist with a photo-verification log confirming each required sign is posted at its statutory location before the season opens; an annual agritourism registration/exemption filing tracker; and an on-site attraction and food-vendor permit tracker flagging any add-on ride or concession requiring its own inspection or health permit.

**Skill fit:** State Agritourism Liability Limitation Act signage requirements and registration rules are public, structured statutory text with a concrete per-location, per-season trigger, and the photo-verification signage log is the same deterministic "confirm the required record is in place before the transaction proceeds" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a farm-visitor-attraction niche this repo has never addressed.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the signage-compliance checklist with photo verification, the annual registration tracker, and the attraction/food-permit tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent agritourism farms.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the National Agritourism Professionals Association (NAPA) member directory and state Farm Bureau agritourism programs reach exactly the small farms currently tracking signage compliance on memory and old printouts with no dedicated system ahead of opening day.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249/mo base + $50/mo add-on for attraction/food-permit tracking. Average client = $299/mo. 30-40 farms × $299/mo = **$8,970-11,960/mo**.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new season reopens the full signage-verification cycle before opening day, every added attraction reopens the inspection/permit tracking requirement, and annual registration recurs on its own state-filing clock — a continuous, season-and-attraction-driven compliance stream even though the farm itself operates only part of the year.

**Biggest risk:** The core business is seasonal, and a farm may treat the subscription as disposable in the off-season. Mitigate with an annual billing option tied to the pre-season signage-verification cycle, and by targeting farms running multiple seasonal attractions (spring u-pick through fall corn maze) that keep the product in active use for more of the year.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 farms ($4.5K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 30-40 farms ($9-12K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding an insurer-facing compliance certificate a farm can hand its liability carrier at annual policy renewal.

### 5. Rodeo Stock Contractor Interstate CVI, Brand-Inspection & Sanctioning-Body Welfare-Equipment Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Rodeo stock contractors — who own and tour a roster of bucking bulls, saddle broncs, and roping stock across dozens of PRCA, IPRA, or independently sanctioned rodeos in a single season, distinct from a livestock hauler or auction dealer who moves animals they don't own — must haul that same roster across state lines under a Certificate of Veterinary Inspection that expires roughly every 30 days, paired with state brand-inspection clearance confirmed at each crossing; a truck turned away at a rodeo gate because its CVI expired two days before arrival, or because a specific head's brand clearance was never obtained, costs the contractor a booked performance fee and damages its standing with that rodeo committee for future bookings. Every sanctioned performance also requires documented compliance with the sanctioning body's own animal-welfare equipment rules — specific flank-strap padding and fit standards, and a mandatory on-site veterinarian sign-off before stock is released into the arena — and a contractor who can't produce that per-event documentation after an animal-welfare complaint is exactly the finding that can suspend its sanctioning-body permit. A small stock contractor running a touring roster of 30-60 head across a spring-through-fall circuit currently tracks per-animal CVI expiration, per-crossing brand-inspection clearance, and per-event welfare-equipment sign-off on a trailer clipboard and a folder of paper health certificates, with no systematic alert confirming a specific truckload is actually cleared to cross before it leaves for the next rodeo.

**What to build:** A per-animal CVI expiration and brand-inspection-clearance tracker gated to each stop on the tour schedule, computing which head are cleared to cross for the next event; a per-event sanctioning-body welfare-equipment and on-site-veterinarian sign-off checklist; and a PRCA/IPRA contractor-permit renewal tracker.

**Skill fit:** State brand-inspection requirements and CVI expiration rules are public, structured agricultural regulatory data with a concrete per-animal, per-crossing trigger, and the tour-schedule-gated clearance tracker is the same deterministic "block the action until the record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a touring-livestock niche entirely distinct from the Livestock Hauler & Commercial Animal Transporter idea and the Independent Livestock Dealer/Order Buyer idea already in this repo, neither of which involves a contractor's own reused touring roster or a sanctioning body's welfare-equipment sign-off requirement.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the per-animal CVI/brand-clearance tracker, the per-event welfare-equipment checklist, and the contractor-permit renewal tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent rodeo stock contractors.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — the Professional Rodeo Cowboys Association (PRCA) Stock Contractors Committee, the International Professional Rodeo Association (IPRA), and American Bucking Bull Inc. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward contractors running a full PRCA or IPRA touring circuit across multiple states, where a single expired CVI turning a truck away at the gate is a real, repeatable revenue loss.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10 contractors ($5.2K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 20-25 contractors ($10.4-13K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary by adding a rodeo-committee-facing clearance export a contractor can send ahead of arrival to confirm every head is cleared before the truck rolls in.
