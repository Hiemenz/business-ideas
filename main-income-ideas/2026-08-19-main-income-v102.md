### 1. Low-Acid Canned Food (LACF) & Acidified Food Processor FDA Scheduled Process Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Small canneries, salsa and sauce makers, and pickling operations producing shelf-stable, hermetically sealed foods fall under FDA's 21 CFR Part 113 (low-acid canned foods) or Part 114 (acidified foods) — regulations built specifically around the risk of *Clostridium botulinum* toxin, which makes this one of FDA's highest-priority food-safety categories with mandatory recall authority. Every facility must register with FDA (Form FDA 2541) before starting operations, but registration alone doesn't authorize production: every distinct product, container size, and thermal process combination requires its own filed "scheduled process" (Form 2541d for LACF, 2541e for acidified foods), established and validated by a qualified processing authority — typically a university extension food-science program — before a single jar can legally be produced and sold. A facility that runs a new recipe or a new jar size without a matching filed scheduled process on record, or whose retort cycle deviates from the filed process's time/temperature/fill-weight parameters without a documented processing-authority evaluation, is exactly the finding an FDA inspection is built to catch, and an unfiled or deviated process is grounds for product seizure and mandatory recall given the botulism risk. Every retort operator must also hold a current Better Process Control School (BPCS) certificate under 21 CFR 113.10 — a credential most small operators earn once and never track renewal or applicability against again. A small cannery or sauce maker running 8-15 SKUs across a growing wholesale account list manages FDA registration currency, per-SKU scheduled-process filing numbers, retort-operator BPCS certification, and process-deviation documentation with a recipe binder and no systematic link between a specific product on today's production schedule and its actual filed process on record.
**What to build:** An FDA facility registration renewal tracker (biennial cycle); a per-SKU scheduled-process registry linking each product/container/process combination to its filed FDA 2541d/2541e number with a "cleared to produce" gate blocking a production run from being scheduled if no matching filed process exists; a retort-operator BPCS certification tracker; and a processing-deviation log requiring documented processing-authority sign-off before any deviant batch can be released for sale.
**Skill fit:** FDA's scheduled-process filing system and BPCS certification requirement are public, structured federal regulatory categories with a well-defined per-SKU and per-operator record model, and the production-eligibility gate is the same deterministic "block the action until the filed record exists" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a thermal-process food-safety trade most small canneries currently track in a recipe binder with no systematic cross-check between today's production run and the actual FDA-filed process it's supposed to match.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the FDA registration tracker, the per-SKU scheduled-process registry with the production-eligibility gate, and the deviation log; land the first 5-8 processors through Specialty Food Association (SFA) member outreach and direct referral relationships with university extension processing-authority programs (Cornell, University of Georgia, and similar food-science departments that validate these processes for small producers and have every incentive to refer ongoing compliance tracking).
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Specialty Food Association's member directory and processing-authority referral relationships, where small canneries and sauce makers already juggle per-SKU scheduled-process paperwork with no dedicated compliance software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249/mo base + $15/mo per filed scheduled process (SKU) tracked. Average client (10 SKUs) = $249 + $150 = $399/mo. 24-30 processors × $399/mo = **$9,576-11,970/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new product launch or jar-size change reopens its own scheduled-process filing requirement before a single unit can be produced, and every retort operator's BPCS certification carries its own currency risk — a continuous, per-SKU compliance stream tied directly to the processor's product-line growth rather than a one-time registration event.
**Biggest risk:** A production run started without a matching filed scheduled process, or a retort deviation released without processing-authority evaluation, is exactly the finding that triggers an FDA seizure and mandatory recall given the botulism risk — the production-eligibility gate is the core protection, built to make the compliant path the only path the tool allows.
**Growth path:** Side project → FDA registration tracker, scheduled-process registry, and deviation log deployed, first 5-8 processor retainers through SFA and processing-authority outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 14-17 processors. Replaces-full-time-salary → 24-30 processors, pushing past $9.58-11.97K/mo.

### 2. Permitted Wildlife Rehabilitator State & Federal USFWS Migratory Bird Permit Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Wildlife rehabilitators — nonprofit centers and individually permitted operators who care for injured, sick, or orphaned native wildlife before release back to the wild — must hold a state wildlife agency rehabilitation permit, renewed annually and often requiring continuing-education hours, a mentor sign-off for new permittees, and a periodic facility inspection against state or NWRA/IWRC minimum-standards caging requirements. Any native bird species in care additionally requires a separate federal migratory bird rehabilitation permit under 50 CFR 21.31, administered by the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service — a rehabber caring for a songbird or raptor under only a state permit, without the federal migratory-bird authorization on file, is committing a federal Migratory Bird Treaty Act violation regardless of state-level compliance, and a lapsed or missing federal permit is exactly the finding a USFWS law enforcement inquiry or a state agency's routine facility check is built to catch. Every rehabilitator must also file an annual disposition report to both the state agency and, for federally permitted species, USFWS — tracking each animal's intake date, species, and outcome (released, transferred, or humanely euthanized) — and an incomplete or late annual report is grounds for permit non-renewal. A small wildlife rehab center or home-based rehabber caring for dozens to hundreds of animals a year across multiple sub-permittees and volunteer transporters manages dual state-and-federal permit currency, per-animal intake-to-disposition logging, and facility-standards readiness with a paper intake log and no systematic cross-check between a specific bird species in care and whether the federal authorization to hold it is actually current.
**What to build:** A dual state-and-federal permit renewal tracker with species-authorization scope per permit and a "cleared to accept" gate flagging any intake species not covered by a current permit; a per-animal intake-to-disposition log structured to generate both the state annual report and the USFWS migratory-bird annual report directly from the same records; and a facility/caging-standards checklist mapped to NWRA/IWRC minimum standards by species group.
**Skill fit:** State wildlife-permit renewal cycles and USFWS's 50 CFR 21.31 authorized-species framework are public, structured regulatory data with a well-defined per-permit and per-animal record requirement, and the intake-eligibility gate plus the dual-report generator are the same deterministic "block the action until the permit clears / generate the required filing from the same underlying record" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a noncommercial wildlife-care trade most small rehabbers currently track in a paper intake log with no systematic link between a specific species accepted today and the federal authorization actually covering it.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the dual permit tracker with the intake-eligibility gate, the intake-to-disposition log generating both required annual reports, and the facility-standards checklist; land the first 5-8 rehabilitators through National Wildlife Rehabilitators Association (NWRA) and International Wildlife Rehabilitation Council (IWRC) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the NWRA and IWRC member directories, both dense national communities of permitted rehabilitators who already juggle dual state/federal permit renewal and annual disposition reporting with no dedicated compliance software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $15/mo per sub-permittee/volunteer transporter tracked under the facility's permit. Average client (6 sub-permittees) = $199 + $90 = $289/mo. 31-38 rehabilitators × $289/mo = **$8,959-10,982/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new animal intake reopens its own species-eligibility check against the current federal authorization, and both the state and federal permits carry their own independent annual renewal and reporting cycle — a continuous, per-intake and per-permit compliance stream tied directly to the rehabilitator's caseload rather than a once-a-year renewal event.
**Biggest risk:** A federally protected migratory bird accepted into care without a current USFWS permit on file is exactly the finding a law enforcement inquiry is built to catch, and a late or incomplete annual disposition report can cost a permit its renewal outright — the intake-eligibility gate and the dual-report generator are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → dual permit tracker, intake-to-disposition log, and facility checklist deployed, first 5-8 rehabilitator retainers through NWRA/IWRC outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 18-22 rehabilitators. Replaces-full-time-salary → 31-38 rehabilitators, pushing past $8.96-10.98K/mo.

### 3. Custom-Exempt Meat Processor & Mobile Slaughter Unit Owner-of-Record Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Custom-exempt meat processors and mobile slaughter units (MSUs) — small locker plants and traveling units that slaughter and process livestock for the animal owner's own consumption — operate under the Federal Meat Inspection Act's custom-exemption provision (9 CFR 303.1), which allows processing without continuous USDA inspection only if every unit of product is stamped "NOT FOR SALE" and tracked back to a specific owner of record, with no owner ever receiving back more finished product than their proportional share of the animal actually slaughtered. A processor who commingles two owners' shares, releases product to someone other than the animal's owner of record, or can't produce clean ownership-chain documentation is exactly the finding a state meat-inspection audit or a USDA FSIS district office review is built to catch — and losing custom-exempt status, or worse, a referral for illegal sale of uninspected meat, can shut the operation down and expose the owner to federal charges. Every animal also generates its own hanging-weight-to-cut-sheet allocation record, since the owner's proportional share is calculated from the animal's actual hanging weight, not a flat estimate, and a processor who can't reconstruct that math for a specific animal has no defense if a customer disputes their return weight. Mobile slaughter units serving a rotating circuit of farms additionally carry their own state MSU operating permit and Sanitation SOP pre-operation inspection log, distinct from a fixed locker plant's paperwork. A small custom locker plant or mobile unit processing dozens of animals a week for a rotating roster of farm-direct customers manages owner-of-record chains, hanging-weight allocation math, and MSU permit/sanitation readiness with a paper tag system and a ledger book.
**What to build:** A per-animal owner-of-record chain tracker (owner, intake date, hanging weight, cut sheet, product returned) with a "cleared to release" gate blocking any release exceeding the owner's proportional share calculated from the animal's actual hanging weight; a Sanitation SOP pre-operation inspection log; and an MSU permit and route-schedule tracker for mobile units serving multiple farm locations.
**Skill fit:** The custom-exemption's owner-of-record and proportional-share requirements under 9 CFR 303.1 are public, structured federal regulatory text with a well-defined per-animal record model, and the release-eligibility gate is the same deterministic "cross-check two records that must match before the action proceeds" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a meat-processing trade most small locker plants currently track with a paper tag system and no systematic cross-check between an animal's actual hanging weight and the product actually released to its owner.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the owner-of-record chain tracker with the release-eligibility gate, the Sanitation SOP log, and the MSU permit/route tracker; land the first 5-8 processors through American Association of Meat Processors (AAMP) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the American Association of Meat Processors' member directory and state meat-processor associations, where small locker plants and mobile units already juggle per-animal ownership documentation and hanging-weight allocation by hand with no dedicated compliance software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299/mo base + $15/mo per head processed weekly on average. Average client (15 head/week) = $299 + $225 = $524/mo. 18-23 processors × $524/mo = **$9,432-12,052/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every animal processed reopens its own owner-of-record chain and hanging-weight allocation check from scratch, and mobile units reopen their Sanitation SOP log at every new stop on the route — a continuous, per-animal compliance stream tied directly to the processor's weekly throughput rather than a once-a-year license renewal.
**Biggest risk:** A commingled owner share or a release exceeding an owner's proportional weight allocation is exactly the finding that can cost a processor its custom-exempt status and expose it to a referral for illegal sale of uninspected meat — the release-eligibility gate is the core protection, built to make an out-of-allocation release structurally difficult rather than a ledger-book oversight.
**Growth path:** Side project → owner-of-record tracker, Sanitation SOP log, and MSU route tracker deployed, first 5-8 processor retainers through AAMP outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 10-13 processors. Replaces-full-time-salary → 18-23 processors, pushing past $9.43-12.05K/mo.

### 4. Egg Handler & Processor USDA EPIA Restricted-Egg & FDA Egg Safety Rule Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Egg handlers and processors — graders, packers, and table-egg producers — operate under the USDA's Egg Products Inspection Act (EPIA), which requires documented tracking and diversion of "restricted eggs" (checks, dirty, incubator rejects, and other eggs unfit for the retail shell-egg market) away from the table-egg supply chain, with recordkeeping a USDA compliance officer can audit at any time. Separately, any producer with 3,000 or more laying hens producing eggs for the table market falls under FDA's Egg Safety Rule (21 CFR Part 118), which mandates a written Salmonella Enteritidis prevention plan covering biosecurity, rodent control, and a strict requirement that eggs be refrigerated to 45°F within 36 hours of lay — and requires a specific environmental-testing-to-egg-testing cascade triggered the moment a environmental swab comes back SE-positive, with the follow-up egg testing required on a fixed timeline that most small producers have never actually had to execute under pressure. A producer who can't document refrigeration timing for a specific lot, or who misses the mandated follow-up testing window after a positive environmental swab, is exactly the finding an FDA Egg Safety Rule inspection is built to catch, and both violations carry the risk of a mandatory recall given Salmonella's status as a leading cause of foodborne illness outbreaks traced to eggs. A small-to-mid egg producer or handler running multiple layer houses and a packing operation manages restricted-egg diversion logging, per-lot refrigeration-timing documentation, and SE-testing-trigger compliance with a packing-room clipboard and no systematic alert when an environmental test result starts a testing clock the operation has to meet.
**What to build:** A restricted-egg diversion log per grading/packing shift confirming checks, dirty, and rejected eggs are documented as routed away from the table-egg stream; a per-lot refrigeration-timing tracker computing the 45°F-within-36-hours window from each lot's lay date and flagging any lot at risk of missing it; and an SE prevention plan environmental-testing-trigger tracker that starts the mandated egg-testing countdown the moment an environmental swab returns positive.
**Skill fit:** EPIA's restricted-egg handling requirements and FDA's Egg Safety Rule testing-trigger cascade are public, structured federal regulatory text with a well-defined per-lot and per-test record model, and the refrigeration-timing tracker plus the testing-trigger countdown are the same deterministic "compute the deadline from the trigger event" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a poultry-product trade most small producers currently track on a packing-room clipboard with no systematic alert when a positive environmental test starts a clock the operation legally has to meet.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the restricted-egg diversion log, the per-lot refrigeration-timing tracker, and the SE-testing-trigger countdown; land the first 5-8 producers through state egg council and United Egg Producers (UEP) affiliated outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — state egg council and United Egg Producers-affiliated networks, where small-to-mid producers already juggle per-lot refrigeration documentation and SE-testing-trigger deadlines with no dedicated compliance software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249/mo base + $10/mo per 1,000 laying hens in flock. Average client (15,000 hens) = $249 + $150 = $399/mo. 24-30 producers × $399/mo = **$9,576-11,970/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every packing shift reopens its own restricted-egg diversion check, every lot produced opens its own refrigeration-timing clock, and any positive environmental test starts a fresh SE-testing countdown at any time — a continuous, per-lot and per-test compliance stream tied directly to the operation's daily production volume.
**Biggest risk:** A lot that misses its 45°F-within-36-hours refrigeration window, or a missed follow-up egg test after a positive environmental swab, is exactly the finding that can trigger a mandatory recall given Salmonella's outbreak history in eggs — the refrigeration-timing tracker and the testing-trigger countdown are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → restricted-egg log, refrigeration-timing tracker, and testing-trigger countdown deployed, first 5-8 producer retainers through UEP and state egg council outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 14-17 producers. Replaces-full-time-salary → 24-30 producers, pushing past $9.58-11.97K/mo.

### 5. State Periodic Motor Vehicle Safety Inspection Station License & Sticker-Control Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Independent auto-repair shops running a state-mandated periodic vehicle safety inspection line — required annually or biennially in states such as Pennsylvania (75 Pa.C.S. § 4726), Massachusetts, and New York — hold a station certification entirely separate from any emissions/smog testing authorization, and every technician performing inspections must hold an individually renewed certified-inspector license, often requiring periodic retraining to stay current. Every inspection sticker or decal issued is a controlled, state-numbered item, and the state expects an exact reconciliation between every sticker a station has been issued, every sticker actually affixed to a vehicle that passed inspection, and every inspection record on file — a sticker that can't be accounted for, or one issued to a vehicle with no matching passed-inspection record, is treated as a potential fraud indicator and is exactly the finding a state compliance audit is built to catch, with station-certification suspension the standard consequence and an existential risk to a shop whose inspection line often anchors its core repair-bay traffic. A shop that lets a technician's inspector license lapse and continues running that technician through the inspection line compounds the exposure, since every sticker issued under a lapsed license is itself a separate violation. A small independent shop running an inspection line alongside its general repair business manages per-technician license currency and sticker-inventory reconciliation with the front-counter clipboard and a shoebox of unused sticker rolls.
**What to build:** A per-technician certified-inspector license renewal tracker with a "cleared to inspect" gate blocking inspection-line assignment for any technician whose license has lapsed; a sticker/decal inventory reconciliation dashboard matching every issued sticker serial number against a logged pass/fail inspection record and flagging any unaccounted-for sticker before a state audit would catch it; and a station-certification renewal tracker.
**Skill fit:** State vehicle-safety-inspection station certification cycles and numbered-sticker issuance rules are public, structured regulatory data with a well-defined per-technician and per-sticker record model, and the inspect-eligibility gate plus the reconciliation dashboard are the same deterministic "block the action until the credential clears / cross-check two records that must match" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a vehicle-safety trade most small shops currently track with a front-counter clipboard and no systematic reconciliation between stickers issued and inspections actually logged.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the technician license tracker with the inspect-eligibility gate, the sticker-reconciliation dashboard, and the station-certification tracker; land the first 5-8 shops through state independent-garage owner associations and Automotive Service Association (ASA) state affiliate outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — state independent-garage owner associations and ASA state affiliate chapters, plus the public state inspection-station licensee roster, a built-in prospecting list of every certified station by name.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $229/mo base + $18/mo per certified inspector on staff. Average client (5 inspectors) = $229 + $90 = $319/mo. 28-35 stations × $319/mo = **$8,932-11,165/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every inspection cycle reopens the sticker-to-record reconciliation check, and every technician's inspector license renews on its own independent cycle — a continuous, per-sticker and per-technician compliance stream tied directly to the shop's daily inspection-line volume rather than an annual paperwork task.
**Biggest risk:** An unaccounted-for sticker or one issued under a lapsed inspector license is exactly the finding that suspends a station's certification and cuts off the inspection-line traffic that anchors much of the shop's repair-bay business — the inspect-eligibility gate and the reconciliation dashboard are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → technician license tracker, sticker-reconciliation dashboard, and station-certification tracker deployed, first 5-8 shop retainers through ASA and state garage-association outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 16-20 shops. Replaces-full-time-salary → 28-35 shops, pushing past $8.93-11.17K/mo.
