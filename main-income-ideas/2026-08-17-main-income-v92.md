### 1. WIC-Authorized Small Grocery & Convenience Store Vendor Compliance
**Problem:** Small grocery and convenience stores authorized to accept WIC benefits under the Special Supplemental Nutrition Program for Women, Infants, and Children operate under 7 CFR Part 246 and their state WIC agency's own vendor manual, which requires every authorized store to stock a minimum variety and quantity of WIC-approved foods drawn from the state's Approved Product List (APL) — a list that changes as manufacturers add or discontinue qualifying SKUs — and a store caught short of the minimum stock during an unannounced compliance-buy visit is exactly the finding a state WIC agency's routine monitoring program is built to catch. Every authorized vendor also has its shelf prices measured against a peer-group competitive price comparison (CPC) conducted by the state agency once or twice a year, and a store whose prices land above its peer-group ceiling risks losing authorization at the next redetermination — the vendor agreement renewal cycle most states run every two to three years, requiring a fresh application, updated ownership disclosures, and often a new store visit before reauthorization. Violations — selling non-WIC-approved items on a WIC transaction, providing cash or credit in place of authorized foods, or a pattern of stock-out findings — trigger 7 CFR 246.12(l)'s escalating sanction ladder of one-year, three-year, or permanent disqualification, and in the roughly 40 states with a SNAP-WIC reciprocal disqualification agreement, a WIC disqualification automatically triggers a parallel SNAP disqualification, wiping out both revenue streams at once. A small independent grocer or convenience-store owner running one to three locations manages minimum-stock currency against a periodically updated APL, redetermination deadlines, and a violation history with no dedicated compliance function beyond a store manager's memory of what's on the shelf.
**What to build:** A minimum-stock checklist per store location cross-referenced against the state's current Approved Product List, flagging any required category running short; a vendor agreement redetermination deadline tracker per location; and a violation/compliance-buy log showing exactly how many findings stand between the store and the next sanction tier.
**Skill fit:** State WIC agency Approved Product Lists and vendor manuals are public, periodically updated regulatory data with a well-defined per-store, per-category minimum-stock requirement, and the stock checklist plus the sanction-tier countdown are the same deterministic "cross-reference against the current public list / count down toward an escalating threshold" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a retail-vendor trade most small grocers currently track by walking their own aisles from memory.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the APL-linked stock checklist, the redetermination tracker, and the violation-log sanction-tier tracker; land the first 5-8 stores through National Grocers Association (NGA) independent-retailer outreach and NACS (National Association of Convenience Stores) small-operator forums.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — NGA's independent-grocer member network and regional state grocers association chapters, where small stores already juggle APL-linked stock requirements with no dedicated compliance software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $75/mo per store location tracked. Average client (a small independent grocery chain running 3 locations) = 3 × $75 = $225/mo. 43-55 chains × $225/mo = **$9,675-12,375/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every periodic APL update reopens the stock-compliance question for every store, every redetermination cycle reopens on its own two-to-three-year clock, and every compliance-buy visit adds a fresh violation-history entry — a continuous, per-location compliance stream tied directly to the store's WIC authorization.
**Biggest risk:** A store caught short of minimum stock during an unannounced compliance-buy visit is exactly the finding a state WIC monitoring program is built to catch, and a resulting disqualification often triggers a parallel SNAP disqualification — the APL-linked stock checklist is the core protection.
**Growth path:** Side project → stock checklist, redetermination tracker, and violation-log tracker deployed, first 5-8 store retainers through NGA/NACS outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 20-28 chains. Replaces-full-time-salary → 43-55 chains, pushing past $9.68-12.38K/mo.

### 2. Cosmetics & Personal-Care Private-Label Manufacturer MoCRA Facility Registration & Product Listing Compliance
**Problem:** Cosmetics and personal-care private-label manufacturers now operate under the Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act of 2022 (MoCRA), which for the first time requires every cosmetic product facility to register with the FDA on a biennial renewal cycle and every marketed product to be separately listed with the FDA, including its ingredient list and the contact information of the "responsible person" — typically the private-label brand owner — and a facility whose registration has lapsed or a product reformulated without an updated listing is exactly the gap MoCRA gave the FDA new authority to act on, including a first-ever mandatory recall power the agency never held over cosmetics before. Every responsible person must also maintain a documented serious-adverse-event reporting system with records retained for six years (three years for small businesses under the statute's revenue threshold) and keep "adequate substantiation" of each product's safety — stability data, a safety assessment, or equivalent documentation — on file per product, a requirement that lands squarely on small contract manufacturers and indie beauty brands with no in-house regulatory-affairs staff. A private-label manufacturer running 30-50 active SKUs across several client brands, with formulations that change as clients reformulate or launch new products, manages facility registration renewal, per-SKU product listing currency, and per-product safety-substantiation documentation with no dedicated compliance function beyond a shared spreadsheet passed between the brand owner and a co-packer.
**What to build:** A facility registration renewal tracker on the biennial FDA cycle; a per-SKU product listing tracker flagging any product whose formulation has changed since its last FDA listing update; a responsible-person adverse-event log meeting MoCRA's documentation and retention timeline; and a per-product safety-substantiation document checklist.
**Skill fit:** MoCRA created a brand-new, structured federal filing regime with clear biennial deadlines and a well-defined per-product record requirement, and the registration tracker plus the per-SKU listing tracker are the same deterministic "compute the renewal deadline / flag the record that's fallen out of sync with reality" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a manufacturing trade with no prior compliance-software category since the statute itself is only a few years old.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the facility registration tracker, the per-SKU listing tracker, and the adverse-event/safety-substantiation trackers; land the first 5-8 manufacturers through Independent Cosmetic Manufacturers and Distributors (ICMAD) member outreach and the Indie Beauty Expo exhibitor list.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — ICMAD's member directory and the indie-beauty and private-label contract-manufacturer networks that grew acutely aware of MoCRA once FDA enforcement discretion windows began closing.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149/mo base + $8/mo per active SKU listed. Average client (a private-label manufacturer with 40 active SKUs) = $149 + $320 = $469/mo. 20-26 manufacturers × $469/mo = **$9,380-12,194/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every facility renews its registration on its own independent biennial clock, every reformulation or new-product launch opens a fresh product-listing event, and the adverse-event log is a continuously monitored record — a continuous, per-SKU compliance stream tied directly to the manufacturer's active product line.
**Biggest risk:** A lapsed facility registration or an un-updated product listing after a reformulation is exactly the gap MoCRA's new FDA recall authority was built to catch — the registration tracker and the per-SKU listing tracker are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → registration tracker, per-SKU listing tracker, and adverse-event/substantiation trackers deployed, first 5-8 manufacturer retainers through ICMAD outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-15 manufacturers. Replaces-full-time-salary → 20-26 manufacturers, pushing past $9.38-12.19K/mo.

### 3. Rent-to-Own (Lease-to-Own) Furniture, Appliance & Electronics Dealer State RTO Act Compliance
**Problem:** Rent-to-own dealers selling furniture, appliances, and electronics under lease-purchase agreements operate under state Rental-Purchase Agreement Acts — adopted in roughly 46 states, each with its own required disclosure set: the cash price of the merchandise, the total of all scheduled payments, the number and timing of payments, and a reinstatement right letting a customer who missed a payment reclaim the same unit within a state-set grace period, commonly 5 to 15 days depending on the state and how many payments the customer had already made. An agreement drawn on the wrong state's disclosure form, or a reinstatement handled outside the applicable grace period, is exactly the violation these statutes exist to police, and many states attach statutory damages plus the customer's attorneys' fees to a violation, with repeat violations drawing state attorney general action against the dealer. Because RTO inventory is tied one-to-one to a specific customer's agreement — a specific serialized washer, sofa, or television — until either payoff or repossession, a small dealer running a couple of storefronts with 200 or more active agreements at any time manages per-state disclosure-form selection, per-agreement reinstatement-window countdowns triggered the moment a payment is missed, and a serialized-unit-to-agreement ledger with no dedicated compliance function beyond a paper agreement file per customer.
**What to build:** A per-state disclosure-form and clause library auto-selected by the store's operating state; a per-agreement reinstatement-window countdown that starts the day a payment is missed and computes the exact state-specific deadline; and a merchandise-unit ledger linking every serialized item to its currently active agreement for repossession and re-rental tracking.
**Skill fit:** State Rental-Purchase Agreement Acts are public, structured statutes with a well-defined per-state disclosure and grace-period lookup, and the disclosure-form library plus the reinstatement countdown are the same deterministic "select the correct rule set by jurisdiction / compute the deadline from the trigger event" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a lease-transaction trade most small dealers currently track through a paper agreement file with no systematic per-agreement deadline tracking.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the disclosure-form library, the reinstatement-window tracker, and the merchandise-unit ledger; land the first 5-8 dealers through Association of Progressive Rental Organizations (APRO) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — APRO's member directory and regional RTO dealer conferences, where small operators already juggle state-specific reinstatement windows across a rotating merchandise inventory with no dedicated compliance software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $3/mo per active agreement tracked. Average client (a two-store RTO dealer running 300 active agreements) = $199 + $900 = $1,099/mo. 9-12 dealers × $1,099/mo = **$9,891-13,188/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new agreement opens its own state-specific disclosure requirement, every missed payment reopens a fresh reinstatement-window clock, and every unit that changes hands re-links to a new active agreement — a continuous, per-agreement compliance stream tied directly to the dealer's active customer book.
**Biggest risk:** An agreement drawn on the wrong state's disclosure form, or a reinstatement mishandled outside the statutory grace period, is exactly the violation a state Rental-Purchase Agreement Act exists to police and carries statutory-damages exposure — the disclosure-form library and the reinstatement-window tracker are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → disclosure library, reinstatement tracker, and merchandise ledger deployed, first 5-8 dealer retainers through APRO outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 6-8 dealers. Replaces-full-time-salary → 9-12 dealers, pushing past $9.89-13.19K/mo.

### 4. Health Plan Third-Party Administrator (TPA) Multi-State License & NAIC Model Act Compliance
**Problem:** Third-party administrators processing claims for self-funded employer group health plans operate under state statutes modeled on the NAIC Third-Party Administrator Statute and its accompanying Regulation, requiring a TPA license in most states where it administers claims for a resident of that state, a surety or fidelity bond at a state-specified minimum coverage amount, a written administrative-services agreement on file with each insurer or plan sponsor spelling out the TPA's delegated authority and the sponsor's retained fiduciary responsibility, and a segregated fiduciary account for any premium or claims funds the TPA handles — funds that under the Model Act may never be commingled with the TPA's own operating account. A TPA licensed in a state where its registration has lapsed, or one whose fiduciary account shows commingled funds, is exactly the finding a state department of insurance market-conduct exam is built to catch, and an out-of-compliance license in even one state can force the TPA to stop administering claims for that state's plan participants entirely. Most states also require an annual report of business — the volume of premium and claims the TPA administered in that state during the prior year — filed on its own per-state schedule. A regional TPA administering plans for employers with employees spread across 15-20 states manages 15-20 independent license renewal cycles, 15-20 different bond-amount requirements, and per-state segregated-account reconciliation with no dedicated compliance function beyond a spreadsheet an office manager updates by hand.
**What to build:** A per-state TPA license renewal tracker capturing each state's bond amount and renewal date; a per-state annual report-of-business filing deadline tracker; and a segregated fiduciary-account reconciliation tracker cross-checking held funds against outstanding claims and premium liability by state.
**Skill fit:** NAIC Model Act adoption and each state's specific bond amount and renewal cycle are public per-state regulatory data with a well-defined per-license record requirement, and the license tracker plus the fiduciary-account reconciliation are the same deterministic "compute the renewal deadline per jurisdiction / cross-check two records that must match" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a health-claims-administration trade most small TPAs currently track through a shared spreadsheet with no systematic per-state cross-check.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the per-state license tracker, the report-of-business filing tracker, and the fiduciary-account reconciliation tracker; land the first 5-8 TPAs through Society of Professional Benefit Administrators (SPBA) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — SPBA's member directory and self-funded-plan broker and benefits-consultant referral networks, where regional TPAs already juggle staggered per-state license renewals with no dedicated compliance software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249/mo base + $18/mo per state licensed in. Average client (a regional TPA licensed in 12 states) = $249 + $216 = $465/mo. 20-26 TPAs × $465/mo = **$9,300-12,090/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every state license renews on its own independent cycle, every annual report of business reopens its own filing event, and the fiduciary-account reconciliation is a continuously monitored record that changes with every claims cycle — a continuous, per-state compliance stream tied directly to the TPA's multi-state footprint.
**Biggest risk:** A lapsed state license or a commingled fiduciary account is exactly the finding a state department of insurance market-conduct exam is built to catch — the per-state license tracker and the fiduciary-account reconciliation are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → license tracker, report-of-business tracker, and fiduciary-account reconciliation deployed, first 5-8 TPA retainers through SPBA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-15 TPAs. Replaces-full-time-salary → 20-26 TPAs, pushing past $9.30-12.09K/mo.

### 5. Medical Specimen Courier & Clinical Lab Logistics Company DOT/OSHA/HIPAA Compliance
**Problem:** Independent medical courier companies transporting diagnostic specimens between draw sites, physician offices, and clinical laboratories handle shipments classified under DOT's UN3373 "Biological Substance, Category B" hazmat rule (49 CFR 171-173), which requires proper triple-packaging and marking plus function-specific hazmat employee training for every driver under 49 CFR 172.700 Subpart H — training that must be completed within 90 days of a driver first handling such materials and refreshed at least every three years under 172.704 — and a driver running a Category B route without current training is exactly the gap a DOT roadside inspection is built to catch, with penalties that can shut the vehicle down mid-route. Because couriers routinely have access to patient specimens and requisition paperwork carrying protected health information, they're typically HIPAA "business associates" of every lab and clinic they serve, requiring a signed Business Associate Agreement (BAA) on file with each client before pickups begin — a courier running specimen pickups for a lab without a current BAA is exactly the gap a HIPAA compliance audit targets, and several lab accreditation bodies additionally require the referring lab to keep the courier's chain-of-custody and temperature-log documentation as part of the lab's own CAP or COLA accreditation file. A small courier company running a rotating pool of a dozen or more drivers and subcontractors across several lab and clinic clients manages per-driver hazmat training currency, per-client BAA status, and per-route chain-of-custody documentation with no dedicated compliance function beyond a dispatcher's memory of who's trained and who isn't.
**What to build:** A per-driver hazmat training currency tracker on the 90-day-initial/3-year-refresher cycle, with a "cleared to run Category B routes" gate; a per-client BAA tracker confirming a signed agreement is on file before specimen pickups begin for that client; and a chain-of-custody and temperature-log tracker per route and specimen batch.
**Skill fit:** DOT hazmat training cycles and HIPAA business-associate requirements are public, structured, deterministic per-driver and per-client record requirements, and the training-currency gate plus the BAA tracker are the same "block the action until the credential clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a healthcare-logistics trade most small couriers currently track through a dispatcher's memory with no systematic cross-check between a specific driver's training status and today's route assignment.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the hazmat training tracker with the route-eligibility gate, the per-client BAA tracker, and the chain-of-custody/temperature-log tracker; land the first 5-8 courier companies through Messenger Courier Association of America (MCAA) member outreach, which runs a dedicated STAT/medical courier segment.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — MCAA's member directory and regional clinical-lab courier operator networks, where small companies already juggle rotating driver pools against Category B training-currency requirements with no dedicated compliance software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $179/mo base + $15/mo per active driver tracked. Average client (a courier company running 12 drivers) = $179 + $180 = $359/mo. 26-33 companies × $359/mo = **$9,334-11,847/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every driver's hazmat training refreshes on its own three-year clock, every new lab or clinic client opens a fresh BAA event, and every route generates its own chain-of-custody record — a continuous, per-driver and per-client compliance stream tied directly to the courier's route volume.
**Biggest risk:** A driver running a Category B route without current hazmat training, or a specimen pickup made without a signed BAA on file, is exactly the gap a DOT roadside inspection or a HIPAA compliance audit is built to catch — the training-currency gate and the BAA tracker are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → training-currency tracker, BAA tracker, and chain-of-custody tracker deployed, first 5-8 courier retainers through MCAA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 15-19 companies. Replaces-full-time-salary → 26-33 companies, pushing past $9.33-11.85K/mo.
