### 1. Independent Cardiovascular Perfusionist Group Practice Multi-Facility Privileging, ABCP Recertification & Perfusion-Record Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Cardiovascular perfusionists — who operate the heart-lung bypass machine and manage ECMO circuits during open-heart surgery — increasingly work through small independent perfusion groups that contract to cover cardiac surgery cases at multiple hospitals and surgery centers rather than as a single hospital's direct employee. Every perfusionist holds national certification as a Certified Clinical Perfusionist (CCP) through the American Board of Cardiovascular Perfusion (ABCP), renewed on a 3-year cycle requiring 36 continuing-education credits (documented through AmSECT-sponsored courses and conferences) or a recertification exam if the CEU threshold isn't met, and in the roughly 20 states that license the profession, a separate state perfusion license runs on its own independent renewal clock layered under that state's practice act. Because every hospital's medical staff office independently verifies and grants clinical privileges — checking CCP certification, state license, and malpractice coverage against its own credentialing-committee cycle, commonly every two years — a perfusionist dispatched to run a bypass circuit at a facility where privileges have lapsed is providing a surgery-critical service outside that facility's own credentialing authority, exactly the gap a Joint Commission survey or a post-case adverse-event review is built to catch. Every single case also requires a completed perfusion record — circuit setup, activated clotting time (ACT) checks at the intervals AmSECT's published Standards and Guidelines specify, and blood-gas results logged throughout the run — since an incomplete perfusion record is the first document a malpractice claim's discovery process or a hospital's quality-assurance review requests after any bypass-related complication. A small perfusion group running 4-10 perfusionists across several hospital contracts currently tracks staggered ABCP CEU accumulation, state license renewal, per-facility privileging deadlines, and per-case perfusion-record completeness on a shared spreadsheet and a stack of paper case logs, with no systematic cross-check confirming a perfusionist dispatched to a specific facility today is simultaneously certified, licensed, and privileged there.

**What to build:** A multi-facility clinical-privileging tracker with a "cleared to run this case" gate flagging any hospital where privileges are approaching or past renewal; an ABCP 3-year CEU-cycle tracker per perfusionist with a hard flag as the recertification deadline nears; a state-license renewal tracker for licensed states; and a per-case perfusion-record completeness checklist (circuit setup, ACT-interval logging, blood-gas documentation) gating a case from being marked closed until the record is complete.

**Skill fit:** ABCP's published CEU-cycle requirements, state perfusion-license renewal rules, and AmSECT's published perfusion-record documentation standard are public, structured professional and regulatory data with a concrete per-facility and per-case trigger, and the "cleared to run this case" gate is the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a cardiac-surgical-support niche entirely distinct from the Independent CRNA Practice (Session 153) and Anesthesiologist Assistant Group Practice (Session 156) ideas already in this repo, neither of which touches perfusion-circuit operation, ABCP certification, or a per-case perfusion-record standard.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the multi-facility privileging tracker, the ABCP CEU-cycle tracker, and the per-case perfusion-record checklist. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent perfusion groups contracting across multiple hospitals.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the American Society of ExtraCorporeal Technology's (AmSECT) member directory and regional chapter meetings reach exactly the small perfusion groups currently reconciling multi-facility privileging and ABCP CEU status on a shared spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149-299/mo per perfusion group depending on perfusionist headcount and number of facility contracts. 40-55 groups × $220/mo avg = $8,800-12,100/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new facility contract reopens the privileging question on that facility's own independent renewal clock, the ABCP CEU cycle recurs on its own rolling three-year schedule for every perfusionist on staff, and every case run generates its own perfusion-record completeness event — a continuous, contract-and-caseload-driven compliance stream tied directly to the group's own growth.

**Biggest risk:** A perfusionist employed directly by a single hospital isn't the buyer for a multi-facility compliance tool. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward independent perfusion groups contracting across several hospitals or covering ECMO call for multiple systems, where the multi-facility privileging load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 groups ($3.3K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 32 groups ($7K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 40-55 groups plus a malpractice-discovery-ready perfusion-record export a group can produce the moment a case is questioned.

### 2. Structural Engineering Firm Statutory Milestone Inspection & SIRS Building-Recertification Deadline Tracker

**Problem:** Structural engineering firms that perform statutory building-safety inspections for condominium and cooperative associations operate against a wave of post-Surfside recertification laws with hard, building-specific deadlines that don't work like a firm's own license-renewal calendar. Florida's SB 4-D Milestone Inspection Program requires every condo or co-op building three stories or taller to complete a Phase 1 milestone inspection at 30 years of age (25 years if within three miles of the coast) and then every 10 years after, calculated individually from each building's certificate-of-occupancy date — and if that Phase 1 report finds substantial structural deterioration, the building's association has a hard 365-day statutory deadline to complete a Phase 2 inspection and any required repairs before facing potential unsafe-structure designation. The same statute requires a separate Structural Integrity Reserve Study (SIRS) every 10 years assessing specific building components (roof, load-bearing walls, waterproofing, electrical, plumbing) that funds the association's reserve requirements, and California's parallel SB 326 mandates exterior-elevated-element (balcony, deck, walkway) inspections on a six-year recurring cycle with its own statutory deadline. A small structural engineering firm serving 40-80 condo and co-op association clients across several counties currently tracks each building's individual milestone/SIRS due date, computed from a different certificate-of-occupancy date and county-specific coastal-distance rule, on a principal's spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific building's Phase 2 deadline, SIRS refresh date, or balcony-inspection cycle is still on track before the statutory clock runs out and the association faces a code-enforcement action.

**What to build:** A per-building milestone-inspection deadline calculator computing each client building's 30-year (or 25-year coastal) Phase 1 due date and 10-year recurring cycle from its certificate-of-occupancy date and county; a Phase 2 365-day countdown tracker triggered automatically whenever a Phase 1 report is logged as finding substantial structural deterioration; a SIRS 10-year refresh tracker; and a jurisdiction-aware module for California's six-year exterior-elevated-element inspection cycle and other states' emerging equivalents.

**Skill fit:** State condominium-safety statutes' inspection-interval formulas and each jurisdiction's certificate-of-occupancy and coastal-distance rules are public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-building trigger, and the Phase 2 365-day countdown is the same deterministic deadline-computation pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a per-client-building compliance object entirely distinct from the PE & Land Surveyor Firm, Architecture Firm, and Landscape Architecture Firm (Session 138) ideas already in this repo, all three of which track a firm's own licensee renewal and stamped-drawing-of-record status rather than a statutory inspection deadline attached to each client building itself.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the milestone-deadline calculator, the Phase 2 countdown tracker, and the SIRS refresh tracker, starting with Florida's SB 4-D formula. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small structural engineering firms serving condo associations in South Florida.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Structural Engineers Association chapters in Florida and the Community Associations Institute's (CAI) engineer-vendor directory reach exactly the small firms currently reconciling dozens of buildings' milestone and SIRS deadlines on a spreadsheet with no dedicated system, at a moment when the statewide statute has created urgent, dated compliance pressure.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-399/mo per firm depending on the number of client buildings tracked. 35-50 firms × $270/mo avg = $9,450-13,500/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new condo-association client adds its own independent milestone-and-SIRS clock, every Phase 1 finding of substantial deterioration opens a fresh 365-day Phase 2 countdown, and every 10-year cycle recurs indefinitely for as long as the building stands — a continuous, portfolio-and-deadline-driven compliance stream tied directly to the firm's own client count.

**Biggest risk:** A firm serving only a handful of buildings in a single county has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward firms carrying 30+ condo-association clients across multiple counties or states with different statutory formulas, where the deadline load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 firms ($4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 30 firms ($8.1K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 35-50 firms plus a code-enforcement-ready deadline export a firm can hand an association board the moment a county inspector's letter arrives.

### 3. Certified Welding Inspector (CWI) & NDT Inspection Firm Multi-Method Certification & Vision-Test Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent third-party welding-inspection and nondestructive-testing (NDT) firms — hired by fabricators, contractors, and equipment owners to verify welds and welded structures meet code before a project passes inspection — manage a credential stack unlike almost any other trade in this repo. Every Certified Welding Inspector (CWI) on staff holds AWS QC1 certification on a 3-year cycle, renewable twice through a documented-experience-and-vision-test endorsement for a full 9-year span before a complete exam retake is required, and every NDT technician separately holds ASNT certification (Level I, II, or III) under SNT-TC-1A or CP-189 — tracked independently *per method*, since ultrasonic testing (UT), radiographic testing (RT), magnetic-particle testing (MT), penetrant testing (PT), and visual testing (VT) each carry their own initial training-and-experience-hour requirement, their own annual near/far/color vision-acuity re-test (mandatory for RT, MT, and PT given the fine-defect-detection requirement), and their own 5-year recertification cycle. Firms serving refinery, pipeline, and pressure-vessel clients additionally need inspectors holding API 510 (pressure vessel), API 570 (piping), or API 653 (aboveground tank) certification, each renewed on its own 3-year CEU-based cycle. A technician inspecting welds under a lapsed CWI certification, or signing off an RT film read after their annual vision test lapsed, produces exactly the kind of inspection report a code official or an owner's own quality-assurance audit throws out — invalidating the underlying weld acceptance and forcing costly re-inspection or rework. A small firm running 6-12 inspectors across CWI and multiple NDT methods currently tracks staggered 3-year CWI cycles, per-method 5-year NDT recertification, and annual vision-test currency on a shared spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific inspector assigned to a specific job today is certified and vision-current for every method that job requires.

**What to build:** A CWI 3-year/9-year cycle tracker per inspector with the endorsement-versus-exam-retake distinction flagged automatically; a per-method ASNT NDT certification tracker (UT/RT/MT/PT/VT) with an annual vision-test currency gate blocking a technician from being assigned to a job requiring a method whose vision test has lapsed; and an API 510/570/653 renewal tracker for inspectors serving pressure-equipment clients.

**Skill fit:** AWS QC1's published CWI renewal structure, ASNT's SNT-TC-1A per-method certification and annual vision-test requirements, and API's inspector CEU-renewal rules are public, structured certifying-body data with a concrete per-inspector, per-method trigger, and the vision-test-currency gate is the same deterministic "block the assignment until the record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a third-party inspection niche entirely distinct from the Structural Steel Fabricator, Crane & Rigging Contractor, and Boiler & Pressure Vessel Service Contractor ideas already in this repo, none of which track an independent inspection firm's own inspector-level, per-method credential stack.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the CWI cycle tracker, the per-method NDT/vision-test gate, and the API inspector renewal tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small independent welding-inspection and NDT firms.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the American Welding Society's (AWS) local section directories and the American Society for Nondestructive Testing's (ASNT) section rosters reach exactly the small inspection firms currently reconciling staggered CWI and per-method NDT/vision-test currency on a spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149-299/mo per firm depending on inspector headcount and number of methods offered. 40-55 firms × $215/mo avg = $8,600-11,825/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every inspector's CWI cycle recurs on its own 3-year clock, every NDT method held reopens its own annual vision-test and 5-year recertification question independently, and every new inspector hired compounds the number of simultaneously tracked method-and-vision records — a continuous, headcount-and-method-driven compliance stream tied directly to the firm's own growth.

**Biggest risk:** A one-person inspection shop holding a single CWI credential has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward firms running 6+ inspectors across multiple NDT methods and API certifications, where the multi-method vision-test-and-recertification load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 16 firms ($3.4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 34 firms ($7.3K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 40-55 firms plus an audit-ready certification-and-vision-test export a firm can hand an owner's quality-assurance team the moment an inspection report is challenged.

### 4. Non-Emergency Medical Transportation (NEMT) Provider Broker Credentialing, Vehicle Inspection & OIG Exclusion-Screening Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Small non-emergency medical transportation companies that drive Medicaid beneficiaries to and from covered appointments operate under a state Medicaid transportation-broker contract — Modivcare, MTM, or LogistiCare in most states, or direct state Medicaid enrollment in the others — each with its own credentialing packet, insurance-minimum requirement, and recredentialing cycle that runs independently of every other broker relationship a company holds, since many small NEMT companies contract with two or three brokers simultaneously to keep vehicles full. Every wheelchair-accessible vehicle must pass a state DOT or DMV inspection on a recurring cycle (commonly annual) verifying lift/ramp function and securement points, and every driver must complete passenger-assistance and defensive-driving training plus a background check before dispatch. The compliance obligation this series' pattern fits most precisely: every employee and contractor who could touch a Medicaid-billed trip must be screened monthly against the HHS Office of Inspector General's List of Excluded Individuals/Entities (LEIE) before that person is dispatched, because federal healthcare-fraud rules treat any Medicaid claim touched by an excluded individual as a False Claims Act violation regardless of whether the company knew about the exclusion — a company that dispatches a driver whose name quietly appeared on the LEIE list mid-month has been generating false claims on every trip that driver completed since. A small NEMT company running 10-25 vehicles across two or three broker contracts currently tracks per-broker credentialing renewal, per-vehicle inspection dates, and driver background/training status on a dispatcher's spreadsheet, with no systematic monthly LEIE screening at all — the exact gap a state Medicaid program-integrity audit or an OIG investigation is built to catch.

**What to build:** A multi-broker credentialing tracker with each contract's own recredentialing deadline and insurance-minimum documentation; a per-vehicle inspection and lift/securement-certification renewal tracker; a driver training-and-background-check tracker; and a monthly automated OIG LEIE screening check cross-referencing every active driver and staff member, with a hard "not clear to dispatch" flag on any match before a trip is assigned.

**Skill fit:** The OIG LEIE is a public, downloadable federal dataset updated monthly, making the automated monthly cross-reference screen a straightforward data-matching pipeline directly suited to a data-engineering background, and the per-broker credentialing and per-vehicle inspection tracking are standard CRUD against each broker's published recredentialing cycle — a "not clear to dispatch" gate that is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a Medicaid-transportation niche entirely distinct from the Private Ambulance & EMS Provider idea and the Medical Courier & Clinical Lab Specimen Transport idea (Session 59) already in this repo, neither of which transports live Medicaid beneficiaries under a broker contract or carries a monthly federal-exclusion-screening obligation of any kind.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the multi-broker credentialing tracker, the per-vehicle inspection tracker, and the automated monthly LEIE screening pipeline. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to small NEMT companies.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Non-Emergency Medical Transportation Accreditation Commission's (NEMTAC) member network and state NEMT association chapters reach exactly the small companies juggling multiple broker contracts and vehicle-inspection deadlines with no dedicated compliance system, and the free LEIE-screening demo (running a company's current roster against the public exclusion list on the spot) is a strong, fear-based opening hook.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-399/mo per company depending on fleet size and number of broker contracts. 35-50 companies × $280/mo avg = $9,800-14,000/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every trip dispatched depends on that month's LEIE screening still being current, every vehicle recurs its own independent inspection clock, and every new broker contract a growing company adds compounds the number of simultaneously tracked credentialing cycles — a continuous, fleet-and-headcount-driven compliance stream tied directly to the company's own growth.

**Biggest risk:** A very small single-vehicle operator running one broker contract has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward companies running 10+ vehicles across multiple broker contracts, where the multi-broker-and-exclusion-screening load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 16 companies ($4.5K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 32 companies ($9K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 35-50 companies plus an audit-ready LEIE-screening-and-credentialing export a company can hand a state program-integrity investigator the moment a claims review arrives.

### 5. Independent Clinical Research Trial Site FDA 1572, IRB Continuing-Review & GCP Training Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent clinical research sites — physician-investigator practices and small site-management organizations that run FDA-regulated drug and device trials for multiple pharmaceutical sponsors and CROs simultaneously — operate entirely within the approval window an Institutional Review Board (IRB) grants each individual protocol, and that window is not indefinite: IRB continuing review typically renews annually (or on whatever shorter interval the IRB sets for a higher-risk protocol), and a site that enrolls or continues treating a single subject after that specific protocol's IRB approval has technically lapsed has been conducting unapproved human-subjects research, a finding an FDA Bioresearch Monitoring (BIMO) inspection or a sponsor audit is built to catch and can trigger a clinical hold on the entire study. Every study also requires a completed Form FDA 1572 (Statement of Investigator) naming the principal investigator and every sub-investigator authorized to perform specific study tasks, backed by a per-study delegation-of-authority log — a task performed by a staff member not listed on that study's own delegation log is exactly the protocol-deviation finding an FDA inspector or sponsor monitor flags first during a site visit. Every person touching a study — from the principal investigator to a coordinator drawing labs — must additionally hold current ICH-Good Clinical Practice (GCP) training, renewed every 2-3 years depending on the specific sponsor or CRO's own requirement, and a staff member performing a regulated study task on lapsed GCP training is a documented protocol violation the moment a monitor's next visit reviews the training file. A small independent site running 4-8 concurrent trials for different sponsors currently tracks per-study IRB continuing-review deadlines, 1572 delegation-log completeness, and per-staff GCP training currency on a shared binder and a coordinator's spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific study task performed today falls within both a currently approved IRB window and a properly delegated, GCP-current staff member's authority.

**What to build:** A per-study IRB continuing-review deadline tracker with a hard "not clear to enroll" flag the moment a protocol's approval window closes; a Form FDA 1572 and delegation-of-authority log tracker gating a study task from being logged until the performing staff member is confirmed delegated for that specific task; and a per-staff, per-sponsor GCP training-currency tracker feeding directly into the delegation gate.

**Skill fit:** FDA's published 21 CFR Part 312/812 investigator obligations, IRB continuing-review interval rules, and ICH-GCP training-renewal requirements are public, structured regulatory and standards-body data with a concrete per-study and per-task trigger, and the "not clear to enroll" and delegation gates are the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a human-subjects-research niche entirely distinct from the Clinical & Consumer Genetic Testing Laboratory idea (Session 67) and every CLIA-accredited diagnostic-laboratory idea already in this repo, none of which touches an IRB approval window, a Form FDA 1572 delegation log, or a GCP-training-currency gate of any kind.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the IRB continuing-review tracker, the 1572/delegation-log gate, and the per-staff GCP training tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent clinical research sites running multiple concurrent sponsor trials.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Society for Clinical Research Sites' (SCRS) member directory and the Association of Clinical Research Professionals' (ACRP) local chapter network reach exactly the small independent sites currently reconciling IRB deadlines and delegation-log completeness on a shared binder with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-399/mo per site depending on concurrent-study volume and staff headcount. 30-40 sites × $270/mo avg = $8,100-10,800/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new sponsor study opens its own independent IRB-continuing-review and 1572-delegation clock, every staff member added to a growing site reopens the GCP-training-currency question, and every existing study's annual IRB renewal recurs indefinitely for the life of the trial — a continuous, study-count-and-headcount-driven compliance stream tied directly to the site's own growth.

**Biggest risk:** A site running a single slow-enrolling study has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward sites running 4+ concurrent sponsor trials with overlapping staff, where the multi-study IRB-and-delegation load genuinely outgrows a shared binder.

**Growth path:** Side project → 14 sites ($3.8K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 26 sites ($7K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 30-40 sites plus a BIMO-inspection-ready 1572-and-delegation export a site can hand an FDA inspector or sponsor monitor the moment a site visit begins.
