### 1. Multi-Platform Menu Management for Restaurant Groups
**Problem:** A restaurant group with 5-20 locations running on DoorDash, Uber Eats, Grubhub, their own website, and in-store POS (Toast, Square, Revel) maintains 4-6 separate menus in 4-6 separate systems. When the kitchen 86s an item, the price changes, or a seasonal special launches, someone has to log into each platform individually and make the change by hand. It takes 45-90 minutes, happens at the worst possible time (mid-service), and errors are constant — a $14 item priced at $11 on one platform, an out-of-stock item still orderable on another. The aggregate revenue loss from pricing inconsistencies and the labor cost of manual updates across a 10-location group easily exceeds $3,000/month. The major platforms (DoorDash, Uber Eats) have APIs for menu management but no tool builds a single source of truth that pushes to all of them plus the group's own website simultaneously. Enterprise restaurant software (Olo, Tillster) solves this but costs $2,000-5,000/month and is built for chains with 50+ locations — not the 5-20 location regional operator.
**What to build:** A menu management hub for multi-location restaurant groups — a master menu editor where the operator defines items, descriptions, prices, modifiers, and availability once, then pushes changes to all connected platforms in a single click. Core integrations: DoorDash Drive API, Uber Eats Menu API, Grubhub Partner API, and webhooks for the major POS systems (Toast, Square). An 86 board that lets a manager mark an item unavailable across all platforms in real time from their phone. A pricing override layer that lets certain platforms have platform-specific pricing (to offset delivery commissions) without maintaining separate full menus. A change audit log showing when each platform last synced and flagging any platform whose menu has drifted from master.
**Skill fit:** The builder's engineering background maps directly to API integration work. DoorDash, Uber Eats, and Grubhub all have documented partner APIs for menu management. The data model is straightforward: items, modifiers, categories, platform-specific overrides, location-specific availability.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — DoorDash and Uber Eats menu push, a web-based master menu editor, and the 86 board. Validate with one restaurant group willing to test; the 86 board alone sells the product before the full integration is complete.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — direct outreach to regional restaurant group operators via Instagram (multi-location restaurant groups are easy to identify), local restaurant associations, and food-tech LinkedIn communities. One reference customer in a metro area generates word-of-mouth to other groups in the same city.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-699/month per restaurant group depending on location count and platform integrations. 30 groups at $450/month average = **$13,500/month**. The natural upsell is analytics: which platform is highest-margin per item, which items have the widest price gap across platforms, which 86s happen most frequently (signals for prep or supply chain fixes).
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Menu changes happen multiple times per week. The 86 board becomes a daily operational tool. Churn is low because switching means rebuilding the menu setup from scratch on a new platform. Every new restaurant group that opens a new location pays more.
**Biggest risk:** Platform API access — DoorDash and Uber Eats gate partner API access. Mitigation: apply for partner API access early and build the product in parallel; in the worst case, a Playwright-based menu sync (browser automation) can bridge the gap for early customers while waiting for API approval.
**Growth path:** Side project → DoorDash + Uber Eats integration live, first 3-5 restaurant group customers. Replaces-part-time-income → 10-15 groups, add Grubhub and POS integrations. Replaces-full-time-salary → 30+ groups, add pricing analytics layer and expand to ghost kitchen operators.

### 2. Accounts Receivable Intelligence for Small B2B Companies
**Problem:** A B2B services company (IT MSP, marketing agency, engineering firm, wholesale distributor) with 50-200 active clients invoices $200K-$2M monthly and collects most of it — but 8-15% of receivables go past 60 days every month, and the collections process is a series of identical "just following up" emails sent manually by whoever has time. The owner has no idea which clients are structurally slow-pay vs. just-late-this-month, which invoices are disputed vs. forgotten vs. stretched-for-cash, or which account manager's portfolio has the worst DSO. The result: $15,000-150,000 sitting in 90+ day aging at any given time, finance staff spending 20% of their time on collections, and cash flow uncertainty that affects hiring and vendor decisions. QuickBooks and Xero have basic aging reports but no intelligence: they tell you what's overdue, not what to do about it or in what order.
**What to build:** An AR intelligence layer that sits on top of QuickBooks, Xero, or NetSuite via API — syncing invoice data, payment history, and client records. The core product: a daily collections priority queue that ranks open invoices not just by age but by predicted days-to-pay (based on that client's historical payment behavior, invoice size, and seasonal patterns), a client payment reliability score for every account, automated multi-channel follow-up sequences (email + SMS) that escalate based on invoice age and client score, a disputed invoice tagging system with resolution tracking, and a DSO dashboard broken down by account manager, client segment, and invoice type. The key insight the builder's data skills enable: most AR tools treat every overdue invoice the same. Predicting which ones will self-resolve in 5 days vs. which ones need a call today concentrates effort where it pays off.
**Skill fit:** API integration with QuickBooks/Xero, a simple ML model (logistic regression or gradient boosting) for days-to-pay prediction trained on each company's own invoice history, and a rules-based escalation engine. The data analytics background is the competitive advantage here — most AR tools were built by fintech companies, not analysts who understand the behavioral patterns in payment data.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — QuickBooks Online API integration, invoice sync, client payment history analysis, and a basic priority queue ranked by predicted days-to-pay. The prediction model can start as rule-based (invoice size × historical client average days-to-pay) and improve with data over time.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — target small B2B companies through QuickBooks ProAdvisor network (accountants who manage books for dozens of small B2B clients and can recommend the tool to their clients), LinkedIn outreach to controllers and finance managers at IT MSPs and agencies, and Slack communities for agency owners (Traffic Think Tank, Smart Agency Masterclass).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-799/month per company depending on invoice volume and user count. 25 companies at $500/month = **$12,500/month**. Clear upsell: a cash flow forecasting module that uses the AR prediction data to project weekly cash receipt timing — worth an additional $150-300/month to any company whose owner watches cash daily.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** The pain is financial and immediate — every dollar recovered faster has a real cost-of-capital value. The product touches the company's daily collections workflow, not a quarterly report. Finance managers who reduce DSO by 8 days on a $500K AR balance recover $13,000 in working capital — a $500/month tool pays for itself in the first week.
**Biggest risk:** Convincing companies to connect their accounting system to a new tool. Mitigation: read-only QuickBooks OAuth with no ability to modify records, a clear data processing agreement, and starting with accountants as the channel who already have trusted access to client systems.
**Growth path:** Side project → QuickBooks integration, priority queue, and automated follow-up sequences live, first 3-5 paying companies. Replaces-part-time-income → 10-15 companies, add Xero and NetSuite, add cash flow forecasting module. Replaces-full-time-salary → 25+ companies, add dispute management and integration with collection agency handoff workflows.

### 3. Service Agreement and Membership Program Management for Home Service Companies
**Problem:** An HVAC, plumbing, or electrical company with $1-5M annual revenue has 200-800 customers. The owner knows that a service agreement program — annual contracts where customers pay $150-300/year for priority scheduling, discounted service calls, and two annual maintenance visits — is the single highest-ROI thing the business can do. It generates predictable recurring revenue, reduces customer churn, and fills the shoulder season calendar. But every owner who tries to build one runs into the same operational nightmare: tracking who's enrolled, when their agreement renews, when their included maintenance visits are due, whether they've used their visit, and collecting the renewal payment — all while dispatching 8 technicians across 40 daily service calls. The result is an "agreement program" that's actually a folder of signed contracts on the owner's desk and a QuickBooks report no one runs. Field management software (ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro) has membership modules but they're buried in enterprise packages that cost $500-1,500/month and require the company to migrate their entire dispatch system to use them.
**What to build:** A standalone service agreement and membership management tool for home service companies — a member database tracking every enrolled customer, their agreement tier (basic tune-up, premium multi-visit, parts-discount club), agreement start and renewal date, number of included visits used vs. remaining, and next scheduled maintenance visit date. An automated renewal engine: 60 days before renewal, send a personalized renewal email with a pay-now link; 30 days before, send a reminder; on renewal date, auto-charge the card on file via Stripe and issue a new agreement. A member visit scheduler that connects to the company's Google Calendar or Jobber/ServiceTitan dispatch board and books the included maintenance visits automatically with customer-facing scheduling links. A revenue dashboard: monthly recurring revenue from agreements, renewal rate, expired agreements not yet renewed (churn pipeline), and the revenue impact of converting X% of one-time customers to members.
**Skill fit:** Straightforward web app — a CRM, a Stripe subscription engine, email automation, and a scheduling widget. No exotic ML required. The builder can use an existing scheduling library (Cal.com open source, or Calendly API) and focus engineering time on the agreement management and renewal logic.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — member database, manual agreement entry, automated renewal email sequence via SendGrid, and Stripe payment collection. Skip the automated scheduling in V1; let the owner's office staff handle scheduling manually from the member list. The renewal automation alone is worth $200/month to any owner running agreements manually.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — HVAC and plumbing business owner communities on Facebook (HVAC-Talk, Plumbers Success International Facebook group, The Blue Collar Millionaire community) are large and active. Owner-operators in these groups share tools constantly. A single demo video of the renewal automation in a Facebook group generates 10-20 inbound inquiries within 48 hours.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149-399/month per company depending on enrolled member count. 50 companies at $250/month = **$12,500/month**. Upsell: a "member acquisition" add-on that automates post-service-call email sequences converting one-time customers into members ($99-149/month additional). A 50-company base with 40% on the upsell adds $2,000-3,000/month.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every home service company that runs a membership program touches this tool every day — renewal emails fire automatically, new member signups happen after every service call, and the scheduled visit calendar is always active. Churn requires migrating member records and rebuilding renewal automation, which no owner bothers doing. The product is mission-critical the moment the company's first 50 members are enrolled.
**Biggest risk:** ServiceTitan releasing a standalone membership module at competitive pricing. Mitigation: the target buyer is the $1-5M revenue company that isn't on ServiceTitan yet and doesn't want to be — the tool is deliberately standalone and lightweight.
**Growth path:** Side project → member database, renewal automation, and Stripe payment live, first 5-8 home service company clients. Replaces-part-time-income → 20-25 companies, add scheduling integration with Jobber and Housecall Pro, add member acquisition automation. Replaces-full-time-salary → 50+ companies, add upsell cadence and seasonal campaign tools (pre-summer AC tune-up campaigns targeting all non-member past customers).

### 4. Subcontractor Daily Report and Change Order Automation
**Problem:** An electrical, framing, or mechanical subcontractor on a commercial construction project spends 45-90 minutes per day per superintendent filling out daily reports and managing change orders — using a combination of Word templates, email threads, and whatever the GC's project management portal (Procore, Autodesk Construction Cloud) requires. The daily report is critical: it documents weather, crew size, work performed, materials delivered, and any delays caused by other trades or the owner — it's the paper trail that supports change order claims and schedule extension requests. But most subs write their daily reports after the fact, pulling from memory, and the reports are thin on the specifics that actually matter when a $200,000 change order dispute lands in arbitration 18 months later. The change order process is worse: the GC sends an RFI or verbal direction, the sub does the work, then 90 days later tries to reconstruct a change order claim from text messages and time cards. An estimated 30-40% of legitimate extra-work claims by subcontractors are either never submitted or settled for far less than their value because the documentation wasn't built in real time.
**What to build:** A subcontractor-first daily report and change order documentation tool — a mobile-first daily report app where the superintendent fills in structured fields (crew count by trade, hours worked, equipment on site, weather, work areas completed, delays with cause attributed to the specific responsible party) and attaches photos tagged by work area and date. The key differentiator: a "potential change event" flag the super can set on any daily report item — when they're told to do something out of scope, change materials, or work in an unscheduled area, they flag it in the daily report the same day. The system then auto-generates a draft change order from the flagged daily report items, pre-populated with the crew hours, materials, and dates already recorded. Formal change order tracking: submitted CO number, GC response, negotiated amount, and approval status. A project-level dashboard showing every open potential change event and every open CO with financial exposure. Export to Procore via API for GCs that require it.
**Skill fit:** A mobile web app (React Native or PWA), structured data entry, photo storage (S3), and a document generator. The Procore API is public and well-documented. The builder's engineering background is sufficient; no specialized construction knowledge is needed beyond the workflow described above.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — structured daily report form, photo upload, potential change event flag, and PDF daily report export. The change order draft generator can come in V2. The daily report alone, generating a clean PDF the super can send to the GC, is worth $100-200/month to a superintendent who's currently maintaining a Word document.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Subcontractor trade association communities (NECA, SMACNA, ASA — American Subcontractors Association) have active member networks and newsletters. LinkedIn outreach to electrical and mechanical superintendents and project managers. Construction tech communities on Slack and Twitter/X. One reference customer at a mid-size electrical subcontractor becomes a case study: "recovered $180,000 in change orders in the first 6 months."
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-599/month per subcontractor company depending on project count and superintendent count. 25 companies at $375/month = **$9,375/month**. The natural upsell is a change order ROI report: this company has $X in documented potential change events still unsubmitted — shown monthly to justify the cost of the tool 10x over.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Daily reports are filled in every single working day. The change order pipeline is live throughout every project. Switching means starting the documentation archive over, which no superintendent accepts mid-project. New project wins generate new project setups. The tool's value compounds: the longer a company uses it, the larger the documentation archive that protects future change order claims.
**Biggest risk:** Subs already using Procore (the GC's platform) feeling like they don't need another tool. Mitigation: the tool is subcontractor-owned and subcontractor-controlled — daily reports live in the sub's system, not the GC's. The Procore integration pushes data to the GC but the sub keeps their own record. Subcontractors who understand the dispute value of maintaining their own documentation independent of the GC's system immediately get it.
**Growth path:** Side project → daily report form, photo upload, potential change event flagging, and PDF export live, first 5 subcontractor clients. Replaces-part-income → 12-15 companies, add change order draft generator and Procore integration. Replaces-full-time-salary → 25+ companies, add owner's financial dashboard aggregating change order exposure across all active projects, expand to specialty trades (concrete, roofing, glazing).

### 5. Dynamic Pricing for Independent Hotels
**Problem:** An independent hotel with 40-120 rooms — not a Marriott, not a Holiday Inn, a family-owned boutique or a regional property — sets room rates the same way it has for 20 years: a flat rate by season, maybe a weekend premium, and manual adjustments when the owner notices the OTAs aren't filling. The Marriotts and Hiltons run sophisticated revenue management systems (IDeaS, Duetto) that adjust pricing hourly based on local demand signals — events, competitor pricing, booking pace, and pickup rate. Those systems cost $1,500-4,000/month and are designed for hotel chains, not independent operators. The result: an independent hotel routinely undersells on high-demand nights (when it could charge $50-100 more and still fill) and over-prices on soft nights (when a $30 drop would fill the last 20 rooms). The revenue difference between a hotel with basic dynamic pricing and one without is typically 8-15% in RevPAR (revenue per available room) — on a 60-room hotel averaging $120/night at 70% occupancy, that's $18,000-34,000/year in recovered revenue. The tools to replicate this exist; the problem is that nobody has packaged them for the independent operator.
**What to build:** A revenue management tool built for independent hotels — connecting to the hotel's property management system (PMS) via the most common PMS APIs: Cloudbeds, Mews, Little Hotelier, and WebRezPro. Pulling booking pickup rate (how fast rooms are filling for each future night), current occupancy vs. historical occupancy for the same dates, local event data (concerts, sports, conferences, graduations from a public events API), and competitor pricing from OTA rate-scraping (legal, widely done in hospitality). Producing a daily rate recommendation for each room type for each of the next 90 nights — with a confidence level and an explanation of the primary driver (high event demand, faster-than-usual pickup, competitor rate increase). A one-click rate push that sends the recommended rate to the hotel's PMS and channel manager. A revenue impact tracker showing the incremental revenue attributed to accepted rate recommendations.
**Skill fit:** API integration with PMS systems (Cloudbeds has a well-documented API), an events data API (PredictHQ or Ticketmaster), OTA rate scraping (a Python scraper for Booking.com and Expedia is 200 lines), and a rule-based pricing engine (start rule-based, evolve toward ML with data). The builder's analytics background is exactly what revenue management requires — it's a data problem disguised as a hospitality problem.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — Cloudbeds API integration, pickup rate calculation from booking data, local event flag from PredictHQ free tier, and a daily rate recommendation email. No dashboard required in V1; a daily email with tomorrow's recommended rate for each room type and the primary reason is enough to start charging $300/month.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — independent hotel owner communities are surprisingly reachable: the AAHOA (Asian American Hotel Owners Association — 20,000+ independent hotel owner members) has an active member network, Cloudbeds has a marketplace where integrations are discoverable, and hospitality Facebook groups (Independent Hotel Show community, Hotel Management Network) are active. One case study with RevPAR improvement data converts the channel.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-699/month per property depending on room count and PMS integration. 30 properties at $450/month = **$13,500/month**. Upsell: a minimum-stay and length-of-stay optimization add-on — recommending 2-night minimums around high-demand weekends to prevent a Friday sellout with an empty Saturday — worth an additional $100-200/month. 30 properties at $550/month blended = $16,500/month.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Revenue management is a daily operational tool — rate recommendations come every morning and the owner or front desk manager acts on them. There's no seasonal cliff; hotels run 365 days. Switching means losing the historical booking data that makes the recommendations accurate, so churn is low after 90 days of use. Every new property the owner opens generates a new subscription.
**Biggest risk:** PMS API access — some PMS systems have restrictive partner programs. Mitigation: start with Cloudbeds (the most API-friendly PMS, used by thousands of independent hotels) and expand from there. Cloudbeds' marketplace listing also provides organic distribution.
**Growth path:** Side project → Cloudbeds integration, pickup rate analysis, event flag, and daily rate recommendation email live, first 3-5 independent hotel customers. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-15 properties, add Mews and Little Hotelier integrations, add dashboard and rate push automation. Replaces-full-time-salary → 30+ properties, add length-of-stay optimization and channel mix analytics (which OTA is cheapest to fill on soft nights), expand to vacation rental management companies managing 10+ properties.
