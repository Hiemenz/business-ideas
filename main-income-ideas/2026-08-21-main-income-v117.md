# Main Income Ideas - 2026-08-21 (Session 117)

## Who this is for
You're 29, you write software and know your way around data — pipelines, scrapers, structured reference data, rules engines. You want to replace a full-time salary ($8-15K+/mo) within 12-24 months with something you build solo in evenings and weekends, no outside funding, no co-founder, and you want the first paying customer inside 90 days. The wedge in this series is always the same: small operators in a regulated industry who have non-optional government or accreditation-body obligations — licenses, permits, certifications, filings, inspections — and no compliance department to track them. You sell a monthly-retainer SaaS that turns their compliance binder into a living calendar with alerts and hard gates. The regulator (or accrediting body) creates the deadline; you sell the peace of mind of never missing it.

### 1. Industrial Radiography & Nondestructive Testing Company NRC/Agreement State Byproduct Material License Compliance
**Problem:** Industrial radiography companies — performing gamma-ray weld and casting inspection for pipeline, pressure vessel, and structural steel fabricators — hold a byproduct material license issued by the U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission or one of the 39 NRC Agreement States under 10 CFR Part 34 ("Licenses for Industrial Radiography and Radiation Safety Requirements for Industrial Radiographic Operations"), naming a Radiation Safety Officer (RSO) responsible for the licensee's entire radiation-safety program. Every portable gamma exposure device (commonly an Ir-192 or Se-75 sealed-source camera) must undergo a documented leak test at intervals not to exceed six months per 10 CFR 34.27, confirming the sealed source has not ruptured — a device found in use past its leak-test due date is one of the most common NRC/Agreement State inspection findings and can trigger an immediate order to cease field operations. Every certified industrial radiographer and radiographer's assistant must hold current certification from an NRC-recognized certifying entity (commonly the American Society for Nondestructive Testing under its Central Certification Program, layered with the NRC's own operator-certification requirement under 34.43) and complete annual refresher safety training. Personnel monitoring is mandatory: every radiographer wears a dosimeter (TLD or OSL badge) exchanged and read on a fixed cycle, with dose results retained and compared against 10 CFR Part 20 occupational dose limits — a badge reading approaching an action level, or a badge never returned for exchange, is a direct radiation-safety-program failure. Radiation survey meters used to confirm the source has fully retracted after every exposure must themselves be calibrated at least annually, and a source used without a currently calibrated survey meter is itself a reportable violation. A small NDT company running 4-10 radiographers and a handful of exposure devices across scattered job sites — refinery turnarounds, pipeline tie-ins, structural steel fabrication shops — tracks per-device leak-test and survey-meter-calibration due dates, per-radiographer certification and dosimetry-badge-exchange currency, and license-renewal status on a paper logbook kept in the truck, and a lapsed leak test or an expired radiographer certification discovered mid-job is exactly the finding an NRC or Agreement State inspector's field audit is built to catch — with consequences ranging from a Notice of Violation to license suspension.
**What to build:** A per-exposure-device leak-test tracker computing the 6-month due date from the last test and blocking a device from being logged "field-ready" once it lapses; a per-radiographer/assistant certification and dosimetry-badge-exchange tracker with a "cleared for field work" gate; a survey-meter calibration tracker; and a license-renewal and RSO-designation tracker.
**Skill fit:** 10 CFR Part 34's leak-test interval, personnel-dosimetry cycle, and survey-meter calibration requirement are public, numerically precise NRC regulatory data, and the due-date computation plus field-readiness gate are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a radioactive-materials trade with genuinely severe consequences for a lapse that most small NDT shops still track on a paper logbook in the truck.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the leak-test tracker with the field-readiness gate, the certification/dosimetry tracker, and the survey-meter calibration tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent NDT/radiography shops.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — American Society for Nondestructive Testing (ASNT) local section meetings and independent NDT shop-owner networks, where small radiography company owners already navigate NRC/Agreement State inspection prep with no dedicated RSO support staff.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per company depending on device/radiographer count. 25-30 companies × $399/mo avg = $9,975-11,970/mo.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every exposure device runs its own independent 6-month leak-test clock regardless of job volume, every radiographer's dosimetry badge exchanges on its own fixed cycle, and every new job site adds a fresh survey-meter-calibration and transportation-documentation event — a continuously recurring compliance stream tied directly to the company's device fleet and radiographer roster.
**Biggest risk:** A device leak-tested past its 6-month window, or a radiographer working without current certification, is exactly the finding behind the industry's most severe NRC/Agreement State enforcement actions — mitigate by making the field-readiness gate a hard block rather than an advisory flag.
**Growth path:** Side project → 10-12 companies ($3.6-4.4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 20-22 companies ($7.6-8.4K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 28-30 companies plus expansion into medical and veterinary radiography licensees operating under the same CFR framework.

### 2. Forensic Toxicology / SAMHSA-Certified Drug & Alcohol Testing Laboratory NLCP Compliance
**Problem:** Independent forensic toxicology laboratories that perform confirmatory GC-MS/MS drug testing for federal workplace and DOT-regulated drug-testing programs must hold and maintain certification under SAMHSA's National Laboratory Certification Program (NLCP), operating under the HHS Mandatory Guidelines for Federal Workplace Drug Testing Programs and, for DOT-regulated specimens, 49 CFR Part 40 Subpart F laboratory requirements — a status entirely separate from and layered on top of any CLIA certification the lab also holds for its non-forensic clinical testing. NLCP certification requires the lab to pass quarterly blind proficiency-testing (PT) rounds — shipped specimens with undisclosed concentrations the lab must analyze and report back within a fixed window, with a single failed round triggering an immediate NLCP inspection and, on a second failure, revocation — plus scheduled on-site NLCP inspections. Every result depends on a documented, unbroken chain of custody from specimen receipt through aliquoting, testing, and storage, and a Laboratory Director or Certifying Scientist meeting HHS's specific personnel-qualification requirements (an advanced degree plus documented forensic toxicology experience) must review and certify every non-negative result before release — a certified result released without the Certifying Scientist's documented review, or a chain-of-custody gap discovered during an employer's for-cause challenge, is exactly the finding that turns into a lost DOT-testing-program client relationship or an NLCP show-cause proceeding. A small independent forensic toxicology lab running a handful of certifying scientists and processing thousands of specimens a month manages per-quarter PT-round tracking, per-specimen chain-of-custody documentation, and Certifying Scientist qualification/CE currency with no dedicated regulatory-affairs function.
**What to build:** A quarterly NLCP proficiency-testing round tracker with a due-date countdown and pass/fail history; a per-specimen chain-of-custody log from receipt through final disposition with a release-block until every required custody link is documented; a Certifying Scientist qualification and CE-hour currency tracker gating who may certify a non-negative result; and an NLCP/state lab-license renewal calendar.
**Skill fit:** HHS's Mandatory Guidelines and 49 CFR Part 40's laboratory-certification requirements are public, structured federal regulatory data with a well-defined per-quarter and per-specimen record requirement, and the PT-round tracker plus the chain-of-custody release gate are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a forensic-laboratory niche distinct from the cannabis, clinical, and genetic testing laboratories already covered elsewhere in this repo, and distinct from the drug-testing collection-site idea in Session 79, which covers the party that draws the specimen rather than the certified lab that confirms it.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the PT-round tracker, the chain-of-custody log with the release gate, and the Certifying Scientist qualification tracker. First 5-8 labs through direct outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Society of Forensic Toxicologists (SOFT) member directory, where small independent lab directors already navigate quarterly PT rounds and chain-of-custody documentation with no dedicated compliance staff.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $399-799/mo per lab depending on specimen volume. 18-22 labs × $549/mo avg = $9,880-12,080/mo.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every quarter opens a fresh PT round the lab must pass to keep its certification, and every specimen received generates its own chain-of-custody documentation event — a continuous, specimen-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the lab's DOT/federal testing-program business.
**Biggest risk:** A failed PT round or a chain-of-custody gap can suspend the lab's NLCP certification outright, ending its DOT-testing-program revenue overnight — mitigate by positioning the PT-round tracker's advance-warning alerts as the product's core, non-optional feature.
**Growth path:** Side project → 8-10 labs ($4.4-5.5K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 15-16 labs ($8.2-8.8K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 20-22 labs plus an upsell NLCP inspection-readiness audit service.

### 3. Donor Human Milk Bank HMBANA Accreditation, Donor-Screening & Pasteurization Compliance
**Problem:** Nonprofit and independent donor human milk banks that screen, pasteurize, and dispense pooled donor breast milk to NICUs and medically fragile infants operate under accreditation from the Human Milk Banking Association of North America (HMBANA), whose Guidelines for the Establishment and Operation of a Donor Human Milk Bank specify a serologic donor-screening panel (HIV-1/2, HTLV-I/II, Hepatitis B and C, and syphilis) that must clear before a donor's milk can be pooled, plus a documented health-history questionnaire and physician or designated screener sign-off — a donor whose serology or health-history screen was never completed, or whose approval lapsed before a subsequent donation, is exactly the gap an HMBANA re-accreditation site visit is built to find. Every pooled batch must go through Holder pasteurization (62.5°C for 30 minutes) with a documented time-temperature log for the run, followed by mandatory post-pasteurization bacteriologic culture testing — a batch cannot be released for dispensing until its culture result comes back clear, and a batch released before culture clearance is one of the most serious findings a state health department or HMBANA audit can make given the direct infection risk to a NICU population. Milk banks in several states additionally hold a state-specific donor human milk bank license, and the sector now includes a growing number of hospital-affiliated donor milk depots and for-profit processors alongside HMBANA-model nonprofits, each carrying its own renewal cycle. A small milk bank running a handful of screening staff and processing dozens of pooled batches a month manages per-donor serology/health-history tracking, per-batch pasteurization-log-to-culture-clearance reconciliation, and HMBANA/state license renewal with no dedicated compliance function.
**What to build:** A per-donor serology and health-history screening tracker with a pooling-eligibility gate blocking any donor's milk from entering a pool until screening clears; a per-batch pasteurization time-temperature log tied to a hard release-block until post-pasteurization culture results come back clear; and an HMBANA accreditation and state license renewal tracker.
**Skill fit:** HMBANA's published guidelines and the Holder-pasteurization-to-culture-clearance sequence are a public, structured "compute the log, block the release" workflow — the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly for biologics-handling businesses, applied to a donor-milk category distinct from the blood/plasma donation center and reproductive/non-reproductive tissue bank ideas already covered elsewhere in this repo (an entirely separate specimen type, accrediting body, and pasteurization-and-culture release mechanism neither of those ideas' data models has any field for).
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the donor screening tracker with the pooling gate, the batch pasteurization/culture log with the release gate, and the accreditation tracker. First 5-8 milk banks through direct outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — HMBANA's member directory of accredited and startup milk banks across North America, a small but well-organized professional community that already coordinates on screening and pasteurization protocol questions.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $399-699/mo per bank/program depending on batch volume, spanning HMBANA-accredited nonprofits, hospital-affiliated donor milk depots, and for-profit processors. 20-24 clients × $549/mo avg = $10,980-13,176/mo.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every pooled batch generates its own pasteurization-log-to-culture-clearance event, and every new donor requires a fresh serology and health-history screen before her milk can enter a pool — a continuous, batch-and-donor-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the bank's ability to legally dispense milk.
**Biggest risk:** The HMBANA-accredited-bank population is small (roughly 30 in North America), capping near-term acquisition speed — mitigate by covering hospital-affiliated donor milk depots and for-profit processors in the same product, since both use the same pasteurization-and-culture release workflow, and by positioning early as new banks continue to open.
**Growth path:** Side project → 8-10 clients ($4.4-5.5K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 16-18 clients ($8.8-9.9K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 22-24 clients plus expansion as new donor milk programs open nationally.

### 4. Gestational Surrogacy Agency State Surrogacy Statute & Escrow/Trust Compensation Compliance
**Problem:** Full-service gestational surrogacy agencies — matching intended parents with a gestational carrier, coordinating legal, medical, and psychological screening, and managing the surrogate's compensation — operate under a rapidly growing and highly state-specific body of surrogacy law: California's Family Code §7960-7962, New York's Child-Parent Security Act (Domestic Relations Law Article 5-C), and the roughly 20 states that have now adopted some version of the Uniform Parentage Act's gestational-agreement provisions, each specifying required contract elements (independent legal counsel for the surrogate paid by the intended parents, documented medical and psychological clearance, and execution of the agreement before embryo transfer) that, if missing, can render the agreement unenforceable or expose the agency to a state consumer-protection claim. Every surrogacy agreement sets out a compensation schedule — a base fee, monthly allowance, and reimbursable medical/incidental expenses disbursed on a milestone schedule (confirmed pregnancy, each trimester, delivery) — that the agency typically administers through an escrow or trust account on the surrogate's behalf, and a disbursement made without the triggering milestone documented, or funds commingled with the agency's own operating account, is the kind of finding that turns into a state attorney general consumer-protection inquiry or a breach-of-contract claim from either party. Agencies must also track each surrogate's state-specific eligibility requirements (age, prior-birth count, screening per ASRM's practice-committee guidelines) before she can be matched to a new intended-parent client. A small agency running a handful of case managers and dozens of active matches simultaneously tracks per-match statutory-requirement checklists, per-milestone escrow disbursement documentation, and per-surrogate eligibility screening on a shared spreadsheet, with no dedicated compliance function — and a disbursement released before its contractual milestone cleared, or a match agreement executed without the state's required independent-counsel or pre-transfer-execution provision, is exactly the gap a state inquiry or client dispute is built to catch.
**What to build:** A per-match statutory-requirement checklist gating contract execution (independent counsel confirmed, medical/psych clearance on file, pre-transfer execution date) against the specific state's surrogacy statute; a per-match escrow/trust disbursement tracker releasing funds only against a documented milestone; and a per-surrogate eligibility and screening tracker.
**Skill fit:** The growing set of state gestational-agreement statutes and ASRM's published screening guidelines are structured legal and professional-society source material, and the milestone-triggered disbursement gate is the same deterministic "block the action until the record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a family-formation agency category distinct from the fertility clinic/reproductive tissue bank idea covered in Session 65, which governs the clinical IVF-and-transfer procedure itself rather than the non-clinical matching-and-escrow business coordinating a gestational arrangement.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the statutory-requirement checklist gate for the 10 most active surrogacy-friendly states, the escrow disbursement tracker, and the surrogate eligibility tracker. First 5-8 agencies through direct outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the third-party-reproduction professional community organized around the Society for Ethics in Egg Donation and Surrogacy (SEEDS) conference and independent surrogacy-agency-owner networks, where agency owners already navigate state-by-state statutory variation with no dedicated compliance staff.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $349-699/mo per agency depending on active-match volume. 22-26 agencies × $499/mo avg = $10,978-12,974/mo.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new match opens its own statutory-checklist and escrow-disbursement chain running the full length of a pregnancy, and each state's surrogacy law keeps evolving as more states adopt Uniform Parentage Act provisions — a structural reason the product's state-requirement matrix never becomes "finished."
**Biggest risk:** A disbursement released before its milestone, or a contract executed without a state's required independent-counsel provision, can render the agreement unenforceable and expose the agency to real liability — mitigate by making the milestone-gated disbursement and the pre-execution statutory checklist hard blocks, not advisory flags.
**Growth path:** Side project → 10-12 agencies ($3.5-4.2K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 18-20 agencies ($9-10K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 24-26 agencies plus expansion into egg-donation-agency compliance under the same state-statute pattern.

### 5. Wilderness Therapy / Outdoor Behavioral Healthcare Program State Youth-Program Licensing & OBH Council Accreditation Compliance
**Problem:** Wilderness therapy and outdoor behavioral healthcare programs — multi-week field-based therapeutic programs for adolescents, run under licensed clinical staff in a backcountry setting — operate under a rapidly tightening set of state "outdoor youth program" licensing statutes enacted in direct response to a string of high-profile program deaths (Utah's R501-19 Outdoor Youth Program administrative rule, and comparable statutes now on the books in Oregon, Montana, and several other states), each mandating specific field-staff-to-participant ratios, a minimum Wilderness First Responder (WFR) or Wilderness EMT certification for field staff on every expedition, and — in the states with the most recent reforms — a defined incident-reporting window (commonly 24-72 hours) to the state licensing agency for any injury, use of physical restraint, or unplanned participant departure. On top of state licensing, most credible programs pursue accreditation from the Outdoor Behavioral Healthcare Council (OBH Council), a private accrediting body whose standards layer clinical-supervision-ratio and field-safety-protocol requirements on top of the state license, renewed on a multi-year cycle with an on-site review. A field staff member whose WFR/WEMT certification lapses mid-expedition, or a reportable incident filed past the state's mandated window, is precisely the finding a state licensing survey or an OBH Council accreditation review is built to catch — and given the population served and the industry's post-tragedy scrutiny, a licensing lapse can end a program's ability to operate in a state overnight. A small program running several field staff, rotating expedition groups, and a handful of concurrent state licenses (many programs operate field sites across state lines) manages per-staff WFR/WEMT certification currency, per-incident reporting-deadline tracking, and per-state license renewal with no dedicated compliance function.
**What to build:** A per-field-staff WFR/WEMT certification currency tracker with a "cleared for expedition" gate blocking an assignment once certification lapses; an incident-reporting countdown computing each state's mandated reporting window from the incident date, with an overdue alert; and a multi-state licensing and OBH Council accreditation renewal tracker.
**Skill fit:** State outdoor-youth-program statutes and OBH Council's published accreditation standards are structured regulatory and accrediting-body data with concrete numeric thresholds — the field-readiness gate and the incident-reporting countdown are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a youth-behavioral-health category distinct from the CARF-accredited substance abuse treatment center (Sessions 34 and 48) and NARR sober living (Session 65) ideas covered elsewhere in this repo, neither of which involves a mobile field-based expedition model, a wilderness-specific staff certification, or a state-mandated incident-reporting window.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the field-staff certification tracker with the expedition-readiness gate, the incident-reporting countdown, and the multi-state licensing tracker. First 5-8 programs through direct outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Outdoor Behavioral Healthcare Council's member-program directory and the National Association of Therapeutic Schools and Programs (NATSP), where small independent program owners already navigate post-tragedy state-licensing scrutiny with no dedicated regulatory-affairs staff.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $399-799/mo per program depending on field-staff and expedition-group count. 20-24 programs × $549/mo avg = $10,980-13,176/mo.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every field staff member's WFR/WEMT certification recurs on its own multi-year cycle, every expedition generates its own incident-reporting exposure, and the regulatory environment keeps tightening as more states adopt outdoor-youth-program statutes — a structural reason the product's compliance surface keeps growing.
**Biggest risk:** A field staff member working an expedition on a lapsed WFR/WEMT certification, or an incident report filed past the state's mandated window, is exactly the finding behind the industry's most severe licensing actions following a string of well-publicized program deaths — mitigate by making the expedition-readiness gate a hard block, not an advisory flag, and by covering the OBH Council accreditation cycle alongside state licensing since most credible programs hold both.
**Growth path:** Side project → 8-10 programs ($4.4-5.5K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 16-18 programs ($8.8-9.9K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 22-24 programs plus expansion into the broader therapeutic-boarding-school segment under NATSP's adjacent accreditation framework.

## This Session's Pick: #1 (Industrial Radiography) for the clearest, most severe compliance failure mode; #5 (Wilderness Therapy) for the sharpest regulatory-momentum story

Industrial radiography stands out because its compliance mechanics are the most unambiguous in this session — a sealed radioactive source, a hard 6-month leak-test clock, and a federal/Agreement-State inspector who can shut down field operations on the spot for a lapse. ASNT's local-section network gives a direct, well-organized channel into exactly the small-shop owners who feel this pain today, and the RSO-support angle (a compliance tool doing work a designated Radiation Safety Officer would otherwise have to do by hand) makes the pitch concrete from the first conversation.

Wilderness therapy is the sharpest momentum bet. State legislatures are actively tightening outdoor-youth-program statutes in direct response to program deaths, which means the compliance surface this product tracks is expanding on its own timeline, not waiting for the builder to find growth — and a program that can show a state surveyor or an OBH Council reviewer a clean, gate-enforced certification and incident-reporting record has a genuinely differentiated pitch in an industry under real public scrutiny.
