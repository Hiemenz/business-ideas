### 1. Special Education Private School IDEA Compliance & State Approval Management
**Problem:** Private special education schools that accept publicly placed students from school districts must comply with a dual regulatory framework: state special education private school approval (each state's department of education must approve the private school as an appropriate placement for special education students, with annual renewal requirements and periodic site inspections), and IDEA (Individuals with Disabilities Education Act) requirements for implementing students' Individualized Education Programs (IEPs). IDEA compliance for each publicly placed student requires: implementing every IEP goal as written with the specified service minutes, collecting progress monitoring data on each IEP goal using the specified measurement method (percentage correct, frequency count, rubric score), issuing periodic progress reports to parents at the same intervals as the school's general education report cards, maintaining documentation of every IEP service minute delivered (which school districts audit during annual reviews and in disputes), and documenting any instances where the school cannot implement an IEP goal as written and the corrective steps taken. Small private special education schools (30-100 students placed by one or more school districts) manage 30-100 simultaneous IEP compliance obligations — each with a different goal set, different service schedule, different reporting frequency, and different district contact — through a combination of paper binders and staff memory, with no systematic tracking that confirms every goal is receiving progress monitoring at the required intervals.
**What to build:** A special education private school IDEA compliance and state approval management service — an IEP implementation tracker (for each publicly placed student: all IEP goals with the specified measurement method, service type, and service minutes per week; a data collection log for each goal recording the date, data point, and measurement — with visual progress graphs per goal; and automatic alerts when a goal has not had a data point recorded within the expected monitoring interval), a periodic progress report generator (producing the IDEA-required progress report for each student at the school's reporting intervals: current progress on each goal with the data trend, projected progress toward the annual goal, and any recommended IEP modifications — formatted for the district's review), an IEP service minute delivery log (tracking actual service minutes delivered per student per service type each week, flagging any week where a student's services fell below the IEP-specified minutes, and documenting any excused absences or service interruptions that account for the gap), a state special education private school approval compliance calendar (tracking the school's state approval renewal deadline, any required annual submission of student census data to the state education agency, and any site visit notification requirements and preparation checklists), and a school district parent communication log (maintaining a record of all IEP-related communications with each student's school district and parent — meeting dates, progress report delivery confirmations, and any written correspondence about IEP amendments or disputes).
**Skill fit:** IDEA IEP implementation requirements and progress monitoring standards are publicly available through the US Department of Education's IDEA regulations and OSEP (Office of Special Education Programs) technical assistance publications — the regulatory framework for IEP progress documentation is well-documented. State special education private school approval requirements are publicly available from each state's department of education. The IEP goal tracking system (a per-student, per-goal data collection log with trend analysis), progress report generator, service minute log, state approval calendar, and parent communication log are data management and document generation problems the builder's engineering skills address directly.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build an IEP goal tracker with data collection log and progress graphing, a periodic progress report generator, a service minute delivery log with shortfall alerts, and a state approval compliance calendar for 10 high-private-special-ed-enrollment states (CA, TX, NY, NJ, PA, FL, MA, IL, OH, CT); first 5-8 private special education school clients through National Association of Private Special Education Centers (NAPSEC) member outreach while the IEP goal tracker is validated against real school IEP goal structures.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through NAPSEC (National Association of Private Special Education Centers) member network and state association for private special education (ASPEN and state chapter) outreach (the concentrated professional communities for private special education school administrators), school district special education director referral networks (SPED directors who place students in private schools evaluate the school's IEP documentation practices during annual reviews and actively refer compliance management services to schools that have documentation gaps), and special education attorney referral networks (attorneys who represent school districts in special education due process proceedings encounter inadequate IEP documentation at private schools in every case and refer compliance tracking services to schools seeking to strengthen their documentation).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per private special ed school: $500-1,200/mo (depending on student count and district complexity — a 40-student school with placements from 2-3 districts: $500/mo; an 80-student school with placements from 10+ districts with different documentation requirements and reporting schedules: $1,200/mo). Managing 15-20 school retainers at $750/mo average = **$11,250-15,000/mo**. Annual state approval renewal documentation packages (one-time $800-1,500 per school for the state approval renewal application package) generate significant supplemental revenue. Reaching 15-20 school retainers requires 3-4 NAPSEC chapter relationships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** IDEA progress monitoring data must be collected on every IEP goal at every required interval — a 60-student school with an average of 6 IEP goals per student generates 360 simultaneous goal tracking obligations, each requiring regular data entry. Progress reports are required at every school-wide reporting period for every student. Annual IEP meetings generate a documentation preparation event for every student's annual review. Due process hearing filings (which are increasing in frequency as districts become more aggressive in reviewing private school IEP implementation) create urgent compliance documentation review engagements.
**Biggest risk:** A school that cannot produce documented progress monitoring data for a student's IEP goal during a due process hearing — because no systematic data collection was occurring — faces a finding of failure to implement the IEP, potentially triggering compensatory education awards and placement changes. Build the IEP goal tracker with a data collection completion alert: any IEP goal that has not had a data point recorded within 10 days past the expected monitoring interval generates an alert to the supervising teacher and the school's IDEA compliance coordinator.
**Growth path:** Side project → IEP goal tracker and progress report generator for 10 target states, service minute log deployed, first 5-8 school retainers through NAPSEC outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 8-10 school retainers, school district SPED director referral relationships established (~$4-7K/mo). ### 2. Workers' Compensation TPA State License & Self-Insured Claims Reserve Compliance
**Problem:** Third-party administrators (TPAs) that manage workers' compensation claims on behalf of self-insured employers must maintain state TPA licenses in every state where they administer claims — most states require a separate TPA license for claims administration, with annual renewal, a qualifying individual (claims administrator) designation requiring continuing education, and in some states a surety bond or financial statement submission. TPAs administering workers' compensation claims for self-insured employers must also: file first reports of injury (FROI) with the state workers' compensation board within state-mandated timeframes (typically 7-14 days of the employer learning of an injury), file subsequent reports as the claim status changes, maintain claims reserves (documented estimates of total claim costs) that accurately reflect current claim value — which state workers' comp regulators review for adequacy, submit periodic statistical reports to state workers' comp statistical reporting organizations, and coordinate excess insurance reporting for claims approaching the self-insured employer's excess insurance attachment point. Small TPAs (managing 500-5,000 claims across 3-15 states) have the same multi-state TPA license and reporting obligations as large national TPAs but with limited compliance staffing.
**What to build:** A workers' compensation TPA compliance management service — a multi-state TPA license portfolio manager (tracking each state's TPA license or registration: license number, renewal deadline, qualifying individual designation, required CE for the qualifying individual, surety bond or financial statement submission requirements, and any state-specific license conditions — with tiered renewal alerts), a FROI and subsequent report filing tracker (for each claim: the injury date, employer notice date, FROI filing deadline by state, FROI submission confirmation, and any subsequent reporting triggers — return to work, claim closure, change in disability status — with the applicable state reporting deadline for each trigger event), a claims reserve adequacy review calendar (tracking each open claim's current reserve amount, the last reserve review date, and the next scheduled reserve review — with alerts for claims approaching reserve review intervals or experiencing claim developments that typically trigger reserve increases), an excess insurance reporting tracker (for each self-insured employer: the excess insurance attachment point, claims approaching the attachment point threshold, required excess carrier notice documentation, and the notice confirmation record), and a state workers' comp statistical reporting compliance calendar (tracking each state's statistical reporting filing deadlines, the data elements required by state, and submission confirmation records).
**Skill fit:** State TPA licensing requirements, FROI filing deadlines, and workers' comp statistical reporting requirements are publicly available from each state's workers' compensation regulatory agency — building a state-by-state compliance database is a regulatory research problem. The TPA license portfolio manager (a per-state license database with renewal calendar), FROI filing tracker (a per-claim reporting deadline tracker), claims reserve review calendar, excess insurance tracker, and state statistical reporting calendar are data management and compliance calendar problems the builder's engineering skills address directly.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a multi-state TPA license portfolio manager with renewal calendar for 20 high-TPA-activity states (CA, TX, FL, NY, PA, IL, OH, GA, NJ, NC, WA, CO, MI, MN, MA, IN, VA, WI, AZ, TN), a FROI filing deadline tracker with state-specific deadlines, a claims reserve review calendar, and an excess insurance threshold tracker; first 5-8 TPA clients through National Society of Accounts Payable (NSAR) - wait, through the Society of Professional Benefit Administrators (SPBA) and ARAWC (Association of Risk and Insurance Managers of Corporations) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through Society of Professional Benefit Administrators (SPBA) and NAAIA (National African American Insurance Association) workers' comp TPA practice group networks, self-insured employer association outreach (SIIA — Self-Insurance Institute of America — whose members are the self-insured employers that hire TPAs, and who frequently refer compliance management services to their TPA vendors), and workers' compensation defense law firm referral networks (defense attorneys who handle self-insured employer claims encounters TPA reporting compliance gaps in every multi-state case and refer systematic tracking services to their TPA clients).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per TPA: $700-2,000/mo (depending on state count and claim volume — a 3-state TPA managing 800 claims: $700/mo; a 12-state TPA managing 3,500 claims with complex excess insurance reporting: $2,000/mo). Managing 10-15 TPA retainers at $1,100/mo average = **$11,000-16,500/mo**. Multi-state TPA license application packages (one-time $500-1,500 per state for initial licensing applications when a TPA expands into a new state) generate significant supplemental revenue during TPA geographic expansion. Reaching 10-15 TPA retainers requires 3-4 SPBA and SIIA member relationships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new workers' comp claim generates a FROI deadline event. Open claims generate continuous reserve review obligations. TPA licenses renew annually across all licensed states simultaneously. Excess insurance reporting triggers occur as high-cost claims develop — generating urgent compliance events throughout the year. State workers' comp statistical reporting creates annual filing obligations that the TPA cannot defer.
**Biggest risk:** A FROI that is filed late — because the claim entered the TPA's system after a reporting deadline had already passed and no alert was generated — can result in a state workers' comp board fine for the self-insured employer. Build the FROI filing tracker with a same-day alert when a new claim is entered: the system calculates the FROI deadline based on the state, the injury date, and the employer notice date, and generates an immediate alert if the deadline is within 48 hours of claim entry.

### 3. Home Health Aide Medicaid Enrollment, Electronic Visit Verification & State Aide Registry Compliance
**Problem:** Home health agencies that provide Medicaid-reimbursed personal care and home health aide services must comply with three distinct regulatory obligations that interact at every visit: Medicaid provider enrollment (agency-level enrollment with the state Medicaid agency, and in some states individual aide enrollment or registration for each aide providing Medicaid-reimbursed services — requiring license or certification verification and periodic re-credentialing), state home health aide training and certification requirements (federal CMS Conditions of Participation require at least 75 hours of training for HHAs serving Medicare/Medicaid patients, and state agencies maintain aide registries that must list every certified aide — aides who are not listed on the state registry cannot legally provide Medicaid home health aide services), and Electronic Visit Verification (EVV — federally mandated under the 21st Century CURES Act for all Medicaid personal care and home health aide services, requiring an EVV system that captures aide identity, visit date, service type, location, and start/end times for every visit). Small home health agencies (serving 50-300 Medicaid clients) must manage aide certification currency, Medicaid enrollment currency for each aide, and EVV data completeness simultaneously — with Medicaid auditors increasingly targeting EVV data gaps (incomplete visit records) and enrollment gaps (visits by aides who were not actively enrolled at the time of service) for recoupment actions.
**What to build:** A home health aide Medicaid compliance management service — an aide enrollment and certification tracking system (for each aide: state Medicaid enrollment status and expiration, state aide registry listing status, training completion documentation — initial 75-hour training certification and any state-required annual in-service hours — and criminal background check currency, with alerts 90/60/30 days before any aide's certification or enrollment expires), a Medicaid EVV compliance monitor (tracking EVV data completeness for each visit: whether the required data elements — aide identity, location, service type, start and end time — were captured by the EVV system, flagging visits with missing or incomplete EVV records that must be corrected before Medicaid billing, and generating a daily EVV gap report showing every visit from the prior day that has an EVV deficiency), a Medicaid claim cross-reference system (cross-referencing each submitted Medicaid claim against the EVV record for the corresponding visit — confirming that a complete EVV record exists before the claim is submitted, and flagging claims that lack a matching EVV record for correction before submission), a state Medicaid enrollment renewal calendar (tracking the agency's state Medicaid provider enrollment renewal deadline, any periodic re-credentialing requirements, and aide enrollment renewal events by state — with alerts before each renewal deadline), and an aide training and in-service compliance calendar (tracking each aide's annual in-service training completion requirements, the training provider, completion dates, and any state-specific competency demonstration requirements).
**Skill fit:** CMS Medicaid EVV requirements, state home health aide training and registry requirements, and Medicaid enrollment requirements are publicly available from CMS and state Medicaid agencies — building a state-by-state compliance database is a regulatory research problem. The aide enrollment tracking system (a per-aide credential database with expiration monitoring), EVV compliance monitor (a per-visit data completeness check), Medicaid claim cross-reference, enrollment renewal calendar, and aide training calendar are data management and compliance monitoring problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. CMS's EVV policy guidance and state EVV system specifications are publicly available for building the EVV data completeness check logic.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build an aide certification and Medicaid enrollment tracking system with expiration alerts, an EVV data completeness monitor with daily gap reporting, a Medicaid claim cross-reference system, and a state Medicaid enrollment renewal calendar for 15 high-Medicaid-home-care-volume states (CA, TX, FL, NY, PA, IL, OH, NC, GA, MI, NJ, VA, WA, MA, AZ); first 5-8 home health agency clients through National Association for Home Care & Hospice (NAHC) member outreach while the aide enrollment tracking system is validated against real agency aide roster and EVV data.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through NAHC (National Association for Home Care & Hospice) member network and state home care association outreach (the concentrated professional communities for home health agency administrators — NAHC's annual conference is the primary industry event), home health software vendor referral networks (platforms like ClearCare, AlayaCare, and WellSky whose agency customers struggle with EVV compliance and Medicaid enrollment tracking), and Medicaid managed care organization (MCO) audit response referral networks (MCOs that audit their home health provider networks for EVV compliance gaps refer compliance management services to agencies with deficiencies).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per home health agency: $400-900/mo (depending on aide count and state complexity — a 40-aide agency in a standard EVV state: $400/mo; a 120-aide agency in a state with individual aide Medicaid enrollment and complex EVV data integration requirements: $900/mo). Managing 20-30 agency retainers at $600/mo average = **$12,000-18,000/mo**. Medicaid EVV audit response documentation packages (one-time $500-1,500 when an agency receives an MCO or state audit notice targeting EVV compliance) generate significant supplemental revenue. Reaching 20-30 agency retainers requires 3-4 NAHC chapter relationships and 1-2 MCO audit response referral partnerships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** EVV data must be verified for every single Medicaid visit — a 100-aide agency providing 1,000 visits per week generates 1,000 weekly EVV compliance verification events continuously. Aide certifications and Medicaid enrollments expire throughout the year on different dates for each aide, creating continuous certification monitoring obligations. Medicaid EVV enforcement is actively increasing — state Medicaid agencies are implementing automated EVV reconciliation that flags claims with missing visit verification data, creating consistent urgency for the compliance monitoring product.
**Biggest risk:** A home health agency that bills Medicaid for visits that lack a complete EVV record — because the EVV data gap was not caught before billing — faces Medicaid overpayment recoupment claims that can total hundreds of thousands of dollars in a single audit cycle. ### 4. Charter School Annual Authorizer Reporting & State Academic/Financial Accountability Compliance
**Problem:** Charter schools operate under a unique accountability structure where they must simultaneously satisfy: annual reporting requirements to their authorizing entity (the entity — school district, state charter board, university, or non-profit authorizer — that approved their charter and holds authority to revoke it), state academic accountability requirements (state education department performance standards that charters must meet to maintain authorization — student proficiency rates, graduation rates, attendance rates, and in many states, specific metrics in the charter's own performance framework), and state financial accountability requirements (annual independent audits submitted to the state education department and authorizer, financial ratios that must meet state-mandated thresholds for liquidity and fund balance, and in some states, quarterly financial reporting to the authorizer). Annual authorizer reports typically require a comprehensive data package: student enrollment and demographic data, attendance data, academic outcome data aligned to the charter's performance framework goals, financial summary, key staff changes, facility compliance documentation, and any required narrative on performance against charter goals. Small charter schools (100-400 students) generate the same annual report obligations as large charter networks but with no dedicated compliance or data analytics staff — the principal or business manager assembles the annual report manually from multiple data systems in the weeks before the authorizer's deadline.
**What to build:** A charter school authorizer reporting and state accountability compliance management service — an annual authorizer report data assembly system (maintaining the school's annual reporting data by category throughout the year: student enrollment and demographic counts, monthly attendance rates by grade, academic outcome data from state assessment results, financial summary from the school's accounting system, and key staff change log — enabling the annual report to be assembled from pre-collected data rather than assembled from scratch in the days before the deadline), a charter performance framework compliance tracker (for each goal in the school's charter performance framework: the performance target for the current year, the current year's actual performance data, and a gap analysis showing whether the school is on track to meet each goal — with an alert when a goal falls below threshold in the mid-year data, providing time for intervention before the annual report is due), a state academic accountability compliance calendar (tracking the state's proficiency rate, graduation rate, and other accountability metric thresholds that the charter must meet, the state's annual testing and reporting schedule, and the school improvement plan submission deadline if the school misses a state threshold), a financial accountability compliance dashboard (tracking the annual audit submission deadline, audit findings and management response documentation, the school's current financial ratios compared against state thresholds, and any quarterly financial report submission deadlines for the authorizer), and an authorizer relationship management log (maintaining a record of all formal authorizer communications — annual report submissions, authorizer site visit scheduling and outcomes, any compliance notices or corrective action plans — providing a complete accountability audit trail).
**Skill fit:** State charter school accountability requirements and authorizer annual reporting formats are publicly available from each state's charter school authorizing agency and state education department — building a state-by-state accountability data framework is a regulatory research problem. The annual report data assembly system (a pre-collected data warehouse organized by report section), performance framework tracker (a per-goal performance monitoring dashboard), state accountability calendar, financial ratio dashboard, and authorizer communication log are data management and compliance calendar problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. National Alliance for Public Charter Schools publishes state-by-state charter authorizing and accountability frameworks that provide a starting database.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build an annual authorizer report data assembly system with section-by-section data collection, a charter performance framework goal tracker with gap analysis, a state academic accountability calendar for 15 high-charter-school-density states (CA, TX, FL, OH, AZ, NC, CO, MI, NY, GA, PA, IN, NV, MN, WA), and a financial ratio compliance dashboard; first 5-8 charter school clients through National Alliance for Public Charter Schools member outreach and state charter school association networks while the report assembly system is validated against real authorizer report formats.

Annual authorizer report preparation packages (one-time $500-1,200 per school for the annual report data assembly and formatting in the year of renewal or comprehensive review) generate significant supplemental revenue. ### 5. Physical & Occupational Therapy Medicare Therapy Cap Documentation & Functional Outcome Reporting
**Problem:** Physical therapy and occupational therapy private practices that bill Medicare for outpatient therapy services must comply with Medicare's therapy billing requirements that impose significant documentation obligations beyond standard clinical note-keeping. The Medicare therapy cap ($2,330 per beneficiary per year for PT and SLP combined, and $2,330 for OT separately in 2026) can be exceeded for medically necessary services only when the treating therapist appends the KX modifier to each claim — and the KX modifier requires that the claim be supported by documentation confirming that services are medically necessary and reasonable, that progress toward functional goals is occurring, and that the complexity of the patient's condition justifies the exception. Additionally, CMS requires functional limitation G-code reporting at evaluation, every 10 treatment visits, and at discharge — reporting the patient's current functional limitation severity and projected functional limitation at discharge using the G-code and severity modifier system. Small PT/OT practices (1-5 therapists, 100-500 Medicare beneficiaries per year) frequently fail to track: which Medicare patients are approaching or exceeding the therapy cap threshold, whether KX modifier documentation requirements are being met for every claim above the cap, and whether functional limitation G-code reporting has been completed at every required reporting interval. CMS therapy cap audits and targeted probe-and-educate reviews are a consistent enforcement mechanism that generates overpayment demand letters for practices with systematic KX modifier documentation deficiencies.
**What to build:** A PT/OT Medicare therapy cap and functional limitation compliance management service — a per-patient Medicare therapy cap tracker (for each Medicare beneficiary: the cumulative annual PT/SLP and OT claim amounts billed to Medicare, the remaining cap headroom before the $2,330 threshold, and an alert when a patient's cumulative billing is approaching the cap threshold — so the practice knows in advance when KX modifier documentation requirements will begin), a KX modifier documentation compliance monitor (for each claim that includes the KX modifier: confirming that the corresponding clinical documentation includes the required elements — confirmation of medical necessity, documentation of continued progress toward functional goals, and documentation of the patient's functional status — and flagging any KX modifier claims where the documentation file doesn't yet contain a documented progress note from the current treatment episode), a functional limitation G-code reporting calendar (for each Medicare patient: tracking the evaluation G-code reporting date, the subsequent required reporting interval — every 10 treatment visits — and the discharge G-code reporting requirement — with alerts before each reporting interval so the therapist knows when G-code reporting is due before the next claim is submitted), a Medicare claim to documentation cross-reference system (cross-referencing each submitted Medicare claim against the required supporting documentation elements — confirming that the claim's service date has a corresponding plan of care, progress note, or re-evaluation as required by the claim type and the patient's stage in the treatment episode), and a therapy cap exception justification documentation library (a library of reusable clinical justification language for common Medicare patient conditions — organized by diagnosis and functional limitation — that therapists can adapt when documenting medical necessity for KX modifier claims, reducing the time required to produce compliant documentation).
**Skill fit:** CMS Medicare therapy billing requirements — the therapy cap thresholds, KX modifier documentation standards, and functional limitation G-code reporting requirements — are published in CMS's Medicare Claims Processing Manual and annual MPFS (Medicare Physician Fee Schedule) updates. Building the per-patient cap tracker (a running annual billing total per patient with cap threshold alerts), KX modifier documentation monitor (a per-claim documentation completeness check), G-code reporting calendar (a per-patient visit count tracker with reporting interval alerts), and claim-to-documentation cross-reference are data management and compliance monitoring problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. American Physical Therapy Association (APTA) and AOTA member directories provide defined buyer communities.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a per-patient Medicare therapy cap tracker with KX modifier threshold alerts, a G-code reporting calendar with 10-visit interval tracking, a KX modifier documentation completeness monitor, and a claim-to-documentation cross-reference for the most common PT/OT claim types; first 5-8 PT/OT practice clients through APTA PPN (Private Practice Section) and AOTA practice networks while the cap tracker and G-code calendar are validated against real practice billing data.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through APTA Private Practice Section (PPS) member network and AOTA OT practice community outreach (the concentrated professional communities for PT/OT practice owners), PT/OT billing company referral networks (billing companies that handle Medicare claims for therapy practices encounter KX modifier documentation deficiencies and G-code reporting gaps at every audit and actively refer compliance monitoring services to their practice clients), and Medicare therapy RAC (Recovery Audit Contractor) audit defense firm referral networks (attorneys and consultants who defend PT/OT practices in RAC audits encounter systematic KX modifier documentation failures that a compliance monitoring tool would have prevented, and refer the tool to practices in their network that have not yet been audited).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per PT/OT practice: $250-600/mo (depending on Medicare patient volume and therapist count — a 2-therapist practice with 80 Medicare patients per year: $250/mo; a 5-therapist practice with 300 Medicare patients per year and high KX modifier billing volume: $600/mo). Managing 30-50 practice retainers at $375/mo average = **$11,250-18,750/mo**. Medicare RAC audit response documentation packages (one-time $500-1,500 per practice when an audit demand letter arrives) generate significant supplemental revenue. Reaching 30-50 practice retainers requires 4-6 APTA PPS state chapter relationships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every Medicare claim above the therapy cap threshold generates a KX modifier documentation compliance event. Every 10 Medicare treatment visits generates a functional limitation G-code reporting event. Therapy cap amounts adjust annually, creating a January 1 reset event for every Medicare patient simultaneously — the beginning of a new cap year is when cap tracker products see the highest adoption urgency.
