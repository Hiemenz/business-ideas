- **Named distribution channel** for the first 10 customers *and* a credible path to the next 10x.

- **No repeats.** Nothing below overlaps any concept across Sessions 1-25, `side-income-ideas/`, or `discovered-problems/`. ### 1. Physician Practice Insurance Prior Authorization Management
**Problem:** Independent physician practices — primary care, orthopedic, cardiology, gastroenterology, and specialty practices — spend an estimated 2-3 hours per physician per day on prior authorization (PA) submissions, follow-up calls, peer-to-peer review requests, and appeal letters required by commercial health plans and Medicare Advantage before they can provide covered services or prescribe specialty medications. A single PA denial, if not appealed within the insurer's response window (typically 30-60 days), permanently forfeits the claim for that service. Most independent practices (3-15 physicians) handle PA with a medical assistant or billing coordinator who manages the process manually — checking each payer's separate PA portal, submitting clinical documentation in the payer's required format, calling payer PA hotlines for status updates, and drafting peer-to-peer review requests when the PA is initially denied. The PA workload has increased 22% per year since 2020 as health plans expand prior authorization requirements to new procedure codes and medication classes, making manual management increasingly untenable without adding headcount.
**What to build:** A prior authorization management service — a PA intake and tracking system (collecting the PA request from the practice's scheduling or EHR workflow: procedure code, diagnosis code, patient insurance information, clinical documentation required, and submission deadline relative to the service date), a payer-specific submission workflow (building and maintaining the submission templates for each major commercial payer's PA portal — Availity, NaviNet, and individual payer portals — pre-filling patient demographic and diagnosis fields from the practice's PA intake data), a PA status monitoring dashboard (tracking each open PA's status across all active payers: submitted, pending, approved, denied, additional information requested, and peer-to-peer review scheduled), a denial management and appeal workflow (identifying PA denials that are clinically inappropriate or procedurally deficient, generating appeal letter drafts with clinical justification language specific to the denial reason code and the applicable payer's appeal process), and a PA outcomes analytics dashboard (tracking approval rates by payer, by procedure code, and by physician — identifying patterns where specific payers or procedure codes have systematic denial rates that warrant payer advocacy or coding review).
**Skill fit:** The payer portal submission templates (each major payer's PA portal accepts structured data with defined field sets; building a practice-specific submission template that pre-fills payer-required fields from the practice's PA intake data is a forms automation problem), the PA status monitoring dashboard (tracking PA outcomes across multiple payer portals with a unified status layer), and the denial pattern analytics (identifying denial rate patterns by payer and procedure code from structured PA outcome data) are data management and workflow automation problems the builder can systematize. CAQH ProView and Availity publish API documentation for PA submission integrations for practices above minimum volume thresholds.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a PA intake form covering the required fields for the 5-6 most common commercial payers in the target market, a PA status tracking dashboard with payer and procedure code fields, a denial letter template library covering the most common denial reason codes (medical necessity not established, service not covered, alternative treatment required), and a PA outcomes analytics report; first 3-5 practice clients through medical billing company referrals and physician practice management associations while the submission templates are validated against real payer portal acceptance rates.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through medical billing companies (who handle claims submission for independent practices and actively refer PA management to specialist services because PA management falls outside their billing scope but affects the claims they submit), MGMA (Medical Group Management Association) member communities (the primary professional association for physician practice managers — PA burden is one of the most consistently discussed operational problems in this community), and specialty physician association administrative communities (orthopedic practice managers and gastroenterology practice administrators are particularly active in seeking PA management solutions because their procedure mix has among the highest PA requirement rates).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per practice: $600-1,200/mo (depending on physician count and PA volume — a 3-physician primary care practice with moderate PA volume: $600/mo; a 10-physician orthopedic practice with high PA volume for surgical procedures: $1,200/mo). Managing 12-18 practice retainers at $850/mo average = **$10,200-15,300/mo**. PA volume grows with practice growth — a practice adding a physician or expanding to a new procedure category increases their PA burden and drives upsells to higher retainer tiers. Reaching 12-18 practices requires 4-6 active medical billing company referral relationships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Prior authorization requirements are expanding every year — health plans add new procedure codes requiring PA in every annual coverage update cycle, making the PA workload grow regardless of whether the practice adds patients. Practices that outsource PA management don't bring it back in-house; adding internal headcount costs $50,000-70,000/year in salary and benefits for a dedicated PA coordinator, making the service's $600-1,200/mo retainer a substantial cost saving. The PA outcome analytics dashboard creates a measurable service deliverable — approval rate improvements and denial appeal win rates — that make client renewal decisions straightforward.
**Biggest risk:** A PA that is submitted incorrectly — wrong procedure code, incomplete clinical documentation, or missed submission deadline — that results in a claim denial creates direct revenue impact for the practice and potential service liability. Build a PA submission review checklist that cross-references each submission against the payer's specific PA requirements before submitting, and track submission confirmation numbers for every PA filed so there is a documented audit trail of every submission attempt. Position the service as the PA submission and tracking layer; the practice's clinical staff remains responsible for providing the clinical documentation that supports the PA request.
**Growth path:** Side project → PA submission templates built for 4-5 major commercial payers, PA status tracking dashboard deployed, denial letter template library covering the 8-10 most common denial reason codes, first 3-5 practice retainers through medical billing company referrals. Replaces-part-time-income → 6-8 practice retainers, MGMA community presence established, PA outcome analytics dashboard generating visible approval rate improvements for clients (~$5-8K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 12-18 practice retainers, specialty practice client tier (orthopedic, GI) generating higher-volume retainers, payer advocacy services for practices with systematic denial patterns at premium hourly rate, pushing past $10-15K/mo.

### 2. Minority/Women/Veteran Business Enterprise Certification Application & Renewal Management
**Problem:** Businesses owned by minorities (MBE), women (WBE), veterans (VOSB), service-disabled veterans (SDVOSB), or disadvantaged individuals (DBE for transportation projects) can access corporate and government supplier diversity set-asides by obtaining certification from the relevant certifying body — NMSDC for MBE, WBENC for WBE, SBA for VOSB/SDVOSB, and state DOT agencies for DBE. Each certification requires a detailed application: ownership documentation (stock certificates, operating agreements, buy-sell agreements), owner biography and resume, personal financial statements, site visit scheduling, interview preparation, and evidence that the certified owner exercises day-to-day management control. Applications typically take 3-6 months and are frequently rejected or returned for additional documentation because applicants don't know the specific evidentiary standards the certifying body applies to ownership control determinations. Annual renewals require updated documentation proving continued ownership and control. A certification management service handles the full application from document gathering through site visit, then manages annual renewal.
**What to build:** A business enterprise certification management service — a certification eligibility assessment tool (determining which certifications the business qualifies for based on ownership structure, owner demographics, and business revenue — NMSDC MBE has a $1B annual revenue cap; WBENC WBE requires 51% women ownership and control; SBA VOSB requires veteran ownership and day-to-day management), a certification application document assembly workflow (collecting ownership documentation, operating agreements, financial statements, owner biographies, and business narrative; building the application package to the specific certifying body's evidentiary standards), a site visit preparation guide (coaching the certified owner on the specific questions certifying body site visitors ask about day-to-day management, hiring decisions, and financial authority — the most common reason certification is denied even when ownership meets the numerical threshold), a certification renewal calendar and documentation management system (tracking each certification's annual renewal deadline, the updated documentation required, and any ownership or management changes that must be reported between renewals), and a multi-certification coordination workflow (managing simultaneous applications to NMSDC, WBENC, SBA, and state DBE programs — each with different documentation standards — in parallel to minimize the total time to certified status).
**Skill fit:** The certification eligibility decision tree (each certifying body publishes explicit ownership percentage, control, and revenue eligibility standards; building a structured eligibility assessment from these published standards is a regulatory criteria mapping problem), the application document checklist (each certifying body publishes its required document list; building a structured document collection workflow is a compliance document management problem), the renewal deadline calendar, and the multi-certification coordination workflow are data management and document assembly problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. No law license or government contractor license is required to provide certification application consulting.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a certification eligibility assessment tool covering NMSDC, WBENC, SBA VOSB/SDVOSB, and the top 5 state DOT DBE programs by volume, a certification application document checklist and assembly workflow for each certification type, a site visit preparation question bank based on published certifying body standards, and a renewal deadline calendar; first 5-8 business owner clients through NMSDC and WBENC local chapter outreach and supplier diversity program coordinator referrals while the application workflow is validated against real certification application outcomes.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through NMSDC regional affiliate councils and WBENC regional partner organizations (the certifying bodies themselves actively connect applicants with application support resources — being listed as a recommended resource with any regional affiliate generates consistent inbound applicants), corporate supplier diversity program offices (large corporate buyers with supplier diversity goals actively help their target suppliers obtain certification and can refer the service to suppliers in their pipeline), and SBA Women's Business Centers and Veteran Business Outreach Centers (federally funded centers that specifically support women-owned and veteran-owned businesses and actively refer certification assistance services).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Per-engagement application fee: $800-2,000 per certification application (depending on the number of certifications sought simultaneously and ownership complexity — a straightforward single WBENC application is $800; a simultaneous NMSDC + WBENC + state DBE application with complex ownership structure is $2,000). Annual renewal management retainer: $200-400/mo per certified business (lower than the application fee because renewal documentation is a subset of the original application). Processing 6-8 new certification applications per month at $1,200 average = **$7,200-9,600/mo**, plus growing renewal retainer base as certified clients compound (50 certified clients × $300/mo = $15,000/mo renewal retainer at scale). Early stage: $8,000-10,000/mo from a mix of new applications and renewal retainers.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every certified business renews annually — each certification application completed becomes a permanent annual renewal engagement, compounding the retainer base over time without any additional client acquisition. Corporate supplier diversity mandates are growing as ESG reporting expands, increasing both the number of businesses seeking certification and the urgency of maintaining current certification status. Certifications expire if renewal is missed, creating an acute urgency event that drives quick conversion from certified business owners who have let their certification lapse.
**Biggest risk:** A certification application that is denied — because the certifying body determines that ownership control doesn't meet their standard despite the owner's numeric ownership percentage — creates client disappointment and refund risk. Build a pre-engagement eligibility screening that reviews ownership documents (operating agreement, stock certificates) before accepting the engagement, and include a clear disclosure that certification denial is possible if the certifying body determines that operational control is not sufficiently concentrated in the certified owner. Offer a money-back partial refund policy for denials resulting from eligibility issues identified in the screening that the client chose to proceed with.
**Growth path:** Side project → certification eligibility assessment built for NMSDC, WBENC, and SBA VOSB/SDVOSB, application document workflow validated against real certification application outcomes, first 5-8 new application engagements through NMSDC/WBENC chapter outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 4-6 new application engagements per month, renewal retainer base growing from initial certified clients, corporate supplier diversity referral channel established (~$4-7K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 6-8 new applications/month, 40-50 certified businesses on annual renewal retainers, SBA resource partner channel producing consistent applicant referrals, pushing past $10-15K/mo.

### 3. Home Health Agency Medicaid Cost Report Preparation & Submission
**Problem:** Home health agencies that provide Medicaid-covered services — skilled nursing visits, therapy services, personal care aide hours — must file annual Medicaid cost reports with their state Medicaid agency (or CMS for Medicare home health agencies filing HCFA-1728A). These cost reports allocate the agency's total costs across service lines, calculate cost-per-visit for each service category, and determine whether the agency is operating above or below their Medicaid reimbursement rate — with settlement adjustments for over- or under-reimbursed costs. The cost report preparation requires: extracting visit counts and service hours by service type from the agency's billing system, allocating overhead and administrative costs across service lines using allowable allocation methodologies, reconciling the cost report trial balance against the agency's audited financial statements, and submitting the completed cost report to the state Medicaid agency's fiscal intermediary within the filing deadline (typically 5 months after fiscal year end). Most small and mid-size home health agencies (under $10M annual Medicaid revenue) don't have a CFO or controller with cost report expertise — they rely on their outside CPA to prepare the cost report, but CPA firms with Medicaid cost report experience are scarce and expensive, charging $8,000-15,000+ for a single cost report preparation.
**What to build:** A home health agency Medicaid cost report preparation service — a cost report data intake and reconciliation workflow (collecting the agency's trial balance, visit log by service type, and payroll allocation data; reconciling cost report revenue and expense line items against the agency's audited financials; and mapping each general ledger account to the appropriate cost report worksheet line), a cost allocation methodology tool (applying the allowable overhead allocation bases — square footage for facility costs, FTE count for administrative costs, visit-weighted allocation for service line costs — and documenting the methodology for state fiscal intermediary review), a cost report worksheet preparation system (populating the applicable state cost report form or CMS HCFA-1728A with the reconciled and allocated data), a settlement projection calculator (estimating the expected cost report settlement amount — the difference between the agency's interim Medicaid payments and the cost-report-determined cost per visit — before submission so the agency's CFO can prepare for a potential settlement liability or receivable), and a filing deadline management and submission coordination tool (tracking each state's cost report filing deadline, extension availability, and submission method for each state where the agency holds a Medicaid provider agreement).
**Skill fit:** The cost report data reconciliation (mapping trial balance GL accounts to cost report worksheet line items is a chart-of-accounts mapping problem), the overhead allocation methodology (applying standardized allocation bases defined in CMS's Provider Reimbursement Manual or state-specific Medicaid billing manuals), the settlement projection calculator (applying the state's reimbursement rate methodology to the submitted cost data to calculate the expected settlement), and the filing deadline calendar are data engineering and financial modeling problems the builder's analytical skills address directly. CMS publishes the HCFA-1728A form instructions and cost allocation guidance publicly; most states publish their home health cost report forms and instructions through their Medicaid agency website.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a cost report data intake questionnaire covering the most common home health service categories (skilled nursing, physical therapy, occupational therapy, speech therapy, personal care aide), an overhead cost allocation tool with the three most common allocation bases, a settlement projection model using the state's published per-visit rate methodology, and a filing deadline calendar for the 5-6 states with the largest home health Medicaid markets (CA, NY, TX, FL, PA, OH); first 3-5 home health agency clients through home health association outreach while the cost report preparation workflow is validated against state fiscal intermediary acceptance.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through state home health associations (every state with significant Medicaid home health volume has a state association — CAHSAH in California, HANY in New York — whose member directories are directly accessible), home health billing company partner networks (billing companies that process home health claims have direct relationships with agency administrators and frequently refer cost report preparation services as a complement to their billing work), and healthcare CPA firm referral networks (CPA firms that audit home health agencies but lack cost report expertise refer preparation services rather than develop the expertise internally).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Per-engagement cost report preparation fee: $2,500-5,000 per agency per annual cost report (significantly below the $8,000-15,000+ CPA firm rate, positioning as a specialized cost-report-only service rather than full audit services). Monthly monitoring retainer between annual cost reports: $300-500/mo per agency (tracking interim payment rates, managing audit correspondence, and updating the agency when cost allocation methodology guidance changes). Completing 3-4 cost report preparations per month (cost reports concentrate in Q2-Q3 for calendar-year agencies) at $3,500 average = **$10,500-14,000/mo** during peak filing season, plus $300-500/mo monitoring retainers on 15-20 agency accounts generating $4,500-10,000/mo in the non-peak months.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Home health agencies file cost reports every year indefinitely — each agency engaged is a permanent annual engagement that compounds as new agencies are added. Cost report settlement determinations directly affect the agency's Medicaid reimbursement rate for subsequent periods, making accurate preparation a financial imperative rather than a compliance checkbox. The scarcity of cost report expertise (most CPAs don't specialize in Medicaid cost reports) means the service faces minimal direct competition in most regional markets.
**Biggest risk:** A cost report prepared with incorrect overhead allocation methodology or non-allowable cost inclusions — discovered during a state fiscal intermediary desk review or field audit — can result in cost report settlement adjustments and repayment demands against the agency. Build the cost allocation methodology documentation to reference the specific CMS Provider Reimbursement Manual section or state Medicaid billing manual citation that authorizes each allocation decision, creating an audit trail that demonstrates compliance with the applicable cost allocation standards at the time of filing.
**Growth path:** Side project → cost report data intake workflow and overhead allocation tool built, settlement projection model validated for 2-3 target state Medicaid programs, first 3-5 annual cost report engagements through state home health association outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 1-2 cost reports per month, monitoring retainer base growing from completed cost report clients, billing company referral channel established (~$4-8K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 3-4 cost reports per month at peak season, 15-20 monitoring retainers generating consistent inter-season revenue, 5-6 state cost report methodologies in the service's validated library, pushing past $10-14K/mo.

### 4. Commercial Insurance Renewal Preparation & Carrier Marketing Submission Coordination for Independent Agencies
**Problem:** Independent commercial insurance agencies renewing accounts — general liability, commercial property, business auto, workers' compensation, and professional liability — must gather updated application information from their insured, prepare marketing submissions for multiple carriers, track quote responses, compare coverage options, and bind the best quote before the renewal date. For accounts renewing with their current carrier, this means completing the carrier's renewal application with updated exposures; for accounts being re-marketed to multiple carriers (because of claims history, coverage gaps, or pricing), it means building carrier-specific submission packages for 4-8 markets simultaneously and tracking each market's quote status, declination, or additional information request. Most independent agencies handle this workflow through a combination of producer notebooks, agency management system (AMS) notes, and email — a process that misses re-marketing deadlines, loses track of which markets have responded, and creates last-minute bind pressure when a quote is received 3 days before renewal. A renewal preparation and marketing coordination service systematizes this workflow as a back-office support service for agency producers.
**What to build:** A commercial insurance renewal preparation and carrier marketing service — a renewal exposure data gathering workflow (structured application questionnaires for each commercial lines product category — GL, property, auto, workers' comp, professional liability — that collect the updated exposure information carriers require: payroll, revenue, vehicle counts, property values, professional services descriptions, and prior claims history), a carrier appetite and submission workflow (matching each account's risk characteristics against the carrier appetite database for target markets — which carriers write which classes of business at which revenue sizes — and building carrier-specific submission packages in each carrier's required format), a marketing activity tracker (tracking each submission sent, each carrier's response — quote received, declined, more information needed — with follow-up triggers for submissions that haven't responded within the standard quote turnaround window), a quote comparison tool (normalizing carrier quotes across the key coverage comparison points: coverage limits, deductibles, exclusions, and premium — generating a coverage comparison matrix the producer can review with the insured), and a renewal binder and policy issuance tracker (tracking the bind confirmation, certificate of insurance issuance, and policy delivery after the account is placed).
**Skill fit:** The carrier appetite database (carriers publish appetite guides, underwriting guidelines, and eligible class codes through their agent portals; building a structured appetite matrix by carrier, product line, and risk class is a data management problem), the exposure data gathering questionnaire templates (each product line's application follows a predictable structure; building standardized questionnaires for the 5-6 most common commercial lines products is a forms design problem), the marketing activity tracker, and the quote comparison normalization tool are data management and workflow automation problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. Insurance agency management systems (Applied Epic, HawkSoft, EZLynx) expose export capabilities for account renewal data that the builder can use to pre-populate renewal questionnaires.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build renewal exposure questionnaires for the 4 most common commercial lines products (GL, commercial property, workers' comp, business auto), a carrier appetite matrix for the 10-15 most active E&S and admitted carriers in the builder's target market, a marketing activity tracker with quote response logging and follow-up triggers, and a quote comparison template; first 5-8 independent agency clients through insurance agency association outreach and agency management system user communities while the renewal workflow is validated against real account re-marketing timelines.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through independent insurance agent associations (PIA, IIABA state associations — the primary communities for independent commercial lines agents who handle their own marketing workflow), agency management system user groups (agents using Applied Epic or EZLynx are the most process-oriented segment of independent agents and most likely to adopt a systematic renewal workflow service), and wholesale broker partner programs (wholesale brokers who place E&S business for independent agents have direct relationships with the agents they serve and can refer marketing workflow services to their agency partners).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per agency: $500-1,000/mo (depending on the agency's commercial lines renewal volume — an agency renewing 15-25 commercial accounts per month: $500/mo; an agency renewing 40-60 commercial accounts per month: $1,000/mo). Managing 15-20 agency retainers at $700/mo average = **$10,500-14,000/mo**. Commercial lines agencies with consistent renewal volumes generate predictable monthly workloads, making the retainer model stable. Agencies that grow their commercial book drive automatic upsells to higher retainer tiers.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Commercial insurance policies renew annually — every account in an agency's book generates a renewal workflow engagement every 12 months, making the service's workload stable and predictable regardless of new business production. Agencies that outsource renewal coordination to the service free their producers to focus on new account prospecting, which grows the agency's book and the service's workload simultaneously. The carrier appetite database and quote comparison tools the builder develops become an institutional knowledge asset that is difficult to replicate quickly, creating a competitive barrier for new entrants in any agency's market.
**Biggest risk:** A renewal that is not marketed in time — because the carrier submission wasn't completed before the underwriting close date — resulting in the insured being uninsured at renewal creates significant E&O exposure for the agency. Build a renewal date monitoring system with automatic alerts at 120/90/60/30 days before each renewal date, and define a service-level agreement specifying the minimum lead time required to complete the marketing submission for each product type. Decline renewal coordination engagements where the timeline is too compressed to complete the carrier marketing process before the renewal date.
**Growth path:** Side project → renewal questionnaires built for 4 core commercial lines products, carrier appetite matrix validated for the target market, marketing activity tracker deployed, first 5-8 agency retainers through PIA and IIABA association outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 8-10 agency retainers, wholesale broker referral channel established, quote comparison tool deployed as a client-facing deliverable (~$4-7K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 15-20 agency retainers, carrier appetite matrix expanded to 25+ markets, agency management system integration reducing data entry friction for renewal data gathering, pushing past $10-14K/mo.

### 5. Funeral Home Pre-Need Trust Fund Regulatory Compliance & Annual State Reporting
**Problem:** Funeral homes that sell pre-arranged funeral plans (pre-need contracts) are required by state law to deposit a percentage of the contract price — typically 75-100% — into a state-regulated trust fund held for the benefit of the consumer until the funeral is performed. These pre-need trust funds are subject to state insurance department or banking department oversight: annual trust fund reporting (reporting total pre-need contracts in force, total trust assets, income earned, withdrawals, and contract cancellations to the state regulatory agency), trust investment compliance (most states restrict pre-need trust investments to approved asset classes and require documentation of investment decisions), consumer disclosure compliance (providing annual trust statements to pre-need contract holders showing their trust balance and accrued interest), and trust reconciliation (reconciling the trust account balance against the total of outstanding pre-need contract liabilities). Funeral homes that fail to maintain compliant pre-need trust programs — missing annual reports, investing trust assets in non-compliant securities, or failing to make required consumer disclosures — face license suspension and civil penalties. Most funeral homes (independent, family-owned operations) manage this compliance with the funeral home director who signed the pre-need license, using a paper ledger and their trust company's quarterly statement, with no systematic reconciliation or annual reporting workflow.
**What to build:** A funeral home pre-need trust compliance management service — a pre-need contract registry (cataloging every active pre-need contract: contract date, contracted services, contract price, trust deposit amount, trust account balance, accrued interest, and contract status — active, cancelled, or completed at need), an annual state regulatory report preparation workflow (preparing the annual pre-need trust report required by each state's insurance department or banking department: total contracts in force, total trust liability, total trust assets, income, withdrawals, and cancellations — in the required state-specific format), a trust investment compliance monitoring tool (tracking the trust account's investment allocation against the state's permitted investment list, alerting when any investment falls outside the approved asset class list, and documenting investment decisions for regulatory review), a consumer disclosure management system (generating and tracking delivery of the annual trust statement required by state law for each pre-need contract holder — showing their trust balance, accrued interest, and contract status), and a trust reconciliation tool (reconciling the total trust asset balance against the aggregate pre-need contract liability monthly, flagging any reconciliation gap that indicates a trust shortfall requiring corrective action).
**Skill fit:** The pre-need contract registry (structured data: contract terms, trust deposit amounts, accrued interest calculations), the annual state report preparation (each state's regulatory report follows a defined format with specific line items; building a data extraction and report generation workflow from the contract registry is a regulatory reporting problem), the trust investment compliance monitoring (each state publishes its approved investment list; building a portfolio comparison against the permitted list is a data matching problem), and the consumer disclosure generation are data management and document generation problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. State funeral regulatory agencies publish their pre-need trust reporting requirements and consumer disclosure standards publicly.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a pre-need contract registry database, an annual state regulatory report generator for 3-5 target states (TX, FL, CA, OH, PA — large funeral home markets with active pre-need regulatory programs), a consumer trust statement generator, and a trust reconciliation calculator; first 3-5 funeral home clients through state funeral directors association outreach while the annual report workflow is validated against state regulatory agency acceptance.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through state funeral directors associations (every state has an active funeral directors association with member directories — TFDA in Texas, FFDA in Florida — whose members include every licensed funeral home selling pre-need contracts), funeral home pre-need insurance company partner programs (companies like Forethought and NGL that underwrite pre-need insurance products have direct relationships with every funeral home that sells their products and can refer compliance management services), and funeral home management consultant networks (consultants who advise funeral homes on operational and financial management routinely encounter pre-need compliance gaps and can refer the service).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per funeral home: $200-400/mo (depending on pre-need contract volume — a small funeral home with 100 active pre-need contracts: $200/mo; a larger operation with 500+ active contracts and multi-location trust accounts: $400/mo). Annual state report preparation fee: $400-800 per funeral home (a one-time annual fee for each state regulatory report, charged on top of the monthly retainer). Managing 35-45 funeral home retainers at $290/mo average = **$10,150-13,050/mo**, plus annual report preparation fees generating a $14,000-36,000 annual revenue spike coinciding with state reporting deadlines. Reaching 35-45 funeral home clients requires established presence in 3-5 state funeral directors associations.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Pre-need compliance is a permanent license requirement for any funeral home selling pre-need contracts — ending the compliance management service would require the funeral home to either develop in-house expertise or find a replacement provider. The consumer trust statements are required annually for every active pre-need contract — the volume grows as the funeral home sells new contracts and doesn't shrink until those contracts are fulfilled (typically 15-25 years after sale), creating a compounding contract registry that grows every year. State regulatory scrutiny of pre-need trust programs has increased following several high-profile funeral home trust fund shortfall incidents, increasing the urgency of systematic compliance for funeral home operators.
**Biggest risk:** A trust reconciliation error that fails to detect a trust shortfall — where the trust's asset value is less than the aggregate pre-need contract liability — that is later discovered by a state regulatory examiner creates both regulatory exposure for the funeral home and liability for the compliance service. Build the monthly trust reconciliation as a triple-check process: trust statement balance from the trust company, aggregate contract liability from the contract registry, and an independent recalculation of accrued interest — requiring all three to agree before the monthly reconciliation is certified.
**Growth path:** Side project → pre-need contract registry built, annual state report generator validated for 2-3 target states, consumer trust statement generator deployed, first 3-5 funeral home retainers through state funeral directors association outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-15 funeral home retainers, pre-need insurance company partner referral channel established, first annual state report preparation fees earned (~$4-6K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 35-45 funeral home retainers across 4-5 states, annual state reporting fee spike creating predictable Q1 revenue surge, consumer disclosure generation service becoming a retention-driving operational feature, pushing past $10-13K/mo.
