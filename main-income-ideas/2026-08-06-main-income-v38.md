### 1. Private Membership Club State Liquor License & Gaming License Compliance
**Problem:** Private membership clubs — country clubs, yacht clubs, athletic clubs, fraternal organization clubs (Elks, Moose, Eagles, American Legion) — that serve alcohol to members hold a distinct state liquor license category that differs significantly from restaurant and bar licenses. Private club liquor licenses typically require: annual renewal with the state alcoholic beverage control (ABC) agency, maintenance of documented membership rosters (the license is conditioned on service only to bona fide members and their accompanied guests — not the general public), compliance with state regulations on guest privileges (most states limit how frequently a non-member can be served in a private club and require guest books or sign-in logs), and in states with licensed dining-room service, a separate meal service minimum requirement. Clubs that have charitable gaming operations — bingo nights, poker fundraisers, pull-tab/jar ticket sales — must maintain separate state gaming licenses for charitable gaming, file financial gaming reports with the state gaming regulatory agency, and comply with limits on permitted game types and session frequency. Small private clubs (200-800 members) process both liquor license renewal and charitable gaming reporting with a volunteer board that often has no professional administrative staff.
**What to build:** A private membership club state liquor and gaming license compliance management service — a state liquor license compliance calendar (tracking the club's ABC license renewal deadline, required annual membership roster submission if required by the state, guest log documentation requirements, and any state-mandated server training certification for club staff — with renewal alerts), a member and guest service documentation tracker (maintaining a member directory that confirms current membership status and renewal dates, and a guest log that records non-member service events with the required documentation — confirming that the club's service pattern meets the state's private club service restrictions), a charitable gaming license portfolio manager (tracking each gaming license or permit: license number, authorized games and sessions, annual renewal deadline, financial surety bond requirement, and state gaming agent designation), a charitable gaming financial reporting system (generating the state-required charitable gaming financial report: gross gaming receipts, gaming expenses, and net proceeds by game type and event — in the format required by the state gaming regulatory agency), and a gaming session log manager (recording each authorized gaming event: date, game type, number of participants, gross receipts, prizes paid, and net proceeds — providing the transaction-level documentation that state gaming regulators request during financial audits).
**Skill fit:** State ABC private club license requirements and charitable gaming regulatory requirements are publicly available from each state's ABC agency and gaming regulatory agency — building a state-by-state compliance database is a regulatory research problem. The member directory (a roster-based membership tracking system), guest log (a per-event guest documentation record), gaming license portfolio manager (a per-license renewal calendar), gaming financial report generator, and gaming session log are data management and document generation problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. National Club Association (NCA) and state fraternal organization association networks provide defined buyer communities.

### 2. Commercial Diving Company OSHA Standards Compliance & Dive Log Management
**Problem:** Commercial diving companies that perform underwater construction, bridge inspection, pipeline inspection, marine salvage, and dam maintenance operations are regulated under OSHA's specific commercial diving standards (29 CFR 1910.401 through 1910.441, Subpart T — Commercial Diving Operations). OSHA commercial diving compliance requirements are unique and detailed: each diving company must maintain a Safe Practices Manual documenting safe diving procedures, equipment maintenance, and emergency procedures specific to the diving modes they operate (SCUBA, surface-supplied air, surface-supplied mixed gas, saturation). Before each dive, a pre-dive checklist must be completed and documented. After each dive, a post-dive record must be completed documenting: date, location, diver names, diving mode, maximum depth, bottom time, and any decompression obligations. Each diver must maintain a current diving medical examination (from a physician familiar with diving medicine, valid for 12 months in most operations). Dive equipment — umbilicals, helmets, regulators, bailout systems — must be inspected before each use and annually by a qualified inspector. Small commercial diving companies (2-15 divers) have the same OSHA compliance documentation obligations as large diving contractors but with no dedicated safety officer.
**What to build:** A commercial diving company OSHA compliance and dive log management service — a Safe Practices Manual compliance tracker (maintaining the diving company's SPM document version history, last review date, and any procedure revisions required by new equipment, new diving modes, or OSHA citation remediation — with an annual review calendar alert), a per-dive pre-dive and post-dive checklist system (generating a pre-dive checklist based on the planned dive's parameters — diving mode, planned depth, water conditions, emergency procedures review — and a post-dive log capturing all OSHA-required data elements including depth, bottom time, decompression obligations, and any diving-related medical symptoms observed), a diver qualification and medical currency tracker (for each diver: diving medical examination expiration date, training certifications by diving mode, emergency first aid certifications, and any entry-level qualification documentation — with alerts when any diver's medical or certification is approaching expiration), a dive equipment inspection log (for each piece of life-support equipment: serial number, last pre-use inspection, last annual inspection by a qualified inspector, and any equipment anomalies noted during use — maintaining the continuous inspection record that OSHA requires for critical diving equipment), and an emergency procedures documentation compliance calendar (confirming that the company's emergency procedures are current, that all active divers have reviewed the emergency procedures within the required interval, and that emergency contact and decompression chamber location information is current for each operating geography).
**Skill fit:** OSHA 29 CFR 1910.401-441 Subpart T commercial diving standards are publicly available — the required pre-dive and post-dive record elements, medical examination requirements, and SPM content requirements are explicitly stated in the regulation. Building the per-dive log system (a structured data entry form capturing all required OSHA data elements), diver medical currency tracker (a per-diver certification expiration calendar), and equipment inspection log (a per-equipment maintenance record) are data management problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. Association of Diving Contractors International (ADCI) member directory provides a defined buyer community.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a per-dive pre-dive/post-dive log with all OSHA Subpart T required data elements, a diver medical and certification currency tracker with expiration alerts, a dive equipment inspection log, and an SPM review calendar; first 5-8 commercial diving company clients through ADCI member outreach while the per-dive log is validated against real diving operation documentation workflows.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through ADCI (Association of Diving Contractors International) member network and commercial diving contractor association outreach (the concentrated professional community for commercial diving company owners and safety officers — ADCI's annual conference is the primary industry event), OSHA compliance consulting firm referral networks (EHS consultants who audit commercial diving operations encounter SPM gaps and dive log deficiencies at every engagement and refer systematic documentation services to their diving company clients), and marine construction general contractor referral networks (GCs who hire commercial diving subcontractors for bridge and marine infrastructure projects increasingly require diving subcontractors to demonstrate systematic OSHA documentation as a subcontract qualification requirement).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per commercial diving company: $350-800/mo (depending on diver count and dive frequency — a 4-diver company performing 20-30 dives per month: $350/mo; a 12-diver company performing 80+ dives per month across multiple projects: $800/mo). Managing 25-35 company retainers at $550/mo average = **$13,750-19,250/mo**. OSHA citation remediation documentation packages (one-time $600-1,500 when a company receives an OSHA Subpart T citation and needs a structured remediation documentation plan) generate significant supplemental revenue. Reaching 25-35 company retainers requires 3-4 ADCI chapter relationships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Pre-dive and post-dive records are required for every commercial dive — a company performing 60 dives per month generates 60 monthly log events continuously. Diver medical examinations expire annually for every active diver. Equipment inspection records must be maintained continuously for all life-support equipment. OSHA enforcement of commercial diving standards through inspection programs targeting maritime industries generates consistent urgency for documentation compliance.
**Biggest risk:** A commercial diver who develops decompression illness after a dive where no post-dive record was maintained — because the company's logging process broke down during a busy project — creates both a worker's comp claim and an OSHA recordable incident without the diving operation record that would clarify the dive's depth and bottom time. Build the post-dive log with a project completion gate: a dive day's work cannot be marked "complete" in the project log until every diver's post-dive record for that day has been submitted.
**Growth path:** Side project → per-dive log with OSHA data elements, diver certification tracker, and equipment inspection log deployed, first 5-8 company retainers through ADCI outreach. ### 3. Hazardous Waste Generator EPA Manifest Tracking & State Generator Registration Compliance
**Problem:** Companies that generate hazardous waste — manufacturers, auto body shops, dry cleaners (if disposing of perc-contaminated waste), hospitals, laboratories, print shops — must comply with EPA's RCRA (Resource Conservation and Recovery Act) hazardous waste management regulations. Key compliance obligations for hazardous waste generators include: maintaining a valid EPA ID number (required before any hazardous waste can be shipped), correctly classifying generated waste as hazardous based on EPA's characteristic (ignitability, corrosivity, reactivity, toxicity) and listed waste standards, completing a hazardous waste manifest for each waste shipment — a multi-copy tracking document that accompanies the waste from the generator to the treatment, storage, or disposal facility (TSDF), receiving a signed copy of the manifest from the TSDF confirming proper disposal within the required timeframe (and initiating an exception report if the signed copy is not received within 35 days of shipment), and complying with state generator requirements that in many states are more stringent than federal standards. Large Quantity Generators (LQGs) face additional requirements including an annual report to the state, emergency preparedness plans, and personnel training. Small and medium generators frequently miss manifest exception reports, lose track of their EPA ID number renewal, and fail to maintain the 3-year manifest record retention requirement systematically.
**What to build:** A hazardous waste generator EPA manifest tracking and state compliance management service — a hazardous waste shipment and manifest tracking system (for each hazardous waste shipment: manifest document number, shipment date, waste type and EPA waste code, quantity shipped, transporter name and EPA ID, designated TSDF, and expected signed copy return date — with an alert at day 35 if the TSDF-signed copy has not been received, triggering the required exception report), a generator quantity classification monitor (tracking the facility's monthly hazardous waste generation volumes by waste stream, calculating the generator category — Large Quantity Generator, Small Quantity Generator, or Very Small Quantity Generator — based on cumulative monthly generation, and alerting when generation volume is approaching a threshold that would change the generator category and impose additional compliance obligations), an EPA ID number compliance tracker (confirming the facility's EPA ID number validity and any state EPA ID renewal or re-notification requirements — with alerts before state EPA re-notification deadlines), a manifest record retention system (maintaining the required 3-year manifest record archive for all outbound shipments, organized by manifest number and shipment date — accessible for EPA and state agency inspection), and a state generator compliance calendar (tracking state-specific requirements beyond federal baseline: state annual hazardous waste report due dates, state LQG emergency plan update requirements, state-mandated waste minimization or pollution prevention documentation, and any state-specific manifest form requirements).
**Skill fit:** EPA RCRA hazardous waste generator requirements are publicly available from EPA's RCRA regulations (40 CFR Parts 260-268) and EPA's hazardous waste generator guidance publications — the manifest tracking requirements, generator category thresholds, and exception reporting requirements are clearly documented. Building the manifest tracking system (a per-shipment record with return deadline monitoring), generator category monitor (a rolling monthly generation quantity calculator with threshold alert), EPA ID tracker, manifest retention archive, and state compliance calendar are data management problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. EPA's e-Manifest system (a federal electronic manifest tracking system) provides data integration opportunities for manifest receipt verification.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a hazardous waste manifest tracking system with 35-day TSDF return deadline alerts and exception report generation, a generator category quantity calculator with threshold change alerts, a manifest record archive organized by manifest number, and a state generator compliance calendar for 15 high-hazardous-waste-generator states (CA, TX, PA, NY, OH, IL, NJ, MI, NC, GA, MA, WA, IN, MO, TN); first 5-8 generator clients through environmental compliance consulting firm referral outreach and industrial association networks while the manifest tracking system is validated against real generator manifest workflows.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through National Solid Wastes Management Association (NSWMA) and Hazardous Waste Management Association member networks, environmental compliance consulting firm referral networks (EHS consultants who audit generator facilities see manifest tracking gaps and exception report failures in every engagement and refer systematic tracking services), and industrial waste transporter referral networks (licensed hazardous waste transporters see generators who struggle with manifest tracking in every pickup and actively refer compliance management tools to their generator clients who have manifest documentation issues).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per generating facility: $250-600/mo (depending on shipment frequency and generator category — a Small Quantity Generator with 4-6 annual shipments: $250/mo; a Large Quantity Generator with weekly shipments and state annual reporting obligations: $600/mo). Managing 30-45 facility retainers at $400/mo average = **$12,000-18,000/mo**. EPA manifest exception report preparation packages (one-time $300-700 per facility when an exception report deadline is triggered) generate concentrated supplemental revenue. Reaching 30-45 facility retainers requires 4-6 active environmental consulting firm referral relationships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every hazardous waste shipment generates a manifest tracking event with a 35-day return deadline. Monthly generation volume monitoring is continuous for every active generator. 3-year manifest records must be maintained continuously. State annual hazardous waste reports create annual filing events for LQGs. EPA and state hazardous waste inspection programs targeting generators create consistent urgency for systematic manifest documentation.
**Biggest risk:** An exception report that is not filed within the required 45-day timeframe (10 days after the 35-day return period) — because the generator didn't notice the signed manifest hadn't been received — constitutes a RCRA violation that can result in an EPA penalty. Build the manifest exception report system with an automatic escalating alert sequence: at day 35, an alert notifies the generator contact; at day 40, the alert escalates to the facility manager; at day 43, the exception report is auto-generated as a draft ready for the generator to review and submit before the day 45 deadline.
**Growth path:** Side project → manifest tracking with 35-day alert, generator category calculator, and manifest archive deployed, first 5-8 facility retainers through environmental consulting referral outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 15-20 facility retainers, waste transporter referral network generating consistent new clients (~$4-7K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 30-45 facility retainers, LQG annual report preparation as high-value Q1 supplemental revenue, pushing past $12-18K/mo.

### 4. Independent Insurance Adjusting Firm Multi-State Adjuster License & CAT Deployment Compliance
**Problem:** Independent adjusting firms (IAFs) that deploy field adjusters to handle property and casualty insurance claims on behalf of insurance carrier clients must maintain state adjuster licenses for every adjuster in every state where that adjuster writes claims. Adjuster licensing requirements vary significantly by state: 38 states currently require property and casualty adjuster licensing, with different pre-license education requirements, CE renewal requirements (typically 24 hours per 2-year renewal period), designated home state for license portability under reciprocity agreements, and annual renewal deadlines. When a catastrophic weather event — a hurricane, tornado outbreak, wildfire, or hailstorm — results in a state disaster declaration, affected states typically issue temporary adjuster licenses for a limited period (30-180 days depending on the state) allowing out-of-state adjusters to work claims for the event. Managing CAT deployment compliance requires: quickly identifying which adjusters need a temporary state license before deploying them, tracking each temporary license's expiration date (because temporary licenses issued during CAT events expire, leaving deployed adjusters unlicensed if not converted to permanent licenses or if the adjuster is not withdrawn from the state by expiration), and monitoring which states are currently in a declared disaster status where temporary licenses are available. An IAF deploying 30-50 adjusters across 15 states simultaneously cannot manage this complexity through a spreadsheet.
**What to build:** An independent adjusting firm multi-state adjuster license and CAT deployment compliance management service — a multi-adjuster, multi-state license portfolio manager (for each adjuster: all current state licenses with license numbers, renewal dates, and CE completion status — with alerts when any adjuster's license is approaching renewal and when CE hours for renewal are below the required total by state), a CE compliance tracker (for each adjuster by state: required CE hours for the current renewal period, CE hours completed with provider and course name, remaining hours needed, and renewal deadline — with pacing alerts when an adjuster is behind the completion rate needed to finish before their renewal deadline), a CAT deployment readiness dashboard (showing each deployed adjuster's current licensing status in every state in the current active CAT zone — quickly identifying which adjusters are not yet licensed in a newly affected state and need a temporary license before deployment), a temporary license tracking system (for each temporary license obtained during a CAT event: issuing state, event number, issue date, expiration date, and whether the adjuster is still deployed in that state — with expiration alerts so adjusters are not left in the field on an expired temporary license), and a state disaster declaration monitor (tracking active state disaster declarations affecting P&C insurance claims — alerting the IAF when a new declaration triggers temporary adjuster licensing availability in the affected state, so the firm can prepare deployment authorization before competitors have theirs).
**Skill fit:** State adjuster licensing requirements are available through NIPR (National Insurance Producer Registry) and each state's department of insurance website — building a state-by-state adjuster licensing database is a regulatory research problem. The multi-adjuster, multi-state license portfolio manager (a per-adjuster, per-state license database with renewal calendar), CE compliance tracker (a per-adjuster CE hour tracking system with state-specific renewal requirements), CAT deployment readiness dashboard, temporary license tracker, and disaster declaration monitor are data management problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. PLRB (Property Loss Research Bureau) and NAPIA (National Association of Public Insurance Adjusters) networks provide access to the buyer community.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a multi-adjuster, multi-state license portfolio manager with CE completion tracking for 38 adjuster-licensing states, a CAT deployment readiness dashboard, a temporary license expiration tracker, and a state disaster declaration alert system; first 5-8 IAF clients through PLRB and Claims Pages (the online community for independent adjusters) while the multi-state license manager is validated against real IAF adjuster rosters.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through Claims Pages and ClaimsJournal.com independent adjuster community outreach (the concentrated professional communities for IAF owners and adjusters), PLRB member conference outreach (insurance carrier claims executives who contract with IAFs evaluate adjuster licensing compliance as a vendor qualification requirement and refer compliance management tools to their IAF vendors), and insurance staffing platform referral networks (platforms like Verisk, Crawford, and Sedgwick who staff independent adjusters for carrier clients require license verification as part of adjuster onboarding and refer systematic license management tools to smaller IAFs in their vendor network).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per IAF: $400-1,000/mo (depending on adjuster count and state diversity — a 15-adjuster firm operating in 8 states: $400/mo; a 40-adjuster firm operating in 20 states with active CAT deployment: $1,000/mo). Managing 18-25 IAF retainers at $650/mo average = **$11,700-16,250/mo**. Post-CAT temporary licensing coordination packages (one-time $300-600 per event for managing temporary license applications for all deployed adjusters following a major CAT event) generate significant supplemental revenue following major weather events. Reaching 18-25 IAF retainers requires 3-4 PLRB chapter and Claims Pages community relationships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Adjuster CE must be completed within each 2-year renewal period continuously. Multi-state license renewals stagger throughout the year, creating continuous renewal events for a multi-adjuster firm. CAT events — hailstorms, hurricanes, tornadoes, wildfires — occur multiple times per year in the US, generating temporary licensing urgency events that create high-value one-time packages. CE tracking in particular creates strong daily engagement because adjusters are completing CE courses throughout the year.
**Biggest risk:** An adjuster who is deployed to work claims in a state where their temporary CAT license has expired — because the expiration date was not tracked — is an unlicensed adjuster writing claims in that state, creating regulatory liability for the IAF and potentially voiding the claim adjustments written by that adjuster. Build the temporary license expiration tracker with a 14-day advance alert: when a temporary license is within 14 days of expiration, the system generates an immediate alert to the adjuster's direct supervisor and the IAF's licensing manager, requiring either withdrawal of the adjuster from that state or conversion to a permanent license.
**Growth path:** Side project → multi-adjuster multi-state license manager with CE tracking and CAT deployment dashboard deployed, first 5-8 IAF retainers through Claims Pages outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 10-12 IAF retainers, post-CAT temporary licensing packages generating event-driven supplemental revenue (~$5-8K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 18-25 IAF retainers, disaster declaration monitoring as a premium feature generating consistent retention, pushing past $11-16K/mo.

### 5. Pharmacy Benefit Manager State PBM Registration & Pharmacy Network Adequacy Compliance
**Problem:** Pharmacy benefit managers (PBMs) — companies that administer prescription drug benefits for health plans, employers, and government programs — face a rapidly expanding state regulatory environment. Since 2017, 40+ states have enacted PBM licensing or registration requirements, and PBMs that operate across multiple states must now maintain state PBM licenses in virtually every operating state. State PBM regulatory requirements vary significantly: some states require a formal license with annual renewal, net worth documentation, surety bonds, and state insurance department approval of PBM contracts with health plans; others require registration with annual report submissions; and several states require PBMs to comply with specific pharmacy network adequacy standards (minimum requirements for pharmacy access within specified geographic distance and drive-time standards for health plan enrollees). Network adequacy compliance typically requires: maintaining a pharmacy network with access within a specified distance standard (e.g., at least one retail pharmacy within 2 miles for urban enrollees, 10 miles for suburban, 15 miles for rural), documenting the network against the state's access standards on an annual basis, and notifying the state insurance department when a pharmacy terminates from the network and the departure creates an access gap. Small regional PBMs (administering benefits for 50,000-500,000 covered lives in 5-15 states) have the same multi-state license and network adequacy compliance obligations as large national PBMs but with limited regulatory affairs resources.
**What to build:** A PBM multi-state registration and network adequacy compliance management service — a multi-state PBM license and registration portfolio manager (tracking each state's PBM license or registration: license type — license vs. registration vs. certificate of authority — annual renewal deadline, net worth or financial statement submission requirement, surety bond amount and expiration, state insurance department filing confirmation, and any state-required contract filing requirements for PBM-health plan contracts — with tiered renewal alerts), a pharmacy network adequacy monitoring dashboard (mapping the PBM's contracted pharmacy network against each state's geographic access standards — identifying ZIP code service areas where the network meets or fails the state's distance and drive-time requirements, and flagging any area where a pharmacy termination has created a potential access gap that requires state notification), a state PBM annual report data collector (gathering the required data elements for each state's annual PBM regulatory report: total covered lives by state, aggregate prescription claim counts, spread pricing amounts where required by state transparency laws, and network pharmacy count — generating each state's report in the required format), a pharmacy contract termination impact assessment (when a network pharmacy gives termination notice, immediately assessing the geographic impact of that pharmacy's exit against the network adequacy standards in every state where that pharmacy is in the network — flagging any state where the termination creates an adequacy gap requiring state notification before the termination becomes effective), and a state PBM law change monitoring tracker (given the rapid pace of state PBM legislation, tracking newly enacted state PBM requirements that may create new registration, reporting, or network adequacy obligations that the PBM needs to address).
**Skill fit:** State PBM registration requirements are publicly available from each state's insurance department — the National Community Pharmacists Association (NCPA) and PCMA (Pharmaceutical Care Management Association) publish state PBM law tracking that provides a starting database. Pharmacy geolocation data (enabling network adequacy geographic analysis) is available from pharmacy directory APIs and CMS pharmacy data sources. The multi-state license portfolio manager (a per-state registration database), network adequacy dashboard (a geographic access analysis using pharmacy location data), annual report data collector, termination impact assessment, and PBM law tracker are data management and geospatial analysis problems the builder's engineering and data analytics skills address directly.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a multi-state PBM license portfolio manager with renewal calendar for 25 states with active PBM licensing requirements, a pharmacy network adequacy monitoring dashboard with ZIP-code level access standard analysis for 5 states with the most specific network adequacy regulations (CA, NY, FL, TX, OH), a pharmacy termination impact assessment tool, and a state annual report data template; first 5-8 PBM clients through PCMA member outreach and state pharmacy association networks while the network adequacy dashboard is validated against real PBM network pharmacy data.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through PCMA (Pharmaceutical Care Management Association) member network and state PBM association outreach (the concentrated professional community for PBM compliance officers), state insurance department PBM licensing informational conference outreach (many states have hosted informational meetings for PBMs regarding new state registration requirements — these events are direct acquisition channels for the target buyer), and health plan compliance consulting firm referral networks (consultants who advise health plans on PBM contract compliance encounter PBM state licensing and network adequacy gaps at every engagement and refer systematic compliance management services to PBMs in their network).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per PBM: $700-2,000/mo (depending on state count and network size — a 5-state PBM with 100,000 covered lives: $700/mo; a 15-state PBM with 400,000 covered lives and complex network adequacy monitoring requirements: $2,000/mo). Managing 10-15 PBM retainers at $1,200/mo average = **$12,000-18,000/mo**. State PBM new registration packages (one-time $600-1,500 per state when a PBM expands into a new state with a licensing requirement or when a state enacts a new PBM registration requirement) generate significant supplemental revenue as the state regulatory environment continues to expand. Reaching 10-15 PBM retainers requires 3-4 PCMA member relationships and 1-2 health plan compliance consulting firm referral partnerships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** State PBM licensing renewals occur annually across all registered states simultaneously. Network adequacy monitoring is a continuous obligation as pharmacy network contracts are added and terminated throughout the year. State PBM legislation is still actively expanding — new state PBM registration requirements enacted since 2017 have averaged 4-6 new state laws per year, each creating new registration and reporting obligations. PBM annual reports to state regulators create annual filing events in every regulated state.
