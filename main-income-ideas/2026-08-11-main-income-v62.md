### 1. Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA) Therapy Practice BACB Certification, RBT Supervision & Payer Authorization Compliance
**Problem:** ABA therapy practices serving autism-spectrum clients employ Board Certified Behavior Analysts (BCBAs) and Board Certified Assistant Behavior Analysts (BCaBAs) credentialed through the Behavior Analyst Certification Board (BACB), recertified every 2 years with 32 continuing-education hours including a mandatory 4 hours of ethics content, and a growing majority of states now require a separate state behavior-analyst license (LBA) renewed on its own cycle entirely independent of the BACB's own timeline. Direct 1:1 therapy is delivered primarily by Registered Behavior Technicians (RBTs) — a paraprofessional credential renewed annually through BACB — who must work under documented BCBA/BCaBA supervision equal to at least 5% of the hours each RBT delivers in a given month, a supervision-ratio requirement the BACB actively audits during credential renewal and that a practice running a dozen RBTs against two or three supervising BCBAs must track individually per technician, per month. Every state now mandates some form of insurance coverage for autism ABA services, but payers authorize treatment in fixed blocks of billable units (under CPT codes 97151-97158) for a defined authorization period — commonly 90 or 180 days — and delivering (and billing) sessions beyond the authorized unit count before a reauthorization is approved is a documented claims-denial and clawback trigger insurers pursue aggressively given ABA's high per-client annual billing volume. A small practice running 10-15 RBTs and dozens of active client authorizations manages staggered BCBA/RBT credential renewal, monthly supervision-hour documentation per technician, and per-client authorized-unit tracking across multiple payers with no dedicated compliance function — and a technician delivering sessions under a lapsed supervision ratio, or a practice billing past a client's authorized unit block before reauthorization clears, is exactly the gap a BACB credential audit or a payer's utilization review is built to catch.
**What to build:** A per-clinician BCBA/BCaBA/RBT credential tracker (BACB certification status, state LBA license, CE hours) with a "cleared to treat" gate; a monthly supervision-hour log computing each RBT's required 5% supervision threshold against hours actually delivered and flagging any technician falling short; and a per-client, per-payer authorization tracker (authorized unit count, units billed to date, authorization period end date) with a hard alert before a client's remaining authorized units run out, prompting reauthorization before service continues.
**Skill fit:** BACB's certification and supervision-ratio requirements and each payer's authorization-unit structure are documented, rules-based sources, and the supervision-hour computation plus the authorized-unit countdown are the same deterministic "compute the compliance threshold from logged activity, alert before it's breached" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a fast-growing therapy category with no prior session coverage in this repo.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the credential tracker with the treat-eligibility gate, the supervision-hour log, and the per-client authorization tracker; land the first 5-8 practices through Association of Professional Behavior Analysts (APBA) outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Association of Professional Behavior Analysts (APBA) member directory and state ABA provider association meetings, where practice owners already navigate RBT supervision-ratio documentation and payer reauthorization timing on spreadsheets that don't scale past a handful of technicians.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $12/mo per RBT tracked. Average client (10 RBTs) = $199 + $120 = $319/mo. 32-38 clients × $319/mo = **$10,208-12,122/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every RBT's supervision hours recompute monthly against actual delivered sessions, every client's authorized-unit balance depletes continuously as sessions are billed, and BCBA/RBT credentials renew on staggered independent cycles — a high-frequency, per-session compliance stream tied directly to a practice's caseload and headcount.
**Biggest risk:** A technician delivering sessions under an undocumented or below-threshold supervision ratio, or a practice billing past a client's authorized unit block, is exactly the gap a BACB audit or payer utilization review is built to catch — the supervision-hour log and the authorized-unit countdown are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → credential tracker, supervision-hour log, and authorization tracker deployed, first 5-8 practice retainers through APBA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 16-20 practice retainers. Replaces-full-time-salary → 32-38 practice retainers, pushing past $10.2-12.1K/mo.

### 2. Podiatry Practice State Board License, DEA Registration & Medicare Global-Period/Therapeutic Shoe Billing Compliance
**Problem:** Podiatrists (Doctors of Podiatric Medicine, DPM) hold a state podiatric medical board license, renewed on a 1-2 year cycle with CE, and those performing in-office surgical procedures or prescribing scheduled post-operative pain medication must additionally hold a DEA registration and, in most states, a parallel state controlled-substance registration, both on renewal cycles distinct from the base license. Every surgical CPT code Medicare reimburses carries a global-period bundle — 0-day, 10-day, or 90-day windows during which follow-up evaluation-and-management visits related to that procedure are considered included in the surgical payment and cannot be separately billed — and CMS's Comprehensive Error Rate Testing (CERT) program has repeatedly flagged podiatry practices specifically for billing global-period visits separately, alongside billing routine foot care as medically necessary without the required systemic-condition (diabetes, peripheral vascular disease) documentation Medicare demands to cover otherwise-excluded routine care. Separately, Medicare's Therapeutic Shoe benefit — covering custom-molded or extra-depth shoes for diabetic patients — requires a specific annual certifying-physician statement plus a fitter's documentation on file before the claim can be submitted, a once-a-year benefit with its own hard documentation gate that a missed or incomplete certifying statement voids entirely regardless of clinical appropriateness. A small practice with 1-3 DPMs manages staggered license/DEA renewal, per-procedure global-period tracking across a busy surgical schedule, and per-patient annual Therapeutic Shoe certification with no dedicated compliance function — and a follow-up visit billed inside an active global period, or a therapeutic-shoe claim submitted without the current-year certifying statement on file, is exactly the finding a CERT or MAC post-payment audit is built to catch.
**What to build:** A per-DPM license, DEA/state controlled-substance registration, and CE tracker with a "cleared to prescribe" gate; a per-procedure global-period tracker computing each surgery's bundled-billing window from the procedure date and flagging any follow-up visit scheduled inside that window before it's billed separately; and an annual Therapeutic Shoe certification tracker per diabetic patient, blocking a shoe claim from being marked "ready to submit" until the current-year certifying-physician statement and fitter documentation are confirmed on file.
**Skill fit:** Medicare's global-surgical-period rules and the Therapeutic Shoe benefit's annual certification requirement are public CMS regulatory sources with well-defined, computable windows, and the global-period tracker plus the annual-certification gate are the same deterministic "compute the bundled window / block the claim until the required document clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a surgical specialty distinct from every other licensed-provider idea in this repo.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the license/DEA tracker, the global-period tracker, and the Therapeutic Shoe certification tracker; land the first 5-8 practices through American Podiatric Medical Association (APMA) state component outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — American Podiatric Medical Association (APMA) state component chapter meetings, where independent practice owners already navigate global-period billing audits and the Therapeutic Shoe benefit's annual paperwork.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $225/mo base + $20/mo per additional DPM tracked. Average client (a mostly-solo practice with one associate, 1.5 additional DPMs) = $225 + $30 = $255/mo. 40-48 clients × $255/mo = **$10,200-12,240/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every surgical procedure opens its own global-period billing window, every diabetic patient's Therapeutic Shoe eligibility resets annually, and DEA/license credentials renew on their own independent cycles — a continuous, procedure-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the practice's surgical caseload.
**Biggest risk:** A follow-up visit billed separately inside an active global period, or a therapeutic-shoe claim submitted without the current-year certifying statement, is exactly the finding a CMS CERT or MAC post-payment audit is built to catch — the global-period tracker and the annual-certification gate are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → license/DEA tracker, global-period tracker, and Therapeutic Shoe certification tracker deployed, first 5-8 practice retainers through APMA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 20-24 practice retainers. Replaces-full-time-salary → 40-48 practice retainers, pushing past $10.2-12.24K/mo.

### 3. Driver's Education School State DMV Licensing, Instructor Certification & Curriculum-Hour Compliance
**Problem:** Driving schools teaching new teen and adult drivers must hold a state DMV or department-of-licensing driving-school license, and every instructor must individually hold a state instructor certification requiring a background check, in-car training, and separate classroom and behind-the-wheel instructor endorsements, renewed on a 1-2 year cycle. Nearly every state mandates a minimum classroom-and-behind-the-wheel curriculum-hour structure (commonly around 30 classroom hours plus 6 behind-the-wheel hours for a teen driver's ed program) a student must complete before the school can issue a completion certificate, and that certificate — commonly a specific state-numbered form — must be filed with the DMV before the student becomes eligible for a provisional license, a reduced permit-holding period, or a graduated-license insurance discount, giving the filing a direct, tangible consequence for the family on the other end of a missed deadline. Every instructional vehicle must meet state equipment requirements (dual brake controls, exterior signage, minimum insurance coverage) and pass a periodic vehicle inspection, and a school found to have issued a completion certificate without the state's minimum curriculum hours actually logged — or that used an instructor whose certification had lapsed — risks license suspension, a finding regulators pursue given the direct public-safety stakes of certifying a new driver as road-ready. A small driving school running several instructors and dozens of students a month manages staggered instructor certification renewal, per-student curriculum-hour documentation across classroom and behind-the-wheel components, and DMV completion-certificate filing deadlines with no dedicated compliance function — and a missed certificate filing, discovered only when a student can't get their provisional license on schedule, is exactly the failure that damages a referral-driven school's reputation fastest.
**What to build:** A per-instructor certification and background-check tracker with a "cleared to teach" gate blocking a class or in-car session from being scheduled under an instructor whose certification has lapsed; a per-student curriculum-hour log (classroom and behind-the-wheel hours logged against the state's required minimums) blocking a completion certificate from being generated until every required hour is confirmed; a DMV completion-certificate filing tracker computing each student's submission deadline from their completion date, with a submission-confirmation flag; and an instructional-vehicle equipment and inspection renewal tracker.
**Skill fit:** State DMV driving-school licensing rules and curriculum-hour requirements are public regulatory text published per state, and the curriculum-hour gate plus the certificate-filing tracker are the same deterministic "block the certificate / compute the deadline from the trigger event" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a training category most small schools currently run on a paper sign-in sheet with no systematic filing cross-check.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the instructor certification tracker with the teach-eligibility gate, the curriculum-hour log, and the DMV filing tracker; land the first 5-8 schools through Driving School Association of the Americas (DSAA) outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Driving School Association of the Americas (DSAA) member directory and state driving-school association meetings, where independent school owners already discuss curriculum-hour documentation and DMV filing turnaround during peak summer enrollment.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149/mo base + $3/mo per student processed monthly. Average client (40 students/mo) = $149 + $120 = $269/mo. 40-48 schools × $269/mo = **$10,760-12,912/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new enrollment opens its own curriculum-hour and filing-deadline sequence, instructor certifications renew on staggered cycles, and a growing school adds new student cohorts continuously through the school year — a per-student, enrollment-volume-driven compliance stream, not a single annual filing.
**Biggest risk:** A completion certificate issued without the state's minimum curriculum hours logged, or a missed DMV filing deadline, is exactly the gap a state audit of the school's records — or a frustrated family — is built to catch; the curriculum-hour gate and the filing-deadline tracker are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → instructor tracker, curriculum-hour log, and DMV filing tracker deployed, first 5-8 school retainers through DSAA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 22-26 schools. Replaces-full-time-salary → 40-48 schools, pushing past $10.76-12.91K/mo.

### 4. Freestanding Sleep Disorder Testing Center AASM Accreditation, RPSGT Credentialing & Staffing-Ratio Compliance
**Problem:** Freestanding sleep disorder testing centers performing in-lab polysomnography (PSG) and home sleep apnea testing pursue accreditation from the American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AASM), a facility-level accreditation many payers require as a condition of reimbursement, re-surveyed roughly every 5 years against AASM's detailed equipment, safety-protocol, and staffing standards — including a mandated overnight technologist-to-patient ratio (commonly no more than 1 technologist per 2 patients during attended studies) that a center must maintain on every single overnight shift, not just at survey time. Every sleep technologist operating PSG equipment must hold a current RPSGT (Registered Polysomnographic Technologist) credential through the Board of Registered Polysomnographic Technologists (BRPT), maintained via continuing-education credits on its own multi-year cycle entirely independent of the center's own AASM accreditation calendar, and in the states that separately license sleep technologists, a state credential runs on yet another independent renewal clock. Every completed study also requires a board-certified sleep physician's interpretation and signed report within a defined turnaround window most payers and referring physicians expect honored consistently, since a delayed report holds up a patient's treatment decision and damages the referral relationship that drives the center's volume. A small center running 1-2 locations and 5-10 technologists manages the AASM accreditation cycle, the overnight staffing-ratio requirement on every shift, staggered RPSGT/state credential renewal, and physician-interpretation turnaround tracking with no dedicated compliance function — and an overnight shift run below the required staffing ratio, or a technologist operating equipment after their RPSGT certification lapsed, is exactly the finding an AASM accreditation survey or a payer credentialing audit is built to catch.
**What to build:** An AASM accreditation renewal and standards-compliance tracker (survey cycle, equipment/safety-protocol documentation); a per-technologist RPSGT/state credential and CE tracker with a "cleared to monitor" gate blocking a shift assignment once a credential lapses; a real-time overnight staffing-ratio dashboard confirming every scheduled shift meets AASM's technologist-to-patient minimum before the shift is finalized; and a per-study physician-interpretation turnaround tracker flagging any signed report approaching or past its expected turnaround window.
**Skill fit:** AASM's accreditation standards, BRPT's RPSGT credentialing structure, and the mandated staffing ratio are public accreditation and certifying-body sources with well-defined, checkable requirements, and the staffing-ratio dashboard plus the technologist-eligibility gate are the same deterministic "verify the compliance condition before the shift runs" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a diagnostic-facility category distinct from every other imaging or dialysis idea already in this repo.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the AASM accreditation tracker, the RPSGT/state credential tracker with the monitor-eligibility gate, and the staffing-ratio dashboard; land the first 5-8 centers through American Association of Sleep Technologists (AAST) outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — American Association of Sleep Technologists (AAST) member directory and independent sleep-center administrator networks, where center managers already navigate AASM's staffing-ratio requirement and staggered RPSGT renewal across multiple techs.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249/mo base + $20/mo per technologist tracked. Average client (5 technologists) = $249 + $100 = $349/mo. 30-36 centers × $349/mo = **$10,470-12,564/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** The overnight staffing ratio must be verified fresh every single shift regardless of accreditation-cycle timing, RPSGT and state credentials renew on staggered independent clocks per technologist, and every completed study opens its own interpretation-turnaround event — a nightly, high-frequency compliance stream tied directly to the center's study volume.
**Biggest risk:** Running an overnight shift below AASM's mandated technologist-to-patient ratio, or a technologist monitoring patients after their RPSGT credential lapsed, is exactly the finding an AASM accreditation survey is built to catch — the staffing-ratio dashboard and the monitor-eligibility gate are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → AASM accreditation tracker, credential tracker, and staffing-ratio dashboard deployed, first 5-8 center retainers through AAST outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 16-20 centers. Replaces-full-time-salary → 30-36 centers, pushing past $10.47-12.56K/mo.

### 5. Orthotics & Prosthetics (O&P) Practice ABC/BOC Certification, State Licensure & Medicare L-Code Documentation Compliance
**Problem:** Orthotists and prosthetists who custom-fabricate and fit external limbs and braces hold certification through the American Board for Certification in Orthotics, Prosthetics & Pedorthics (ABC) or the Board of Certification/Accreditation (BOC), renewed on a roughly 2-year cycle with continuing education, and in the states requiring it, a separate state O&P practice license runs on its own independent renewal clock. O&P facilities must maintain Medicare DMEPOS accreditation and a per-location surety bond much like a durable medical equipment supplier, but every claim is billed under specific HCPCS L-codes covering custom-fabricated devices — among the codes CMS's Supplemental Medical Review Contractor audits most aggressively given the high per-device reimbursement and a documented history of medical-necessity fraud in the category — and a compliant claim requires a Written Order Prior to Delivery (WOPD) signed by the prescribing physician plus a Detailed Product Description substantiating the specific device's medical necessity, both of which must be on file before the device can legally be delivered and billed. A small practice running 2-5 certified practitioners and dozens of active fabrication cases manages staggered ABC/BOC and state license renewal per practitioner, per-device WOPD/Detailed Product Description documentation across every L-code claim, and DMEPOS accreditation/bond renewal with no dedicated compliance function — and a custom device delivered and billed without a complete WOPD or Detailed Product Description on file is exactly the documentation gap a CMS post-payment audit targets for clawback, given how frequently O&P claims are singled out for review.
**What to build:** A per-practitioner ABC/BOC certification and state license tracker with a "cleared to fabricate/bill" gate; a DMEPOS accreditation and per-location surety bond renewal tracker; and a per-device L-code billing-eligibility gate blocking a claim from being marked "ready to submit" until the WOPD, the Detailed Product Description, and supporting medical-necessity documentation are all confirmed on file.
**Skill fit:** ABC/BOC's certification structure and CMS's L-code documentation requirements for custom-fabricated devices are public certifying-body and federal regulatory sources, and the billing-eligibility gate is the same deterministic "block the claim until the required documentation clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a clinician-level fabrication category distinct from both the podiatry practice and the DME supplier ideas already in this repo.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the practitioner certification tracker, the DMEPOS accreditation/bond tracker, and the L-code billing-eligibility gate; land the first 5-8 practices through American Orthotic & Prosthetic Association (AOPA) outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — American Orthotic & Prosthetic Association (AOPA) member directory and state O&P society meetings, where independent practice owners already navigate the WOPD/Detailed Product Description documentation chain CMS audits most aggressively in this specialty.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249/mo base + $20/mo per certified practitioner tracked. Average client (3 practitioners) = $249 + $60 = $309/mo. 35-42 clients × $309/mo = **$10,815-12,978/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every custom-fabricated device generates its own WOPD-to-Detailed-Product-Description documentation chain, and ABC/BOC and state license renewals recur per practitioner on independent cycles — a per-device compliance stream tied directly to the practice's fabrication caseload.
**Biggest risk:** Delivering and billing a custom device without a complete WOPD or Detailed Product Description on file is exactly the documentation gap CMS's Supplemental Medical Review Contractor targets for clawback in this heavily audited specialty — the billing-eligibility gate is the core protection.
**Growth path:** Side project → practitioner certification tracker, DMEPOS/bond tracker, and billing-eligibility gate deployed, first 5-8 practice retainers through AOPA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 18-22 practices. Replaces-full-time-salary → 35-42 practices, pushing past $10.82-12.98K/mo.

## This Session's Pick: #1 for Fastest-Growing, Underserved Niche; #5 for Sharpest Audit-Clawback Story
