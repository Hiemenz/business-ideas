# Main Income Ideas - 2026-07-31 (Session 13)

## Who this is for
Builder profile: 29, software engineering + data analytics skills. Same bar as Sessions 1-12: not "$500-3K/mo of side money" but a solo-buildable path with a credible route to **$8-15K+/mo** — full-time-salary-replacement income — within 12-24 months.

## Ground rules for every idea in this series
- **Solo-buildable start.** One person, evenings/weekends, no funding, no cofounder required to get the first version live.
- **Realistic path to $8-15K+/mo**, not a side-money ceiling — every idea states the math plainly, no TAM hand-waving.
- **Time to first dollar < 90 days.** Long enterprise sales cycles are disqualifying by default.
- **Named distribution channel** for the first 10 customers *and* a credible path to the next 10x.
- **Boring is fine.** Low-glamour, real-pain problems beat trendy ones.
- **No repeats.** Nothing below overlaps a concept already covered across Sessions 1-12, `side-income-ideas/`, or `discovered-problems/`. This session covers: probate estate personal property liquidation coordination (distinct from real estate wholesaling, unclaimed property recovery, and government surplus arbitrage — this is commission-based liquidation of personal property through the right sale channels for estate executors); pre-need funeral planning and insurance service (distinct from senior living placement — this is selling insurance products tied to future funeral services, a different product, different buyer motivation, different licensing); utility grid interconnection consulting for small energy projects (distinct from solar installer permit tracking in side-income and drone inspection in Session 9 — this is the grid-connection application process, a multi-year regulatory workflow with no current coverage); independent telecom agent with residual commissions (distinct from the manufacturers' rep in Session 12 — different product category, subscription-service pricing, and a residual commission structure that compounds differently); and veterinary practice insurance billing (distinct from therapy practice RCM in Session 12 and pharmacy reimbursement in Session 4 — a different clinical sector with a different payer system driven by consumer pet insurance).

### 1. Probate Estate Personal Property Liquidation Coordination
**Problem:** When someone dies and their estate enters probate, the executor — often a non-professional family member — must liquidate personal property (furniture, vehicles, jewelry, art, collectibles, clothing, tools, household goods) before the estate can close. Most executors have no idea how to match specific categories of personal property to the right sale channel: a signed piece of art belongs at an auction house, a vehicle at a dealer or private sale, a coin collection with a numismatic dealer, and household goods at an estate sale company — but putting everything in one estate sale (the path of least resistance) routinely leaves 30-60% of value on the table. An executor who realizes this has happened can't undo it; one who gets professional guidance before the sale captures dramatically more value for the beneficiaries.
**What to build:** A personal property liquidation coordination service — an intake and inventory tool (documenting all personal property categories, estimated values, and time constraints for estate closing), a sale-channel recommendation engine (matching each asset category to its optimal outlet: estate sale company, auction house by specialty, dealer buyout, online marketplace, or charitable donation for tax deduction), a vendor coordination workflow (obtaining bids from the right buyers for each category, managing the logistics of multiple simultaneous sale channels), and a proceeds tracking and distribution summary for the executor's probate accounting — charged as a commission on gross liquidation proceeds (15-25% of total, split across channels).
**Skill fit:** Direct on the inventory, vendor-matching, and proceeds-tracking tooling (cataloging personal property and matching assets to sale channels by type, condition, and estimated value is a structured classification and data-organization problem); the vendor relationship side (knowing which estate sale companies, auction houses, and dealers to call for which categories) is learnable domain knowledge built rapidly through a handful of completed estates.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends for an intake form, asset-category classification tool, and a commission-tracking spreadsheet; the first 2-3 estates are coordinated manually while the vendor network and channel-matching methodology are built from real experience.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through probate attorneys (who are involved in every estate that goes through probate and regularly see executors overwhelmed by personal property), trust officers at community banks and wealth management firms (who serve as successor trustees and need exactly this service for every estate they administer), and estate planning attorneys (who can mention it when preparing wills with clients who have significant personal property).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** 15-20% commission on gross liquidation proceeds; a mid-range estate with $40,000 in personal property generates $6,000-8,000 in commission; coordinating 4-6 active estates/mo at that value = **$10-25K/mo**, with larger estates (art collections, vintage vehicles, substantial household furnishings) generating single-estate commissions of $15,000-50,000+.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Probate is not discretionary — every person who dies with personal property creates an estate, and the US sees approximately 2.9 million deaths annually, generating a continuous, non-cyclical supply of estates that need this service regardless of economic conditions. Each probate attorney relationship generates a predictable referral volume based on how many estates they administer per year, making the revenue modeling unusually stable once the referral network is established.
**Biggest risk:** Estates have beneficiaries who scrutinize every expense, and a commission structure on liquidation proceeds can draw objections if the executor doesn't have clear authority to pay it from the estate — establish the service agreement directly with the executor (the legally authorized party), ensure the probate attorney confirms the arrangement is consistent with the estate's authority, and consider offering a flat-fee alternative for executors who prefer a fixed cost over a percentage structure.
**Growth path:** Side project → first 2-3 estate liquidations through probate attorney referrals, vendor network established in one metro (1-2 estate sale companies, 2-3 auction house contacts, vehicle dealer relationship), proceeds-tracking and commission tool live. Replaces-part-time-income → 3-5 active estates/mo, 3-4 probate attorney referral partners, methodology systematized across property categories (~$6-12K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 5-8 active estates at any time across a mix of sizes, trust officer relationships at 2-3 banks adding institutional referral volume, pushing past $12-25K/mo with individual large estates providing meaningful commission spikes.

### 2. Pre-Need Funeral Planning & Insurance Service
**Problem:** Most Americans have no plan for their own funeral expenses, which average $8,000-15,000 for a full-service funeral with burial — a cost that lands on grieving family members at the worst possible moment, often on a credit card or by depleting savings. Pre-need funeral planning, where someone arranges and pays for their own funeral in advance (locking in today's prices and sparing family the decisions), is a well-established but poorly distributed product: funeral homes sell it directly but have limited reach beyond their own customer base, and most financial advisors don't specialize in it. An independent pre-need insurance agent builds a referral pipeline through estate planners, elder law attorneys, and senior communities and earns commission on every plan sold — without being tied to any single funeral home's inventory or geographic service area.
**What to build:** A pre-need insurance brokering practice — a client intake and planning workflow (capturing funeral preferences: burial vs. cremation, service scope, geographic preference, budget), a comparison tool for pre-need insurance carriers operating in the state (most states have 2-5 major carriers: NGL, SCI, Forethought, Homesteaders Life), a plan illustration and premium calculator (showing the client their guaranteed cost lock-in and monthly premium options), and an estate integration document (a one-page summary the client gives to their estate attorney and family documenting their pre-arranged plan) — requiring a state life insurance license to sell the underlying product.
**Skill fit:** Moderate on the tooling side (carrier comparison, premium calculation, and document generation are structured data and template problems); the sales and relationship-building side (building referral pipelines through estate attorneys and senior centers) is the primary success driver and requires consistent outreach rather than technical skill. The license (life insurance, typically 40-60 hours of pre-licensing study + a state exam) is the key prerequisite, achievable in 4-6 weeks before making the first referral call.
**MVP scope:** 4-6 weeks for life insurance licensing; 1-2 weekends for a carrier comparison spreadsheet and client planning worksheet; distribution outreach begins immediately after licensing since the workflow is simple enough to run manually from the first client.
**Time to first $:** 45-75 days including licensing — distribute through elder law and estate planning attorneys (who discuss end-of-life financial planning with clients constantly and have no current referral for pre-need insurance), hospice social workers (who encounter families dealing with imminent need and wish they'd planned ahead), senior center activity directors, and financial advisors who don't currently offer pre-need products themselves.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Commission on pre-need insurance is typically 15-25% of the plan's face value, paid at policy issuance; a $10,000 plan generates $1,500-2,500 commission; a $15,000 plan generates $2,250-3,750. Writing 6-10 plans/mo through a steady referral pipeline = **$12-25K/mo** in commission, with larger plans (cremation with memorial service + pre-paid cemetery arrangements bundled) reaching $20,000-30,000 face value and generating $3,000-7,500 per sale.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Each elder law attorney or hospice social worker referral partner has a consistent annual volume of clients making end-of-life financial decisions — a single busy elder law attorney may have 5-10 active clients per month who are appropriate for a pre-need referral conversation, creating a predictable lead flow from each established referral relationship. Unlike most sales, pre-need has essentially no competitive alternative from the buyer's perspective: the client either plans ahead with this product or doesn't plan at all, removing the "I'll shop around" dynamic that erodes conversion in other commission-based services.
**Biggest risk:** Pre-need insurance regulation is state-specific and in some states more restrictive than standard life insurance — some states require a pre-need funeral contract license in addition to (or instead of) a life insurance license. Research the specific state licensing requirements before beginning, and verify that the pre-need insurance carrier's compensation structure complies with state pre-need regulations before signing the agent agreement.
**Growth path:** Side project → life insurance license obtained, first 3-5 plans written through direct outreach to 1-2 elder law attorneys and one hospice social worker, carrier relationships established (~$4-10K in first commissions). Replaces-part-time-income → 4-6 plans/mo, 3-4 estate attorney and hospice referral partners active, planning workflow systematized (~$7-12K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 8-12 plans/mo, referral network expanded to financial advisors and senior centers, larger bundled plans increasing average commission per sale, pushing past $12-25K/mo.

### 3. Utility Grid Interconnection Consulting for Small Energy Projects
**Problem:** A small solar farm, community solar project, battery storage system, or small wind installation can't connect to the electric grid and start generating revenue until it completes the utility's interconnection process — a multi-stage regulatory workflow involving an application, feasibility study, system impact study, and facilities study, each with its own data requirements, timelines (often 12-36 months total), and opportunities to object or negotiate. Most small project developers (building 1-5 MW projects, not 500 MW utility-scale farms) don't have in-house interconnection expertise and can't afford the engineering firms that charge $300-500/hour for the same work. Interconnection delays are the most common single reason small clean energy projects fail to reach commercial operation — representing billions in stranded capital annually.
**What to build:** An interconnection process management service — an application preparation tool (organizing the project's technical specifications — site location, equipment specs, single-line diagram requirements — into each utility's specific application format), a study-phase tracking and response system (monitoring study progress, flagging when the utility issues information requests or cost estimates that require a developer response, and tracking milestone deadlines that trigger the developer's right to withdraw or proceed), a cost estimate review workflow (analyzing whether a utility's facilities upgrade cost estimate is reasonable compared to comparable interconnection costs for similar projects in the same region, and identifying grounds to challenge overestimates), and a utility communication management log — charged as a monthly retainer per active project.
**Skill fit:** Direct on the process-tracking and document-preparation tooling (interconnection applications are structured technical submissions; study tracking is deadline and milestone management; cost estimate analysis is a data comparison problem — all squarely in the builder's skillset). The technical engineering content (electrical load flow studies, protection relay settings) is the utility's work product, not the consultant's; the consultant's role is managing the process, responding to information requests, and identifying when the utility's timeline or cost estimate deviates from norms.
**MVP scope:** 3-4 weekends to build a project-intake and milestone-tracking tool for the interconnection process (using FERC's published interconnection rules and a target utility's tariff as the rule set), map the specific application requirements for 2-3 utilities in a target region, and build a cost-estimate comparison database from publicly available interconnection queue data (FERC eLibrary and state utility commission records publish this data).
**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — distribute through small solar project developers (found through state renewable energy associations and the interconnection queues themselves — FERC and state utility commissions publish their interconnection queues listing every pending project by developer name, making the prospect list essentially public), solar project finance attorneys, and commercial solar installers who frequently encounter clients stalled in interconnection.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $1,500-4,000/mo per active project retainer (scaled to project size and utility complexity); managing 5-8 active projects simultaneously (a realistic pipeline for a solo consultant, since each project needs 2-6 hours of management attention per month between study milestones) = **$10-25K/mo**, with individual project retainers running 18-36 months for the duration of the interconnection process.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Interconnection consulting generates multi-year retainer income per project — a developer who engages the service during the application phase stays engaged through each study phase and the final facilities agreement, generating continuous monthly revenue for 18-36 months per project without any re-selling. As the consultant builds a track record of successfully navigating interconnection for specific utilities, that expertise becomes a genuine competitive moat: developers choose their interconnection consultant partly based on who has successfully completed projects with the specific utility they're dealing with.
**Biggest risk:** Interconnection timelines are determined largely by utility processing speed, which is outside the consultant's control — a project that stalls in a utility queue for 24 months generates retainer income but also creates client frustration that can damage the relationship even when the delay is entirely the utility's doing. Set explicit expectations during engagement setup that interconnection timelines are utility-driven, and document every utility communication and delay to demonstrate active management rather than passive waiting.
**Growth path:** Side project → first 2-3 project retainers through developer contacts in state renewable energy associations, interconnection tracking tool live for one utility's tariff, first study-phase responses drafted. Replaces-part-time-income → 4-6 active project retainers across 2-3 utilities in one ISO/RTO region, queue data analysis adding value in cost-estimate challenges (~$7-15K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 7-10 active projects, expertise established for specific utilities where prior successful completions are documented, inbound referrals from project finance attorneys and developers, pushing past $12-25K/mo.

### 4. Independent Telecom Agent (Residual Commission Model)
**Problem:** Small and mid-size businesses — a 20-person law firm, a 50-person manufacturing operation, a multi-location retail chain — are chronically oversold on telecom services (internet, phone, SD-WAN, hosted PBX, cellular fleet plans) by carrier direct reps whose incentive is to lock them into the highest-cost contract possible. Most businesses renew the same telecom contract at the same (or higher) price with no competitive review, because neither their accountant nor their IT consultant has the carrier relationships or the time to run a competitive telecom bid on their behalf. An independent telecom agent represents the business's interests (not the carrier's), shops the account across multiple carriers, negotiates terms, and earns commission from the winning carrier — creating a rare alignment of interest: the agent only wins if the business gets a better deal.
**What to build:** A telecom advisory and agent practice — a current-state audit tool (documenting the business's existing services, contract terms, pricing, and end-of-term dates), a carrier comparison and bid management workflow (submitting the business's requirements to multiple carriers and normalizing the bids into a consistent comparison format), a contract review and term negotiation tracker (tracking SLAs, termination liability, and auto-renewal clauses across competing proposals), and an ongoing account management relationship (monitoring service performance and initiating re-bids at each contract end) — operating through a master agent agreement with a telecom master agent partner (companies like Telarus, Intelisys, or Sandler Partners aggregate independent agents, provide back-office support, and pay commissions from carriers).
**Skill fit:** Direct on the bid-normalization and contract-comparison tooling (transforming carrier proposals — each with different pricing structures, service definitions, and contract terms — into a consistent comparison format is exactly the structured-data comparison problem the builder handles well); joining a master agent (which handles carrier relationships, commission processing, and regulatory compliance) removes the carrier-contracting complexity from the solo agent's plate entirely.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weeks to select and join a master agent partner (most have a simple online application and onboarding process); 2-3 weekends for the current-state audit tool and carrier bid comparison template; the first accounts are worked manually through existing professional contacts before the tooling is optimized.
**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — distribute through IT managed service providers (MSPs) who manage client infrastructure but typically don't handle telecom procurement and will refer or co-sell for a split commission), commercial insurance agents (who interact with the same SMB decision-makers and often know when a client just signed a bad telecom renewal), and accountants (who see telecom on the P&L and know when it's anomalously high).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Telecom commissions: upfront spiff of $500-3,000 per new account placed, plus monthly residual of 8-15% of the monthly recurring charge (MRC) for the life of the contract. A business paying $3,000/mo for internet + hosted PBX generates $240-450/mo in residual commission indefinitely. Building a book of 40-60 SMB accounts averaging $2,000/mo in MRC = $80,000-120,000/mo in total managed MRC × 10% average residual = **$8-12K/mo in residual commissions** that recur without any re-selling effort, compounding as new accounts are placed on top of the existing book every month.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Telecom residuals are the purest compounding income model in this series — every account placed adds to a base that never resets, contracts run 2-3 years by default (locking in the residual stream), and the agent's income grows every month simply by not losing clients rather than by winning new ones. After 24-36 months of consistent account placement, the residual base alone can exceed $10-15K/mo without any new sales activity in that period.
**Biggest risk:** Master agents and carriers occasionally restructure commission plans, reducing residual rates or converting them to shorter-term structures — vet the master agent partner carefully (Telarus and Intelisys have the strongest reputations for commission reliability and protection), and diversify the book across multiple carriers rather than concentrating in one whose commission terms could change unilaterally.
**Growth path:** Side project → master agent agreement signed, first 3-5 SMB accounts placed through IT MSP and accountant referrals, first residual checks arriving ($500-1,500/mo from early accounts). Replaces-part-time-income → 15-20 accounts placed, residual base growing monthly, upfront spiffs supplementing while the residual compounds (~$4-7K/mo and climbing without new accounts). Replaces-full-time-salary → 40-60 accounts, residual base generating $8-12K/mo passively, new account placements each month adding to a base that is never subtracted from, pushing past $12-15K/mo.

### 5. Veterinary Practice Pet Insurance Billing Service
**Problem:** Pet insurance has grown dramatically — major carriers (Trupanion, Healthy Paws, Embrace, ASPCA Pet Health Insurance, Nationwide) now cover tens of millions of pets — but most veterinary practices still handle insurance claims awkwardly: they give clients a paper invoice to submit themselves, some practices accept direct reimbursement but struggle with the claim submission workflow, and almost none have a systematic process for tracking outstanding claims, appealing denials, or helping clients understand what's covered before care is provided. A veterinary practice that implements a smooth pet insurance workflow sees faster payment, higher client satisfaction, and more willingness to authorize recommended care — but the typical practice is run by veterinarians, not billing specialists, and nobody has time to build the process.
**What to build:** A veterinary pet insurance billing service — a per-visit claim submission workflow (submitting claims to the client's insurance carrier immediately after the visit using the carrier's API or portal, rather than waiting for the client to do it), a carrier-specific coverage verification tool (checking the client's policy benefits before care is provided, so the veterinarian knows what's likely covered when recommending a treatment plan), a denial tracking and appeal workflow (categorizing denials by carrier and reason code, drafting appeals with the required supporting medical record documentation), and a monthly performance report per practice (claims submitted, claims paid, denials by carrier, average days to payment) — charged as a flat monthly retainer per practice.
**Skill fit:** Direct on the claims data side (claim submission workflows, denial reason code analysis, carrier-specific coverage rules, and payment reconciliation are structured data problems; building the per-carrier coverage verification and denial pattern tools is data engineering work the builder does faster than a typical veterinary office manager). HIPAA for human healthcare doesn't apply to veterinary records (animals are property, not persons under HIPAA), removing the most significant compliance burden from the therapy RCM model covered in Session 12.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends for a carrier claim submission tracker and denial log for the 3-4 highest-volume pet insurance carriers (Trupanion, Healthy Paws, Embrace cover the majority of market share); the first 2-3 practice clients are onboarded manually while the per-carrier workflow is validated against real claim responses.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through veterinary practice management consultants (who advise practices on operations and billing), veterinary association continuing education events (where practice managers, not just veterinarians, attend), and direct outreach to multi-doctor veterinary practices (which generate higher claim volume and have more administrative staff who can champion the service internally).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $400-900/mo per veterinary practice (retainer scales with practice size: a 2-doctor practice is $400-500/mo; a 5-doctor specialty hospital is $700-900/mo); 15-25 active practice clients = **$8-18K/mo**, with the retainer base sticky since switching billing services mid-year disrupts the practice's active claim pipeline and ongoing carrier relationships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Pet insurance penetration is growing rapidly (from under 3% of US pets insured in 2020 toward an expected 10%+ by 2030), meaning the claim volume at every veterinary practice is increasing every year without any additional client acquisition effort — the service naturally earns more from existing clients as more of their patients are insured, creating a built-in revenue growth mechanism within the existing client base.
**Biggest risk:** Pet insurance carriers have different and sometimes opaque coverage determination rules, and a denied claim that the practice and client expected to be covered creates frustration directed at the billing service rather than the insurer — build robust pre-visit coverage verification as the primary value driver so clients are informed before care rather than surprised by a denial after, shifting the service's positioning from reactive claims filing to proactive coverage guidance.
**Growth path:** Side project → first 2-3 veterinary practice retainer clients through practice management consultant referrals, carrier submission workflow live for Trupanion and Healthy Paws (the two largest carriers), denial tracking tool validated. Replaces-part-time-income → 7-10 active practice clients, 2-3 referral partners generating consistent new practice introductions, denial appeal workflow systematized per carrier (~$4-8K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 15-25 active practices, multi-doctor specialty hospital clients adding higher-revenue accounts, coverage verification tool differentiating from any manual billing alternative, pushing past $10-18K/mo.

## This Session's Pick: Start With #5, Track #4 as the Highest-Compounding Parallel Bet

**Clearest, fastest start:** Idea #5 (veterinary pet insurance billing) has the shortest path to a paying client in this batch — the distribution channel (veterinary practice management consultants and multi-doctor practices) is reachable by direct outreach within days, no licensing is required, HIPAA complexity is absent (veterinary records are not covered), and the retainer model starts generating monthly recurring revenue from the first practice onboarded. The skill fit on the claims data and denial pattern tooling is direct, and the built-in revenue growth (more insured pets each year means more claim volume from existing practices) creates a compounding dynamic even without adding new clients.

**Highest-compounding parallel bet:** Idea #4 (independent telecom agent) has the most purely passive long-term income structure of this session — once an account is placed and the contract is live, the residual arrives monthly without any additional work, stacking on top of every prior month's placements. The master agent model removes the carrier contracting complexity entirely, leaving the solo agent to focus on account development and bid management. Building the telecom book alongside veterinary billing shares the same referral network (accountants and IT consultants know both SMB telecom clients and veterinary practice clients), making the two ideas natural complements in a shared outreach strategy rather than competing demands on a limited time budget.
