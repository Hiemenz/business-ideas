### 1. Vendor Contract Renewal Calendar for Operations Managers
**Problem:** An operations manager at a company with 30-150 employees manages 40-80 vendor relationships: software subscriptions, equipment leases, office supply contracts, cleaning service agreements, security monitoring contracts, copier leases, and dozens of others. Each vendor has a renewal date, an auto-renewal clause (often 30-60 days' notice required to cancel before the renewal locks in), and a contract rate that was negotiated under different market conditions. The operations manager finds out a contract auto-renewed at a bad rate by seeing the charge on the corporate card — after the cancellation window has already closed. Every operations manager who has been burned by a missed cancellation window now has a recurring anxiety: which contract is auto-renewing next month that I don't know about? Manual tracking in a spreadsheet decays within weeks as new contracts are added and old ones are forgotten.
**What to build:** A vendor contract renewal calendar — the operations manager uploads each vendor contract as a PDF (or enters the key terms manually: vendor name, contract value, renewal date, cancellation notice required, auto-renewal clause). For PDF uploads, the tool extracts renewal date and auto-renewal notice period using PDF parsing on standardized contract language (a heuristic parser handles the majority of standard SaaS and service contracts). The core output: a rolling 90-day renewal calendar showing every contract renewing in the next 90 days, sorted by the cancellation notice deadline (the last day to cancel without triggering automatic renewal). Alerts at 90, 60, and 30 days before each renewal, and a final alert at the cancellation notice deadline: "Your contract with [Vendor] auto-renews on Oct 15. Last day to cancel without triggering renewal: Sep 14 (today)." A vendor spend dashboard: total annual contract value by category, average cost per vendor, and a flag for contracts where the per-unit rate hasn't been renegotiated in 2+ years.
**Skill fit:** PDF contract parsing using pdfplumber or PyMuPDF for text extraction, with regex patterns for common renewal date formats ("This agreement shall automatically renew for successive one-year terms unless either party provides written notice of non-renewal at least 30 days prior"). The calendar view is a date-sorted list with status indicators. Alert emails via SendGrid. The spend dashboard is a category-tagged aggregation. The hardest part is the PDF parser — but standard SaaS and service contracts are structured enough that a heuristic parser handles 70-80% of uploads accurately, with manual entry as the fallback.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — manual contract entry form (vendor, value, renewal date, notice period), rolling 90-day calendar, and email alerts at configured intervals. PDF extraction is V2. An operations manager who enters 40 contracts in an hour and immediately sees that 3 are renewing in the next 45 days — two of which they had forgotten — has gotten immediate value before the first billing cycle.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — operations managers and office managers are reachable through operations-focused communities (Operations Nation, Ops community Slack groups), LinkedIn (search "operations manager" + any industry), and CFO Connect (CFOs who manage the same renewal exposure from the finance side). A post: "built a tool that tells you which vendor contracts are auto-renewing before the cancellation window closes" — every operations manager has a horror story to contribute.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149-349/month per company. Average company at $229/month. 45 companies × $229/month = **$10,305/month**. Upsell: a vendor negotiation benchmarking report ($79/month add-on) — showing what comparable companies pay for the same vendor categories (sourced from anonymized user data), so the operations manager walks into a renewal negotiation knowing whether their current rate is above or below market.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** New vendor contracts are signed continuously as the company grows. The contract history — renewal dates, negotiated rates, contract terms — accumulates in the tool, making it the authoritative vendor record for the company. An operations manager who has 60 contracts tracked in the system won't rebuild that in a spreadsheet.
**Biggest risk:** Contract upload resistance — uploading confidential vendor contracts to a third-party tool requires trust in data security. Mitigation: offer an on-premise or self-hosted option for security-sensitive companies; emphasize SOC 2 compliance in the roadmap; and for the MVP, position the manual entry path (enter key terms only, not the contract itself) as the default.
**Growth path:** Side project → manual contract entry, 90-day calendar, and email alerts live, first 5-10 company clients. Replaces-part-time-income → 15-20 companies, add PDF extraction for common contract formats, add the vendor benchmarking module. Replaces-full-time-salary → 45+ companies, add a team workflow (two people review each renewal — operations manager flags, CFO approves cancel/renew decision), expand to legal departments managing client and supplier contracts.

### 2. Schedule and Quality Control for Commercial Cleaning Companies
**Problem:** A commercial cleaning company with 15-40 cleaners services 60-150 recurring accounts on weekly, biweekly, or monthly schedules. Managing substitutions (a cleaner calls out sick at 4 PM for a 6 PM shift), quality check visits (the account manager needs to inspect 10-15 accounts per week but has no systematic way to track which accounts were last inspected), and client complaints (a client emails about a missed trash can in the conference room and nobody records it against the account) is done across a shared Google Calendar, a group text, and the account manager's memory. When a client cancels after 18 months without warning, the cleaning company has no historical record of the quality check visits or complaints that preceded the cancellation — and no ability to identify which other accounts are at similar risk.
**What to build:** A scheduling and quality control tool for commercial cleaning companies — a recurring schedule engine where each account's service cadence (weekly on Thursdays, biweekly on Tuesdays) is configured once, and the weekly schedule is auto-generated for the operations manager. A sub-out flow: when a cleaner can't make their shift, the manager marks them unavailable and the tool shows which other cleaners on the roster are available and unassigned for that time slot. A quality check log: the account manager checks in at a client location via a mobile web form, rates the cleanliness of 5 standard areas (entry, restrooms, breakroom, offices, trash), adds a photo if anything is substandard, and submits. Every quality check is stored against the account record. A complaint log: client complaints (via email, phone, or the tool's client portal) are logged per account and linked to the cleaner assigned that visit. An account health dashboard: every account, its last quality check date, its complaint count in the last 90 days, and a simple risk flag (accounts with 2+ complaints and no quality check in 30+ days are flagged).
**Skill fit:** The recurring schedule engine is a cadence-based date generator (given a frequency and day of week, produce the next N service dates). The sub-out flow is a roster availability check. The quality check form is a mobile-optimized web form with photo upload to S3. The complaint log is a simple event record per account. The account health dashboard is a per-account aggregation of check dates and complaint counts. No complex backend.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — recurring schedule engine, weekly schedule view, quality check mobile form with photo, complaint log, and account health dashboard. An operations manager who can see, for the first time, which accounts haven't had a quality check in 45+ days — and who attributes a client cancellation to 3 logged complaints that nobody followed up on — has the data to run their business differently.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — commercial cleaning company owners are reachable through the Building Service Contractors Association International (BSCAI) member community, cleaning industry Facebook groups (Commercial Cleaning Business Owners — 20,000+ members), and local janitorial supply distributors who have relationships with multiple cleaning companies. A case study showing "prevented a $24,000/year account cancellation by catching a complaint pattern early" is highly compelling in those communities.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-449/month per company depending on account and cleaner count. Average company at $299/month. 35 companies × $299/month = **$10,465/month**. Upsell: a client communication portal ($79/month add-on) — a web portal where each client can log service requests, view their upcoming cleaning schedule, and see their account's quality check history, reducing the volume of "what time are you coming this week?" calls to the operations manager.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** The recurring schedule generates itself every week without any input from the manager. The quality check and complaint history accumulates per account, making the account health dashboard more meaningful with each passing month. A cleaning company with 12 months of account health data — showing which accounts have the highest complaint frequency by building type or cleaner assignment — has operational intelligence they didn't have before.
**Biggest risk:** Cleaner tech adoption — some cleaning crews are not smartphone-comfortable. Mitigation: the quality check form is a mobile web page (no app download), the schedule is viewable via SMS link, and the operations manager can log quality checks on behalf of cleaners who prefer to report verbally.
**Growth path:** Side project → recurring schedule, quality check form, complaint log, and account health dashboard live, first 5-8 company clients through BSCAI outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-18 companies, add client communication portal, add a cleaner performance dashboard (quality check scores by cleaner across all their assigned accounts). Replaces-full-time-salary → 35+ companies, add a client invoicing integration (generate recurring invoices from the confirmed service schedule), expand to residential cleaning companies with similar scheduling and quality dynamics.

### 3. Punch List and Close-Out Management for Home Builders
**Problem:** The final 2-4 weeks of a home construction project generate a punch list — dozens of small defects and incomplete items that need to be resolved before the owner takes possession: a door that doesn't close flush, a paint touch-up in the master bedroom, a missing outlet cover in the garage, a grout line that needs caulking in the bathroom. The builder's superintendent walks the home with the buyer, writes items on a paper form or in a Notes app, assigns each item to the responsible subcontractor via a text message, and then tracks resolution by texting the sub again and physically re-walking the home. Across a builder completing 30-100 homes per year, each with 20-60 punch list items, managing this in text messages and paper forms means items get missed, subs forget, and the close-out drags on — delaying the certificate of occupancy, delaying the buyer's move-in, and creating the exact bad experience that generates 1-star reviews right at the point when the buyer would otherwise be most excited.
**What to build:** A mobile-first punch list and close-out management tool — the superintendent walks the home with their phone, taps to add each punch list item (voice-to-text description, photo, room tag, and responsible sub selected from a dropdown), and submits. Each item is immediately sent to the responsible sub as an SMS with the photo and a due date. The sub receives a link where they can mark the item complete and upload a completion photo. The superintendent gets a notification and can approve the completion from their phone or flag it for re-inspection. A close-out dashboard per home: total items, items completed, items pending by sub, overdue items (past due date with no completion photo). A builder-level view across all active homes: close-out percentage per home, subs with the highest open item counts, and a projected close date based on current completion velocity. Automated escalation: items overdue by 3+ days trigger an escalation alert to the superintendent.
**Skill fit:** The punch list item model is simple: description, photo (S3), room, sub, due date, status. The SMS delivery to subs is Twilio. The sub completion flow is a mobile-optimized page linked from the SMS — no account required for the sub. The close-out dashboard is a status aggregation per home. The builder-level view is a roll-up aggregation across homes. Mobile-first photo capture is a standard HTML file input. The entire product is buildable without a native app.
**MVP scope:** 2 weekends — punch list item creation via mobile web form with photo, SMS notification to sub, sub completion flow, and the per-home close-out dashboard. A superintendent who goes from managing 45 punch list items across 8 text message threads to seeing them all in one dashboard — and who closes a home 5 days faster than average — has a product that justifies itself on the first project.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — home builders are reachable through the National Association of Home Builders (NAHB) member community, local Home Builders Association (HBA) chapters, and Buildertrend and CoConstruct user communities. Construction technology consultants who work with small builders on software adoption are a high-leverage referral channel.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-499/month per builder depending on annual home volume. Average builder at $329/month. 32 builders × $329/month = **$10,528/month**. Upsell: a buyer communication module ($79/month add-on) — the buyer gets a read-only portal showing their home's punch list status in real time ("14 of 23 items complete"), eliminating the daily "how many items are left?" calls to the builder's office.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new home starts a new punch list, automatically generating close-out tracking without any configuration. The sub completion photo history per home becomes the builder's warranty defense library — if a buyer claims a window was never fixed, the tool shows the completion photo and timestamp. That risk-reduction value alone justifies the subscription to a builder who has faced a warranty dispute.
**Biggest risk:** Sub adoption friction — adding a new system for subs who are already being texted by 5 different builders. Mitigation: the sub interaction is 100% SMS-based with a mobile-optimized single-page completion form — no app, no account, no training. The sub receives a text with a photo and a link; they tap the link, upload their photo, and hit complete. That's the entire sub workflow.
**Growth path:** Side project → mobile punch list, SMS sub notification, sub completion flow, and dashboard live, first 5-8 builder clients through NAHB chapter outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-18 builders, add buyer communication portal, add Buildertrend integration for importing home and sub data. Replaces-full-time-salary → 32+ builders, add warranty item tracking (close-out items that become warranty claims are linked in the same system), expand to commercial tenant improvement (TI) contractors with identical punch list dynamics.

### 4. Reorder Point Alerts for Small Manufacturers
**Problem:** A small manufacturer producing physical goods — a craft food company, a supplement brand, a contract electronics assembler — runs out of a critical component and shuts down production for 3-5 days while waiting for an emergency order. The inventory levels for that component existed in a spreadsheet or in their ERP system, but nobody was watching them. The production manager knows intellectually that the reorder point for Component X is 500 units (enough to cover 10 days of production at current run rate while waiting for the supplier's 7-day lead time), but when day-to-day production pressure is high, nobody is manually checking inventory levels against reorder thresholds. Enterprise ERP systems (NetSuite, SAP) handle this automatically, but they cost $30,000-100,000/year — priced entirely out of range for a manufacturer doing $500K-$5M in annual revenue.
**What to build:** A reorder point alert system for small manufacturers — a lightweight inventory monitoring tool where each component or raw material is configured with: current stock level, average daily consumption (calculated from recent production history or entered manually), supplier lead time (in days), and a safety stock buffer (the extra inventory beyond the reorder point the owner wants to hold). The tool calculates the reorder point automatically: average daily consumption × (lead time + safety stock days). Each day, the tool checks current inventory levels (updated via manual entry, a CSV import from the manufacturer's existing inventory tool, or direct integration with QBO inventory or Katana MRP) against the computed reorder point and fires an alert when any component drops below threshold: "Component X is at 480 units. Reorder point: 500 units. Supplier lead time: 7 days. Suggested order quantity: 1,000 units." A component dashboard: every tracked component, current stock, reorder point, days of supply remaining at current consumption rate, and the last order date and quantity.
**Skill fit:** The reorder point calculation is deterministic arithmetic (reorder point = daily consumption × lead time + safety stock). The alert logic is a threshold comparison run daily per component. The consumption rate calculation is a rolling average from the last 30 days of production data (entered manually or imported). QBO inventory integration via the QBO API for manufacturers already using QBO's inventory module. Katana MRP has an API for manufacturers using purpose-built MRP software. The dashboard is a standard inventory status view.
**MVP scope:** 1-2 weekends — manual component entry with reorder point configuration, daily threshold check, email alert, and the component dashboard. A production manager who averts a 3-day production shutdown by ordering a component 4 days before it would have run out — saving $8,000-15,000 in lost production time — has justified the tool's annual cost in a single event.
**Time to first $:** 14-30 days — small manufacturers are reachable through the National Federation of Independent Business (NFIB), the Manufacturing Extension Partnership (MEP) network (federally funded advisors who work with small manufacturers), and manufacturing-specific Facebook groups and LinkedIn communities. A simple demo — "enter your components, set your lead times, get an email before you run out" — is immediately compelling to any production manager who has ever shut down a line waiting for parts.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $99-299/month per manufacturer depending on component count and integration tier. Average manufacturer at $179/month. 60 manufacturers × $179/month = **$10,740/month**. Upsell: a supplier performance tracker ($49/month add-on) — logging actual delivery date vs. promised delivery date per order per supplier, computing supplier on-time delivery rate over the last 12 months, so the manufacturer knows which supplier's lead time estimates are reliable and which require a larger safety stock buffer.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Production continues every day, consuming components and triggering reorder alerts automatically. The consumption history per component accumulates, making the average daily consumption calculation more accurate as the production mix evolves. A manufacturer who has 6 months of consumption history and supplier performance data in the tool has a procurement intelligence baseline they didn't have before.
**Biggest risk:** Manual inventory update discipline — if the manufacturer doesn't update current stock levels after each production run, the alert thresholds become inaccurate. Mitigation: the daily end-of-production SMS check-in (a text to the production manager with a link to update stock levels for the components consumed that day) reduces the friction of manual updates; and integrations with QBO inventory and Katana MRP eliminate the manual step entirely for manufacturers already using those systems.
**Growth path:** Side project → manual component entry, reorder point calculation, daily alert, and dashboard live, first 10-15 manufacturer clients through MEP network outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 25-35 manufacturers, add QBO inventory integration, add Katana MRP integration, add supplier performance tracker. Replaces-full-time-salary → 60+ manufacturers, add a production scheduling view (which production runs can be completed with current inventory vs. which are blocked by component shortages), expand to restaurant groups tracking perishable ingredient reorder points.

### 5. Commission Statement Reconciliation for Independent Insurance Agencies
**Problem:** An independent insurance agency earning commissions from 8-20 carriers receives a separate commission statement from each carrier every month — each in a different format, with different column names, different policy identifier conventions, and different calculation methodologies. The agency principal or office manager spends 6-10 hours per month downloading these statements, converting them to a consistent format, matching each commission line to the corresponding policy in their agency management system (Applied Epic, Hawksoft, QQ Catalyst), and identifying discrepancies where the carrier paid less than expected or failed to pay on a renewed policy altogether. Commission errors and omissions are common — industry estimates suggest 3-7% of commissions owed to independent agencies go uncollected annually because the reconciliation is too labor-intensive to do thoroughly. For an agency earning $400,000/year in commissions, that's $12,000-28,000 in money on the table.
**What to build:** A commission statement reconciliation tool — the agency uploads each carrier's commission statement as a CSV or PDF, and the tool normalizes the data to a standard schema (policy number, carrier, commission amount, policy type, effective date) using carrier-specific parsing rules (a library of pre-built parsers for the 30 most common carrier statement formats). The tool matches each statement line against the agency's policy records — pulled from their agency management system via API or a CSV export — and surfaces three outputs: matched policies (commission received matches expectation), discrepancies (commission received differs from calculated expectation by more than $X), and missing policies (a policy the agency expected to receive commission on that doesn't appear in the carrier statement at all). A discrepancy resolution workflow: the agency flags each discrepancy for follow-up with the carrier, and the tool tracks the resolution status. A monthly commission dashboard: total expected, total received, total variance, and a carrier-by-carrier breakdown showing which carriers have the most discrepancy history.
**Skill fit:** CSV parsing and normalization across multiple carrier formats (a library of regex-based column mappers per carrier). PDF extraction for carriers that only provide PDF statements. Applied Epic has an API (available to technology partners); Hawksoft and QQ Catalyst have CSV export functions. The matching logic is a fuzzy join on policy number + carrier + effective date, with configurable tolerance for policy number format variations across systems. The discrepancy resolution workflow is a simple status state machine. This is a data engineering problem with a clear output — a natural fit for the builder's background.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends — CSV upload and normalization for the top 5 carrier statement formats (State Farm, Travelers, Nationwide, Progressive, Allstate — covering the majority of most agencies' volume), manual policy record CSV import, matching logic with discrepancy flagging, and the commission dashboard. An agency that finds $4,200 in underpaid commissions in the first month has covered the tool's annual cost in one reconciliation run.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — independent insurance agency principals are reachable through the Independent Insurance Agents & Brokers of America (IIABA / Big "I") member community, Applied Epic and Hawksoft user groups, and insurance agency Facebook groups (Insurance Agency Owners Alliance — 15,000+ members). A commission consultant who works with agencies on revenue optimization is a high-leverage referral channel — they're already having the "you're leaving money on the table" conversation.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-499/month per agency depending on carrier count and commission volume. Average agency at $329/month. 32 agencies × $329/month = **$10,528/month**. Upsell: a carrier performance benchmarking report ($79/month add-on) — showing which of the agency's carriers have the highest historical error rate and the longest average resolution time for disputed commissions, so the agency principal knows which carriers to audit most carefully each month.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Commission statements arrive monthly from every carrier, triggering a new reconciliation run automatically. The carrier parser library improves as new statement formats are added. An agency with 12 months of reconciliation history — and a documented list of carriers where discrepancies are most common — has a negotiating asset with those carriers at contract renewal time.
**Biggest risk:** Carrier statement format variability — even within one carrier, statement formats change when the carrier upgrades their billing system. Mitigation: the parser library is versioned per carrier, with a fallback to a generic CSV normalizer when the format doesn't match the expected template; and agencies can flag a "format not recognized" error that triggers a human-assisted parser update within 48 hours.
**Growth path:** Side project → top-5 carrier CSV parsers, policy record import, matching logic, and commission dashboard live, first 5-8 agency clients through Big "I" outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-18 agencies, add PDF extraction for carriers that only provide PDF statements, add Applied Epic API integration. Replaces-full-time-salary → 32+ agencies, add carrier performance benchmarking module, expand to Medicare supplement and ACA health insurance agencies with identical multi-carrier commission reconciliation dynamics.
