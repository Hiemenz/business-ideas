### 1. Auto Salvage Yard / Vehicle Recycler NMVTIS Reporting, State License & Environmental Fluid-Handling Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent auto salvage yards and vehicle recyclers acquire wrecked, totaled, and end-of-life vehicles and must report every vehicle obtained and every vehicle crushed or otherwise permanently destroyed to the National Motor Vehicle Title Information System (NMVTIS) within the timeframe their state requires (commonly 5-30 days), since NMVTIS exists specifically to prevent title-washing and stolen-vehicle laundering, and a yard with a pattern of late or missing NMVTIS reports draws exactly the kind of state DMV or attorney-general scrutiny that can suspend its salvage license. Every yard must also hold a state salvage-dealer or auto-recycler license — commonly requiring a surety bond, a fenced and screened storage-yard inspection, and a separate parts-reseller registration — renewed annually or biennially depending on the state. Before a vehicle is crushed, state and EPA rules require draining and properly disposing of motor oil, coolant, gasoline, refrigerant, and removing mercury switches and lead wheel weights, with many states requiring a certified drain log per vehicle as part of an environmental compliance file a state inspector can request at any time. A yard processing 30-80 vehicles a month currently tracks NMVTIS report status, license renewal, and per-vehicle drain/disposal documentation across a paper intake ledger and a separate spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific vehicle was NMVTIS-reported and environmentally processed before its parts are released for sale or the hulk is sent to the crusher.

**What to build:** A per-vehicle intake-to-disposition ledger that generates the required NMVTIS report (obtained, and separately, crushed/destroyed) with a due-date countdown per state's specific reporting window; a state salvage-dealer license and bond renewal tracker; a per-vehicle environmental drain/disposal checklist (oil, coolant, refrigerant, mercury switches, lead weights) gating a vehicle from being marked ready-for-crush until every item is logged; and a portfolio dashboard flagging any vehicle whose NMVTIS report is overdue before a state audit finds it first.

**Skill fit:** NMVTIS reporting is a structured public API/data-submission problem, and matching a state's specific reporting-window rule against each vehicle's intake date is a deadline-computation-and-alert pattern — a strong fit for a developer with data-pipeline experience and no salvage-industry credential required to build it.

**MVP scope:** Two to three weekends: intake ledger with NMVTIS report-status tracking, license/bond renewal calendar, and the per-vehicle drain checklist. First 5-8 clients from the Automotive Recyclers Association (ARA) member directory and state auto-recyclers association chapter rosters.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — direct outreach to ARA-member independent salvage yards, offering a free NMVTIS reporting-gap check (cross-referencing a yard's recent intake log against what's actually been reported) as the opening hook.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $250-500/mo per yard depending on monthly vehicle throughput. At 30 yard clients × $350/mo average = $10,500/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every vehicle acquired and every vehicle crushed generates its own independent NMVTIS reporting deadline, and the environmental drain-and-disposal checklist recurs on every single unit processed — a continuous, throughput-driven compliance stream tied directly to the yard's own volume of business.

**Biggest risk:** Larger recycler chains (LKQ, Pull-A-Part) run their own internal systems; differentiate by targeting the long tail of independently owned yards processing under 100 vehicles/month, where a principal is still tracking NMVTIS status by hand.

**Growth path:** Side project serving 6-8 independent yards → ARA regional conference presence → 30+ clients replaces-full-time-salary.

### 2. Household Goods Moving Company FMCSA Operating Authority, Tariff & Valuation-Disclosure Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Interstate household goods moving companies must hold FMCSA operating authority (an MC number specifically for household goods carriage, separate from a general USDOT number) alongside process-agent designations (BOC-3 filing) in every state they operate, and must maintain and follow a published tariff for interstate rates. Before every interstate move, FMCSA requires the mover to provide the shipper the "Your Rights and Responsibilities When You Move" booklet and a binding or non-binding written estimate, and to document the shipper's affirmative choice between released-value (minimal, weight-based) and full-value protection valuation coverage — a missing or improperly documented valuation election is one of the most common findings in an FMCSA compliance review and exposes the mover to liability disputes it can't win without the signed election on file. Movers must also participate in a FMCSA-qualified arbitration program for interstate loss-and-damage disputes and keep that enrollment current. A small mover running 15-40 interstate moves a month currently tracks operating-authority renewal, BOC-3 process-agent coverage, and per-move valuation-election documentation on a dispatcher's spreadsheet, with no systematic check confirming every move going out the door today has a signed estimate and valuation election on file before the truck leaves.

**What to build:** An FMCSA operating-authority and BOC-3 process-agent renewal tracker; a per-move document checklist (rights-and-responsibilities booklet delivered, written estimate, signed valuation-coverage election) gating a move from being marked dispatch-ready until every required document is on file; a published-tariff version log tied to each move's quoted rate; and an arbitration-program enrollment tracker.

**Skill fit:** FMCSA's published household-goods-carrier regulations (49 CFR Part 375/376) map cleanly into a per-move document-completeness gate, the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly — a data-modeling and workflow problem well within reach of a developer with no trucking-industry credential of their own required to build it.

**MVP scope:** Two to three weekends: operating-authority/BOC-3 renewal tracker, the per-move document-completeness gate, and the tariff version log. First 5-8 clients from the American Trucking Associations' Moving & Storage Conference member directory and state moving-and-storage association rosters.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — direct outreach to small interstate movers identified in state moving-and-storage association directories, offering a free per-move document audit as the opening hook.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $300-600/mo per moving company depending on monthly move volume. At 25 mover clients × $420/mo average = $10,500/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every single interstate move reopens the valuation-election and document-completeness question, and operating-authority and BOC-3 renewals recur on their own independent clocks — a continuous, move-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the company's own booking calendar.

**Biggest risk:** National van lines (Allied, United, Mayflower) run their own compliance systems for their agent networks; differentiate by targeting small independent interstate movers operating outside a major van-line affiliation, where a dispatcher is still tracking valuation elections by hand.

**Growth path:** Side project serving 5-6 small movers → Moving & Storage Conference channel presence → 25+ clients replaces-full-time-salary.

### 3. Judgment Recovery / Repossession Agency State License, Bond & Breach-of-the-Peace Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent repossession agencies performing self-help repossession of vehicles, equipment, and other secured collateral on behalf of lenders operate under UCC Article 9's "no breach of the peace" standard — a legal line that isn't self-defining, and a repossession that crosses it (entering a closed garage, physical confrontation with the debtor) exposes the agency, and often the lender who hired it, to a wrongful-repossession lawsuit with real damages. Most states require repossession agents to hold a separate state recovery-agent or repossessor license, distinct from a bail-enforcement license, commonly requiring a surety bond, a criminal-background check, and completion of a state-approved training course renewed periodically. State law and lender contracts typically require a specific post-repossession notice — the Notice of Sale or right-to-cure letter — sent to the debtor within a fixed window (commonly 10-15 days) before the collateral can be sold at auction, and a missed or defective notice can void the lender's ability to pursue a deficiency judgment, a mistake the lender will trace straight back to the agency that handled the repo. An agency running 10-30 field agents recovering collateral for several lender clients simultaneously currently tracks agent licensing/bond status, per-repossession incident documentation (photos, timestamps, location, any contact with the debtor), and post-repo notice deadlines on a dispatcher's spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific repo was documented cleanly and its notice deadline is on track before a lender's compliance department asks.

**What to build:** A per-agent state license, bond, and training-renewal tracker; a per-repossession incident log (timestamped photos, GPS location, any debtor contact noted) that builds a defensible breach-of-the-peace record automatically; a post-repossession notice deadline tracker computing each state's specific notice window from the repo date and flagging any file approaching its deadline unsent; and a per-lender-client reporting dashboard rolling up recovery activity for the lender's own compliance file.

**Skill fit:** State recovery-agent licensing rules and each state's notice-of-sale timeline are public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-agent and per-repossession trigger, and the incident-documentation log (photo/GPS/timestamp capture building a legal defense record) is a straightforward mobile-data-capture and workflow problem for a developer with no repossession-industry credential of their own required to build it.

**MVP scope:** Two to three weekends: agent license/bond tracker, the incident-documentation log with photo/GPS capture, and the notice-deadline tracker. First 5-8 clients through the American Recovery Association (ARA — the repossession industry's trade group) member directory.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — direct outreach to American Recovery Association member agencies, offering a free breach-of-the-peace documentation audit of recent repo files as the opening hook.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $250-500/mo per agency depending on field-agent headcount and repossession volume. At 30 agency clients × $350/mo average = $10,500/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every single repossession generates its own incident-documentation and notice-deadline event, and every agent's license/bond recurs on its own independent renewal clock — a continuous, volume-and-headcount-driven compliance stream tied directly to the agency's own caseload.

**Biggest risk:** A wrongful-repossession lawsuit is a serious liability event the tool can't fully prevent, only document defensibly; mitigate by positioning the product explicitly as a documentation and deadline-compliance aid, not a legal-judgment tool, and by building the incident log to capture exactly the evidence a defense attorney would need.

**Growth path:** Side project serving 5-6 small agencies → American Recovery Association conference presence → 30+ clients replaces-full-time-salary.

### 4. Elevator & Escalator Service Contractor QEI Inspection, Mechanic Licensure & Per-Unit Certificate of Operation Tracker

**Problem:** Independent elevator and escalator service contractors maintain and inspect vertical-transportation equipment under ASME A17.1 (elevators) and A18.1 (platform lifts) — a code adopted with variations by nearly every state and many municipalities. Technicians performing maintenance must hold a state elevator-mechanic license, renewed periodically with continuing-education requirements, while technicians performing code-mandated inspections must separately hold a national Qualified Elevator Inspector (QEI) certification through the National Association of Elevator Safety Authorities (NAESA International), renewed on its own multi-year cycle. Critically, every individual elevator or escalator unit a contractor services carries its own state- or city-issued Certificate of Operation, renewed on a periodic (commonly annual) cycle tied to a passed inspection — a contractor servicing an apartment building, hospital, or office tower with 5-40 individual units has to track every single unit's own Certificate of Operation expiration and inspection-due date independently, since a building operating even one elevator past its Certificate of Operation expiration is operating illegally and exposes both the building owner and the servicing contractor to a stop-use order the moment a code official notices. A contractor running 8-15 mechanics and QEI inspectors across a portfolio of 200-500 individual units currently tracks mechanic/QEI license status and each unit's own Certificate of Operation cycle on a service-manager's spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming a technician dispatched to inspect or service a specific unit today is both currently licensed/certified and that the unit's own paperwork is on track.

**What to build:** A mechanic-license and QEI-certification renewal tracker per technician; a per-unit Certificate of Operation tracker across every building/unit in the contractor's service portfolio, computing each unit's own inspection-due date and flagging units approaching or past expiration; and a "cleared to service/inspect" gate cross-referencing a technician's current licensure against the unit type being serviced before a work order is dispatched.

**Skill fit:** State elevator-mechanic licensing rules, NAESA's published QEI renewal cycle, and each jurisdiction's Certificate of Operation inspection-interval rule are public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-technician and per-unit trigger — the same deterministic deadline-and-gate pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a vertical-transportation niche entirely distinct from the boiler & pressure vessel service contractor idea already in this repo, whose ASME/NBIC chief-inspector framework covers different equipment under a different code body with no QEI credential or per-unit Certificate of Operation mechanic at all.

**MVP scope:** Two to three weekends: mechanic/QEI license tracker, the per-unit Certificate of Operation tracker, and the service-dispatch eligibility gate. First 5-8 clients through the National Association of Elevator Contractors (NAEC) member directory.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — direct outreach to NAEC-member small and mid-size independent elevator service contractors, offering a free portfolio audit of their unit-level Certificate of Operation status as the opening hook.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $400-800/mo per contractor depending on the size of the unit portfolio serviced. At 20 contractor clients × $550/mo average = $11,000/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every individual unit in a growing service portfolio carries its own independent Certificate of Operation renewal cycle, and every technician's mechanic license and QEI certification recur on their own clocks — a continuous, portfolio-and-headcount-driven compliance stream tied directly to the contractor's own client growth.

**Biggest risk:** The largest national elevator companies (Otis, Kone, Schindler, ThyssenKrupp) run their own internal systems; differentiate by targeting the independent regional service contractors who compete against them on price and responsiveness but lack in-house compliance software.

**Growth path:** Side project serving 4-6 small contractors → NAEC regional chapter presence → 20+ clients replaces-full-time-salary.

### 5. Athletic Trainer (AT) Multi-Site Practice License, BOC Certification & Facility Coverage-Assignment Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Athletic trainers who cover multiple high schools, colleges, or industrial/occupational-health clients under a staffing or outreach-services contract — increasingly the model for AT groups contracted by hospital sports-medicine departments to staff several school districts at once — must hold a state AT license (required in nearly every state under its own practice act) and national certification through the Board of Certification for the Athletic Trainer (BOC), renewed on a rolling multi-year reporting period requiring continuing-education units split across specific required domains (evidence-based practice, and others BOC designates). Every facility an AT covers independently verifies and documents that AT's credentialing before granting sideline/clinical coverage authorization, on its own renewal cycle set by the school district's athletics department or the hospital's credentialing office — an AT covering six schools across a football and basketball season has six separate facility-authorization clocks running simultaneously alongside their own state license and BOC cycle. Because an athletic trainer is frequently the only medical provider on-site at a practice or game, a coverage gap — an AT assigned to a Friday-night game whose state license quietly lapsed, or whose facility authorization at that specific school was never renewed — is discovered at the worst possible moment, during an athlete injury, when the AT's authority to act is exactly what a subsequent liability review examines first. A multi-site AT group covering 6-15 facilities with 4-10 staff ATs currently tracks state license and BOC cycle status per AT, plus per-facility authorization renewal, on a coordinator's shared spreadsheet, with no systematic cross-check confirming the AT assigned to tonight's game at a specific school is currently licensed, BOC-current, and authorized at that facility.

**What to build:** A state license and BOC certification-cycle tracker per AT with a CEU-domain breakdown; a per-facility authorization renewal tracker across every school, college, or industrial client the group covers; and a game/event coverage-assignment gate cross-referencing the assigned AT's current license, BOC status, and facility authorization before the assignment is confirmed on the schedule.

**Skill fit:** State AT-licensure renewal rules and BOC's published CEU-cycle requirements are public, structured professional data with a concrete per-AT and per-facility trigger, and the coverage-assignment gate is the same deterministic "block the assignment until the compliance record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a sports-medicine staffing niche entirely distinct from the Physical Therapy Direct Access idea already in this repo, which tracks a different licensed profession's own state-board framework with no BOC credential or per-facility event-coverage-assignment mechanic of any kind.

**MVP scope:** Two to three weekends: the license/BOC-cycle tracker, the per-facility authorization tracker, and the coverage-assignment eligibility gate. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to hospital-system sports-medicine outreach programs and independent multi-site AT staffing groups.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the National Athletic Trainers' Association (NATA) state affiliate directories and district meetings reach exactly the multi-site AT groups currently reconciling license, BOC, and facility-authorization status on a shared spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-399/mo per AT group depending on staff headcount and number of facilities covered. At 30 group clients × $300/mo average = $9,000/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every game, practice, and event covered reopens the coverage-assignment eligibility question, the BOC reporting-period cycle recurs on its own independent clock for every AT on staff, and every new facility contract a growing group adds compounds the number of simultaneously tracked authorization records — a continuous, coverage-volume-and-headcount-driven compliance stream tied directly to the group's own growth.

**Biggest risk:** A single AT employed directly by one school isn't the buyer for a multi-facility coverage-assignment tool. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward multi-site AT groups and hospital sports-medicine outreach programs covering 6+ facilities, where the coverage-assignment load genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project serving 5-6 small AT groups → NATA district/state affiliate channel presence → 30+ clients replaces-full-time-salary.
