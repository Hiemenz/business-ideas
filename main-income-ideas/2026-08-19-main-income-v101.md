### 1. House & Structure Mover Oversize-Load Permit & Utility-Coordination Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** House-moving and structural-relocation contractors — who jack up and transport entire houses, barns, and modular buildings down public roads — operate under state house-mover contractor licensing (such as Texas Occupations Code Chapter 1104's dedicated "House Movers" statute, which requires state registration, a surety bond, and specific equipment standards) layered on top of a state DOT oversize/overweight load permit required for every single move, each permit tied to an exact route, structure dimensions, and an approved travel window that is frequently restricted to overnight hours to minimize traffic disruption. A move that starts without a current permit matching the structure's actual measured dimensions, or one that drifts past its approved travel window because of a delay, is exactly the finding a state DOT enforcement stop is built to catch, and a structure stranded mid-route blocking a public road while permits are sorted out is both a public-safety incident and a business-ending liability event. Every move above a certain height also requires coordinating with every utility company along the route to temporarily raise or de-energize power, phone, and cable lines at each crossing point, and a mover who arrives on move night to find a utility crew hasn't been scheduled to lift a line is exactly the gap that turns a planned overnight move into a stranded structure by daylight. Larger or wider moves also require a documented pilot-car/escort assignment matched to the load's width and height under the specific state's escort-threshold rules. A small house-moving contractor running several moves a month across a multi-county service area manages per-move permit currency, per-move utility-coordination scheduling, and per-move escort-requirement compliance with no dedicated function beyond the owner's paper permit folder and a string of phone calls to utility companies.
**What to build:** A per-move permit tracker capturing route, structure dimensions, permit number, and approved travel window with a "cleared to move" gate blocking dispatch until a current, dimension-matched permit is on file; a utility-coordination checklist per move confirming every line-clearance appointment along the route is scheduled and confirmed before the move date; and a pilot-car/escort assignment tracker matching escort count and positioning to the load's width and height against the specific state's threshold rules.
**Skill fit:** State DOT oversize/overweight permit systems and house-mover licensing statutes are public, structured regulatory data with a well-defined per-move record requirement, and the permit-eligibility gate plus the utility-coordination checklist are the same deterministic "block the action until the credential clears / confirm the prerequisite step before the deadline" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a structural-relocation trade most small movers currently track through a paper permit folder and a string of utility-company phone calls with no systematic cross-check between a specific move's approved dimensions and today's actual permit on file.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the per-move permit tracker with the move-eligibility gate, the utility-coordination checklist, and the escort-assignment tracker; land the first 5-8 contractors through International Association of Structural Movers (IASM) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the International Association of Structural Movers' member directory and state house-mover licensee lists (public records maintained by the same DOT agencies that issue the oversize-load permits), where small operators already juggle per-move permit renewals and utility scheduling with no dedicated compliance software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $349/mo base (up to 5 moves/month) + $25/mo per additional move that month. Average client (8 moves/month) = $349 + 3×$25 = $424/mo. 23-28 contractors × $424/mo = **$9,752-11,872/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every single move reopens its own permit-dimension match, utility-coordination schedule, and escort-requirement check from scratch, with no move ever inheriting compliance from the last one — a continuous, per-move compliance stream tied directly to the contractor's job volume rather than an annual renewal event.
**Biggest risk:** A move started without a current, dimension-matched permit, or one that reaches a utility crossing with no line-clearance appointment scheduled, is exactly the failure that strands a structure mid-route on a public road — the permit-eligibility gate and the utility-coordination checklist are the core protections, both built to catch the gap before move night rather than during it.
**Growth path:** Side project → permit tracker, utility-coordination checklist, and escort tracker deployed, first 5-8 contractor retainers through IASM outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 14-17 contractors. Replaces-full-time-salary → 23-28 contractors, pushing past $9.75-11.87K/mo.

### 2. Structural Steel Erection Contractor OSHA Subpart R Erection-Plan & Connector Certification Compliance
**Problem:** Structural steel erection contractors — the ironworker crews who assemble a building's steel frame — operate under OSHA's steel erection standard (29 CFR 1926 Subpart R), which requires a site-specific erection plan on file before work begins, documented controlled decking zone procedures for any level where decking is being installed without full perimeter fall protection, and individual fall-protection compliance for every connector working the leading edge above the standard's height threshold. A crew that starts erecting steel without an approved site-specific plan on file, or that runs a controlled decking zone without the specific procedural documentation Subpart R requires, is exactly the finding an OSHA steel-erection-focused inspection is built to catch, and Subpart R violations are consistently among OSHA's most heavily cited construction standards given how directly they tie to fall fatalities. Every ironworker performing structural welds must also hold current AWS D1.1 structural welding certification — a credential that requires periodic re-testing or documented welding continuity to stay valid — and every connector must have completed documented competent-person fall-protection training specific to steel erection, since a crew that sends an uncertified welder to make a structural connection or an untrained ironworker to work the leading edge is exactly the staffing gap a general contractor's own safety audit or an OSHA inspection checks first. A small erection subcontractor running 10-20 ironworkers across several concurrent job sites manages per-project erection-plan documentation, per-ironworker welding-certification currency, and per-ironworker fall-protection training with no dedicated compliance function beyond a foreman's job-site binder.
**What to build:** A per-project site-specific erection plan document tracker confirming an approved plan is on file before a project is marked cleared to start; a per-ironworker AWS D1.1 welding-certification and competent-person fall-protection-training tracker with a "cleared to erect" gate blocking assignment of an uncertified welder to structural connections or an untrained ironworker to leading-edge connector work; and a controlled decking zone compliance checklist per project level confirming the required procedural documentation is complete before decking begins.
**Skill fit:** OSHA Subpart R's erection-plan and connector-certification requirements, plus AWS D1.1's welding-certification renewal cycle, are public, structured regulatory data with a well-defined per-project and per-ironworker record requirement, and the erection-eligibility gate plus the certification tracker are the same deterministic "block the action until the credential clears / confirm the required document is on file before the action proceeds" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a vertical-construction trade most small erection subcontractors currently track through a foreman's job-site binder with no systematic cross-check between a specific ironworker's current certification and today's connector assignment.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the erection-plan document tracker, the per-ironworker certification tracker with the erect-eligibility gate, and the controlled-decking-zone checklist; land the first 5-8 contractors through American Institute of Steel Construction (AISC) Certified Erector program member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the AISC Certified Erector program's member list and regional ironworker contractor associations, where small erection subcontractors already juggle per-ironworker welding-certification renewals and site-specific erection-plan documentation with no dedicated compliance software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299/mo base + $15/mo per certified ironworker tracked. Average client (12 ironworkers) = $299 + $180 = $479/mo. 20-25 contractors × $479/mo = **$9,580-11,975/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new project reopens its own site-specific erection-plan requirement, every ironworker's welding certification and fall-protection training renew on independent cycles, and every controlled decking zone opens its own procedural-documentation event — a continuous, per-project and per-ironworker compliance stream tied directly to the contractor's job volume.
**Biggest risk:** A structural weld made by an uncertified welder, or a connector working the leading edge without documented fall-protection training, is exactly the finding an OSHA steel-erection inspection is built to catch after a fall incident — the erect-eligibility gate is the core protection, built to make an out-of-compliance assignment structurally difficult rather than a foreman's oversight.
**Growth path:** Side project → erection-plan tracker, ironworker certification gate, and decking-zone checklist deployed, first 5-8 contractor retainers through AISC outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-15 contractors. Replaces-full-time-salary → 20-25 contractors, pushing past $9.58-11.98K/mo.

### 3. AABB Relationship Testing Program Collection-Site Chain-of-Custody Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Legally admissible paternity and relationship DNA testing — the kind accepted in custody proceedings, child support cases, and immigration filings — depends on sample collection happening at a site accredited under the AABB Relationship Testing Program's chain-of-custody protocol: photo-ID verification of every party, a witness present for the actual sample collection, tamper-evident sealing of the specimen, and a fully completed custody form linking the sample to the verified individual before it ever reaches the accredited laboratory. A collection network coordinating dozens of mobile phlebotomists, urgent-care partners, and notary-adjacent collection agents across a region has no room for a broken link in that chain — a missing witness signature, an unsealed kit, or an incomplete custody form is exactly the gap that voids the test's legal admissibility outright, turning what the client paid for into a worthless result the moment it's challenged in court. Every collection site or agent must also maintain current chain-of-custody training documentation, since dispatching an untrained agent to a legal collection is itself a basis for the accredited lab refusing to process the result. A small collection network running 15-25 active sites and mobile agents across a metro region or state manages per-site training currency and per-case custody-document completeness with no dedicated compliance function beyond a coordinator's shared spreadsheet and a stack of paper custody forms waiting to be checked before shipment.
**What to build:** A per-collection-site/agent chain-of-custody training-currency tracker with a "cleared to collect" gate blocking a legal-case assignment to any site or agent whose training documentation has lapsed; a per-case chain-of-custody completeness checklist (photo-ID verification, witness signature, tamper-evident seal, completed custody form) that must clear before a kit is marked ready to ship to the accredited lab; and a case-status tracker linking collection date, shipment, and lab turnaround so a coordinator can see at a glance which cases are still missing a required document.
**Skill fit:** The AABB Relationship Testing Program's chain-of-custody protocol is a public, structured checklist requirement with a well-defined per-case and per-site record model, and the collection-eligibility gate plus the custody-completeness checklist are the same deterministic "block the action until the credential clears / require every element of the record before the transaction proceeds" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a specimen-collection trade most small networks currently coordinate through a shared spreadsheet with no systematic cross-check between a specific case's custody documentation and whether it's actually complete before shipment.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the per-site training tracker with the collection-eligibility gate, the per-case custody-completeness checklist, and the case-status tracker; land the first 5-8 collection networks through outreach to AABB-accredited labs' affiliate/franchise collection-site programs.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — direct outreach to AABB Relationship Testing Program-accredited laboratories' regional collection-site and affiliate networks, which already depend on dozens of independently run collection sites getting the chain-of-custody protocol right on every single case.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249/mo base + $10/mo per active collection site/agent tracked. Average client (15 sites) = $249 + $150 = $399/mo. 24-30 networks × $399/mo = **$9,576-11,970/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new legal case reopens its own custody-completeness check from intake through shipment, and every collection site's training currency has to be reconfirmed on its own renewal cycle — a continuous, per-case compliance stream tied directly to the network's legal-testing case volume.
**Biggest risk:** A sample shipped with an incomplete custody form or collected by an agent whose training had lapsed is exactly the gap that voids the test's legal admissibility, turning a paid result into a worthless one the moment opposing counsel challenges it — the collection-eligibility gate and the custody-completeness checklist are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → training tracker, custody-completeness checklist, and case-status tracker deployed, first 5-8 network retainers through accredited-lab affiliate outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 14-17 networks. Replaces-full-time-salary → 24-30 networks, pushing past $9.58-11.97K/mo.

### 4. USDA APHIS Class C Mobile & Traveling Animal Exhibitor Itinerary & CVI Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Mobile and traveling animal exhibitors — petting zoos, traveling animal shows, and educational animal programs licensed as Class C exhibitors under the Animal Welfare Act (7 U.S.C. § 2131 et seq.) — must file an advance-notice interstate travel itinerary with APHIS before touring across state lines, and every animal on the roster must carry a Certificate of Veterinary Inspection (CVI) that stays current within each destination state's own window, commonly 30 days, before that animal can legally cross into that state. An exhibitor whose CVI has expired mid-tour, or one that arrives at a venue without a filed itinerary matching the actual route, is exactly the finding an APHIS inspector or a state agriculture department checkpoint is built to catch, and a Class C license suspension over a lapsed itinerary or expired health certificate shuts down every booked venue date on the calendar at once. Every facility and transport vehicle used to house and move the animals must also pass its own APHIS facility inspection, and a small touring operation running a roster of 10-25 animals across a multi-state summer fair-and-festival circuit manages per-animal CVI currency, per-state itinerary filings, and facility/vehicle inspection readiness with no dedicated compliance function beyond the owner's paper folder of vet certificates carried in the truck.
**What to build:** An interstate travel itinerary tracker per state/venue computing each APHIS advance-notice filing deadline from the planned tour schedule; a per-animal CVI currency tracker with a "cleared to travel" gate blocking an animal from being loaded for a state-line crossing if its CVI has expired or won't remain current through the destination state's required window; and a facility/vehicle inspection-readiness checklist confirming APHIS's housing and transport standards are documented before each tour leg.
**Skill fit:** APHIS's Class C exhibitor itinerary-filing requirement and each state's CVI-currency window are public, structured regulatory data with a well-defined per-animal and per-state record requirement, and the travel-eligibility gate plus the itinerary tracker are the same deterministic "block the action until the credential clears / compute the deadline from the trigger event" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a touring-exhibition trade most small operators currently track through a paper folder of vet certificates with no systematic cross-check between a specific animal's CVI window and today's planned state-line crossing.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the itinerary tracker, the per-animal CVI gate, and the facility/vehicle inspection checklist; land the first 5-8 exhibitors through Zoological Association of America (ZAA) member outreach ahead of the spring/summer touring season.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Zoological Association of America's member directory and mobile-exhibitor Facebook and forum groups, where small touring operations already juggle per-animal CVI renewals and per-state itinerary filings across a busy fair-and-festival season with no dedicated compliance software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $12/mo per animal on the traveling roster. Average client (20 animals) = $199 + $240 = $439/mo. 21-27 exhibitors × $439/mo = **$9,219-11,853/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every state-line crossing reopens the CVI-currency check for the entire traveling roster, and every new tour leg opens its own itinerary-filing deadline — a continuous, per-crossing compliance stream tied directly to the exhibitor's touring schedule rather than a once-a-year license renewal.
**Biggest risk:** An animal transported across a state line on an expired CVI, or a tour leg run without a matching filed itinerary, is exactly the finding that can suspend the Class C license and cancel every booked venue date at once — the travel-eligibility gate and the itinerary tracker are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → itinerary tracker, CVI gate, and inspection checklist deployed, first 5-8 exhibitor retainers through ZAA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 12-15 exhibitors. Replaces-full-time-salary → 21-27 exhibitors, pushing past $9.22-11.85K/mo.

### 5. State DMV-Authorized Vehicle Registration & Title Service Provider Compliance Tracker
**Problem:** Private businesses authorized to process vehicle registration renewals and title transfers on a state's behalf — under programs such as California DMV's Business Partner Automation program, Florida's network of private tag agencies, and Texas's Full Service Deputy program — hold that authorization only as long as the location maintains a current surety bond, keeps every processing employee background-cleared and state-trained, and reconciles every transaction's collected fees against the funds it actually remits to the state on the required schedule. A location whose remittance falls short of what it collected, or one that lets an employee process registration transactions before completing the state's required training, is exactly the finding a state DMV compliance audit is built to catch, and a revoked authorization doesn't just end that violation — it cuts off the location's entire registration-service revenue line overnight, since the authorization is what lets the business process transactions at all. A small operator running several authorized locations manages per-location bond currency, per-transaction remittance reconciliation, and per-employee training status with no dedicated compliance function beyond the office manager's end-of-day cash-out spreadsheet.
**What to build:** A per-location authorization and surety-bond renewal tracker; a per-transaction remittance reconciliation dashboard matching daily fees collected against funds actually deposited with the state and flagging any shortfall before the state's own audit would catch it; and a per-employee training-certification tracker with a "cleared to process" gate blocking transaction access for any employee whose state-required training isn't current.
**Skill fit:** State DMV third-party authorization programs and their bonding/training requirements are public, structured regulatory data with a well-defined per-location and per-employee record requirement, and the reconciliation dashboard plus the process-eligibility gate are the same deterministic "cross-check two records that must match / block the action until the credential clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a government-agent retail-services trade most small operators currently track through an end-of-day cash-out spreadsheet with no systematic reconciliation between collected fees and remitted funds.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the authorization/bond tracker, the remittance reconciliation dashboard, and the per-employee training gate; land the first 5-8 operators through state-specific tag-agency and registration-service-provider associations.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — state tag-agency and vehicle-registration-service-provider associations (such as the networks of authorized agents operating under Florida's county tax collector agreements and California's Business Partner Automation program), where small multi-location operators already juggle per-location bond renewals and daily remittance reconciliation with no dedicated compliance software.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299/mo base + $20/mo per authorized processing employee tracked. Average client (6 employees across locations) = $299 + $120 = $419/mo. 22-28 operators × $419/mo = **$9,218-11,732/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every single transaction reopens the collected-versus-remitted reconciliation check, and every employee's training status has to be reconfirmed on its own renewal cycle — a continuous, per-transaction compliance stream tied directly to the operator's daily processing volume rather than an annual filing.
**Biggest risk:** A remittance shortfall discovered during a state DMV compliance audit, or a transaction processed by an untrained employee, is exactly the finding that can revoke a location's authorization and cut off its entire registration-service revenue line — the reconciliation dashboard and the process-eligibility gate are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → authorization tracker, reconciliation dashboard, and training gate deployed, first 5-8 operator retainers through tag-agency association outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 13-16 operators. Replaces-full-time-salary → 22-28 operators, pushing past $9.22-11.73K/mo.
