### 1. Deer & Big-Game Processor State Wildlife-Agency CWD Sampling, Carcass-Movement & Hold-Period Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent deer and big-game processing shops that butcher, package, and return venison and other wild-game meat to hunters operate under their state wildlife or fish-and-game agency's chronic wasting disease (CWD) management rules rather than USDA/FSIS — a completely different regulator than any custom-exempt livestock slaughter framework, since the animal was killed in the field, never presented live. In CWD management zones (an expanding footprint as CWD spreads to new states and counties every season), a processor must collect a lymph-node or obex tissue sample from every carcass brought in and submit it to the state's designated testing lab, hold that carcass — and refrain from releasing processed meat to the hunter — until the test result clears, and follow state rules restricting the movement of high-risk carcass parts (brain, spinal column, lymph nodes) across county or state lines, since transporting a whole, unprocessed carcass out of a CWD zone is itself a citable violation independent of the test result. A processor who releases meat before a pending CWD result clears, or who accepts an out-of-zone carcass moved in violation of the state's carcass-transport rule, is exactly the finding a state game warden's shop inspection during hunting season is built to catch, and a positive CWD result traced back to meat already released to a hunter is both a public-health notification obligation and a liability exposure the shop can't undo. A busy processor running dozens to hundreds of carcasses through a compressed six-to-ten-week hunting season manages per-carcass sample submission, hold-status, and state-specific carcass-part movement rules on a paper intake tag stapled to each hanging carcass and a wall calendar, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific carcass's CWD sample was actually submitted and its result actually cleared before that customer's meat goes out the door.

**What to build:** A per-carcass intake and CWD sample-submission tracker with a "cleared to release" gate blocking any processed-meat pickup until the corresponding lab result is logged as negative; a state-specific carcass-part movement-rule reference tied to the hunter's harvest location, flagging any intake that violates the processor's state's cross-boundary transport restriction; and a season-volume dashboard showing every carcass currently in hold status across a compressed intake surge.

**Skill fit:** State wildlife-agency CWD zone maps, sample-submission requirements, and carcass-movement restrictions are public, structured regulatory data with a concrete per-carcass hold-and-release trigger, and the "cleared to release" gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a wild-game-processing niche entirely distinct from the custom-exempt Mobile Slaughter Unit and rendering/deadstock-hauler ideas already in this repo, neither of which handles a hunter-killed carcass or a state-wildlife-agency CWD sample.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the per-carcass CWD sample/hold tracker with the release gate, the state-specific movement-rule reference, and the season-volume dashboard. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent deer processing shops ahead of the fall hunting season.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — state taxidermist and meat-processor trade associations and the National Deer Association's processor-facing outreach reach exactly the small shop owners currently tracking CWD sample submission and carcass hold status on a paper tag during their single, compressed high-volume season with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-399/mo per shop (billed seasonally, September-January) depending on carcass volume and whether the shop sits inside an active CWD management zone. 26-34 shops × $290/mo avg = $7,540-9,860/mo during peak season, with a smaller off-season base-fee tier sustaining the relationship year-round.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every carcass intake generates its own sample-and-hold event regardless of how routine the deer, every CWD zone boundary that expands into a new county reopens the movement-rule question for shops near that boundary, and a processor's own volume grows every season as CWD testing requirements spread to more states — a continuous, season-and-zone-expansion-driven compliance stream tied directly to the shop's harvest-season throughput.

**Biggest risk:** Revenue is heavily concentrated in a compressed fall season, and a shop outside any active CWD zone has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by pricing the CWD-zone tier higher, by expanding the product to include general customer-order and locker-inventory tracking that earns its keep year-round, and by qualifying leads toward states with growing, not shrinking, CWD zone footprints.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 shops ($3.5K/mo peak) → replaces-part-time-income → 20 shops ($5.8K/mo peak) → replaces-full-time-salary at 26-34 shops plus a game-warden-facing compliance export shops can produce during a routine season inspection.

### 2. Motorcycle Rider Training School State Curriculum Approval & MSF RiderCoach Certification Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent motorcycle rider training schools that teach the Basic RiderCourse (or a state-specific equivalent) hold a state DMV/DOT approval to operate under that state's licensed curriculum, and every individual RiderCoach who teaches a class must hold a current certification earned through the Motorcycle Safety Foundation's (or a state's own) Instructor Preparation course, kept current through a periodic Coach Prep Update — a RiderCoach teaching a class on a lapsed certification is exactly the finding a state motorcycle safety program's compliance audit is built to catch, and it invalidates the state DMV skills-test waiver every student in that class was counting on to skip the in-person riding exam. Schools must also maintain their motorcycle fleet against the curriculum's specific requirements — displacement-class range coverage, helmet and eye-protection loaner inventory meeting DOT/ANSI standards, and a documented pre-course equipment-safety check — since a course run on a motorcycle outside its approved displacement class, or with loaner helmets that don't meet the standard, is a finding independent of how well the instruction itself went. Every completed course also generates a student completion record that must be submitted to the state DMV within a set window to activate that student's skills-test waiver, and a submission delayed or lost is a student who shows up at the DMV to license their motorcycle and discovers the waiver was never recorded. A school running several RiderCoaches across a full spring-through-fall course calendar manages per-instructor certification currency, fleet/equipment compliance, and per-student DMV submission status on a program coordinator's spreadsheet and a filing cabinet of paper completion cards, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific RiderCoach's certification and a specific course's fleet compliance are both current before that class's roster is confirmed.

**What to build:** A per-RiderCoach certification and Coach Prep Update currency tracker with a "cleared to teach" gate blocking any class assignment once a certification lapses; a fleet/equipment compliance tracker covering displacement-class coverage and loaner-helmet standards compliance; and a per-student DMV completion-submission tracker flagging any graduate whose waiver record hasn't yet posted to the state system.

**Skill fit:** State DMV motorcycle-training-program approval rules and MSF's RiderCoach certification-renewal cycle are public, structured regulatory data with concrete per-instructor and per-student triggers, and the "cleared to teach" gate plus the DMV submission tracker are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a rider-training niche entirely distinct from the CDL commercial truck driving school idea already in this repo, which registers with an entirely different federal registry (FMCSA's Training Provider Registry) for an entirely different vehicle class and license endorsement.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the RiderCoach certification gate, the fleet/equipment compliance tracker, and the DMV submission tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent motorcycle rider training schools.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Motorcycle Safety Foundation's own RiderCoach and training-site directory, plus state motorcycle-safety-program administrator contact lists, reach exactly the small independent schools currently tracking instructor certification and DMV submission status on a program coordinator's spreadsheet with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249-499/mo per school depending on RiderCoach count and seasonal course volume. 24-30 schools × $359/mo avg = $8,616-10,770/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every RiderCoach's certification and Coach Prep Update renews on its own independent cycle for as long as they're teaching, every course session generates its own DMV-submission event regardless of how routine the class, and every new instructor the school adds reopens the per-person certification question — a continuous, staffing-and-course-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the school's seasonal enrollment.

**Biggest risk:** A very small school running one or two RiderCoaches on a fixed weekend schedule has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward schools running multiple RiderCoaches across a full riding-season calendar with high course-completion volume, where the DMV-submission-and-certification calendar genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 schools ($4.3K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 20 schools ($7.2K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 24-30 schools plus a state-program-audit-ready compliance export schools can hand an inspector during a routine curriculum-approval review.

### 3. Fire Door & Opening-Protective Inspection Company NFPA 80/105 Annual Functional-Test & FDAI Certification Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Companies that perform the annual functional inspection of fire-rated doors and other opening protectives — required in commercial, healthcare, and institutional buildings under NFPA 80 (Fire Doors and Other Opening Protectives) and NFPA 105 (smoke door assemblies) — must document, per individual door assembly, that the label is legible and intact, the clearance and gap tolerances fall within code, the self-closing device fully closes and latches the door, and the hardware functions as installed; a door inspected and passed without every one of these individually documented is exactly the finding a fire marshal or accreditation-body life-safety survey is built to catch, and a facility that can't produce a current annual inspection record for a specific door is a life-safety-code deficiency independent of whether the door itself is actually sound. Most working inspectors hold the Door and Hardware Institute's Fire Door Assessor (FDAI) certification, renewed on its own periodic cycle distinct from any state contractor license, and a technician performing inspections on a lapsed FDAI credential produces a report a hospital's or school district's own accreditation survey may not accept as valid documentation. A facility client — a hospital, school district, or multi-tenant office portfolio — typically has hundreds to thousands of individual fire-door openings across multiple buildings, each on its own annual inspection anniversary, and a deficiency found on a specific door (a self-closer that doesn't fully latch, a gap out of tolerance) triggers a documented corrective-action and re-inspection requirement with its own follow-up deadline. A small inspection company running several FDAI-certified technicians across a portfolio of building clients manages per-door inspection-due dates, per-technician FDAI currency, and per-deficiency corrective-action follow-up on a spreadsheet exported from a building's door schedule, with no systematic cross-check confirming a specific door's annual inspection and a specific deficiency's corrective re-inspection are both actually current before the client's next accreditation survey.

**What to build:** A per-door-opening inspection-due-date tracker imported from a client's door schedule, generating each door's annual inspection calendar and flagging any opening past due; a per-technician FDAI certification currency tracker with an "eligible to inspect" gate; and a per-deficiency corrective-action tracker logging the specific finding, the required fix, and a follow-up re-inspection deadline until the door clears.

**Skill fit:** NFPA 80/105's per-door functional-test criteria and DHI's FDAI certification-renewal cycle are public, structured code and accrediting-body data with a concrete per-opening and per-technician record requirement, and the door-schedule-import-and-due-date-calendar pattern is the same deterministic system this series builds repeatedly, applied to a passive-fire-protection niche entirely distinct from the boiler/pressure-vessel and crane/rigging ideas already in this repo, neither of which inspects a building's opening protectives at all.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the per-door inspection-due-date tracker, the per-technician FDAI currency gate, and the per-deficiency corrective-action tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent fire door inspection companies.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Door and Hardware Institute's (DHI) FDAI-certified inspector directory and its regional chapter meetings reach exactly the small independent inspection companies currently reconciling per-door due dates and corrective-action follow-up against a client's building door schedule with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $399-799/mo per inspection company depending on the size of its client building portfolio and total door count under management. 18-24 companies × $549/mo avg = $9,882-13,176/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every individual door opening recurs on its own independent annual inspection clock for as long as the building operates, every deficiency found generates its own corrective-action-and-re-inspection event, and every new building client the company signs multiplies the number of simultaneously tracked door-inspection records — a continuous, portfolio-growth-driven compliance stream tied directly to the company's client base.

**Biggest risk:** A company inspecting only one or two small buildings with a low door count has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads toward companies servicing hospital, school-district, and multi-building institutional portfolios with hundreds of doors, where the per-door due-date calendar genuinely outgrows a spreadsheet.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10 companies ($5.5K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 16 companies ($8.8K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 18-24 companies plus a fire-marshal/accreditation-survey-ready compliance export companies can hand a client ahead of a life-safety inspection.

### 4. Whale Watching & Marine Wildlife Viewing Tour Operator NOAA MMPA Approach-Distance & Seasonal Vessel-Speed Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Whale watching and marine wildlife viewing tour operators run their vessels under the same USCG small-passenger-vessel Certificate of Inspection any charter needs, but their genuinely distinguishing compliance burden is federal marine-mammal protection law: NOAA's Marine Mammal Protection Act prohibits any act that could "harass" a marine mammal, operationalized through species-specific minimum approach distances — commonly 100 yards for humpback whales in Alaska and Hawaii — that tighten sharply to a federally mandated 500-yard buffer around endangered North Atlantic right whales under the Endangered Species Act, and a captain who lets a vessel drift inside that buffer, even briefly while maneuvering for a better viewing angle, is exactly the finding a NOAA Office of Law Enforcement patrol or a passenger's own cellphone video is built to catch, carrying real civil-penalty exposure per incident. Layered on top, NOAA designates seasonal Management Areas along the East Coast where any vessel 65 feet or longer must hold to a 10-knot speed limit during the North Atlantic right whale's calving season — a rule that applies regardless of whether a right whale is actually sighted that day, since the restriction is date-and-zone triggered rather than sighting-triggered, and an operator who doesn't track which zones are currently active and which dates the restriction covers can rack up a documented speed violation with no whale ever in view. Operators pursuing NOAA's voluntary Whale SENSE recognition — a credential that reassures environmentally conscious customers and several tour-booking platforms now surface in search results — face their own periodic naturalist-training and guideline-adherence renewal to keep the designation current. A tour operator running several boats and captains across a full viewing season manages species-specific approach-distance training, active Seasonal Management Area status by date and zone, and Whale SENSE renewal on a captain's memory of the rules and a printed NOAA guidance sheet taped in the wheelhouse, with no systematic cross-check confirming today's route and speed actually comply with whichever zone and species restriction currently applies.

**What to build:** A Seasonal Management Area calendar showing which zones are currently active by date and the vessel-length/speed rule that applies, cross-referenced against the operator's planned route; a per-captain approach-distance training and briefing-currency tracker covering each species the operator regularly encounters; and a Whale SENSE recognition renewal tracker with a naturalist-training-completion log.

**Skill fit:** NOAA's Seasonal Management Area boundaries, effective dates, and species-specific approach-distance rules are public, structured federal regulatory data with a well-defined date-and-zone trigger, and the active-zone calendar cross-referenced against a route is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a marine-wildlife-tourism niche entirely distinct from the scuba dive charter and marina operator ideas already in this repo, neither of which has any wildlife-approach-distance or seasonal-speed-restriction concept at all.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the Seasonal Management Area calendar with the route cross-check, the per-captain approach-distance training tracker, and the Whale SENSE renewal tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent whale watching and marine wildlife tour operators.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Whale SENSE program's own participating-operator directory and the Pacific Whale Watch Association / regional whale-watch operator associations along the Northeast and Pacific coasts reach exactly the small independent operators currently tracking seasonal speed zones and approach-distance rules on a printed wheelhouse sheet with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per operator depending on fleet size and number of vessels subject to the Seasonal Management Area speed rule. 22-28 operators × $429/mo avg = $9,438-12,012/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** The Seasonal Management Area calendar recurs on its own fixed annual cycle for as long as the operator runs whale tours, every new captain hired reopens the approach-distance training question, and Whale SENSE recognition renews on its own periodic cycle — a continuous, season-and-staffing-driven compliance stream tied directly to the operator's fleet and route footprint.

**Biggest risk:** A NOAA enforcement action or public embarrassment from a documented too-close approach is a severe reputational event in an industry that markets itself on responsible wildlife viewing. Mitigate by making the active-zone-and-route cross-check a pre-departure hard check rather than an advisory flag, and by qualifying leads toward operators running multiple vessels across an East Coast or Pacific Northwest route where the zone-and-species calendar genuinely outgrows a wheelhouse printout.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 operators ($5.1K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 20 operators ($8.6K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 22-28 operators plus a NOAA-facing compliance export operators can produce ahead of a Whale SENSE renewal review.

### 5. Private Wildland Fire Suppression Contractor NWCG Incident Qualification "Red Card" & EERA Equipment-Inspection Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Private wildland fire suppression contractors — independent engine crews, water-tender operators, and hand crews contracted to the U.S. Forest Service, BLM, and state forestry agencies under the Interagency Incident Business Management Handbook — must keep every firefighter's National Wildfire Coordinating Group (NWCG) Incident Qualification Card current before that person can be dispatched to an incident; each position-specific card (Firefighter Type 2, Engine Boss, Water Tender Operator) requires an annual physical work-capacity test (the "pack test") and NWCG refresher training (RT-130), and a crew member dispatched with a lapsed card is exactly the finding a dispatch-center resource-order audit is built to catch, with a contractor's entire crew pulled from an active assignment if the gap surfaces mid-incident. Every engine, water tender, or dozer the contractor operates must separately pass an annual agency equipment inspection — verified carrying capacity, pump and tank specifications, tool-cache completeness — before it can be listed and dispatched under an Emergency Equipment Rental Agreement (EERA), and equipment inspected past its due date or missing a required cache item is grounds for the agency to reject that unit's dispatch the moment a fire call comes in, at the worst possible time for a contractor counting on that unit's daily rental revenue. Because wildfire season creates a compressed, unpredictable surge of dispatch activity across a contractor's full roster of crew members and equipment simultaneously, a contractor running several engines and a rotating crew roster manages per-person red-card expiration and pack-test scheduling, and per-unit EERA inspection currency, on a crew-boss's paper roster binder, with no systematic cross-check confirming every crew member assigned to a specific engine, and that engine itself, are both currently eligible for dispatch before the agency's resource order comes through.

**What to build:** A per-firefighter NWCG Incident Qualification Card tracker with pack-test and RT-130 refresher scheduling and a "cleared to dispatch" gate; a per-unit EERA equipment-inspection currency tracker flagging any engine, tender, or dozer approaching its annual inspection due date; and a crew-to-equipment dispatch-eligibility cross-check confirming every assigned crew member and their assigned unit are simultaneously current before a resource order is accepted.

**Skill fit:** NWCG's qualification-card requirements and the Interagency Incident Business Management Handbook's EERA equipment-inspection standards are public, structured federal regulatory data with concrete per-person and per-unit triggers, and the "cleared to dispatch" gate is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a wildland-fire-contracting niche this repo has never addressed and that shares no qualification-card mechanism, dispatch-agency relationship, or equipment-rental-agreement structure with any other trade already in this repo.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the per-firefighter red-card/pack-test tracker with the dispatch-eligibility gate, the per-unit EERA inspection tracker, and the crew-to-equipment cross-check. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent wildland fire contractors.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the National Wildfire Suppression Association (NWSA), whose membership is built almost entirely of small private wildland fire contractors, reaches exactly the crew bosses and owners currently tracking per-person red-card expiration and per-unit EERA inspection status on a paper roster binder with no dedicated compliance system.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $349-699/mo per contractor depending on crew headcount and number of EERA-listed units. 20-26 contractors × $479/mo avg = $9,580-12,454/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every firefighter's NWCG card and annual pack test recur on their own independent clock for as long as they're on the roster, every EERA-listed unit's inspection renews on its own annual cycle, and every new crew member or engine the contractor adds ahead of a growing fire season multiplies the number of simultaneously tracked qualification-and-inspection records — a continuous, roster-and-fleet-growth-driven compliance stream tied directly to the contractor's dispatch capacity.

**Biggest risk:** A crew member dispatched on a lapsed red card, or a unit dispatched past its EERA inspection due date, is a severe safety and contract-eligibility event that can pull an entire crew from an active incident. Mitigate by making the dispatch-eligibility gate a hard block rather than an advisory flag, and by qualifying leads toward contractors running multiple engines and a growing crew roster where the staggered qualification-and-inspection calendar genuinely outgrows a paper binder.

**Growth path:** Side project → 10 contractors ($4.8K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 16 contractors ($7.7K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 20-26 contractors plus an agency-facing dispatch-readiness export contractors can produce ahead of each fire season's resource-order registration.
