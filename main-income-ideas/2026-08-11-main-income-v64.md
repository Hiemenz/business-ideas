### 1. Commercial Taxidermist State Fish & Wildlife License, Tagged-Species Record-Keeping & CITES Documentation Compliance
**Problem:** Commercial taxidermists — who prepare and mount game animals, fish, and birds for hunting and fishing clients — must hold a state taxidermy license issued by the state fish & wildlife or department of natural resources agency (required in nearly every state for anyone charging a fee to mount game), renewed annually or biennially, commonly with a facility inspection and, in some states, a bond or liability insurance requirement. Every regulated game species accepted for mounting must be logged against the client's hunting or fishing tag/license number, species, and date and location taken, because a state game warden's routine or complaint-triggered inspection checks the taxidermist's intake log against the client's harvest record to confirm the animal was legally taken — a mismatched or missing tag entry is one of the most common findings behind a taxidermy license suspension. Specimens of federally protected migratory birds require a U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service possession/taxidermy permit and species-specific record-keeping under the Migratory Bird Treaty Act, and any specimen involving an internationally protected species (African game trophies and other CITES-listed species) requires the client's USFWS Form 3-177 declaration on file before the taxidermist can legally take possession, since CITES enforcement sweeps have targeted taxidermists holding undocumented protected-species mounts as aggressively as the importer. A small taxidermy studio processing dozens of specimens a month across a compressed fall hunting season logs intake tag numbers, license renewal, and CITES documentation with no dedicated compliance function — and a specimen accepted without a verified tag number, or a CITES-listed mount held without a Form 3-177 on file, is exactly the gap a game warden inspection or a USFWS enforcement sweep is built to catch.
**What to build:** A per-specimen intake log (species, client hunting/fishing license and tag number, date/location taken, specimen photo) with a "cleared to accept" gate flagging any specimen missing a verifiable tag number before intake is finalized; a federal migratory bird and CITES-species documentation tracker requiring the client's USFWS permit or Form 3-177 declaration on file before a protected-species specimen can be logged as accepted; and a state license/facility-inspection renewal tracker.
**Skill fit:** State taxidermy licensing rules and species-specific federal documentation requirements (Migratory Bird Treaty Act, CITES) are public regulatory sources with a well-defined, checkable record structure, and the tag-verification gate plus the protected-species documentation gate are the same deterministic "block intake until the compliance record is confirmed" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a seasonal trade most small studios currently run on a paper intake ticket with no systematic tag or CITES cross-check.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the specimen intake log with the tag-verification gate, the protected-species documentation tracker, and the license renewal tracker; land the first 5-8 studios through National Taxidermists Association (NTA) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — National Taxidermists Association (NTA) member directory and state taxidermist association meetings, where studio owners already navigate tag-verification and CITES paperwork ahead of every hunting season.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $99/mo base + $2/mo per specimen logged monthly. Average client (a studio logging 45 specimens/mo during peak season) = $99 + $90 = $189/mo. 50-60 studios × $189/mo = **$9,450-11,340/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every specimen accepted generates its own tag-verification and, where applicable, CITES documentation event, intake volume compounds heavily during the fall hunting season, and license/facility renewal recurs on its own cycle — a high-frequency, per-specimen compliance stream tied directly to seasonal intake volume.
**Biggest risk:** A specimen accepted without a verified harvest tag, or a CITES-listed mount held without the client's Form 3-177 declaration on file, is exactly the fact pattern a game warden inspection or USFWS enforcement sweep is built to catch — the tag-verification gate and the protected-species documentation gate are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → intake log, tag-verification gate, and CITES tracker deployed, first 5-8 studio retainers through NTA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 22-28 studios. Replaces-full-time-salary → 50-60 studios, pushing past $9.45-11.34K/mo.

### 2. Indoor Trampoline & Adventure Park ASTM F2970, State Trampoline-Court Statute & Court-Monitor Certification Compliance
**Problem:** Indoor trampoline parks and adventure parks (foam pits, ninja-warrior courses, climbing walls) operate under ASTM F2970, the industry-adopted standard covering court inspection, padding/netting maintenance, and posted rules signage, and in the growing number of states that have enacted a specific trampoline-court or inflatable-amusement-device statute — distinct from a traditional mechanical carnival-ride statute — facilities must hold a state or local permit renewed annually, often with a facility inspection. Every court and attraction requires a documented daily pre-opening inspection (springs, pads, netting integrity, foam-pit depth) before the facility opens to the public, and a growing number of state statutes and insurers require "court monitor" staff to complete a facility-specific safety-monitoring certification (through the International Association of Trampoline Parks, IATP, or an equivalent operator-training program) before they can supervise a court, renewed on its own cycle independent of the facility's own permit. Every injury incident above a defined severity threshold must be logged and, in states with an incident-reporting statute, reported to the state agency within a set window — a facility that cannot produce a complete daily pre-opening inspection log or current court-monitor certification roster during a post-incident investigation faces the sharpest liability exposure in the trade, since a plaintiff's attorney in a trampoline-injury suit routinely subpoenas exactly these records first. A small independent park running 1-2 locations and 15-30 staff manages the facility permit/inspection cycle, daily pre-opening inspection logs across multiple courts, staggered court-monitor certification renewal, and incident-reporting deadlines with no dedicated compliance function — and a missing pre-opening inspection log entry, or a court supervised by an uncertified monitor, discovered during a post-injury investigation is exactly the gap that turns a routine incident into an uninsurable liability finding.
**What to build:** A per-court daily pre-opening inspection checklist (springs, padding, netting, foam-pit depth) with a hard block preventing a court from being marked "open" until the day's inspection is logged; a per-staff court-monitor certification tracker with a "cleared to supervise" gate blocking a court assignment once certification lapses; an incident log capturing every reportable injury with the state's required notification deadline computed from the incident date; and a facility permit/inspection renewal tracker.
**Skill fit:** ASTM F2970 and the growing set of state trampoline-court statutes are public standards and regulatory text with a well-defined, checkable daily-inspection structure, and the pre-opening inspection gate plus the court-monitor certification gate are the same deterministic "block the action until the compliance record clears" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a fixed-facility recreation category distinct from the traveling carnival mechanical-ride operator idea covered in an earlier session.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the pre-opening inspection checklist, the court-monitor certification tracker with the supervise-eligibility gate, and the incident log; land the first 5-8 parks through International Association of Trampoline Parks (IATP) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — International Association of Trampoline Parks (IATP) member directory and independent park operator networks, where facility managers already navigate daily inspection logging and court-monitor certification renewal across multiple courts.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249/mo base + $15/mo per certified court monitor tracked. Average client (a park with 15 monitors) = $249 + $225 = $474/mo. 20-25 parks × $474/mo = **$9,480-11,850/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every operating day generates a fresh pre-opening inspection requirement per court, court-monitor certifications renew on staggered independent cycles, and any injury incident opens its own state-notification countdown — a daily, per-court compliance stream tied directly to the park's operating calendar.
**Biggest risk:** A court opened without a logged pre-opening inspection, or supervised by an uncertified monitor, is exactly the fact pattern a post-injury liability investigation or state inspection is built to catch — the pre-opening inspection gate and the supervise-eligibility gate are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → inspection checklist, court-monitor tracker, and incident log deployed, first 5-8 park retainers through IATP outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 10-13 parks. Replaces-full-time-salary → 20-25 parks, pushing past $9.48-11.85K/mo.

### 3. Crime Scene & Biohazard Trauma Cleanup Company OSHA Bloodborne Pathogen & Licensed Waste-Disposal Reconciliation Compliance
**Problem:** Crime scene and biohazard trauma cleanup companies — firms that remediate unattended deaths, crime scenes, and hoarding/biohazard conditions in homes and vehicles — operate under OSHA's Bloodborne Pathogen Standard (29 CFR 1910.1030), requiring every technician handling blood or other potentially infectious material to complete initial and annual training, maintain a documented exposure control plan, and use specified PPE and decontamination procedures, with OSHA citing this precise standard most frequently against small remediation firms during a complaint-triggered inspection. Because these companies generate biohazardous waste but are not themselves a licensed medical waste treatment facility, every job's waste must be packaged per state and DOT infectious-substance rules and handed off to a state-permitted medical waste transporter for disposal, with the generator (the cleanup company) required to retain the transporter's manifest or disposal receipt as proof the waste was properly disposed — a job closed out without a matching disposal receipt on file is exactly the gap a state environmental agency audit or an insurer's claims-integrity review targets, since most trauma cleanup work is billed through a homeowner's insurance claim and insurers increasingly request the disposal manifest before releasing payment. Many states also require the company itself to hold a bioremediation or crime-scene-cleanup-specific business registration, and certification through the American Bio-Recovery Association (ABRA) or IICRC's trauma-scene certification, while not universally mandated, is a de facto requirement most insurance adjusters and law enforcement referral contacts expect before referring a job. A small trauma cleanup operation running 2-6 technicians and dozens of jobs a year manages staggered BBP training/exposure-control documentation, per-job waste-disposal manifest reconciliation against every completed job, and business registration renewal with no dedicated compliance function — and a technician working past their annual BBP retraining date, or a job closed without a reconciled disposal receipt, is exactly the finding an OSHA inspection or an insurer's post-claim audit is built to catch.
**What to build:** A per-technician BBP training and exposure-control-plan currency tracker with a "cleared to respond" gate blocking a job dispatch once training lapses; a per-job biohazard waste chain-of-custody tracker linking the job to the licensed medical waste transporter's pickup manifest, with a hard flag for any closed job missing a reconciled disposal receipt; and a state business registration/certification (ABRA, IICRC) renewal tracker.
**Skill fit:** OSHA's Bloodborne Pathogen Standard and state medical waste generator-to-disposal reconciliation requirements are public federal and state regulatory sources, and the dispatch-eligibility gate plus the per-job disposal-receipt reconciliation are the same deterministic "block the assignment / confirm the required document is on file" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a remediation trade where the compliance obligation sits on the waste generator's side of the chain, not the hauler's.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the BBP training tracker with the dispatch-eligibility gate, the per-job disposal-receipt reconciliation tracker, and the registration/certification tracker; land the first 5-8 companies through American Bio-Recovery Association (ABRA) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — American Bio-Recovery Association (ABRA) member directory and IICRC-certified trauma-scene restoration firm outreach, where small operators already navigate insurer requests for disposal manifests on every claim.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $179/mo base + $20/mo per technician tracked. Average client (4 technicians) = $179 + $80 = $259/mo. 40-48 companies × $259/mo = **$10,360-12,432/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every job opens its own waste chain-of-custody reconciliation event, BBP training recurs annually per technician, and insurers requesting disposal manifests before releasing claim payment make the reconciliation a revenue-blocking event, not just a compliance one — a per-job compliance stream tied directly to call volume.
**Biggest risk:** A job closed without a reconciled disposal receipt from the licensed medical waste transporter, or a technician dispatched past their BBP training lapse, is exactly the finding an OSHA inspection or an insurer's post-claim audit is built to catch — the dispatch-eligibility gate and the disposal-receipt reconciliation tracker are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → BBP tracker, disposal-receipt reconciliation, and registration tracker deployed, first 5-8 company retainers through ABRA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 20-24 companies. Replaces-full-time-salary → 40-48 companies, pushing past $10.36-12.43K/mo.

### 4. Underground Utility Locating Contractor State One-Call/811 Damage-Prevention & Locate-Ticket Response Compliance
**Problem:** Private underground utility locating contractors — hired by excavators, homeowners, and utilities themselves to mark the location of buried gas, electric, water, and telecom lines before digging — operate under state one-call/811 damage-prevention statutes, which require every locate request ("ticket") to be responded to within a defined statutory window (commonly 2 business days, sometimes shorter for emergency tickets) with the buried facility either marked or a "clear"/"no conflict" response filed, and a locator who marks a line inaccurately or misses a ticket's response deadline is a documented cause of the utility strikes state damage-prevention boards investigate, holding the locating contractor liable under the state's statute since an inaccurate or late locate is the direct proximate cause the excavator relied on before digging. Locators performing work for a specific utility client operate under that utility's own locate-accuracy service-level agreement, commonly requiring 95%+ on-time response and tracked per-locator, and a contractor falling below the utility's SLA threshold risks losing the utility contract that generates the bulk of its ticket volume. Every completed locate must be photo-documented (marked location, ticket number, technician, timestamp) since that photo record is the contractor's primary defense if a strike occurs and liability is disputed later, and NULCA (National Utility Locating Contractors Association) certification, while not universally state-mandated, is a de facto requirement most utility clients specify in their locating vendor contracts. A small locating contractor running 5-15 locators and hundreds of tickets a week manages per-ticket response-deadline tracking across overlapping utility and state jurisdictions, per-locator on-time performance against each utility client's SLA, and photo-documentation completeness with no dedicated compliance function — and a ticket that ages past its statutory response window, or a completed locate missing its photo-documentation record when a strike investigation follows, is exactly the gap a state damage-prevention board inquiry or a utility SLA audit is built to catch.
**What to build:** A per-ticket intake and response-deadline tracker computing each ticket's statutory due date/time from its receipt timestamp, with a hard countdown flag as a ticket approaches its response window; a per-locator on-time performance dashboard computing each technician's completion rate against both the state statutory deadline and any client-specific SLA threshold; and a per-locate photo-documentation log (marked location photo, ticket number, timestamp, technician) linked to its ticket, flagging any completed locate missing its required documentation.
**Skill fit:** State one-call/811 damage-prevention statutes and their statutory response windows are public regulatory text with well-defined, computable deadlines, and the ticket response-deadline countdown plus the photo-documentation completeness flag are the same deterministic "compute the deadline from the trigger event, confirm the required record is on file" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a locating trade most small contractors currently track on a paper ticket board with no systematic response-time or documentation cross-check.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the ticket response-deadline tracker, the per-locator SLA dashboard, and the photo-documentation log; land the first 5-8 contractors through National Utility Locating Contractors Association (NULCA) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — National Utility Locating Contractors Association (NULCA) member directory and state damage-prevention council meetings, where small locating contractors already navigate statutory response-window compliance and utility-client SLA reporting on a paper ticket board.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $149/mo base + $15/mo per locator tracked. Average client (5 locators) = $149 + $75 = $224/mo. 45-55 contractors × $224/mo = **$10,080-12,320/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every incoming ticket opens its own statutory response-deadline countdown, on-time performance recomputes continuously against every utility client's SLA, and photo-documentation accumulates with every completed locate — a high-frequency, per-ticket compliance stream tied directly to ticket volume, not an annual filing.
**Biggest risk:** A ticket that ages past its statutory response window, or a completed locate missing its photo-documentation record when a utility strike investigation follows, is exactly the fact pattern a state damage-prevention board inquiry or a utility SLA audit is built to catch — the response-deadline countdown and the photo-documentation log are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → ticket tracker, SLA dashboard, and documentation log deployed, first 5-8 contractor retainers through NULCA outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 24-28 contractors. Replaces-full-time-salary → 45-55 contractors, pushing past $10.08-12.32K/mo.

### 5. Dental Laboratory FDA Facility Registration, Device Listing & Work-Authorization Documentation Compliance
**Problem:** Dental laboratories that fabricate crowns, bridges, dentures, and other custom dental appliances from a dentist's prescription operate as FDA-regulated device manufacturers under 21 CFR Part 807 — every lab must register its facility annually with the FDA and list the specific device types it produces, and a lab that lets its annual registration renewal lapse is legally barred from shipping devices in interstate commerce until it re-registers, a fact many small labs discover only when a shipment is questioned. In the states that require it (a growing minority), the lab itself must hold a state dental laboratory registration or license separate from any individual dental technician credential, renewed on its own cycle. Every case a lab accepts must be accompanied by a written work authorization (prescription) from the treating dentist specifying the materials, shade, and design — FDA and state dental board guidance both treat a device fabricated without a complete work authorization on file as an adulterated/misbranded device, and the work authorization must be retained for a specific statutory period (commonly matching the state's dental-record retention rule) and produced on demand during an FDA inspection or a state dental board complaint investigation tracing a patient issue back to the lab. Labs using specific alloys or materials in removable devices must also track material lot/batch numbers per case for traceability, since an adverse-reaction investigation (a metal allergy or device failure) requires the lab to identify exactly which material lot went into a specific patient's device. A small lab running 5-15 technicians and dozens of active cases a week manages FDA registration/listing renewal, per-case work-authorization completeness, and material lot traceability with no dedicated compliance function — and a case fabricated without a complete work authorization on file, or a lapsed FDA registration discovered when a shipment is flagged, is exactly the gap an FDA inspection or state dental board inquiry is built to catch.
**What to build:** An FDA facility registration and device-listing renewal tracker with tiered alerts well ahead of the annual deadline; a per-case work-authorization compliance gate blocking a case from being marked "in production" until the dentist's complete written prescription (materials, shade, design) is on file; and a material lot/batch tracker linking each case's finished device to the specific material lot used, for rapid traceability if an adverse-reaction investigation follows.
**Skill fit:** FDA's 21 CFR Part 807 facility registration/device listing requirements and state dental laboratory registration statutes are public federal and state regulatory sources with well-defined renewal cycles, and the work-authorization gate plus material lot traceability tracker are the same deterministic "block production until the required document clears / trace the record back to its source" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a fabrication trade distinct from every prior clinical dental-practice idea in this repo, since a dental lab holds no patient-treatment relationship at all and instead operates as an FDA device manufacturer working from another provider's prescription.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the FDA registration/listing tracker, the work-authorization compliance gate, and the material lot traceability tracker; land the first 5-8 labs through National Association of Dental Laboratories (NADL) member outreach.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — National Association of Dental Laboratories (NADL) member directory and state dental laboratory association meetings, where independent lab owners already navigate FDA registration renewal and work-authorization documentation with no dedicated compliance staff.
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199/mo base + $12/mo per technician tracked. Average client (6 technicians) = $199 + $72 = $271/mo. 35-42 labs × $271/mo = **$9,485-11,382/mo**.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every accepted case opens its own work-authorization compliance event, material lot tracking accumulates with every case produced, and FDA registration/listing renews annually regardless of case volume — a continuous, per-case compliance stream tied directly to the lab's production volume.
**Biggest risk:** A case fabricated without a complete work authorization on file, or a device shipped after the lab's FDA registration lapsed, is exactly the gap an FDA inspection or state dental board complaint investigation is built to catch — the work-authorization gate and the registration renewal tracker are the core protections.
**Growth path:** Side project → FDA registration tracker, work-authorization gate, and material lot tracker deployed, first 5-8 lab retainers through NADL outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 18-22 labs. Replaces-full-time-salary → 35-42 labs, pushing past $9.49-11.38K/mo.
