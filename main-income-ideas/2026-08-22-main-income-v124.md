### 1. Industrial Ammonia Refrigeration System Service Contractor OSHA PSM & EPA RMP Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Contractors who install, service, and maintain the anhydrous-ammonia refrigeration systems used in cold-storage warehouses, food and beverage processing plants, and ice rinks operate at the center of two federal frameworks that both trigger once a facility's ammonia charge crosses 10,000 pounds: OSHA's Process Safety Management standard (29 CFR 1910.119), which requires a documented Process Hazard Analysis (PHA) revalidated at least every five years, a Mechanical Integrity program with scheduled inspection and pressure-testing intervals for compressors, relief valves, and piping, written Management-of-Change (MOC) documentation for any system modification, and Process Safety Information kept current with the as-built system; and EPA's Risk Management Program (40 CFR Part 68), which requires a Risk Management Plan resubmitted on its own five-year cycle documenting a worst-case release scenario and the facility's accident-prevention program. The service contractor is frequently the party a small cold-storage or food-plant client actually depends on to keep this documentation current, since most client facilities have no in-house process-safety engineer. A small ammonia-refrigeration service contractor running technicians across dozens of client systems tracks each client's PHA revalidation due date, mechanical-integrity inspection intervals for pressure-relief devices and piping, MOC records for every system change the contractor itself performs, and each client's RMP five-year resubmission deadline — usually on a service-ticket spreadsheet with no systematic cross-check, and a client whose PHA lapses past its five-year window or whose relief valve inspection interval slips is exactly the finding an OSHA PSM inspection or an EPA RMP audit is built to catch, with a catastrophic ammonia release being one of the most severe possible outcomes in industrial refrigeration.

**What to build:** A per-client PHA revalidation tracker computing the five-year due date from the last completed analysis; a mechanical-integrity inspection and pressure-testing interval tracker for relief valves, compressors, and piping scoped to each client's specific equipment inventory; a Management-of-Change documentation log for every system modification the contractor performs, tied to the specific client facility; and an RMP five-year resubmission tracker flagging any client approaching its filing window.

**Skill fit:** OSHA's PSM mechanical-integrity interval requirements and EPA's RMP five-year resubmission cycle are published, structured federal regulatory data, and the per-client, per-equipment interval computation is the same deterministic "compute the due date from the last-completed event, flag the lapse" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to an industrial-refrigeration service trade this repo has never addressed.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the PHA revalidation tracker, the mechanical-integrity interval tracker, and the RMP resubmission tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent ammonia-refrigeration service contractors.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Refrigerating Engineers & Technicians Association (RETA) runs an active member directory and regional chapter meetings reaching exactly the small independent service contractors who currently track PHA and RMP deadlines across dozens of client systems by hand.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $249-449/mo per contractor depending on client-facility count and equipment portfolio size. 35-45 contractors × $339/mo avg = $11,865-15,255/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every client facility runs its own independent PHA five-year clock, mechanical-integrity inspection intervals recur continuously for the life of every serviced system, and RMP resubmission cycles renew independently per facility — a continuous, client-portfolio-driven compliance stream that scales directly with how many facilities the contractor services.

**Biggest risk:** A contractor servicing only small systems below the 10,000-lb PSM/RMP threshold has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads for contractors servicing mid-size and large cold-storage and food-processing clients specifically, where the PSM/RMP dual-trigger genuinely applies and the stakes of a missed interval are severe.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 contractors ($4.1K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 28 contractors ($9.5K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 35-45 contractors plus a client-facing PSM/RMP audit-readiness export contractors can hand their own clients during an OSHA or EPA inspection.

### 2. Private Cord Blood & Birth-Tissue Bank FDA HCT/P & AABB Accreditation Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Private cord blood and birth-tissue banks that collect and cryogenically store umbilical cord blood and cord tissue for families at birth operate under FDA's Human Cells, Tissues, and Cellular/Tissue-Based Products regulation (21 CFR Part 1271), which requires the bank to register as an HCT/P establishment, perform a documented donor-eligibility determination on the birth mother under Subpart C's communicable-disease screening and infectious-disease testing requirements before a unit can be released for any future use, and maintain the collection-to-storage chain of custody for every unit indefinitely. Most private banks additionally pursue AABB accreditation for cord blood banking, a periodic on-site inspection covering the collection kit's cold-chain integrity, processing timeline (cord blood must typically be processed within a defined window after collection to remain viable), and long-term cryostorage tank monitoring — an accreditation most obstetric practices and hospital labor-and-delivery departments now require before they'll authorize a collection kit to be used on their unit. A courier chain-of-custody gap between hospital collection and the bank's processing lab, or a donor-eligibility record with an incomplete communicable-disease screening, is exactly the finding an FDA biennial establishment inspection or an AABB accreditation survey is built to catch — and for a bank whose entire value proposition to a family is "this unit will still be viable and usable in 20 years," a documentation gap discovered at the moment a family actually needs the unit is the worst possible failure. A small or mid-size private bank managing a growing library of client family units tracks per-unit donor-eligibility documentation, courier chain-of-custody from hospital to lab, cryostorage tank monitoring logs, and AABB re-accreditation currency — usually across a mix of a laboratory information system built for testing rather than long-term family-record custody and a paper collection-kit tracking log.

**What to build:** A per-unit donor-eligibility and communicable-disease-screening documentation tracker gating whether a unit is marked release-eligible; a courier chain-of-custody log capturing collection time, transit time, and receipt-at-lab time against the bank's required processing window; a cryostorage tank temperature-monitoring and maintenance log per storage unit; and an AABB accreditation renewal and on-site-survey-readiness tracker.

**Skill fit:** 21 CFR Part 1271's donor-eligibility and recordkeeping requirements and AABB's cord blood banking standards are published, structured federal and accrediting-body data, and the courier chain-of-custody-to-processing-window computation is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a birth-tissue banking category distinct from the blood/plasma donation center idea already in this repo, whose donor-eligibility framework operates under an entirely separate CFR part with no birth-event, family-owned-specimen model at all.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the donor-eligibility tracker, the courier chain-of-custody log, and the cryostorage monitoring tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent and regional private cord blood banks.

**Time to first $:** 30-60 days — the Cord Blood Association (CBA) and AABB's cord blood banking subsection both run member directories and annual conferences reaching exactly the small and mid-size private banks navigating Part 1271 and AABB accreditation without a dedicated regulatory-affairs department.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $399-799/mo per bank depending on stored-unit volume and accreditation scope, since a lost AABB accreditation or an FDA warning letter is a business-threatening event for a bank whose entire pitch rests on long-term custodial trust. 20-28 banks × $549/mo avg = $10,980-15,372/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every new client birth generates a fresh donor-eligibility and chain-of-custody record that must be retained indefinitely, cryostorage monitoring runs continuously for the life of every stored unit, and AABB re-accreditation recurs on its own cycle — a permanently accumulating, unit-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the bank's ability to keep the custodial promise its entire business model depends on.

**Biggest risk:** The buyer population is a small, specialized niche of a few dozen private cord blood banks nationally, since the large national players (Cord Blood Registry, ViaCord) run in-house regulatory-affairs teams. Mitigate by explicitly targeting independent and regional banks and by pricing at the higher end given the custodial stakes.

**Growth path:** Side project → 8-10 banks ($4.4-5.5K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 16-18 banks ($8.8-9.9K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 20-28 banks plus a hospital-partnership compliance-status export banks can share with labor-and-delivery departments to win new collection-site authorizations.

### 3. ISPM-15 Wood Packaging Material Heat-Treatment & Certification Facility ALSC Accreditation & Treatment-Log Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Companies that heat-treat wood pallets, crates, and dunnage for export under the International Plant Protection Convention's ISPM-15 standard must hold accreditation from the American Lumber Standards Committee (ALSC) as an approved wood packaging material treatment provider, subject to a periodic on-site quality-mark audit reviewing the facility's kiln calibration records and treatment-log completeness. Every treated lot requires a documented kiln run proving the wood reached a minimum core temperature of 56°C (133°F) for at least 30 continuous minutes — the specific heat-treatment schedule ISPM-15 mandates to eliminate wood-boring pests — and only lots with a complete, retrievable treatment record may legally bear the IPPC-approved "HT" stamp; a facility that applies the stamp to an under-treated or undocumented lot is exactly the finding an ALSC compliance audit or a foreign customs inspector is built to catch, and a shipment discovered at a foreign port bearing an invalid or fraudulent HT stamp can be rejected, fumigated, or destroyed entirely at the exporter's expense — a consequence that quickly ends the relationship between the pallet treater and the exporters who depend on its stamp being valid. A small heat-treatment facility running one or more kilns and stamping dozens of lots a week tracks per-lot kiln temperature/duration records, per-kiln calibration and maintenance currency, and ALSC accreditation renewal — usually on paper kiln logs and a stamping logbook with no systematic cross-check confirming a stamped lot's underlying treatment record is actually complete before the stamp is applied.

**What to build:** A per-lot kiln-run tracker recording core temperature and duration against the ISPM-15 56°C/30-minute threshold, with a "cleared to stamp" gate blocking the HT mark from being logged as applied until the treatment record is complete; a per-kiln calibration and maintenance renewal tracker; an ALSC accreditation renewal and audit-readiness tracker; and a stamped-lot traceability log linking each shipment's HT-marked pallets back to their specific kiln-run record for customs-inquiry response.

**Skill fit:** ISPM-15's treatment-temperature standard and ALSC's accreditation requirements are published, structured standards-body data with a concrete numeric threshold, and the "cleared to stamp" gate blocking the mark until the treatment record clears is the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a wood-packaging export-compliance category this repo has never addressed.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the per-lot kiln-run tracker with the stamping gate, the kiln calibration tracker, and the ALSC accreditation tracker. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent pallet and crate heat-treatment facilities.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the National Wooden Pallet & Container Association (NWPCA) runs an active member directory and annual conference reaching exactly the small independent heat-treatment facilities that supply export-bound pallets and currently track kiln records on paper.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-349/mo per facility depending on kiln count and lot volume. 45-60 facilities × $269/mo avg = $12,105-16,140/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every treated lot generates a fresh kiln-run record that must be complete before a stamp is applied, every kiln recurs on its own calibration cycle, and ALSC accreditation renews independently — a continuous, lot-volume-driven compliance stream that scales directly with how many export shipments the facility's customers move.

**Biggest risk:** The largest pallet manufacturers often run in-house quality-control systems tied to their own ERP. Mitigate by explicitly targeting independent single- and multi-kiln heat-treatment facilities that supply export packaging to third-party shippers, where the stamping-gate discipline is still enforced by a logbook rather than a system.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 facilities ($4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 35 facilities ($9.4K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 45-60 facilities plus a customs-inquiry traceability-report add-on for facilities whose exporter clients ship into stricter-enforcement countries.

### 4. COBRA & Benefits-Continuation Third-Party Administrator DOL Notice-Deadline & IRC §4980B Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent COBRA administrators — the specialized third-party administrators small and mid-size employers hire specifically to manage continuation-coverage obligations under their group health plans — operate under a Department of Labor notice-and-deadline framework triggered every time a covered employee experiences a qualifying event (termination, reduction in hours, divorce, or a dependent aging off the plan): an Election Notice that DOL regulations require be sent within 44 days of the qualifying event, a 60-day election window during which the qualified beneficiary may elect coverage, and a premium grace period (a minimum 30 days under federal law) before coverage can be terminated for nonpayment. A notice sent late, sent to the wrong address, or missing DOL's required content elements exposes the employer client to an IRS excise tax under IRC §4980B — calculated per day, per affected qualified beneficiary, and capable of escalating quickly across a family of several dependents — plus a separate ERISA statutory penalty of up to $110/day the Department of Labor can assess directly, and litigation from a beneficiary who was denied coverage because of a defective notice. A small COBRA administration firm managing continuation-coverage obligations for dozens of employer clients tracks each client's qualifying-event log, the 44-day election-notice deadline computed from each event's specific trigger date, the 60-day election window, and the premium grace period for every enrolled qualified beneficiary — usually on a spreadsheet built for one client at a time, with no systematic cross-client deadline view, and a missed 44-day notice deadline discovered only when a beneficiary's attorney sends a demand letter is exactly the gap this framework is built to catch.

**What to build:** A per-client, per-qualifying-event log capturing the trigger date and computing the 44-day election-notice deadline automatically; an election-window and premium-grace-period tracker per qualified beneficiary; a notice-content and delivery-proof archive documenting exactly what was sent, when, and to what address for every notice generated; and a cross-client deadline dashboard surfacing every employer's upcoming and overdue notice obligations in one view.

**Skill fit:** DOL's COBRA notice-timing regulations and IRC §4980B's excise-tax trigger are published, structured federal regulatory data with concrete numeric deadlines, and the qualifying-event-to-notice-deadline computation is the same deterministic "compute the deadline from the trigger event, flag the lapse before it becomes a penalty" pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a benefits-administration niche distinct from the Health Plan TPA idea already in this repo, whose compliance core is claims adjudication and network adequacy rather than continuation-coverage notice timing.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the qualifying-event/notice-deadline tracker, the election-window and grace-period tracker, and the notice-delivery archive. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent COBRA administration firms.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Employers Council on Flexible Compensation (ECFC) and the Society for Human Resource Management's (SHRM) benefits-administration vendor community both reach small independent COBRA administrators directly, supplemented by state-level employee-benefits broker associations whose member brokers frequently refer clients to independent COBRA administration vendors.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $199-399/mo per administrator, priced per employer-client portfolio managed. 45-60 administrators × $269/mo avg = $12,105-16,140/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every qualifying event across every employer client triggers its own independent 44-day-notice-to-60-day-election-to-30-day-grace-period sequence, and a busy administrator manages dozens of these in parallel continuously — a permanent, client-portfolio-and-event-volume-driven compliance stream tied directly to the administrator's core service promise to its employer clients.

**Biggest risk:** Larger national COBRA administration vendors (WEX, ADP) already run enterprise systems built for this exact workflow. Mitigate by explicitly targeting small independent regional administrators and benefits brokers offering COBRA administration as an add-on service, where the compliance function is still a spreadsheet per client rather than a purpose-built system.

**Growth path:** Side project → 15 administrators ($4K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 35 administrators ($9.4K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 45-60 administrators plus a defensible-delivery-proof export administrators can hand an employer client facing a DOL inquiry or beneficiary lawsuit.

### 5. Confined-Space Rescue & Standby-Service Provider OSHA 1910.146 Site-Evaluation & Technician-Certification Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Companies that provide contracted standby confined-space rescue teams to industrial clients — refineries, food-processing plants, water-treatment facilities, and grain-handling operations that authorize entry into permit-required confined spaces (tanks, silos, sewers, manholes) — operate under OSHA's Permit-Required Confined Space standard (29 CFR 1910.146), which requires the rescue service itself to complete and document a site-specific evaluation of each client facility's actual confined spaces before it can be relied upon as that facility's designated rescue provider, and to practice a rescue at each client site at least annually — a rescue team that has never drilled on a specific client's actual tank geometry, entry/exit points, and hazard profile does not meet the standard's own definition of an evaluated rescue service, regardless of how qualified its technicians are in the abstract. Every technician on the roster must additionally hold and keep current an NFPA 1006 Technical Rescuer certification matched to the specific rescue discipline (rope, confined space, trench), along with medical-monitoring and respirator-fit-test currency required to enter a permit space during an actual rescue. A small rescue-service company running a roster of technicians on call across a dozen or more industrial client sites tracks each client's site-specific evaluation and annual practice-drill due date, each technician's NFPA 1006 certification and medical/fit-test currency, and equipment-inspection logs for rescue gear (tripods, winches, SCBA, air-monitoring equipment) — usually on paper drill logs and a certification binder, and a client site whose annual practice drill lapses, or a technician dispatched past their certification's currency window, is exactly the gap an OSHA inspection following an actual confined-space incident is built to catch — with a botched or absent rescue during a real emergency being among the most severe possible outcomes in industrial safety.

**What to build:** A per-client site-evaluation and annual practice-drill tracker computing each facility's next-due drill date and flagging any site approaching or past its window; a per-technician NFPA 1006 certification, medical-monitoring, and respirator-fit-test currency tracker with a "cleared to respond" dispatch gate; an equipment-inspection log for rescue gear tied to each piece's own inspection interval; and a client-facing readiness-status export the rescue service can hand an industrial client during its own OSHA inspection to demonstrate a current, evaluated rescue arrangement is in place.

**Skill fit:** OSHA's 1910.146 site-evaluation-and-annual-drill requirement and NFPA 1006's technician-certification structure are published, structured federal regulatory and standards-body data, and the per-client drill-due-date computation plus the per-technician dispatch-eligibility gate are the same deterministic pattern this series builds repeatedly, applied to a standby industrial-safety-service category this repo has never addressed.

**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build the site-evaluation/drill tracker, the technician certification/dispatch-gate tracker, and the equipment-inspection log. First 5-8 clients through direct outreach to independent confined-space rescue service providers.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — the Occupational Safety and Health Administration's own recognized rescue-service network and the Confined Space Rescue Providers community on the International Society of Confined Space Rescue's (ISCSR) member directory reach exactly the small independent rescue-service companies currently tracking site-drill schedules and technician certifications across dozens of client contracts by hand.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $299-599/mo per rescue-service company depending on client-site count and technician roster size. 30-40 companies × $429/mo avg = $12,870-17,160/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every client site's annual practice drill recurs on its own independent clock for the life of the service contract, every technician's NFPA 1006 certification and medical/fit-test currency renews on its own cycle, and every new industrial client the company signs adds a fresh site-evaluation record — a continuous, client-portfolio-and-headcount-driven compliance stream tied directly to the rescue service's legal standing as each client's designated confined-space rescue provider.

**Biggest risk:** A rescue-service company serving only one or two client sites has a comparatively low-stakes tracking need. Mitigate by qualifying leads for companies serving a multi-site industrial client portfolio specifically, where the annual per-site drill schedule and multi-technician certification matrix genuinely outgrow a paper binder.

**Growth path:** Side project → 12 companies ($5.1K/mo) → replaces-part-time-income → 24 companies ($10.3K/mo) → replaces-full-time-salary at 30-40 companies plus a multi-discipline (rope, trench, confined-space) certification-matrix upsell for companies expanding their service offering beyond confined-space rescue alone.
