# Main Income Ideas - 2026-08-29 (Session 155)

## Who this is for

A 29-year-old with software engineering and data analytics skills who wants to replace a full-time salary — targeting $8-15K+/mo — within 12-24 months, building solo during evenings and weekends with no outside funding. Every idea below needs to be buildable by one person, needs a realistic path to its first paying client inside 90 days, and needs a named, reachable distribution channel (a trade association, a regulator's public directory, a licensing board roster) rather than "run ads and hope."

## Ground rules for every idea in this series

- Solo-buildable start. No team, no funding round, no agency-style delivery model.
- Realistic path to $8-15K+/mo, shown with explicit per-client pricing math.
- Time to first dollar under 90 days.
- Named distribution channel (association, regulator directory, licensing board roster) — not "run ads."
- No repeats. This session covers five regulated industries never addressed in this repo's 154 prior sessions (825 ideas): **charitable/nonprofit gaming operators**, **payment facilitators & merchant-services ISOs**, **direct-selling/MLM companies**, **public warehousemen & 3PL cold-storage operators**, and **municipal solid waste & recycling haulers**. Each is explicitly distinguished below from similar-sounding industries already covered in this repo:
  - **Charitable gaming (bingo/raffle/casino night)** is nonprofit-only fundraising gaming under state charitable-gaming acts (net-proceeds-to-charity rules, licensed bingo workers, no gaming-revenue tax). This is a different regulatory universe from the tribal gaming (NIGC compliance, Session covering federally-recognized tribal casinos), commercial card room/poker room (state Gambling Control Act licensing for for-profit card rooms), and video gaming terminal route operator (state gaming board revenue-share licensing) ideas already in this repo — all three of those are for-profit commercial gaming operations with entirely different licensing bodies and revenue structures.
  - **Payment facilitators/ISOs** register with and are policed by the private card networks (Visa Global Registration Program, Mastercard Registration Program) and must maintain PCI DSS compliance and oversee sub-merchant PCI attestation — a private contractual compliance surface. This is distinct from the money services business/money transmitter idea already covered (public FinCEN/state-DFI money-transmission licensing for entities that hold and move customer funds) and the merchant cash advance broker idea already covered (a lending-product compliance problem, not a card-network-registration problem).
  - **Direct selling/MLM companies** must comply with the FTC Business Opportunity Rule (16 CFR Part 437) and state business-opportunity statutes (Louisiana, Georgia, Maryland, etc.), including anti-pyramid retail-sales-ratio documentation. This has no equivalent in the franchisor FTC Franchise Rule idea already covered in this repo — franchise disclosure (16 CFR Part 436) is a completely separate rule governing a different business format with no retail-ratio/anti-pyramid requirement.
  - **Public warehousemen/3PL cold-storage operators** are governed by UCC Article 7 (negotiable/non-negotiable warehouse receipt law) and general state warehouseman licensing/bonding for general merchandise and cold storage. This is distinct from the grain elevator/grain warehouse idea already covered (USDA/state agricultural-commodity-specific bonding under grain warehouse acts) and the customs bonded warehouse idea already covered (CBP import-duty-deferral compliance under 19 CFR) — neither of those involves UCC Article 7 receipt-integrity risk for general merchandise.
  - **Municipal solid waste & recycling haulers** operate under local hauler-licensing ordinances (often 10-20 separate city/county permits per hauler) and state organics-diversion mandates like California SB 1383. This is distinct from every other waste-stream idea already in this repo — medical waste transporter (DOT infectious-substance rules), hazardous waste transporter (RCRA manifest/EPA ID), C&D debris recycling facility (state processing permit), used oil collector (EPA Part 279), and landfill/transfer station operator (RCRA Subtitle D permit) — each of those is a federal waste-stream-specific track, while general trash/recycling hauling is a local-ordinance-and-diversion-mandate track with no federal waste-stream trigger.

### 1. Charitable Gaming (Bingo, Raffle & Casino Night) License, Worker & Recordkeeping Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Nonprofits that run bingo halls, raffles, and casino nights as fundraisers must obtain and annually renew a charitable/nonprofit gaming license from a state gaming regulator — the Texas Lottery Commission's Charitable Bingo Division under the Bingo Enabling Act, the Ohio Attorney General's Charitable Gaming office, the Illinois Gaming Board under the Charitable Games Act, or county/city licensing under California Penal Code §326.3-326.5. Licensees must file quarterly or annual financial reports itemizing gross receipts, prizes paid, and expenses to prove net proceeds went to the stated charitable purpose (many states require 100% of net proceeds go to charity, with strict caps on rent, worker pay, and equipment cost as a percentage of gross). Individual callers and workers often must be separately registered or licensed and background-checked. Equipment — bingo balls, cards, pull-tab dispensers, raffle ticket stock — frequently must be purchased from a state-licensed distributor with serialized inventory tracked back to the license. A small VFW post, church, or fraternal lodge running weekly bingo has none of this automated: a missed quarterly filing or an unregistered worker calling numbers can suspend the license and, in several states, trigger personal liability for the organization's officers.

**What to build:** A license/permit renewal calendar seeded with state-specific charitable-gaming deadlines (start with Texas, Ohio, Illinois, Michigan, Wisconsin — the five largest charitable-bingo markets); a worker roster with registration status and background-check expiration tracking; per-session revenue/prize/expense entry that rolls up automatically into the state-required quarterly/annual report format (auto-filled PDF where the state provides a fillable form); and an equipment purchase log tying serialized bingo/pull-tab/raffle inventory to the licensed distributor invoice, so an inspector's spot-check reconciles instantly.

**Skill fit:** This is a data-entry-and-report-generation problem — CRUD plus PDF form-filling against a public regulatory calendar — well within reach for a solo engineer with no domain-specific licensing of your own required to build it.

**MVP scope:** Two to three weekends: single-state (Texas) license tracker, worker roster, per-session ledger, and quarterly-report PDF generator. First 5-8 clients from the Texas Lottery Commission's public list of licensed bingo organizations (a public record you can mail/call directly) and the Texas Bingo Association member directory.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — cold outreach to Texas Lottery Commission-licensed bingo organizations plus a booth or newsletter mention at a Texas Bingo Association meeting.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $150-300/mo per nonprofit organization, or $400-600/mo per bingo-hall operator managing multiple tenant organizations' compliance on shared premises. At 40 nonprofit clients × $200/mo = $8,000/mo; layering in 15 hall operators × $500/mo = $7,500/mo brings a realistic ceiling to roughly $11,500-15,500/mo blending both segments.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Quarterly/annual filings, worker re-registration, and equipment purchase reconciliation are non-optional, recurring events tied directly to the organization's ability to keep operating its single largest fundraising activity — usage doesn't lapse between sessions the way a discretionary tool would.

**Biggest risk:** State charitable-gaming rules diverge sharply (Texas requires near-100% net proceeds to charity; other states allow higher worker-pay percentages), so a generalized report-form engine is hard to build up front. Mitigate by shipping one state fully before adding a second, rather than trying to generalize prematurely.

**Growth path:** Side project serving Texas bingo halls → Texas Bingo Association channel partnership → expand state-by-state (OH, IL, MI, WI) → replaces-part-time-income at ~20 clients → replaces-full-time-salary at ~50 clients across multiple states.

### 2. Payment Facilitator / Merchant Services ISO Card-Network Registration & PCI DSS Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent Sales Organizations (ISOs) and Payment Facilitators (PayFacs) that resell card processing must register with Visa (Visa Global Registration Program) and Mastercard (Mastercard Registration Program) under a sponsoring acquiring bank, and that registration must be renewed annually per card brand, per sponsor-bank relationship — many ISOs work with two or three sponsor banks/processors simultaneously and must track each registration separately. ISOs must maintain their own PCI DSS compliance (Self-Assessment Questionnaire or QSA audit depending on volume) and, as PayFacs, must actively monitor and document sub-merchant PCI DSS attestation status under PCI DSS 4.0's third-party service-provider oversight requirements (12.8/12.9) — sub-merchants are tiered by transaction volume with different attestation deadlines, and non-compliant sub-merchants generate monthly non-compliance fees ($10-50/merchant/month) that flow back to the ISO from the card brands. ISOs must also maintain a current registered-DBA list and correctly report terminated-for-cause merchants to Visa's TMF and Mastercard's MATCH inquiry files, and must document, state by state, why their fund-flow model qualifies for the payment-processor exemption from money-transmitter licensing (a 50-state legal-citation exercise most small ISOs never formalize until an examiner asks).

**What to build:** A card-brand registration renewal tracker (Visa/Mastercard, per sponsor bank); a sub-merchant PCI attestation rollup dashboard that ingests CSV/portal exports from the ISO's acquiring processors and flags merchants approaching their tier deadline before brand non-compliance fees kick in; a MATCH/TMF termination-filing log; and a 50-state money-transmitter processor-exemption citation library the ISO can hand an examiner on demand.

**Skill fit:** The core problem — aggregating sub-merchant PCI status from multiple processor exports into one rollup that flags risk before a deadline — is a data-engineering pipeline problem, directly playing to an analytics background; the registration-renewal calendar is standard CRUD.

**MVP scope:** Two to three weekends: registration renewal calendar, CSV importer for sub-merchant PCI attestation feeds (most processors already export this in some form), and a rollup dashboard. First 5-8 clients from the Electronic Transactions Association (ETA) member directory, the trade association for the payments/ISO industry.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — direct outreach to ETA member ISOs plus payments-compliance LinkedIn groups.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $500-1,500/mo per ISO client depending on sub-merchant portfolio size (small ISOs run 200-20,000 sub-merchants). At 15 small-to-mid ISO clients × $800/mo average = $12,000/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Annual per-brand, per-sponsor-bank re-registration and ongoing monthly sub-merchant PCI monitoring are contractually mandatory obligations enforced by the card networks with real financial penalties for lapses, driving continuous paid usage rather than a one-time setup.

**Biggest risk:** Incumbent PCI-scanning vendors (ControlScan/Sysnet, SecurityMetrics) already sell into this space at the scanning layer; differentiate by owning the registration-and-rollup-dashboard layer they don't cover, not by competing on PCI scanning itself.

**Growth path:** Side project serving 3-5 small ISOs → ETA conference presence → 15+ clients replaces-full-time-salary.

### 3. Direct Selling / Multi-Level Marketing Company State Business Opportunity Registration & FTC Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Companies using a direct-selling/network-marketing distributor model must register as a "business opportunity" or "seller-assisted marketing plan" before recruiting distributors in states that require it — Louisiana (La. R.S. 51:1801 et seq.), Georgia (Ga. Code §10-1-410), and Maryland (Business Opportunity Sales Act) among them — each with its own annual renewal, bond, and disclosure-document filing. Federally, the FTC's Business Opportunity Rule (16 CFR Part 437) requires specific disclosure documents whenever earnings claims are made to prospects, and the FTC's post-Herbalife/Vemma/AdvoCare enforcement posture treats a company's ability to show that more than half of its sales go to non-participant retail customers (rather than to distributors buying inventory to qualify for commissions) as the central anti-pyramid defense. Companies must retain distributor compensation records, and the Direct Selling Association's best-practice — increasingly treated as an expected legal safe harbor — is an annually published Income Disclosure Statement showing average earnings by distributor rank. A mid-size direct-selling company with a few thousand active distributors currently tracks none of this in one place: state filing deadlines live with outside counsel, the retail-ratio calculation is a one-off spreadsheet exercise before an FTC inquiry, and the income disclosure statement is assembled manually once a year under time pressure.

**What to build:** A state business-opportunity registration/renewal tracker for the filing states with a document library for each state's required disclosure filing; FTC Business Opportunity Rule disclosure-document version control that timestamps which document version went to which distributor cohort (critical legal defensibility if challenged later); a retail-ratio dashboard that ingests the company's own distributor sales export and computes the retail-vs-personal-purchase ratio on a rolling basis, flagging pyramid-risk patterns (e.g., distributors whose volume is disproportionately personal purchases) before a regulator does; and an income disclosure statement builder that computes average and median earnings by rank from the same sales export.

**Skill fit:** Computing retail ratios and per-rank earnings distributions from a distributor sales CSV/API export is a data-pipeline and statistics problem — a strong match for a data-analytics background, well beyond what a generic compliance-calendar tool would need.

**MVP scope:** Two to three weekends: state registration/renewal tracker for the ~6 filing states, FTC disclosure-document template library, and a CSV importer that computes retail ratio and rank-earnings. First 5-8 clients via the Direct Selling Association (DSA) member directory and referrals from MLM-focused law firms (e.g., Babener & Associates, Grimes & Reese) who advise these companies and can recommend compliance tooling to clients.

**Time to first $:** 45-60 days — this is a smaller, higher-touch market (roughly 200-400 meaningfully active MLM/direct-selling companies in the US), so expect a longer, relationship-driven sales cycle through DSA and referring attorneys rather than volume outreach.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $800-2,000/mo per company given the legal/regulatory stakes involved. At 8 clients × $1,200/mo average = $9,600/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Annual re-registration across filing states, ongoing quarterly retail-ratio monitoring, and yearly income disclosure statement production are recurring obligations tied to active state AG and FTC enforcement risk against non-compliant MLMs — companies don't stop paying for this while they keep recruiting distributors.

**Biggest risk:** A small, high-touch market where losing one client meaningfully dents revenue, and direct-selling companies churn/fail at a higher rate than typical SMBs; mitigate by targeting the more established DSA-member companies (materially longer average tenure) and making the retail-ratio dashboard a genuinely differentiated, sticky compliance-defense tool rather than a checklist.

**Growth path:** 3-4 direct-selling clients as proof → DSA member-directory and law-firm-referral outreach → 8-10 clients replaces-full-time-salary.

### 4. Public Warehouseman / Cold-Storage & Dry-Goods 3PL Operator State License, Bonding & UCC Article 7 Warehouse Receipt Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Third-party logistics operators storing general merchandise or cold-storage goods for hire — as opposed to agricultural-commodity grain warehouses or customs-bonded facilities — issue warehouse receipts governed by UCC Article 7, which imposes statutory content requirements (location, storage rate, goods description, signature) and a duty of reasonable care, with the warehouseman liable for loss or damage. Negotiable receipts create lienholder rights that must be tracked meticulously: issuing a second negotiable receipt against goods already pledged under an outstanding receipt is a classic warehouse-finance fraud exposure that has produced real losses in US commodity-financing scandals. Many states additionally require a public warehouseman's license or registration and a bond sized to average on-hand inventory value, renewed periodically. Cold-storage facilities holding FDA-regulated food layer on FDA food-facility registration (biennial renewal) and FSMA Sanitary Transportation Rule temperature-logging obligations. Small and mid-size public warehousemen — the vast majority of the industry by facility count — run this on spreadsheets and QuickBooks with no systematic check against double-issuing a receipt or letting a bond fall out of proportion with inventory on hand.

**What to build:** A license/bond renewal tracker; a warehouse-receipt issuance ledger that records every negotiable and non-negotiable receipt against a specific inventory lot/bin and blocks issuing a second negotiable receipt against a lot that already has one outstanding; a customer-inventory reconciliation dashboard comparing physical count against receipts outstanding; FDA food-facility biennial-registration and temperature-log-compliance tracking for cold-storage clients; and a bond-coverage-versus-average-inventory-value calculator flagging under-bonding risk before a regulator's periodic review does.

**Skill fit:** Matching physical inventory movements against issued receipts to prevent double-issuance is fundamentally an inventory-reconciliation and ledger-integrity data problem — a direct fit for data-engineering strengths, with the licensing/FDA-registration calendar as standard CRUD on top.

**MVP scope:** Two to three weekends: license/bond renewal tracker, receipt issuance ledger with a double-issuance check, and FDA food-facility biennial-renewal reminder. First 5-8 clients from the International Warehouse Logistics Association (IWLA) member directory, the trade association for third-party public warehousing.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — direct outreach to IWLA-member small and mid-size public warehousemen.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $400-900/mo per facility depending on receipt volume and whether cold-storage/FDA-registration features are used. At 20 facility clients × $600/mo average = $12,000/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every receipt issued, every inventory movement, and every bond-renewal cycle is a recurring, non-optional event with real fraud and liability exposure if mishandled — this isn't a set-it-and-forget-it compliance calendar, it's a transactional ledger the warehouseman touches on every shipment.

**Biggest risk:** Larger 3PL operators already run enterprise WMS platforms (Manhattan, SVT) that may seem to cover this; differentiate by targeting the long tail of small/mid public warehousemen under roughly 200,000 sq ft who run on spreadsheets, and by focusing on the legal-compliance layer (receipt validity, bond adequacy) rather than competing as a general WMS.

**Growth path:** 5 small warehouse clients → IWLA conference sponsorship/presence → 20+ clients replaces-full-time-salary.

### 5. Municipal Solid Waste & Recycling Hauling Company Local License, Weight-Ticket & Diversion-Reporting Compliance Tracker

**Problem:** Independent trash and recycling hauling companies — residential and commercial dumpster, roll-off, and front-load collection — must hold a hauler license or permit in most cities and counties where they operate, and a hauler serving a metro area often needs separate licenses or franchise agreements in 10-20 different municipalities, each with its own renewal date, insurance-certificate submission requirements (commonly naming the municipality as additional insured with locally-set minimum coverage), and vehicle-decal renewal. Increasingly, states and cities mandate diversion reporting: haulers must report tonnage sent to landfill versus diverted to recycling or composting, driven by laws like California's SB 1383 organics-diversion mandate, Massachusetts' commercial waste-ban reporting, and local franchise-agreement reporting clauses. Landfill and transfer-station weight tickets must be retained and reconciled against both customer billing and the mandated diversion report. A regional hauler running 15-30 trucks across a metro area currently tracks municipal license renewals in a shared spreadsheet, files diversion reports manually from paper weight tickets, and has no systematic way to confirm an insurance certificate reached every municipality before its deadline.

**What to build:** A per-municipality hauler-license/permit renewal calendar — the core pain point, given 10-20 separate city/county deadlines and portals per hauler; automated insurance-certificate generation and distribution naming each municipality as additional insured ahead of its specific deadline; and weight-ticket ingestion (photo/OCR or CSV export from landfill scale software) reconciled against customer accounts and rolled up into the state/city-mandated diversion-reporting format, starting with California's SB 1383 hauler reporting requirement.

**Skill fit:** OCR/data-ingestion of weight tickets and multi-jurisdiction deadline tracking is a solid data-engineering problem, and the diversion-rate calculation and mandated-report generation plays directly to an analytics background.

**MVP scope:** Two to three weekends targeting California first, since SB 1383's hauler-reporting mandate creates urgent, non-optional pain right now: license/permit tracker, weight-ticket CSV importer, and an SB 1383-format diversion-report generator. First 5-8 clients from the California Refuse Recycling Council (CRRC) or a regional National Waste & Recycling Association (NWRA) chapter directory.

**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — direct outreach to CRRC/NWRA-member independent and regional haulers ahead of an SB 1383 reporting deadline.

**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** $500-1,200/mo per hauling company depending on number of municipalities served and truck count. At 18 clients × $700/mo average = $12,600/mo.

**Why this can go beyond side money:** Every municipality-licensing renewal, every insurance-certificate deadline, and every diversion-reporting cycle recurs on its own schedule independent of the others, and a lapse in any one can suspend the hauler's right to operate in that jurisdiction — this is continuous operational infrastructure, not a periodic checklist.

**Biggest risk:** National haulers (Waste Management, Republic Services) run their own systems, but the vast majority of the roughly 10,000+ US hauling companies are small/regional operators (per NWRA industry data) without in-house compliance software — target that segment specifically rather than competing for the majors.

**Growth path:** California SB 1383 wedge with 5-8 clients → expand to other states with diversion mandates (Washington, Massachusetts, Vermont, Colorado) → 18+ clients replaces-full-time-salary.
