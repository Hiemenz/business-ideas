### 1. Professional Employer Organization (PEO) State Registration & Client Workers' Comp Compliance
**Problem:** Professional Employer Organizations (PEOs) that co-employ workers on behalf of client companies must maintain state PEO licenses or registrations in every state where they have co-employed workers — currently required in AR, CA, CO, FL, IL, ME, MI, MN, NJ, NY, OK, OR, TX, UT, and several other states with active PEO licensing frameworks. Each state's PEO license or registration requires: annual renewal with financial statements demonstrating net worth above state thresholds (typically $50,000-$250,000 depending on state), surety bond or letter of credit filing (required in several states to protect employee wages and benefits), a qualifying responsible party designation, and in some states an annual client list submission. Beyond their own licensing, PEOs must manage the workers' compensation master policy that covers all co-employed workers across all client companies: maintaining accurate payroll reporting to the workers' comp carrier by client and by job classification (the classification accuracy obligation is similar to that of any employer, but the PEO manages it across dozens or hundreds of client businesses simultaneously), ensuring every client's workers are covered under the master policy with correct rates, and processing mid-policy-year adjustments when clients add employees in new job classifications or expand into new states. Small PEOs (50-300 client companies) have the same multi-state licensing and workers' comp master policy complexity as large national PEOs but with limited compliance and HR infrastructure.
**What to build:** A PEO state registration and workers' comp master policy compliance management service — a multi-state PEO license portfolio manager (tracking each state's PEO license or registration: license number, annual renewal deadline, net worth threshold, surety bond or LOC expiration and renewal, qualifying responsible party designation status, and any state-required annual client list submission — with tiered renewal alerts for each state's licensing requirements), a client workers' comp enrollment and classification tracker (for each client company: the client's business type and primary workers' comp classifications, the employees covered under the master policy by classification, and any mid-year classification changes when clients hire workers in new job categories — generating the payroll reporting data required by the workers' comp carrier for accurate premium allocation), a workers' comp master policy coverage audit calendar (tracking the master policy renewal date, annual auditor schedule for the policy-year payroll audit, the documents required for the audit — client payroll by classification — and any state-specific workers' comp reporting requirements for co-employed workers), a state-specific employer compliance dashboard (for each state where the PEO has co-employed workers: the state unemployment insurance account status, state new hire reporting deadlines by state, and any state-specific co-employer payroll tax compliance obligations), and a client onboarding compliance workflow (documenting each new client's co-employment agreement execution, state new hire reporting for newly co-employed workers, workers' comp classification assignment, and state-specific onboarding documentation for any state with specific PEO-co-employer disclosure requirements).
**Skill fit:** State PEO licensing requirements are publicly available from each state's licensing agency — NAPEO (National Association of Professional Employer Organizations) publishes state-by-state PEO regulatory guidance. The multi-state PEO license portfolio manager (a per-state license database with net worth and bond tracking), client workers' comp classification tracker (a per-client, per-classification payroll reporting tool), master policy audit calendar, and client onboarding workflow are data management problems the builder's engineering skills address directly.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a multi-state PEO license portfolio manager with net worth and surety bond compliance for the 14 states with active PEO licensing requirements, a client workers' comp enrollment and classification tracker, a master policy audit calendar, and a client onboarding workflow; first 5-8 PEO clients through NAPEO member outreach while the multi-state license manager is validated against real PEO licensing portfolios.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through NAPEO (National Association of Professional Employer Organizations) member network and regional PEO association outreach (the concentrated professional community for PEO owners and compliance officers — NAPEO's annual conference is the primary industry event), PEO workers' comp insurance carrier referral networks (carriers like AmTrust, ICW, and Employers Holdings that write workers' comp master policies for PEOs encounter multi-state licensing gaps at every renewal and refer compliance management services to their PEO clients), and PEO accounting firm referral networks (CPA firms that audit PEO financial statements for state licensing net worth requirements see licensing compliance gaps at every engagement and refer systematic tracking services to their PEO clients).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per PEO: $700-1,800/mo (depending on state count and client count — a 3-state PEO with 80 client companies: $700/mo; a 10-state PEO with 250 client companies and complex workers' comp classification diversity: $1,800/mo). Managing 10-15 PEO retainers at $1,100/mo average = **$11,000-16,500/mo**. New state licensing application packages (one-time $600-1,500 per state when a PEO expands into a new state with a licensing requirement) generate significant supplemental revenue during PEO geographic expansion. Reaching 10-15 PEO retainers requires 3-4 NAPEO chapter relationships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** PEO state license renewals occur annually in every licensed state simultaneously. Workers' comp master policy annual audits generate a predictable high-intensity annual engagement for every client. Client onboarding compliance events occur continuously as PEOs add new client companies throughout the year. NAPEO estimates 487 PEOs operating in the US — even 3% market penetration reaches the income target.
**Biggest risk:** A PEO operating without a valid state license in a required state — because the license lapsed during renewal — faces immediate regulatory action from the state labor or insurance department, and co-employment arrangements in that state may become legally void. Build the multi-state license portfolio manager with a 90-day advance renewal alert for each state, and a dashboard that requires the PEO compliance manager to confirm renewal action taken before the license expiration date.
**Growth path:** Side project → multi-state PEO license portfolio manager for 14 PEO-licensing states, client workers' comp tracker, and master policy audit calendar deployed, first 5-8 PEO retainers through NAPEO outreach. ### 2. Hospice Agency Medicare Conditions of Participation & CAHPS Survey Compliance
**Problem:** Hospice agencies that participate in the Medicare hospice benefit must comply with Medicare's Conditions of Participation for Hospices (42 CFR Part 418) — a regulatory framework administered by CMS and enforced through state agency certification surveys. Key CoP compliance obligations include: each hospice patient must have a plan of care developed by the interdisciplinary group (IDG) that is reviewed and updated by the IDG at least every 15 days, or more frequently as the patient's condition changes; the IDG must meet at specified intervals with physician, RN, MSW, and chaplain (or other spiritual care provider) participation — meeting dates, attendees, and clinical decisions must be documented; the hospice must provide at least 5% of its total patient care hours from volunteers (a CMS calculation reviewed during certification surveys); each patient's Hospice Election Statement must be properly executed and maintained; and the hospice must achieve a minimum response rate on the Hospice CAHPS (Consumer Assessment of Healthcare Providers and Systems) patient/family survey, which is mailed to bereaved family members and provides patient satisfaction data publicly reported on the CMS Care Compare website. Small hospice agencies (serving 50-200 patients at any time) have the same CoP compliance obligations as large national hospice organizations but with limited quality and compliance staff.
**What to build:** A hospice Medicare CoP compliance management service — an IDG plan of care review cycle tracker (for each admitted patient: the IDG review interval — 15 days as standard — the date of the last IDG review, and the date of the next required review, with alerts when a patient's next review date is within 3 days and when a review has not been documented by the required date), an IDG meeting documentation compliance calendar (tracking each scheduled IDG meeting: date, required team members in attendance, patients reviewed, and meeting documentation completion — with alerts when attendance records are incomplete or when a required team discipline was absent from a meeting), a volunteer service hour compliance dashboard (tracking aggregate volunteer hours provided across all patients in the current certification period, calculating the 5% volunteer hour threshold based on total paid patient care hours, and alerting when volunteer hours are falling below the required percentage with enough lead time to arrange additional volunteer involvement), a Hospice Election Statement documentation monitor (tracking that every admitted patient has a properly executed Hospice Election Statement on file with the required physician certification of terminal diagnosis and six-month life expectancy — alerting when a patient's election statement is missing required signatures or approaching the physician recertification deadline), and a Hospice CAHPS survey performance dashboard (tracking the agency's Hospice CAHPS response rate and publicly reported domain scores, with a family communication workflow for the bereavement period that may increase survey response rates — and alerts when response rates fall below the CMS threshold that triggers potential survey reporting penalties).
**Skill fit:** CMS Conditions of Participation for Hospices (42 CFR Part 418) are publicly available — the specific IDG meeting frequency requirements, plan of care update standards, volunteer hour calculations, and Hospice Election Statement requirements are clearly stated in the regulation. The IDG plan of care review cycle tracker (a per-patient review calendar with due date alerting), IDG meeting documentation calendar, volunteer hour percentage calculator, Election Statement monitor, and CAHPS performance dashboard are data management and compliance monitoring problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. National Hospice and Palliative Care Organization (NHPCO) member directory provides a defined buyer community.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build an IDG plan of care review cycle tracker with 15-day alert system, an IDG meeting documentation calendar with attendance tracking, a volunteer service hour percentage calculator, and a Hospice Election Statement completeness monitor; first 5-8 hospice agency clients through NHPCO member outreach while the IDG review cycle tracker is validated against real hospice patient census and review documentation workflows.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through NHPCO (National Hospice and Palliative Care Organization) member network and state hospice association outreach (the concentrated professional communities for hospice agency administrators — NHPCO's annual conference is the primary industry event), hospice billing company referral networks (billing companies that process hospice Medicare claims encounter CoP compliance documentation gaps that affect claim processing and actively refer compliance management services to their hospice agency clients), and hospice accreditation body referral networks (ACHC and Joint Commission, which accredit hospice agencies for Medicare certification purposes, see IDG documentation and volunteer hour compliance gaps at every survey and refer systematic compliance tracking tools to agencies they've surveyed).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per hospice agency: $400-900/mo (depending on patient census and compliance complexity — a 60-patient census community hospice: $400/mo; a 150-patient census agency with multiple service locations and complex CAHPS management needs: $900/mo). Managing 20-30 agency retainers at $600/mo average = **$12,000-18,000/mo**. Medicare certification survey preparation packages (one-time $800-2,000 per agency in the year of a scheduled certification survey) generate significant supplemental revenue. Reaching 20-30 agency retainers requires 3-4 NHPCO state chapter relationships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** IDG plan of care reviews must be completed every 15 days for every hospice patient — a 100-patient census agency has up to 7 IDG review cycles completing every day. IDG meetings occur weekly or bi-weekly for each clinical team. Volunteer hour compliance is a continuous tracking obligation throughout the certification period. Hospice CAHPS surveys are mailed quarterly for every bereaved family — performance management is an ongoing rather than point-in-time obligation.
**Biggest risk:** An IDG plan of care review that is overdue — documented after the 15-day interval has passed — is a CoP deficiency finding that state agency surveyors commonly cite during routine certification surveys. Build the IDG review cycle tracker with a hard-cutoff alert: when a patient's review date passes without a documented review, the system changes the patient's status to "OVERDUE" with a red flag on the compliance dashboard — visible to the agency administrator until the overdue review is documented.
**Growth path:** Side project → IDG review cycle tracker and volunteer hour calculator deployed, first 5-8 agency retainers through NHPCO outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 10-12 agency retainers, billing company referral pipeline generating consistent new clients (~$4-7K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 20-30 agency retainers, Medicare certification survey preparation packages as high-value annual revenue events, pushing past $12-18K/mo.

### 3. US Goods Importer CBP Customs Bond, HTS Classification & ACE Entry Compliance
**Problem:** Companies that regularly import goods into the US must maintain a continuous customs bond with CBP (Customs and Border Protection) in an amount that is reviewed annually — CBP targets the bond amount at approximately 10% of duties, taxes, and fees paid in the prior 12 months, and CBP may "accelerate" (terminate) a continuous bond that has become inadequate relative to the importer's activity, leaving the importer without coverage for pending entries. Beyond bond adequacy, CBP's Priority Trade Issues program and focused assessment audits frequently target: HTS (Harmonized Tariff Schedule) classification accuracy (incorrect tariff classifications understate duties and are one of the most common CBP penalty triggers under 19 USC 1592), country of origin accuracy (particularly important for Section 301 tariffs on Chinese goods, Section 232 tariffs on steel and aluminum, and other country-specific tariff programs), and first sale valuation eligibility (for importers using first sale appraisement, CBP documentation requirements are specific and frequently unsatisfied). Small importers ($500K-$10M in annual import value) typically use a customs broker for entry preparation but lack internal tracking of: cumulative duty payments for bond adequacy monitoring, HTS classification consistency across product lines and entry preparer changes, and country of origin program eligibility documentation for tariff preference claims.
**What to build:** A US importer CBP compliance management service — a continuous customs bond adequacy monitor (tracking cumulative annual duty, tax, and fee payments as entries are filed, calculating the estimated current continuous bond requirement (10% of annual duties), and alerting when cumulative payments are approaching a level where the current bond amount may become inadequate — giving the importer time to voluntarily increase the bond before CBP issues an acceleration notice), an HTS classification program management system (maintaining the importer's product catalog with assigned HTS codes, binding ruling numbers where applicable, and classification rationale notes — with version tracking when HTS codes are updated in the annual tariff schedule revision — and a classification review calendar flagging products whose classifications haven't been reviewed since the last annual HTS update), a country of origin documentation tracker (for each imported product: the country of origin, applicable tariff program — MFN, Section 301, Section 232, USMCA, CAFTA, etc. — required supporting documentation for any tariff exclusion or preference claim, and a documentation currency check confirming that supplier certificates and manufacturer declarations are current), an ACE entry filing accuracy dashboard (reviewing entry data quality after each filing by the customs broker: confirming that the HTS code matches the product's assigned code in the classification program, that the country of origin matches the documented origin, and that the dutiable value is consistent with the product's purchase order amount), and a CBP focused assessment and audit readiness tracker (maintaining the documentation package for CBP's standard focused assessment document request: 5 years of import records organized by HTS chapter, supplier invoices, country of origin documentation, and the importer's internal classification procedures documentation).
**Skill fit:** CBP continuous bond requirements, HTS tariff classification standards, and country of origin documentation requirements are publicly available from CBP's CSMS (Customs and Trade Automated Interface Requirements) and CBP regulations (19 CFR). Building the bond adequacy monitor (a running cumulative duty payment tracker with bond threshold calculation), HTS classification program (a per-product classification database with annual update tracking), and country of origin documentation tracker are data management problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. National Customs Brokers & Forwarders Association of America (NCBFAA) member networks provide referral pathways to the target buyer community.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a continuous customs bond adequacy monitor with annual duty accumulation tracking, an HTS classification program database with annual schedule update flagging for the 20 most-commonly-imported HTS chapters, a country of origin documentation tracker with tariff program eligibility flags for Section 301 and USMCA, and an ACE entry accuracy dashboard; first 5-8 importer clients through NCBFAA member referral outreach and trade compliance association networks while the bond adequacy monitor is validated against real importer entry data.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through NCBFAA (National Customs Brokers & Forwarders Association of America) member network and customs broker referral relationships (customs brokers who file entries for importers see classification inconsistencies and bond inadequacy risks at every entry and actively refer compliance program management services to their larger importer clients), trade compliance professional association outreach (Customs and International Trade Bar Association, and ICPA — International Compliance Professionals Association — whose members include the in-house trade compliance staff at mid-size importers who need systematic tools), and international trade attorney referral networks (attorneys who defend importers in CBP penalty proceedings encounter HTS classification and country of origin documentation deficiencies in every case and refer compliance program management to importers in their network who have not yet been targeted).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per importer: $500-1,200/mo (depending on product line complexity and import volume — a 15-HTS-code importer with straightforward country of origin: $500/mo; a 100-HTS-code importer with USMCA preference claims and Section 301 exclusion management: $1,200/mo). Managing 15-20 importer retainers at $800/mo average = **$12,000-16,000/mo**. CBP focused assessment audit response documentation packages (one-time $2,000-5,000 when an importer receives a CBP focused assessment notification) generate significant supplemental revenue. Reaching 15-20 importer retainers requires 4-6 active customs broker referral relationships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** Continuous bond adequacy monitoring is a daily obligation as entries are filed. HTS classification accuracy must be maintained for every product imported continuously. Annual HTS tariff schedule revisions (effective January 1 each year) create a classification review event for every product in every importer's catalog simultaneously — a concentrated annual urgency event. Section 301 tariff exclusion expirations and new tariff program announcements create recurring urgent classification and origin determination events throughout the year.
**Biggest risk:** An importer whose continuous bond is accelerated by CBP — because the bond became inadequate relative to cumulative duties and CBP didn't receive a voluntary bond increase — must post individual entry bonds for every subsequent shipment until a new continuous bond is established, disrupting supply chain operations significantly. Build the bond adequacy monitor with a threshold alert at 80% of the current bond amount: when cumulative annual duties reach 80% of 10x the current bond, the system generates an immediate alert recommending a proactive bond increase before CBP calculates the potential inadequacy.
**Growth path:** Side project → bond adequacy monitor, HTS classification database for 20 key chapters, and country of origin tracker deployed, first 5-8 importer retainers through customs broker referral outreach. Replaces-part-time-income → 8-10 importer retainers, annual HTS schedule update reviews generating concentrated January revenue events (~$5-8K/mo). Replaces-full-time-salary → 15-20 importer retainers, CBP focused assessment response documentation as high-value supplemental revenue, pushing past $12-16K/mo.

### 4. Competitive Telecom Carrier Multi-State FCC & State PUC Authorization Compliance
**Problem:** Competitive telecommunications carriers — CLECs (Competitive Local Exchange Carriers), IXCs (interexchange carriers), VoIP providers offering telephone exchange service, and wireless resellers — must maintain regulatory authorizations at both the federal and state levels. Federal compliance requirements include: FCC CLEC or IXC authorization, USAC Universal Service Fund contribution registration (required for any entity providing interstate telecommunications), annual FCC Form 499-A (Telecommunications Reporting Worksheet) and quarterly 499-Q filings reporting revenues that determine USF contribution assessments, and FCC E-Rate and Lifeline program compliance for carriers participating in those programs. State compliance requires: a Certificate of Public Convenience and Necessity (CPCN) or equivalent state authorization in every state where the carrier provides intrastate service (required in most states, with state PUC approval processes that take 30-180 days for new applications and annual regulatory fee assessments), state universal service fund contribution compliance (many states have state-level USF programs with separate contribution requirements), and tariff or price list filings in states that still regulate local exchange carrier rates. Small CLECs and competitive carriers (operating in 3-10 states) frequently lack the dedicated regulatory affairs staff to manage multi-state PUC compliance, and commonly discover state authorization gaps when expanding into a new state or during a state PUC audit.
**What to build:** A competitive telecom carrier FCC and state PUC compliance management service — a multi-state PUC authorization portfolio manager (tracking each state's CPCN or equivalent authorization: authorization number, any annual renewal requirements and fees, the state PUC's regulatory fee assessment basis and payment record, any tariff filing obligations and tariff revision history — with alerts before annual regulatory fee payment deadlines), an FCC Form 499-A and 499-Q filing compliance calendar (tracking the annual 499-A filing deadline (April 1 for prior-year revenues) and quarterly 499-Q filing deadlines, the revenue categories required for accurate contribution assessment, and filing confirmation records), a USF contribution payment tracker (tracking federal USF contribution invoices and payment history, state-level USF contribution requirements in states with state USF programs, and any contribution payment disputes or adjustments), a new state entry authorization workflow (when the carrier wants to expand into a new state: the state PUC application requirements, required filing fees, processing timeline, application submission tracking, and interim operating authority documentation if available while the CPCN application is pending), and a state PUC annual compliance filing calendar (tracking each state's annual compliance report or registration renewal, any state-required network outage reporting obligations, and state-mandated consumer protection compliance certifications).
**Skill fit:** FCC Form 499 filing requirements, USAC contribution procedures, and state PUC CPCN requirements are publicly available from FCC and each state's PUC website — building a multi-state telecom regulatory database is a regulatory research problem. The multi-state PUC portfolio manager (a per-state authorization database with fee and renewal calendar), FCC 499 filing calendar, USF contribution tracker, new state entry workflow, and state annual compliance calendar are data management and compliance calendar problems the builder's engineering skills address directly. NTCA and CTIA member directories and the Competitive Carriers Association (CCA) provide defined buyer communities.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a multi-state PUC authorization portfolio manager for the 20 largest CLEC operating states (CA, TX, NY, FL, IL, PA, OH, MI, NJ, NC, GA, VA, WA, MA, MN, AZ, CO, WI, IN, TN), an FCC Form 499-A and 499-Q filing calendar, a USF contribution payment tracker, and a new state entry authorization checklist; first 5-8 CLEC or competitive carrier clients through Competitive Carriers Association (CCA) and NTCA member outreach while the multi-state PUC portfolio manager is validated against real carrier authorization portfolios.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through CCA (Competitive Carriers Association) and ITTA (Internet and Television Association) member networks (the concentrated professional communities for competitive telecom carrier operators), telecom regulatory law firm referral networks (attorneys specializing in FCC and state PUC proceedings encounter multi-state authorization compliance gaps at every state entry matter and refer systematic compliance tracking services to carrier clients in their network), and VoIP industry association networks (cloud communications providers and hosted VoIP companies that qualify as telecom carriers have the same FCC Form 499 and state authorization compliance obligations and are an underserved compliance management buyer segment).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per carrier: $500-1,200/mo (depending on state count and service type complexity — a 3-state CLEC: $500/mo; a 10-state carrier with FCC IXC authorization, USAC E-Rate participation, and multiple state PUC annual compliance filings: $1,200/mo). Managing 15-20 carrier retainers at $800/mo average = **$12,000-16,000/mo**. New state PUC CPCN application packages (one-time $1,000-2,500 per state for application preparation and processing management) generate significant supplemental revenue during carrier geographic expansion. Reaching 15-20 carrier retainers requires 3-4 CCA and ITTA chapter relationships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** FCC Form 499-Q filings are required quarterly — four filing events per year for every carrier. State PUC annual regulatory fees and compliance filings create annual compliance events in every operating state simultaneously. State PUC audits of CPCN holders are increasing as states expand their telecommunications regulatory oversight. USAC annual true-up calculations create year-end contribution adjustment events for every carrier.
**Biggest risk:** A competitive carrier operating in a state without a valid CPCN — because the carrier failed to obtain authorization before offering service — may face state PUC enforcement action including customer notification requirements, refund obligations, and civil penalties. Build the new state entry authorization workflow with a pre-revenue check: before a carrier marks a state as "offering service," the system requires that a CPCN application has been submitted and a processing reference number has been documented — preventing unauthorized service commencement.

Replaces-part-time-income → 8-10 carrier retainers, new state CPCN application packages generating supplemental revenue (~$5-8K/mo). ### 5. Mortgage Servicer Multi-State NMLS License & CFPB Regulation X Consumer Protection Compliance
**Problem:** Mortgage servicers — companies that collect monthly mortgage payments, manage escrow accounts, and handle loss mitigation for borrowers in default — must maintain state mortgage servicer licenses in most states where they service mortgages (currently required in 45+ states, each with annual NMLS renewal, net worth requirements ranging from $25,000 to $500,000+ by state, surety bond requirements that scale with servicing portfolio volume, and state-specific mortgage servicer examination obligations). Beyond licensing, mortgage servicers must comply with CFPB's Regulation X (Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act implementing regulation) consumer protection requirements that impose specific response timeframes: a Qualified Written Request (QWR) — any written request from a borrower to dispute account information or request servicing records — must be acknowledged within 5 business days and substantively resolved within 30 business days (extendable to 45 business days with notice); an error resolution notice must be acknowledged within 5 business days and resolved within 30 business days; and a request for information must be fulfilled within 30 business days. CFPB examinations of mortgage servicers routinely find QWR and error resolution response time failures at small servicers that manage QWRs through a general customer service inbox without systematic tracking. Small mortgage servicers (servicing 1,000-25,000 loans in 5-20 states) have the same multi-state licensing and CFPB Regulation X obligations as large national servicers but with no dedicated compliance department.
**What to build:** A mortgage servicer multi-state license and CFPB Regulation X compliance management service — a multi-state NMLS mortgage servicer license portfolio manager (tracking each state's mortgage servicer license or registration through NMLS: license number, annual renewal deadline, net worth threshold, surety bond amount and expiration, state examination due date, and any state-specific reporting requirements — with tiered renewal alerts well in advance of each state's deadline), a QWR and error resolution intake and response tracking system (logging each QWR or error resolution notice received: date received, borrower name, loan number, issue description, 5-business-day acknowledgment deadline, 30-business-day resolution deadline — with alerts when the acknowledgment or resolution deadline is approaching or missed), a Regulation X request for information tracker (separately tracking borrower requests for servicing account information: receipt date, the 30-business-day response deadline, response content, and confirmation of delivery — distinct from QWR tracking because the regulatory framework treats information requests and error resolutions as separate compliance categories), a state mortgage servicer examination readiness calendar (tracking each state's examination cycle — most states examine mortgage servicers every 2-4 years on a rotating schedule — the date of the last examination, the expected next examination window, and the documents typically requested in each state's examination request package), and a servicing escrow account compliance dashboard (tracking escrow account analysis completion requirements — annual escrow analysis for each escrow account, surplus or shortage identification, and borrower notification requirements under RESPA — with alerts when any escrow account is overdue for its annual analysis).
**Skill fit:** CFPB Regulation X QWR and error resolution requirements (12 CFR 1024.35 and 1024.36) are clearly published in CFPB regulation, including response timeframes and required content — building the intake and response tracking system from this regulatory framework is straightforward. State mortgage servicer licensing requirements are available through NMLS Resource Center's state licensing requirements database. The multi-state NMLS license portfolio manager (a per-state license database with bond and renewal calendar), QWR/error resolution tracker (a per-request intake-to-resolution calendar with deadline alerting), Regulation X information request tracker, examination readiness calendar, and escrow analysis dashboard are data management and compliance calendar problems the builder's engineering skills address directly.
**MVP scope:** 2-3 weekends to build a multi-state NMLS mortgage servicer license manager with net worth and bond tracking for 25 high-volume mortgage states (CA, TX, FL, NY, PA, IL, OH, NC, GA, NJ, VA, WA, AZ, CO, MI, MA, MD, MN, IN, WI, TN, SC, MO, OR, KY), a QWR and error resolution intake tracker with 5-day and 30-day CFPB deadline alerts, a Regulation X information request tracker, and a state examination readiness calendar; first 5-8 mortgage servicer clients through Mortgage Bankers Association (MBA) Servicer Council outreach while the QWR intake tracker is validated against real servicer compliance workflows.
**Time to first $:** 30-45 days — distribute through MBA (Mortgage Bankers Association) Servicer Council and MISMO member networks (the concentrated professional communities for mortgage servicer compliance officers), state mortgage banking association outreach (each state's MBA chapter hosts servicer compliance committee meetings where compliance resource providers can present directly to the target buyer), and mortgage servicer CFPB examination defense law firm referral networks (attorneys who represent servicers in CFPB supervisory exams encounter QWR response time failures in every exam and actively refer systematic tracking services to servicer clients in their network who have not yet been examined).
**Income ceiling (realistic, with math):** Monthly retainer per mortgage servicer: $700-2,000/mo (depending on state count and loan portfolio size — a 5-state servicer with 3,000 loans: $700/mo; a 20-state servicer with 20,000 loans and high QWR volume: $2,000/mo). Managing 10-15 servicer retainers at $1,200/mo average = **$12,000-18,000/mo**. CFPB examination preparation documentation packages (one-time $2,000-5,000 when a servicer receives a CFPB examination scheduling letter) generate significant supplemental revenue. Reaching 10-15 servicer retainers requires 3-4 MBA Servicer Council chapter relationships.
**Why this can go beyond side money:** QWRs and error resolution notices arrive continuously throughout the year from borrowers — a 10,000-loan servicer receives QWRs consistently every week. Annual escrow analysis requirements apply to every escrow account in the servicing portfolio simultaneously.

Build the QWR intake tracker with a multi-channel intake capture requirement: QWRs received by mail, fax, and email are all logged to the system at receipt with an automatic 5-business-day acknowledgment calendar entry generated immediately, and the acknowledgment letter is prepared from a system-generated template.
