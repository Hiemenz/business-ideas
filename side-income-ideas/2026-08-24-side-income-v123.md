# Side Income Ideas - 2026-08-24 (Round 123)

Simple operational tracker tools for self-employed individuals and micro-businesses. These are NOT compliance tools — they're practical day-to-day workflow trackers that replace the messy spreadsheet, whiteboard, or notebook a solo operator currently uses to run their book of business. Each idea targets a different type of self-employed operator not already covered in this repo, names a real distribution channel, and gives realistic per-account pricing and a time to first dollar.

---

### 1. Vintage Typewriter Repair & Restoration Technician Intake, Parts-Sourcing & Return Tracker

An independent typewriter repair technician servicing manual and electric machines for collectors, writers, and vintage-office enthusiasts has to track each unit's specific make, model, and mechanical quirks — a 1940s manual portable and a 1970s IBM Selectric need entirely different parts and adjustment procedures — alongside a running parts-sourcing list, since replacement platens, ribbons in discontinued spool sizes, and specific type-bar segments are increasingly sourced from a small handful of donor-machine partouts and specialty vendors rather than any current supplier. A technician running eight to fifteen concurrent repair jobs at different stages currently tracks per-machine fault history and ordered-but-not-yet-arrived parts on a paper shop ticket and a memory of which donor machine still has a working carriage-return spring, and a machine reassembled with the wrong-era platen because nobody flagged the compatibility issue is a rework that blows a customer's pickup date, while a customer's heirloom machine returned without documenting its pre-repair condition is a dispute waiting to happen if a cosmetic scratch gets blamed on the shop.

The tool maintains a per-machine intake and fault-diagnosis log tracking make/model-specific known issues, prior repair history, and pre-repair condition photos; a parts-sourcing tracker flagging ordered, backordered, and in-hand parts (including donor-machine partout inventory) against each open job; and a completed-repair and return-status board sequencing pickup-ready machines against customer contact info.

Monetization: $15-25/mo per technician. Distribution: r/typewriters and the Typewriter Collectors community, the Herman Price Vintage Typewriter Museum's repair-referral network, and regional typewriter-collector and "typosphere" Facebook groups where independent repair technicians already trade parts-sourcing and donor-machine tips. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 2. Sewing Machine & Serger Repair Technician Service Ticket & Tension-Adjustment Tracker

An independent sewing machine and serger repair technician servicing home sewists and small alteration shops has to track each machine's specific brand, model, and stitch-mechanism type — a vintage mechanical Singer, a modern computerized embroidery machine, and a four-thread serger each require entirely different timing adjustments, tension calibration, and belt/motor parts, and a machine returned with tension improperly reset is a customer back at the counter within a week with puckered seams. A technician running a steady stream of walk-in and mail-in repairs currently tracks per-machine service history, tension/timing adjustment notes, and parts on order on a paper repair tag and a shop-parts drawer, and a repeat customer's machine serviced without any record of what was adjusted last time is a technician re-diagnosing the same problem from scratch, while a mail-in repair shipped back without the original tension settings noted is a customer immediately calling to say it's stitching worse than before.

The tool maintains a per-machine service history log (brand, model, and adjustment/timing notes from every visit) tied to a parts-on-order tracker; a walk-in versus mail-in job queue with shipping and return-tracking status; and a customer machine-ownership record flagging repeat-service patterns that might indicate a machine nearing end of economical repair.

Monetization: $15-25/mo per technician. Distribution: r/sewing repair-service threads and the Association of Sewing and Design Professionals member network, plus local quilting-guild and fabric-shop referral Facebook groups where independent repair techs already trade service-ticket and tension-adjustment workflow tips. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 3. Vintage & Film Camera Repair Technician CLA Job & Parts-Cannibalization Tracker

An independent film camera repair technician performing clean-lubricate-adjust (CLA) service and shutter/light-seal repair on vintage mechanical and early electronic cameras has to track each camera's specific model quirks — a 1960s all-mechanical rangefinder and a 1980s electronic-shutter SLR fail in completely different ways, and light-seal foam, shutter-curtain material, and specific mirror-box parts are frequently sourced only by cannibalizing a "parts body" bought specifically for that purpose. A technician running a dozen or more cameras through the bench at different repair stages currently tracks per-camera fault diagnosis, CLA checklist completion, and which parts body a given cannibalized component came from on a paper repair tag and a shelf of unsorted parts bodies, and a camera reassembled with a light seal that wasn't actually replaced because the CLA checklist step got skipped during a busy week is a customer's first roll of film ruined by light leaks, while a parts body cannibalized without logging what was taken from it is a technician who can't tell, three jobs later, whether that body still has a usable shutter.

The tool maintains a per-camera intake and CLA-checklist tracker (light seals, shutter timing test, meter calibration) with pre- and post-repair test-roll notes; a parts-body inventory log tracking which components have already been harvested from each donor camera; and a job-status board sequencing walk-in, mail-in, and long-lead-time restoration projects separately.

Monetization: $15-25/mo per technician. Distribution: r/AnalogCommunity and r/camerarepair practitioner threads, the Vintage Camera Rangefinder Forum, and film-photography collective Facebook groups where independent repair techs already trade CLA-checklist and parts-body-sourcing tips. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 4. Vintage Guitar Amplifier Repair & Restoration Technician Service Ticket & Tube/Component Tracker

An independent guitar amplifier repair technician servicing tube amps for gigging musicians and collectors has to track each amp's specific chassis layout, tube complement, and known failure points — a 1960s point-to-point-wired tube amp and a modern hybrid amp fail through entirely different mechanisms, and a customer's amp that buzzes intermittently on stage needs a documented diagnostic history rather than a fresh guess every time it comes back on the bench. A technician running several amps through the shop at once alongside a rotating tube and capacitor inventory currently tracks per-amp repair history, tube-swap records, and parts on hand on a paper service tag and a bench-side parts bin, and a gigging musician's amp returned without re-biasing after a tube swap is a blown power tube at the next show, while a vintage amp serviced without documenting which original components were replaced (versus preserved for collector value) is a restoration dispute with a collector who wanted originality preserved wherever possible.

The tool maintains a per-amp service history log (chassis notes, tube complement, and bias settings from every visit) tied to a tube/capacitor/component inventory tracker; an originality-versus-replacement log for vintage amps flagging which components were swapped for collector-value documentation; and a gigging-musician rush-queue distinct from long-term collector restoration projects.

Monetization: $15-25/mo per technician. Distribution: r/guitaramps and r/AmpRepair practitioner threads, the Music Electronics Forum, and regional gigging-musician and guitar-tech Facebook groups where independent amp techs already trade service-ticket and bias-setting workflow tips. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 5. Custom Fishing Rod Builder Build-Queue & Component Inventory Tracker

An independent custom fishing rod builder assembling rods to a customer's specific action, length, and guide-spacing preferences has to track each build's exact blank model, guide set, reel seat, and wrapping-thread color against a rotating component inventory, since a rod blank ordered in the wrong action or power rating is a build that has to be scrapped and restarted, and a customer's build spec involving a specific guide brand currently on backorder is a completion date the builder can't promise until the part actually arrives. A builder running several custom rods through different build stages at once — blank prep, guide placement, thread wrapping, epoxy curing — currently tracks each build's component list and stage status on a paper build sheet and a component-parts shelf, and a rod epoxy-cured before its guide spacing was actually verified against the customer's casting-style spec is a rework that costs a full cure cycle, while a custom color-matched thread wrap ordered without checking current stock is a build stalled mid-wrap waiting on a re-order.

The tool maintains a per-build component checklist (blank model/action, guide set, reel seat, and thread color) matched to the customer's original spec; a build-stage tracker sequencing blank prep, guide placement, wrapping, and epoxy-cure across multiple concurrent builds; and a component inventory view flagging low-stock guides, blanks, and thread colors before a build gets scheduled against them.

Monetization: $15-25/mo per builder. Distribution: r/RodBuilding and the Rod Building Forum, the Custom Rod Builders Guild member network, and regional fishing-tackle-shop and tournament-angler Facebook groups where independent builders already trade build-queue and component-sourcing workflow tips. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 6. Neon Sign Fabricator Custom Order, Tube-Bending Job & Material Tracker

An independent neon sign fabricator hand-bending glass tubing into custom signage for bars, storefronts, and artists has to track each order's specific glass-tube pattern, bend sequence, and gas-fill type (neon versus argon/mercury for different colors) against a job that can take multiple bench sessions to complete, since a single cracked bend late in a complex pattern means restarting that section from raw tubing rather than salvaging the work already done. A fabricator running several custom sign orders through different build stages — pattern layout, tube bending, electrode sealing, gas filling and processing, transformer wiring — currently tracks per-order pattern specs, glass-tubing stock, and stage completion on a paper shop order and a tube-stock shelf sorted by diameter and color, and a sign shipped with the wrong gas fill for its intended color is a customer receiving a sign that doesn't match the approved mockup, while a custom order that sits mid-bend for weeks with no tracked stage status is a client repeatedly calling for an update the fabricator can't give with confidence.

The tool maintains a per-order pattern and specification log (tube diameter, bend pattern, gas-fill type, and transformer requirements) tied to a build-stage tracker sequencing bending, sealing, gas-filling, and wiring; a glass-tubing and electrode stock inventory flagging low supply before a job is scheduled against it; and a client-facing mockup-to-completion status view for order updates.

Monetization: $19-29/mo per fabricator. Distribution: r/neon and neon-bender practitioner threads, the Neon Sign Museum/glass-bending workshop network, and regional sign-shop and bar/restaurant-vendor Facebook groups where independent neon fabricators already trade build-stage and material-sourcing workflow tips. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.
