# Side Income Ideas - 2026-08-24 (Round 121)

Simple operational tracker tools for self-employed individuals and micro-businesses. These are NOT compliance tools — they're practical day-to-day workflow trackers that replace the messy spreadsheet, whiteboard, or notebook a solo operator currently uses to run their book of business. Each idea targets a different type of self-employed operator not already covered in this repo, names a real distribution channel, and gives realistic per-account pricing and a time to first dollar.

---

### 1. Permanent Jewelry (Welded Bracelet) Pop-Up Artist Event Booking, Chain Inventory & Multi-Venue Tracker

An independent permanent jewelry artist who welds a thin gold-filled or sterling chain directly onto a customer's wrist or ankle at both a fixed studio and a rotating circuit of pop-up events — bridal showers, corporate parties, mall kiosks, and festival booths, where much of the revenue actually comes from — has to track a rotating chain and clasp inventory by metal type, gauge, and remaining spool length across every venue type, since a bridal-party pop-up can burn through an entire spool of a popular gold-filled style in one sitting. An artist running several weekend pop-ups a month alongside studio walk-ins currently tracks chain stock and event bookings on a phone notes app and a chain spool count eyeballed before loading the car, and a pop-up that runs out of a popular chain style halfway through a bridal party is a line of disappointed guests and lost add-on sales, while a double-booked event date because a client's inquiry got buried in Instagram DMs is a canceled appointment and a damaged referral relationship with the venue that booked her.

The tool maintains a per-event booking calendar (venue, date, estimated guest count, and deposit status) kept separate from a walk-in studio appointment queue; a chain and clasp inventory tracker by metal type, gauge, and remaining spool length with a pre-event "loaded kit" checklist; and a per-customer welded-jewelry history log (chain style, weld date, and any resize or re-weld requests) for repeat customers building out a stack.

Monetization: $15-25/mo per artist. Distribution: r/PermanentJewelry and TikTok/Instagram permanent-jewelry-artist creator communities, an emerging Permanent Jewelry Association member network, and mobile pop-up-event vendor Facebook groups where independent welders already trade event-logistics and chain-inventory tips. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 2. Pinball & Arcade Machine Restoration Technician Unit Intake, Parts-Sourcing & Route Tracker

An independent pinball and arcade machine restoration technician repairing and restoring machines for home collectors, barcades, and a small paid route of machines placed on location has to track each unit's specific make-and-model quirks — a 1970s electromechanical machine and a modern digital board need entirely different diagnostic approaches — alongside a running parts-sourcing list, since replacement boards, flipper coils, and specific playfield decals are often out-of-production and sourced from a small handful of specialty vendors and online parts-swap forums. A technician running eight to twelve concurrent restoration jobs at different stages currently tracks model-specific fault history and ordered-but-not-yet-arrived parts on a paper shop ticket and a memory of which vendor had the last working flipper coil, and a machine reassembled with a wrong-generation part because nobody flagged the compatibility issue is a rework that blows a collector's delivery date, while a route machine's coin box left uncollected for a month because nobody tracked the pickup schedule is real cash sitting exposed in a public venue.

The tool maintains a per-machine intake and fault-diagnosis log tracking model-specific known issues and repair history; a parts-sourcing tracker flagging ordered, backordered, and in-hand parts against each open job; and a route-collection schedule and revenue-split ledger for machines placed on location.

Monetization: $15-25/mo per technician. Distribution: r/pinball and r/arcade restoration-focused threads, the Pinball Expo and IFPA (International Flipper Pinball Association) technician network, and regional pinball/arcade collector Facebook groups where independent restorers already trade parts-sourcing and route-management tips. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 3. Vinyl Record Cleaning, Grading & Consignment Restoration Service Batch & Shop-Account Tracker

An independent vinyl record cleaning and restoration service that deep-cleans, regrades, and preps records for resale — working with individual collectors, estate-sale liquidators, and small record shops on consignment — has to track each batch's specific cleaning method (an ultrasonic run, a manual wet-clean, or a full refurbishment for a warped or heavily soiled record each carry different time and fluid costs) against a before-and-after grading standard, since a record's resale value swings sharply on its graded condition, and separately manage a per-shop consignment ledger tracking which records are out for sale, at what list price, and under what commission split. A service running through dozens of records a week currently tracks batch status, before/after grades, and shop consignment splits on a spreadsheet and a stack of grading slips rubber-banded to sleeves, and a record shipped back to a consignor ungraded or mis-graded is a pricing dispute, while a consignment sale that never gets reconciled against the shop's payout is real money the service can't account for.

The tool maintains a per-batch cleaning-method and before/after grading tracker (method used, time/chemical cost, and pre- and post-clean grade); a per-shop consignment ledger tracking items out for sale, list price, and commission split with a sold/unsold status; and a client intake queue distinguishing individual collector drop-offs from bulk estate-sale lots.

Monetization: $15-25/mo per service. Distribution: r/VinylCollectors and r/vinyl cleaning-service threads, the Record Store Day retailer network and independent record-shop-owner Facebook groups where cleaning services already source consignment relationships, and regional record-collector-show vendor communities. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 4. Home-Movie & Media Digitization Service (VHS/Cassette/Film Reel) Order Queue & Format Tracker

An independent media digitization service converting a client's boxes of VHS tapes, MiniDV cassettes, audio cassettes, and Super 8/16mm film reels into digital files has to track each physical item's specific format and playback-equipment requirement — a VHS deck, a MiniDV camcorder, a reel-to-reel film scanner, and a cassette deck are four entirely different machines, several of them themselves aging and prone to failure mid-transfer — alongside a per-item progress status, since a single client order can span 40-80 individual tapes or reels moving through capture, cleanup, and file-delivery stages at different speeds. A service processing several client orders a month currently tracks item-level format and progress on a paper intake sheet and a shelf of loosely sorted boxes, and a tape returned to the wrong family, or a reel marked "digitized" that was actually never captured because the film scanner jammed mid-run, is an irreplaceable family memory the service can't recover from.

The tool maintains a per-item intake log (format, physical condition notes, and the specific playback equipment required) tied to a capture-cleanup-delivery stage tracker; a client order manifest matching every digitized file back to its original physical item and household; and an equipment-maintenance log flagging any capture deck or scanner approaching a service interval before it fails mid-transfer.

Monetization: $15-25/mo per service. Distribution: r/AnalogCommunity and r/VHS digitization-service threads, the Association of Moving Image Archivists (AMIA) practitioner network for archival-minded operators, and local genealogy-society and estate-cleanout Facebook groups where families already ask for digitization-service referrals. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 5. Pipe Organ Tuner & Technician Rank-by-Rank Service History & Multi-Venue Tracker

An independent pipe organ tuner and technician servicing church, theater, and concert-hall instruments has to track each organ's rank-by-rank tuning history — a pipe organ can hold anywhere from a few hundred to several thousand individual pipes organized into ranks, each tuned on its own schedule and prone to drifting differently with seasonal temperature and humidity swings inside the sanctuary — alongside a running mechanical-fault log (a cracked bellows, a sticking key, a failing blower motor) kept distinct from routine tuning notes, and a client roster where each venue has its own service cadence, some quarterly, some scheduled only ahead of a major event like Christmas or Easter. A technician servicing dozens of instruments across a multi-county circuit currently carries rank-by-rank tuning notes and mechanical repair history in a paper logbook from console to console, and a rank left untuned because last visit's handwritten notes were illegible is an organist discovering a sour note mid-service, while a blower motor flagged as failing at the last visit but never followed up on is an instrument that goes silent the Sunday before Easter.

The tool maintains a per-instrument rank-by-rank tuning history log with a seasonal-drift-pattern note per rank; a mechanical-fault and repair-follow-up tracker kept distinct from routine tuning visits; and a per-client service-cadence scheduler flagging any organ approaching its due visit ahead of a known high-stakes date.

Monetization: $19-29/mo per technician. Distribution: the American Institute of Organbuilders (AIO) and the Organ Historical Society (OHS) member directories, regional chapters of the American Guild of Organists where church music directors already refer technicians, and pipe-organ-technician trade forums where independent tuners trade rank-tracking and route-scheduling tips. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.

### 6. Above-Ground Pool Installer Seasonal Install Queue, Kit Manifest & Warranty Tracker

An independent above-ground pool installer who assembles steel-wall and resin pool kits for residential backyards during the compressed spring installation rush has to track each job's specific kit model and parts manifest — wall panels, uprights, liner size, and a filter/pump package that all vary by pool diameter and depth — alongside a site-prep checklist confirming the ground is level and cleared before a crew shows up with a multi-hundred-pound kit that can't easily be repositioned once assembly starts, and a liner and equipment warranty-registration deadline per installation, since a manufacturer's liner warranty often requires registration within a set window after install. An installer running multiple installs a week through a short season currently tracks kit manifests, site-prep confirmations, and warranty deadlines on a paper work order and a garage shelf of stacked kit boxes, and a crew that shows up to a yard nobody confirmed was leveled is a wasted trip during the installer's busiest and shortest season, while a liner warranty that never gets registered because nobody tracked the deadline is a customer stuck paying out of pocket for a manufacturing defect months later.

The tool maintains a per-job kit manifest and parts checklist matched to the specific pool model and size ordered; a site-prep confirmation checklist (ground level, clearance, and utility-locate status) gating crew dispatch; and a liner/equipment warranty-registration deadline tracker per installation with a completion confirmation.

Monetization: $19-29/mo per installer. Distribution: r/AboveGroundPools and pool-installer trade forums, dealer networks tied to major above-ground kit manufacturers, and local pool-supply-retailer Facebook groups where independent installers already trade site-prep and warranty-tracking tips. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.
