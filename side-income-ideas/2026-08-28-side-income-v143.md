# Side Income Ideas - 2026-08-28 (Round 143)

Simple operational tracker tools for self-employed individuals and micro-businesses. These are NOT compliance tools — they're practical day-to-day workflow trackers that replace the messy spreadsheet, whiteboard, or notebook a solo operator currently uses to run their book of business. Each idea targets a different type of self-employed operator not already covered in this repo, names a real distribution channel, and gives realistic per-account pricing and a time to first dollar.

---

### 1. Apostille & International Document Authentication Service Chain-of-Custody & Turnaround Tracker

An independent apostille and international document authentication agent — helping individuals and businesses get birth certificates, diplomas, corporate powers-of-attorney, and adoption paperwork recognized abroad — has to track each document through a multi-step authentication chain that varies by both the document's home state and its destination country, since a document bound for a Hague Apostille Convention country needs only notarization plus a state Secretary of State apostille, while one bound for a non-Hague country needs an additional embassy or consulate legalization step, and an agent who mixes up the two chains sends a client's document back for a redo that delays an overseas job start, a foreign property closing, or an international adoption already on a tight court timeline. An agent also has to track which state office a given document is currently sitting at, since processing times swing from a few days to several weeks depending on the state and the season, and a client checking in mid-week deserves a real answer rather than "let me call and check," which is what happens when status lives only in the agent's memory of which envelope went out when.

The tool maintains a per-document intake record (document type, origin state, destination country, and Hague/non-Hague status auto-flagged) that generates the correct authentication-chain checklist for that combination; a status-stage tracker (notarized, mailed to state, at state office, at embassy, returned to client) with client-facing update capability; and a per-state processing-time log built from the agent's own job history so future clients get an honest turnaround estimate instead of a guess. Monetization: $75-200 in service fees per document processed (the agent, not the software, sets pricing — this tool is a $25-40/mo subscription for the agent to manage the pipeline). Distribution: r/immigration and international-adoption Facebook groups, referral relationships with immigration attorneys and international-relocation ("expat") communities, and local Google Business Profile visibility for "apostille service [city]" searches. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 2. Residential & Commercial Window Cleaning Route Operator Property Profile & Weather-Hold Scheduling Tracker

An independent window cleaner (or small 2-4 person crew) running a recurring route of residential homes on quarterly cleanings and storefronts on monthly or biweekly cycles has to track each property's exact window count, story height (ground-floor pole work versus second-story ladder or water-fed pole work), and any complicating detail like storm windows or screens that need removal and reinstallation, since quoting a job from memory instead of a documented property profile is how a crew shows up under-equipped or under-quotes a house that turns out to have twice the panes the operator remembered. Weather adds a constant scheduling complication, since window cleaning can't happen in rain or high wind, and an operator running a full week's route needs a fast way to push a rained-out stop to the next available slot without losing track of who got skipped, especially when a commercial storefront client is paying for a specific day (often right before a weekend) and a missed reschedule is a client complaint waiting to happen.

The tool maintains a per-property profile (window count and type, story height and access method, screen/storm-window notes) that generates an accurate quote and a route sequence by neighborhood and day; a weather-hold rescheduling flow that automatically proposes the next open slot when a day gets rained out; and a per-visit before/after photo log for commercial clients who want proof of service for their own property records. Monetization: $25-40/mo for a solo residential-route operator, or $150-300/mo for a small company managing 100+ recurring commercial and residential stops. Distribution: the International Window Cleaning Association (IWCA) member directory and regional window-cleaning trade Facebook groups and forums. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.

### 3. Freelance IT Support & Managed Services Technician Client Asset & Ticket Tracker

An independent IT consultant serving 15-40 small-business clients — law offices, dental practices, small retailers, none of them big enough for an in-house IT department — takes support requests over text, email, and phone calls with no central system, and quickly loses track of which laptop, router, or point-of-sale terminal belongs to which client, what its warranty status is, and when it was last patched, so a routine "the printer's down again" call turns into ten minutes of remembering which client and which printer before the technician can even start troubleshooting. A technician billing a flat monthly retainer also struggles to show clients what they're actually paying for, since without a visible ticket log and a maintenance record, a client sees a monthly invoice and no evidence of work — a common reason solo IT consultants lose retainer clients to scope-creep resentment or a slicker-looking competitor, even when the actual service was fine.

The tool maintains a per-client device and asset inventory (make, model, warranty expiration, OS patch level); a ticket queue logging request, resolution, and time spent per client; a recurring-maintenance schedule (patching cycles, backup verification checks) per client; and a simple auto-generated monthly client report showing tickets closed and systems maintained, turning invisible retainer work into a visible deliverable. Monetization: $40-80/mo per technician — a lightweight single-user alternative to enterprise RMM/PSA platforms like ConnectWise or Datto, which are overbuilt and priced for multi-technician MSPs rather than a solo operator. Distribution: r/msp and r/sysadmin communities, local Chamber of Commerce small-business-vendor referral groups, and regional CompTIA user-group meetups. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 4. Low-Voltage Structured Cabling & Access-Control Installer As-Built Documentation Tracker

An independent low-voltage contractor installing structured data cabling, security-camera runs, and keycard/fob access-control systems for small offices and retail spaces has to document every cable run's source, destination, cable ID, and TIA-568 test-certification result, along with a labeled patch-panel port map, since a job finished without that documentation means the next troubleshooting call — a dead port, a request to add a new access-controlled door — starts with the installer re-tracing the entire install from memory or, worse, a different technician who was never on the original job trying to reverse-engineer it. An installer also has to track which access-control reader and controller model is assigned to which door, along with the credential format in use, since a client who wants to add a new employee's fob or reconfigure which doors a badge opens needs that device inventory on hand rather than a callback visit just to find out what hardware is even installed.

The tool maintains a per-job cable-run and port-map record (source, destination, cable ID, and test-certification result) that generates a clean labeled as-built diagram to hand the client at project closeout; an access-control device inventory (reader/controller model, door assignment, credential format) per site; and a warranty and service-call history log per client location. Monetization: $50-100/mo per installer or small firm — the as-built diagram alone is a differentiator, since a frequent client complaint in this trade is finishing a job with no documentation handed over at all. Distribution: BICSI (Building Industry Consulting Service International) member forums and local electrical/low-voltage contractor association chapters. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.

### 5. Independent Life Coach Client Goal, Session-Note & Package-Credit Tracker

An independent, ICF-certified life coach running 15-30 active clients on rolling monthly coaching packages has to track each client's stated goals, session-by-session notes, and between-session action items or "homework," since a coach walking into a session without a quick recap of what the client committed to last time comes across as generic rather than personally invested — the opposite of what clients are paying a premium for. A coach also has to track each client's package-credit balance (how many prepaid sessions remain on a 6- or 12-session package) and renewal timing, since a coach relying on a notebook per client has no easy way to see who's about to run out of sessions and needs a renewal conversation, or worse, who's gone quiet and stopped booking without anyone noticing until the client has effectively churned.

The tool maintains a per-client goal log and session-notes history; an action-item tracker with completion status the coach can reference at the start of the next session; a package-credit balance with renewal-due alerts; and a simple client-progress timeline the coach reviews before each call. Monetization: $20-35/mo per coach — a lightweight alternative to expensive all-in-one coaching-platform suites (built for course creators and group programs) that most solo 1:1 coaches don't need and won't pay for. Distribution: International Coaching Federation (ICF) local chapter meetups and coach-directory groups on Facebook and LinkedIn. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 6. Axe-Throwing Lounge & Mobile Axe-Throwing Operator Lane Booking, Waiver & Equipment Tracker

An independent axe-throwing venue operator — or the growing niche of mobile axe-throwing trailer operators who bring lanes to private parties and corporate events — has to manage lane and session bookings against a hard party-size capacity per lane, collect a signed liability waiver from every participant before they pick up an axe, and track which axes and target boards are approaching the end of their safe usable life, since a cracked axe head or a target board worn past its safe throwing surface is exactly the kind of equipment failure that turns a fun night into an injury and a liability claim. An operator currently splitting this across a generic booking widget, a stack of paper waivers, and memory of "that one axe that's been feeling loose" routinely double-books a lane during a busy Saturday and has no clean way to prove, after an incident, that every participant that night actually signed a waiver.

The tool maintains a lane/session booking calendar enforcing party-size capacity limits per lane; digital waiver capture with a searchable signature archive built around WATL-recommended liability practices; a per-axe usage-count log with an automatic retirement flag once an axe passes its safe-use threshold; a target-board wear and replacement log; and a coach/marshal certification tracker for staff supervising walk-up sessions. Monetization: $60-120/mo per venue, bundling booking, waiver management, and equipment tracking that many small venues currently stitch together from 2-3 separate subscriptions. Distribution: the World Axe Throwing League (WATL) affiliated-venue directory and axe-throwing-owner Facebook groups. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.
