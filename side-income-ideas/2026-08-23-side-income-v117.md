# Side Income Ideas - 2026-08-23 (Round 117)

Simple operational tracker tools for self-employed individuals and micro-businesses. These are NOT compliance tools — they're practical day-to-day workflow trackers that replace the messy spreadsheet, whiteboard, or notebook a solo operator currently uses to run their book of business. Each idea targets a different type of self-employed operator not already covered in this repo, names a real distribution channel, and gives realistic per-account pricing and a time to first dollar.

---

### 1. Independent Tailor & Clothing Alterations Shop Garment, Measurement & Order Tracker

An independent tailor running a storefront or home-based alterations business takes in a steady flow of garments — hemming, taking in a suit, replacing a zipper, a full wedding-dress alteration — each needing its own precise body-measurement record, a specific alteration spec (how much to take in, which seam, what thread and fabric match), and a promised pickup date that can't slip for a client who needs the garment for a specific event. A tailor running dozens of garments through the shop at once currently tracks whose garment is whose, what alteration was actually agreed to, and which pickup date is coming up on a stack of paper tags pinned to each item and a measurement notebook by client name, and a garment altered to the wrong measurement because the notebook entry was illegible is a redo that eats the job's margin, while a rush wedding alteration that slips past its promised date because it got buried under regular tickets is a bride calling in a panic three days before the wedding.

The tool maintains a per-client measurement profile (updated at each visit, since bodies change) with a per-garment alteration spec sheet and before/after photo, an order queue sorted by promised pickup date rather than drop-off order with a rush/event-date priority flag, and a fabric/notion (thread, zipper, button) reorder list triggered by upcoming jobs.

Monetization: $15-25/mo per tailor. Distribution: r/sewing and r/tailoring practitioner threads, the Association of Sewing and Design Professionals (ASDP) member directory, and independent tailor shop Facebook groups where operators already trade measurement-record and rush-order-queue workflow tips. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 2. Shoe & Leather Goods Repair (Cobbler) Shop Repair Ticket & Materials Tracker

An independent cobbler repairing shoes, boots, and leather goods — resoling, replacing a heel, restitching a bag handle, dyeing a scuffed leather jacket — takes in a bench full of concurrent jobs, each needing its own repair spec (sole material and thickness, heel height match, thread color, dye shade) and a specific cure or dry time before the item can be handed back, while sourcing a growing inventory of soles, heels, leather dyes, and adhesives by brand and size. A cobbler running a full bench of twenty or more items at once currently tracks whose shoe is whose, what repair was quoted, and which items are still curing versus ready for pickup on a shelf of numbered claim tickets and a mental inventory of which sole stock is running low, and a pair resoled in the wrong material because the ticket didn't specify is a customer who notices immediately, while running out of a specific heel size mid-week with no reorder flagged is a job that can't be finished on the promised date.

The tool maintains a per-item repair ticket (repair type, material/color spec, and cure-time status) with a claim-ticket-to-pickup matching system, a materials inventory (sole stock, heel sizes, dyes, adhesives) by brand and quantity with reorder alerts, and a repair-history log per returning customer for matching a prior job's exact material and color.

Monetization: $15-25/mo per shop. Distribution: r/shoerepair and r/leathercraft practitioner threads, the Shoe Service Institute of America (SSIA) member directory, and independent cobbler shop Facebook groups where operators already trade materials-inventory and claim-ticket workflow tips. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 3. Multi-Booth Antique & Vintage Dealer Inventory, Booth-Rent & Consignment Tracker

An independent antique and vintage dealer renting booth space in several antique malls across a region has to track which piece is sitting in which booth at which mall, what it's priced at, and when each mall's monthly booth rent is due, while also managing a subset of pieces taken on consignment from other collectors who expect a specific split of the sale price paid out on their own schedule. A dealer running inventory across three or four antique malls currently tracks item location, pricing, and consignor payout obligations on a paper inventory ledger carried between malls and a mental map of which mall still owes a rent check, and a piece that sold at one mall but never gets removed from the price list at another location is a phantom listing confusing repeat customers, while a consignor who isn't paid out within the promised window after their piece sells is a collector who takes their next batch of inventory to a competing dealer instead.

The tool maintains a per-item inventory record (photo, description, price, and current booth/mall location) with a sold/unsold status synced across every location the dealer sells from, a per-mall booth-rent due-date tracker, and a consignor roster tracking each consigned item's sale status and the payout split owed and its due date.

Monetization: $15-25/mo per dealer. Distribution: r/antiques and r/vintage seller threads, the National Antique & Art Dealers Association member network, and regional antique-mall-dealer Facebook groups where independent booth renters already trade multi-location inventory and consignor-payout workflow tips. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 4. Freelance Proofreader & Copyeditor for Independent Authors Project Queue & Style-Sheet Tracker

An independent proofreader or copyeditor working with self-published authors and small presses takes on a rotating slate of manuscripts, each needing its own style sheet (the author's chosen spelling variants, hyphenation preferences, character-name spellings, and citation style) tracked consistently across a single project and, for repeat clients, carried forward to their next book so a returning author's series doesn't suddenly shift conventions between volumes. An editor running six to ten concurrent manuscripts at different pass stages (first pass, author-query resolution, final proof) currently tracks which manuscript is at which stage, what style-sheet decisions were made and why, and which deadline is coming up next on a folder of marked-up Word documents and a running mental list, and a manuscript returned to an author with an inconsistency the editor's own earlier style-sheet decision should have caught is a credibility hit that costs a repeat client, while a deadline missed because two projects' turnaround windows silently overlapped is an author whose launch date now depends on an editor running behind.

The tool maintains a per-project style sheet (spelling variants, hyphenation, name spellings, and citation-style decisions) searchable and reusable across an author's future books, a project-stage queue (first pass, query resolution, final proof) with per-project deadline tracking, and a client history log noting prior-book style decisions for fast onboarding of a returning author's next manuscript.

Monetization: $12-20/mo per editor. Distribution: r/editors and r/selfpublish practitioner threads, the ACES: The Society for Editing member community, and independent freelance-editor Facebook groups where proofreaders and copyeditors already trade style-sheet and project-queue workflow tips. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 5. Freelance Wardrobe Stylist Client Closet, Pull-Sheet & Boutique Return Tracker

An independent wardrobe stylist working personal styling clients, wedding parties, and small editorial or commercial shoots has to track each client's closet inventory and sizing so a personal-styling session builds on what a client already owns rather than starting from scratch, and for a shoot or event, maintain a "pull sheet" of items borrowed from boutiques and showrooms — each with its own return-by date and condition requirement — since a stylist who returns a pulled garment late or damaged loses that boutique's willingness to lend for the next job. A stylist running several concurrent personal clients alongside occasional shoot work currently tracks client sizing and closet notes, pulled-item return deadlines, and which boutique loaned which piece on a phone notes app and a garment rack of borrowed clothes with paper tags, and a pulled dress returned two days late because nobody was tracking the boutique's specific return window is a relationship the stylist needs for the next booking now strained, while a personal-styling client re-asked the same sizing questions at every session because nothing was recorded is a client who feels like they're starting over every time.

The tool maintains a per-client closet and sizing profile (measurements, color preferences, and existing wardrobe notes) built up across sessions, a per-shoot pull sheet (item, source boutique, borrow date, and required return-by date with condition notes) with a return-deadline alert, and a boutique/showroom relationship log tracking on-time-return history for maintaining lending trust.

Monetization: $15-25/mo per stylist. Distribution: r/fashion and r/PersonalStyling practitioner threads, the Association of Image Consultants International (AICI) member network, and independent wardrobe-stylist Facebook groups where stylists already trade pull-sheet and closet-tracking workflow tips. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 6. Fine Art & Antique Restoration Conservator Client Piece, Condition Report & Treatment Tracker

An independent conservator restoring paintings, frames, ceramics, and antique furniture for private collectors, estates, and small galleries has to document each piece's condition on intake with detailed photos and notes before any treatment begins — since a conservator's before-treatment condition report is the reference point a client, an insurer, or a future appraiser will compare against — then track the specific treatment plan (cleaning solvents, consolidation materials, retouching pigments) and its multi-stage progress for a piece that may sit in the studio for weeks or months. A conservator running eight to fifteen concurrent pieces at different treatment stages currently tracks each piece's intake condition photos, treatment plan, and materials used on a mix of a camera roll and a paper studio log, and a piece returned to a client without a clear before/after condition record is a conservator with no documentation to fall back on if the client later disputes what was done or an insurer questions the piece's condition, while a treatment plan that isn't recorded precisely is a conservator who can't reproduce or explain their own materials choice on a follow-up piece from the same collection years later.

The tool maintains a per-piece intake condition report (photos, existing damage notes, and provenance details provided by the client) with a treatment plan and materials log tracked through each treatment stage, a before/after photo comparison generated automatically for client handoff and insurance documentation, and a client/collection history for collectors who bring in multiple pieces over time.

Monetization: $19-29/mo per conservator. Distribution: r/artrestoration and r/AskConservators practitioner threads, the American Institute for Conservation (AIC) member directory, and independent conservation-studio Facebook groups where conservators already trade condition-reporting and treatment-documentation workflow tips. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.
