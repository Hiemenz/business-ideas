# Side Income Ideas - 2026-08-13 (Session 68)

## Who this is for
Simple, fast-to-build operational tracker tools for self-employed operators and micro-businesses — not compliance software, just practical day-to-day workflow tools that replace spreadsheets and sticky notes. Each idea targets a distinct type of self-employed operator not already covered in this repo.

## Ground rules for every idea in this series
- Simple, fast-to-build workflow trackers — no regulatory compliance angle.
- Monetization stated per account with a named distribution channel.
- Time to first dollar under 5 weeks.
- **No repeats.** This session's six operator types — potters/ceramic studio artists, duct cleaning technicians, mobile hair stylists, corporate headshot & business photographers, food & product photographers, and custom window treatment/blinds installers — are distinct from every prior session: pottery and ceramic studio work has never appeared as its own operator type (distinct from every other craft/maker idea already in this repo — the handmade jewelry maker idea, the custom woodworking shop idea, and the leatherworker idea all track a commission-and-material-inventory problem, but a potter's core problem instead is a kiln firing-schedule and batch queue, since bisque and glaze firings run in fixed-capacity kiln loads on their own multi-day schedule independent of any single order, plus a per-piece glaze-recipe and clay-body record that must be reproduced consistently across batches, and a class-registration roster when the studio also teaches — a firing-batch-and-glaze-chemistry problem no other maker's commission tracker holds); duct cleaning has never appeared (distinct from the window & gutter cleaning route idea, the mobile pressure washing route idea, and the chimney sweep idea already in this repo, all of which clean exterior surfaces on a route — a duct cleaning technician's core problem instead is a per-system HVAC configuration record — vent count, duct material, and system accessibility — plus before/after debris or airflow documentation used to prove the job was actually done, and a multi-year service-interval reminder tied to the system type rather than a fixed weekly/monthly route cadence, an interior-HVAC-specific record no exterior-surface cleaning tracker holds); mobile hair styling has never appeared (distinct from the mobile nail technician idea, the mobile massage therapist idea, and the freelance bridal/event makeup artist idea already in this repo — every other personal-care mobile professional in this repo tracks a service/session history, but a hair stylist's core problem is a precise color-formula record — brand, developer volume, and exact mixing ratio — that must be reproduced exactly at the client's next visit or risk visibly damaged or mismatched hair, a formula-reproducibility problem none of the other personal-care trackers hold); corporate headshot and business photography has never appeared (distinct from the portrait & newborn photographer idea and the wedding photographer idea already in this repo, both of which track a single family or couple's individual session — a headshot photographer's core problem instead is a batch-scheduling grid for corporate group bookings, where an entire office is scheduled in 10-15 minute slots across a single on-site day, plus a licensing/usage-rights tracker for corporate deliverables since internal-use and press/marketing-use licenses often carry different fee tiers, a B2B batch-scheduling-and-usage-rights problem neither a portrait nor a wedding photographer's individual-session tracker holds); food and product photography has never appeared (distinct from every other photography idea in this repo, including portrait, wedding, and headshot photography — a food or product photographer's core problem is a per-client shot list and styling-note record, since prop sourcing and food-styling details must be reproduced exactly if a reshoot is needed weeks later, plus a deliverable-and-usage-rights tracker for commercial and e-commerce licensing terms, a styling-continuity-and-commercial-licensing problem no portrait, wedding, or headshot tracker holds); and custom window treatment and blinds installation has never appeared (distinct from the landscape lighting installer idea and every other installer-type idea already in this repo — a blinds or window-treatment installer's core problem is a per-window measurement record — exact width, height, and mount type for each individual opening — that must match a custom-manufactured product weeks later when it arrives from the factory, plus a vendor-order-to-install-date tracker across a multi-week manufacturing lead time, a measurement-precision-and-custom-order-lag problem no other installer tracker in this repo holds).

### 1. Potter & Ceramic Studio Artist Kiln Firing, Glaze Recipe & Commission Tracker

Independent potters and ceramic studio artists producing wheel-thrown or hand-built pieces for commission clients, wholesale accounts, and craft fair sales need to track a kiln firing schedule and batch queue, since a bisque firing or glaze firing runs a full kiln load over 8-24 hours and pieces from different clients or projects often share the same firing — meaning a delayed or failed firing affects everything loaded that cycle, not just one order — a per-piece glaze-recipe and clay-body record (specific glaze combination, application method, and firing cone/temperature) that must be reproduced exactly if a client orders a matching piece or a wholesale account reorders a bestselling glaze, and a class-registration roster with per-session project tracking when the studio also teaches wheel-throwing or hand-building classes, because a potter running 15-30 pieces through the studio at any given time across commissions, wholesale production, and personal work can't rely on memory for which pieces are queued for the next glaze firing versus already fired and ready for pickup, and losing track of an exact glaze recipe means a reordered piece comes out a visibly different color than the original. Most independent potters currently manage this across a paper studio log and a phone photo album with no structured link between a piece's current stage, its intended kiln load, and the exact glaze recipe it needs.

The tool maintains a kiln firing schedule and batch queue (grouping in-progress pieces by their target firing date and load), a per-piece and per-client glaze-recipe library recording the exact combination and firing cone used, and a commission/wholesale order tracker linking each order to its production stage from greenware through bisque, glazing, and final firing.

Monetization: $15-25/mo per studio account. Distribution: r/Pottery and r/Ceramics threads, the American Craft Council (ACC) member directory, and local craft fair and clay guild Facebook groups. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.

### 2. Duct Cleaning Technician System Profile & Service-Interval Tracker

Independent duct cleaning technicians who service residential and light-commercial HVAC systems need to track each client's system configuration — vent and register count, duct material (sheet metal, flex, or fiberboard), and system accessibility notes like a hard-to-reach attic run or a crawlspace entry point — because arriving without knowing the vent count or accessibility quirks of a specific home means underquoting the job's actual time on-site, before/after documentation (photos or a debris-weight log) proving the job removed a meaningful amount of buildup, since duct cleaning is a service where customers can't visually verify the work was thorough once vents are closed back up and a documented before/after record protects the technician against a dispute, and a multi-year service-interval reminder since manufacturers and industry guidance recommend duct cleaning every 3-5 years rather than on a seasonal route cadence, because a technician managing 100-200 residential accounts across a service territory can't rely on memory for which homes are approaching their multi-year due window versus were serviced last year, and a job quoted without knowing a specific home's duct material and accessibility risks a flat-rate quote that doesn't cover the actual labor once the crew is on-site. Most independent duct cleaning technicians currently manage this across a paper work order pad and a folder of before/after phone photos with no structured link between a home's system profile and its next scheduled service window.

The tool maintains a per-client system profile (vent count, duct material, and accessibility notes), a before/after documentation log per job building a defensible service record, and a multi-year service-interval dashboard surfacing every home approaching its next recommended cleaning window.

Monetization: $15-25/mo per technician account. Distribution: r/HVAC and r/ductcleaning threads, the National Air Duct Cleaners Association (NADCA) member directory, and local HVAC contractor referral networks. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 3. Mobile Hair Stylist Client Color-Formula & Appointment Tracker

Independent mobile hair stylists who travel to clients' homes for cut, color, and styling appointments need to track each client's precise color formula — brand, developer volume, and exact mixing ratio — since a color that isn't reproduced exactly at the next visit can leave a client with visibly damaged hair or an obvious color mismatch at the roots, an appointment and service history log noting what was done at each visit and how long it took, because accurate scheduling depends on knowing whether a specific client's last color service ran the standard 90 minutes or stretched to three hours for a complex correction, and a product and supply inventory sized to the day's scheduled appointments, since running short on a specific developer volume or a client's exact shade mid-appointment means an incomplete service at someone's kitchen table, because a stylist booking 4-6 appointments a day across scattered client homes can't rely on memory for which client's formula uses a 20-volume versus 30-volume developer, and showing up without enough of a specific product for a color-heavy appointment wastes the visit entirely. Most independent mobile stylists currently manage this across a paper formula card kept in a color kit and a booking app's calendar with no structured link between a client's exact formula history and what needs to be restocked before the next appointment with that client.

The tool maintains a per-client color-formula record (brand, developer volume, and mixing ratio) that stays exact across visits, an appointment and service-history log tracking what was done and how long it took per client, and a product inventory checklist matched against the day's scheduled appointments and their specific formula requirements.

Monetization: $15-25/mo per stylist account. Distribution: r/Hairstylist and r/Hair threads, local mobile stylist and salon-suite Facebook groups, and StyleSeat/booking-platform pro community outreach. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 4. Corporate Headshot & Business Photographer Batch Scheduling & Usage-Rights Tracker

Independent corporate headshot photographers who shoot on-site at company offices need to track a batch-scheduling grid for group bookings, since a single corporate client often books an entire office of 20-80 employees into 10-15 minute slots across one day, and a no-show or a late employee cascades delays through the rest of the schedule if there's no structured way to see the day's slot-by-slot status in real time, a per-client licensing and usage-rights tracker, because corporate headshot contracts commonly distinguish internal use (intranet, email signatures) from external press or marketing use, each carrying a different fee tier, and a delivery-and-retake log tracking which employees' galleries have been delivered and which employees requested a retake, because a photographer running several corporate shoot days a month across different companies can't rely on memory for which client's contract licenses external marketing use versus internal-only, and losing track of a scheduled retake means an employee's headshot never gets updated in the company directory. Most independent corporate headshot photographers currently manage this across a shared spreadsheet the client's HR contact fills in and a separate gallery-delivery email thread with no structured link between the day's slot schedule, the client's specific usage-rights terms, and outstanding retake requests.

The tool maintains a batch-scheduling grid organizing a shoot day into named time slots with real-time status per slot, a per-client usage-rights tracker recording the licensed use tier (internal, press, or marketing) tied to the contract, and a delivery/retake log tracking each employee's gallery-delivery status and any pending retake request.

Monetization: $19-29/mo per photographer account. Distribution: r/photography and r/corporatephotography threads, local Chamber of Commerce photographer referral networks, and LinkedIn corporate photography community outreach. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.

### 5. Food & Product Photographer Shot List & Usage-Rights Tracker

Independent food and product photographers shooting for restaurants, CPG brands, and e-commerce sellers need to track a per-client shot list and styling-note record, since a reshoot weeks or months later — a new seasonal menu item, a restocked product line — needs to reproduce the exact prop selection, background surface, and food-styling details (garnish placement, sauce application technique) used in the original shoot, a deliverable and usage-rights tracker recording which images were licensed for which use (menu print, website, paid social ads, third-party retailer listings often carry separate licensing terms), and a per-shoot prop and equipment checklist since a food shoot depends on specific props, backgrounds, and sometimes a food stylist's kit that must travel to the shoot location, because a photographer running several client shoots a month across restaurant and e-commerce clients can't rely on memory for which client's product photos are licensed for retailer use versus social-only, and losing track of a shoot's exact styling notes means a reshoot doesn't visually match the original catalog images. Most independent food and product photographers currently manage this across a folder of shoot-day phone notes and a client email thread with no structured link between a shot list's styling details and what usage rights were actually licensed for the delivered images.

The tool maintains a per-client shot list and styling-note record (props, background, and food-styling details) that stays reproducible across reshoots, a deliverable and usage-rights log tracking which images are licensed for which use, and a per-shoot prop/equipment checklist so nothing is forgotten on location.

Monetization: $19-29/mo per photographer account. Distribution: r/FoodPhotography and r/photography threads, local restaurant association and food-business Facebook groups, and Etsy/e-commerce seller community outreach for product photographers. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.

### 6. Custom Window Treatment & Blinds Installer Measurement & Order Tracker

Independent window treatment and blinds installers who measure and install custom blinds, shades, and shutters need to track a per-window measurement record — exact width, height, mount type (inside or outside mount), and any obstruction notes (a window crank, an uneven sill) — since that measurement becomes a custom manufacturing order that can't be corrected once cut, and a mismeasured window discovered only when the finished product arrives weeks later means a costly remake at the installer's expense, a vendor order-to-install-date tracker, because custom window treatments typically carry a multi-week manufacturing lead time and an installer running several active jobs needs to know which orders are still in production versus ready to schedule for install, and a per-job product and hardware tracker (brackets, valances, and any specialty mounting hardware specific to the product ordered) so an install day doesn't stall over a missing bracket, because an installer managing 15-25 active jobs across different manufacturing lead times can't rely on memory for which client's order shipped versus is still three weeks out, and a mismeasured window caught only at install time turns a scheduled visit into a wasted trip and an unhappy customer waiting weeks for a remake. Most independent installers currently manage this across a paper measurement sheet per job and a vendor order-confirmation email thread with no structured link between a window's exact measurement and the order status of the custom product being manufactured for it.

The tool maintains a per-window measurement record (dimensions, mount type, and obstruction notes) tied to the manufacturing order placed from it, a vendor order-to-install-date tracker surfacing every job's current production status, and a per-job hardware checklist matched to the specific product ordered.

Monetization: $15-25/mo per installer account. Distribution: r/windowtreatments and r/HomeImprovement threads, local interior design and home-staging referral networks, and Window Covering Manufacturers Association (WCMA) dealer community outreach. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.
