# Side Income Ideas - 2026-08-18 (Session 95)

## Ideas

### 1. Independent Pool Cleaning & Maintenance Technician Route & Chemical-Log Tracker

Pool techs running a solo residential/light-commercial route juggle dozens of properties with different equipment (salt vs. chlorine systems, variable-speed pumps, different filter types), each needing a weekly or biweekly visit where chemical levels get tested and adjusted. Most techs currently track this with a paper clipboard or a generic notes app, which makes it easy to lose track of which pool is on which chemical dosing history, miss a filter-cleaning cycle, or forget that a specific customer's pool has a quirky piece of equipment that needs special handling — and a missed or wrong chemical adjustment can turn a pool green within days, triggering an angry customer call and an unplanned emergency visit that blows up the week's route.

The tool maintains a per-pool profile (equipment type, filter model, chemical dosing history, gate codes/access notes, service-day assignment) and a route view showing the week's stops in visit order, with a quick chemical-reading entry (chlorine, pH, alkalinity) that auto-flags out-of-range readings against target bands and logs the dose applied so the next visit's tech (or the same tech three weeks later) can see the trend rather than guessing fresh each time. A customer-facing view can optionally show the latest reading and next scheduled visit, which techs use as a value-add to justify their monthly rate against unlicensed competitors.

Monetization: $25-45/mo per technician for the route/logging tool, or $79-129/mo for a multi-tech pool service company tier with team route assignment. Distribution: the Pool & Hot Tub Alliance (PHTA) member directory and regional pool service Facebook groups, which are highly active given how seasonal and word-of-mouth-driven this trade is. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks, since the core route/chemical-log workflow is simple enough to demo directly to techs found in these groups.

### 2. Freelance Personal Chef & Private Chef Client Menu & Meal-Prep Schedule Tracker

Independent personal chefs cooking for individual households (weekly meal-prep drop-offs) or private chefs working recurring dinner-party/event engagements for a handful of high-value clients need to track very different information per client: dietary restrictions and allergies (which carry real liability if missed), portion counts and container preferences, rotating menu history so the same dish doesn't repeat too soon, and a shopping list that aggregates ingredients across that week's scheduled clients so grocery runs are efficient rather than client-by-client. Doing this in a spreadsheet works for two or three clients, but chefs scaling past five or six regular households lose track of who's allergic to shellfish, whose menu was repeated last month, and how much a given week's total shopping list actually costs against what they're charging.

The tool maintains a client roster with dietary-restriction flags, a rotating menu-history log per client to avoid repeats, a weekly meal-prep schedule showing which households get which dishes on which day, and an aggregated shopping list generator that pulls ingredient quantities across all of that week's scheduled meals into one consolidated list, with a running per-client food-cost calculation so the chef can see margin per household rather than guessing.

Monetization: $19-35/mo for solo personal chefs managing under 15 households, $49-69/mo for private chefs running larger recurring-event books. Distribution: the United States Personal Chef Association (USPCA) member forum and the American Personal & Private Chef Association (APPCA), both active communities of independent chefs who already pay for certification and liability insurance and are receptive to workflow tools. Time to first dollar: 3-5 weeks via direct outreach in these association forums.

### 3. Independent Farrier Horse Roster & Shoeing-Cycle Route Tracker

Farriers (horseshoers) run a route of dozens to over a hundred horses across multiple barns, each on its own 6-8 week trim/shoe cycle that has to stay roughly consistent for hoof health — miss a horse's window by too much and it risks lameness, which damages the farrier's reputation with the barn owner. Each horse also has its own shoeing specifics (barefoot trim vs. front shoes only vs. all-around, therapeutic pad requirements, a specific horse's tendency to be difficult to shoe) that a farrier currently tracks from memory or a paper index card system, which breaks down once the roster grows past what one person can hold in their head, especially across multiple boarding barns visited on a rotating schedule.

The tool maintains a per-horse profile (owner/barn, shoe type, cycle length, last-serviced date, handling notes, therapeutic/pad requirements) with an auto-calculated next-due date per horse, a route view grouping upcoming due horses by barn/location to plan efficient driving loops, and a service-history log per horse showing shoe-type changes over time (useful when a vet or new barn owner asks about a horse's shoeing history).

Monetization: $19-29/mo for a solo farrier tracking up to 150 horses, scaling to $39/mo for larger rosters. Distribution: the American Farrier's Association (AFA) member directory and regional farrier association Facebook groups, plus state horseshoeing school alumni networks where new farriers building a route are actively looking for tools to professionalize their business. Time to first dollar: 4-6 weeks, since farriers are a geographically dispersed, word-of-mouth-driven trade best reached through AFA regional chapter meetings.

### 4. Freelance Court Reporter & Legal Videographer Assignment & Transcript-Deadline Tracker

Independent court reporters (stenographers) and legal videographers working depositions, hearings, and arbitrations for multiple law firms and court-reporting agencies juggle assignments with hard, court-imposed transcript delivery deadlines (often 7, 14, or 30 days from the proceeding depending on whether it's expedited, and missing these deadlines carries professional and sometimes financial penalty), plus equipment logistics (which stenotype/audio setup travels to which job) and per-firm billing rates that vary since different agencies and direct-hire attorneys pay different page rates and rush fees. Freelancers working through multiple agencies simultaneously currently track this across agency-specific portals plus their own notes, making it easy to lose sight of which transcript is due when across a busy multi-agency week.

The tool maintains an assignment calendar showing each deposition/hearing with its transcript-due deadline (calculated from the proceeding date and the specific turnaround tier ordered — expedited, daily, or ordinary), a per-client/agency rate sheet so invoicing reflects the correct page and exhibit rates, and a production-status tracker per job (rough draft done, proofed, delivered, invoiced) so nothing falls through when juggling a dozen concurrent transcripts across different firms.

Monetization: $25-40/mo per reporter/videographer. Distribution: the National Court Reporters Association (NCRA) and state court reporter association job boards and forums, where freelancers already coordinate around agency relationships and are vocal about deadline-tracking pain in member discussions. Time to first dollar: 4-6 weeks via NCRA state chapter outreach.

### 5. Mobile Welder & Fabricator Job Quote & Equipment-Certification Tracker

Independent mobile welders taking on-site repair, fabrication, and custom-build jobs (farm equipment repair, fence/gate fabrication, structural repairs) need to track a pipeline of quoted, scheduled, and completed jobs across scattered rural or industrial properties, along with the specific welding process and material each job requires (a job needing certified structural welds for a commercial client is different from a simple farm-gate repair), and — for welders taking on any structural, pressure-vessel, or code-stamped work — their own welding certification renewal dates (AWS certifications typically require re-testing or continuity documentation every 6 months to stay current for code work). Currently most mobile welders track jobs via text-message threads and a paper invoice book, which makes it easy to lose a quoted job in the pipeline or accidentally take on certified work after a lapsed certification.

The tool maintains a job pipeline (quoted → scheduled → in-progress → completed → invoiced) with per-job material and process notes, a mileage/travel log for accurate on-site pricing, a simple materials cost estimator feeding into the quote, and a personal certification tracker with renewal alerts for any AWS or code-specific welding certifications the welder holds and needs to keep current for higher-paying certified work.

Monetization: $19-29/mo per welder. Distribution: the American Welding Society (AWS) local section meetings and mobile-welding-focused Facebook and Reddit communities (r/Welding has an active mobile/independent welder contingent), where solo operators actively discuss quoting and scheduling pain points. Time to first dollar: 3-5 weeks through direct outreach in these communities.

### 6. Independent Pet Sitter & Dog Walker Client, Pet Profile & Visit-Confirmation Tracker

Solo pet sitters and dog walkers managing a client base of recurring daily-walk clients plus intermittent vacation pet-sitting bookings need to track detailed per-pet information (feeding schedule, medication dosing and timing, behavioral quirks, vet contact and emergency authorization) that carries real liability if gotten wrong, alongside a booking calendar mixing recurring daily walks with one-off multi-day sitting jobs that have to be confirmed with the client and logged with visit-completion proof (a photo or timestamp) since pet owners increasingly expect confirmation their pet was actually visited. Sitters juggling 20-40 active clients across a mix of recurring and occasional bookings lose track of which dog needs a specific medication at a specific time or which client's vacation booking still needs a deposit confirmed.

The tool maintains a pet profile per animal (feeding/medication schedule, behavioral notes, vet and emergency-contact info, access/gate codes) linked to a booking calendar distinguishing recurring walks from one-off sitting jobs, a simple visit-completion log where the sitter marks each stop done (with an optional photo attached) that can generate a client-facing visit summary, and a deposit/payment status flag per booking so unpaid vacation-sitting jobs don't get overlooked.

Monetization: $15-25/mo for solo dog walkers, $35-49/mo for sitters running a larger multi-pet-sitter operation. Distribution: Pet Sitters International (PSI) and the National Association of Professional Pet Sitters (NAPPS) member directories, both active associations where independent operators already pay for bonding/insurance and are a natural fit for a workflow tool addressing liability-sensitive record-keeping. Time to first dollar: 2-4 weeks, since dog walking/pet sitting is a large, easily-reached freelancer population with straightforward Facebook-group and association-forum outreach.
