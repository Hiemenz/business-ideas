# Side Income Ideas - 2026-08-29 (Round 145)

Simple operational tracker tools for self-employed individuals and micro-businesses. These are NOT compliance tools — they're practical day-to-day workflow trackers that replace the messy spreadsheet, whiteboard, or notebook a solo operator currently uses to run their book of business. Each idea targets a different type of self-employed operator not already covered in this repo's prior 144 rounds, names a real distribution channel, and gives realistic per-account pricing and a time to first dollar.

---

### 1. Eyelash Extension Artist Client Refill-Cycle, Formula & Patch-Test Tracker

An independent lash artist applying classic, hybrid, or volume eyelash extensions has to track each client's exact lash-map specifications — curl type, length-and-thickness sequence by eye zone, adhesive brand used — since a client returning for a two-to-three-week refill needs the technician to reapply the same look rather than guess from memory, and a lash artist juggling 40-60 regular clients on staggered refill schedules loses that consistency the moment it lives only in their head. An artist also has to track each client's patch-test result and any adhesive sensitivity noted at a prior appointment, since applying a new adhesive brand or bonding agent to a client with a known sensitivity without re-testing is exactly the kind of allergic-reaction incident that ends a client relationship and can trigger a state cosmetology board complaint. A lash artist running a full weekly book currently tracks lash maps, refill-due dates, and patch-test history on paper client cards or a generic booking app that captures the appointment time but not the formula and sensitivity data needed for a safe, consistent refill.

The tool maintains a per-client lash-map record (curl, length/thickness sequence, adhesive brand) with a refill-due countdown auto-calculated from the last full set or fill; a patch-test and adhesive-sensitivity log flagging any client who needs a fresh patch test before a new product is used; and a before/after photo log per session for consistency reference and liability documentation. Monetization: $15-25/mo per lash artist. Distribution: the National Association of Lash Artists (NALA) member directory and lash-artist Facebook and Instagram professional groups. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 2. Mobile Small Engine & Outdoor Power Equipment Repair Technician Job & Parts Tracker

An independent mobile technician who repairs lawnmowers, chainsaws, generators, snowblowers, and other outdoor power equipment on-site at a customer's home or small landscaping company has to track each unit's exact make, model, and engine type before diagnosing a problem, since carburetor kits, ignition coils, and belts are model-specific down to the part number, and a technician who shows up without confirming the right part for that specific engine wastes a truck roll and has to reschedule. A technician also has to track seasonal demand spikes — spring startup tune-ups and fall winterization work arrive in a predictable annual wave — and a small commercial landscaping-company client with a dozen mowers and trimmers needs its whole fleet serviced on a schedule the technician can see at a glance rather than reconstruct from a stack of handwritten tickets. A technician running a full route currently tracks unit specs, parts ordered, and seasonal service-due dates on a paper repair tag and a supplier order slip, with a wrong-part order or a missed seasonal tune-up costing a wasted trip and an annoyed landscaping client whose equipment fails mid-season.

The tool maintains a per-unit equipment profile (make, model, engine type, and repair history) generating the correct parts list for a given symptom; a seasonal service-due tracker flagging spring tune-up and fall winterization windows per customer and per commercial fleet; and a parts-on-hand inventory tracker by common SKU with a reorder alert. Monetization: $15-25/mo per technician. Distribution: the Equipment & Engine Training Council (EETC) certified-technician network and r/smallengine and small-engine-repair Facebook groups. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 3. Custom Embroidery & Screen Printing Shop Order, Production-Run & Garment-Inventory Tracker

An independent embroidery or screen-printing shop producing custom apparel for youth sports teams, corporate clients, and local events has to track each order's exact garment style/size breakdown, thread-color or ink-color matching (embroidery thread numbers or Pantone spot-color ink matches), and digitized-design or screen-burned-film file linked to that specific customer's logo, since a re-order six months later needs the same thread numbers and the same digitized file pulled up instantly rather than re-created from scratch. A shop also has to track blank-garment inventory by style, color, and size against incoming orders, since a rush order for 50 team shirts discovered mid-week to be short three size-XL blanks in the right color is a missed delivery date, and screen-printing shops specifically have to track which screens are currently burned with which design so a repeat order doesn't require re-burning a screen that's sitting on a rack unused. A shop running a full production queue currently tracks garment specs, thread/ink matches, digitized files, and blank inventory on a paper work-order pad and a shared folder of design files, with a wrong thread-color reorder or a stockout discovered mid-run costing a delayed delivery and a customer explaining to their team why the shirts aren't ready.

The tool maintains a per-customer design library (digitized embroidery file or screen file, thread/ink color matches) linked to every past and current order; a per-order garment-size breakdown and blank-inventory check before the order is marked ready to run; and a screen/digitized-file reuse tracker so a repeat customer's job pulls the existing file instead of re-creating it. Monetization: $25-40/mo per shop. Distribution: the Printing United Alliance's embroidery and screen-printing segment and r/embroidery and r/screenprinting communities. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 4. Freelance Live Sound Engineer & Mobile PA Provider Gig, Equipment & Tech-Rider Tracker

An independent live sound engineer who brings a mobile PA system to weddings, corporate events, and small-venue concerts has to track each gig's specific technical requirements — a band's tech rider listing input channel count, monitor-mix needs, and stage-plot layout — since showing up to a multi-piece band's gig with a PA configured for a single acoustic performer means a scramble to reconfigure while a client's guests are already arriving. An engineer also has to track which cables, microphones, and speaker cabinets are packed for which gig and which pieces are currently out being repaired or loaned to a colleague for a bigger show, since a mobile audio rig is a large inventory of individually replaceable components and a missing DI box or a dead XLR cable discovered on-site with no backup is a gig that starts late or sounds wrong. A sound engineer booking several gigs a month currently tracks tech riders, equipment-case contents, and gig logistics (load-in time, venue power availability, parking) on a paper folder per gig and a mental inventory of gear, with a missing cable or a mismatched rider discovered only once the truck is already unloaded at the venue.

The tool maintains a per-gig tech-rider and stage-plot record with channel-count and monitor-mix requirements; an equipment-case inventory tracker flagging any gear currently out for repair or loaned out before a gig's packing list is finalized; and a gig-logistics checklist (load-in time, venue power, parking/access notes) built from the engineer's own history at repeat venues. Monetization: $20-35/mo per engineer. Distribution: the Association of Sound Designers and local musicians' union (AFM) sound-engineer referral lists, plus r/livesound and mobile-DJ/live-sound Facebook groups. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 5. Mobile Outboard & Marine Engine Mechanic Service-Interval & Parts Tracker

An independent mobile marine mechanic who services outboard motors, sterndrives, and personal watercraft engines at marinas and customers' driveways has to track each engine's exact make, model, and hour-meter reading to know which maintenance interval it's due for — outboard manufacturers specify service intervals by engine hours (commonly every 100 hours) rather than calendar time, and a mechanic who services a boat without checking the actual hour-meter reading against the customer's own maintenance log risks skipping an overdue impeller replacement or lower-unit oil change that leaves the customer stranded on the water. A mechanic also has to track model-specific parts — impellers, spark plugs, lower-unit seals, and fuel filters vary by engine family and year — and a marina-based mechanic running a full service route across dozens of boats needs to know which parts are on the truck before promising a same-day service window. A mechanic running a full season's route currently tracks engine specs, hour-meter service history, and parts needs on a paper service tag per boat, with an overdue maintenance interval or a wrong-part order discovered only when a customer's engine fails on the water mid-season.

The tool maintains a per-engine profile (make, model, year, and hour-meter reading) computing the next service-interval due date from the engine's own usage pattern; a model-specific parts checklist generated per scheduled service; and a season-start/haul-out scheduling tracker for marina-based customers whose boats come in and out of the water on a predictable seasonal cycle. Monetization: $20-30/mo per mechanic. Distribution: the American Boat & Yacht Council (ABYC) certified-technician network and marina bulletin boards/regional boating Facebook groups. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.

### 6. Wedding & Event Videographer Shoot Schedule, Footage & Deliverable-Revision Tracker

An independent wedding and event videographer books out a full season of Saturdays and has to track each client's shot list, ceremony timeline, and specific must-capture moments (first look, vows, toasts, first dance) alongside the raw footage files from multiple cameras and audio recorders that need to be organized and backed up before editing begins, since a single wedding can generate hundreds of gigabytes across three or four memory cards and a misplaced or unlabeled card is irreplaceable footage a client can never get back. A videographer also has to track each client's deliverable package (highlight reel, full ceremony video, raw footage archive) against the specific revision rounds included in their contract, since a client requesting a fourth round of edits when their package includes two free rounds is scope creep that erodes margin on every job if it isn't tracked and flagged at the time of the request. A videographer running a full wedding season currently tracks shot lists, footage backup status, and deliverable revision counts on a paper contract folder and scattered notes, with a lost card or an untracked extra revision round discovered only when a client is unhappy months after the wedding.

The tool maintains a per-event shot list and ceremony timeline built from the client's intake questionnaire; a raw-footage card/drive intake log confirming every camera and audio source is backed up before the crew leaves the venue; and a deliverable-and-revision tracker counting edit rounds used against what the client's package includes, with a flag before an out-of-scope revision request is quietly absorbed for free. Monetization: $25-40/mo per videographer. Distribution: WeddingWire and The Knot vendor community forums, and regional wedding-videographer Facebook groups. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.
