# Side Income Ideas - 2026-08-27 (Round 135)

Simple operational tracker tools for self-employed individuals and micro-businesses. These are NOT compliance tools — they're practical day-to-day workflow trackers that replace the messy spreadsheet, whiteboard, or notebook a solo operator currently uses to run their book of business. Each idea targets a different type of self-employed operator not already covered in this repo, names a real distribution channel, and gives realistic per-account pricing and a time to first dollar.

---

### 1. Residential Well Pump & Water System Service Technician Per-System Service History & Warranty Tracker

An independent technician servicing residential well pumps, pressure tanks, and pressure switches has to know each property's exact system configuration — submersible or jet pump, tank size and pre-charge pressure, well depth — before a service call, since showing up to a no-water emergency without knowing whether the property runs a shallow jet pump or a deep submersible means guessing at the right replacement part on the truck. A technician also has to track each component's install date and manufacturer warranty, since a pressure tank or pump motor that fails inside its warranty window should be a parts-only claim rather than a bill the homeowner eats, and a technician who doesn't check the install date before quoting a full replacement both overcharges a covered customer and loses the trust that drives referrals. Seasonal cottage and cabin properties add their own wrinkle: a system that needs to be properly drained and shut down each fall and recommissioned each spring, and a technician running a multi-property cottage-country route can't rely on memory for which camps are already winterized versus which are still waiting for a fall visit before the first hard freeze. A technician running a full route of wells, cottages, and rural homes currently tracks system specs, warranty dates, and seasonal shutdown status on a paper service card per property and a shop-truck parts guess, and a wrong-part truck roll or a warranty claim missed because nobody checked the install date is a wasted trip or an eaten repair bill.

The tool maintains a per-property system profile (pump type, tank size and pre-charge spec, well depth, and install date) that surfaces before every service call; a warranty-window checker flagging any failed component still inside its manufacturer coverage before a customer is billed directly; and a seasonal shutdown/startup tracker for cottage and cabin properties flagging every camp still awaiting its fall winterization or spring startup visit. Monetization: $15-25/mo per technician. Distribution: r/HVAC-adjacent well-and-pump trade Facebook groups, the National Ground Water Association's pump-installer affiliate network, and rural hardware-store and farm-supply bulletin boards where independent well-pump technicians already trade parts and warranty tips. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 2. Portable Generator Rental Operator Fleet, Fuel & Storm-Season Delivery Tracker

An independent operator renting out portable and towable generators for storm-outage backup power, job sites, and events has to know exactly which unit in the fleet is available, out on rental, or down for service at any given moment, since a generator promised for a same-day storm-outage delivery that's actually still sitting on a customer's property from last week because nobody logged the overdue return is a truck rolling out with nothing to deliver during the exact week demand spikes hardest. An operator also has to track each unit's fuel type (gas, diesel, or propane), run-hours since its last oil change and filter service, and load capacity, since dispatching an undersized unit to a customer needing to run a sump pump and a furnace simultaneously is a callback the same night, and a unit sent out past its service interval is the generator most likely to fail mid-rental. Storm-season demand also creates a compressed multi-day surge where every unit in the fleet needs to be turned around, fueled, and redelivered as fast as possible, and an operator running a full fleet across scattered residential and commercial customers currently tracks unit status, fuel type, and service intervals on a yard whiteboard and a driver's memory, with a generator dispatched still low on fuel or overdue for service discovered only when a stressed customer calls back during a power outage.

The tool maintains a per-unit fleet status board (available, out on rental, in service) with fuel type and load-capacity specs surfaced at dispatch; a run-hours maintenance tracker flagging any unit due for oil/filter service before it goes back out; and a storm-season delivery/pickup route board sequencing a compressed surge of same-day deliveries and returns. Monetization: $19-29/mo per operator. Distribution: r/generators and portable-power-rental Facebook groups, the American Rental Association's (ARA) small-operator member directory, and regional storm-prep and hurricane-season trade shows where independent generator rental operators already trade fleet-utilization and dispatch tips. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 3. Event AV, Sound & Lighting Equipment Rental Company Inventory, Booking & Damage-Return Tracker

An independent company renting out PA speakers, mixers, wireless microphones, and stage lighting rigs for weddings, corporate events, and private parties has to know exactly which pieces of equipment are booked for which event and when they need to be back in the warehouse for the next client's setup, since a mixer promised for a Saturday corporate event that's actually still packed in last night's wedding client's vehicle because nobody confirmed the return is a booking the company can't fulfill. A company also has to track each event's exact equipment list against what's actually loaded onto the truck, since a lighting rig missing one truss clamp or a speaker cable left behind turns a professional setup into a scramble in front of a paying client's guests. Every returned piece also needs a condition check before it goes back into inventory for the next booking, since a damaged XLR cable or a cracked speaker cabinet reintroduced into the rental pool without being flagged is the next client's problem, not the one who actually caused the damage, and a company running several concurrent weekend bookings across a shared equipment pool currently tracks reservations, packing lists, and return condition on a booking spreadsheet and a warehouse shelf count, with a double-booked speaker or an undocumented damage claim discovered only when the next client shows up to load in.

The tool maintains a per-item inventory calendar showing what's booked, in-transit, and available across every piece of equipment in the pool; a per-event packing-list checklist confirming every item on the quote sheet actually loaded onto the truck; and a post-event condition-and-return log flagging any damaged or missing piece before it's booked out to the next client. Monetization: $19-29/mo per company. Distribution: r/eventproduction and mobile-DJ/AV-rental Facebook groups, The Bash and GigSalad vendor community boards where AV rental companies already list their services, and regional event-production trade association meetups. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 4. Paint-Your-Own-Pottery Studio Operator Bisque Inventory, Glaze-and-Fire Queue & Party-Booking Tracker

An independent walk-in paint-your-own-pottery studio has to track its bisque-ware inventory by shape and SKU, since a studio that runs out of a popular mug or figurine shape mid-Saturday has to turn away a paying customer who came in specifically wanting to paint that piece. A studio also has to move every painted piece through its own glaze-and-fire queue — the customer paints it, staff apply a clear glaze coat, and the piece goes into a kiln firing batch before it's ready for pickup days later — and a studio running dozens of customer pieces through the queue every weekend can't rely on memory for whose piece is still waiting for its glaze coat versus whose is already fired and sitting on the pickup shelf, and a piece that gets lost in the queue or fired in the wrong batch is a customer who made a special trip back only to find their piece isn't ready. Birthday parties and group bookings add their own scheduling layer, since a studio has to reserve painting-table capacity and enough bisque inventory in advance for a 12-kid birthday party without accidentally double-booking that same time slot with walk-in traffic. A studio running a full weekend of walk-ins and scheduled parties currently tracks bisque stock, piece-in-progress status, and party bookings on a shelf count and a paper sign-in sheet, and a customer's painted piece that sits unfired for two extra weeks because it fell through the cracks of the glazing queue is a bad review from someone who won't come back.

The tool maintains a bisque-ware inventory tracker by shape/SKU with a low-stock alert before a popular piece runs out; a per-piece glaze-and-fire queue moving each customer's painted item from glazing through kiln-batch assignment to a ready-for-pickup date with a customer notification trigger; and a party/event booking calendar reserving table capacity and bisque inventory against the studio's regular walk-in traffic. Monetization: $19-29/mo per studio. Distribution: r/Pottery and paint-your-own-pottery studio owner Facebook groups, the Rockler and local craft-supply co-op bulletin boards, and children's-party planning and Nextdoor community groups where independent studios already book birthday parties. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 5. Custom Epoxy River Table & Live-Edge Woodworking Maker Slab Inventory, Resin-Pour & Cure-Schedule Tracker

An independent maker building custom epoxy river tables and live-edge furniture has to track a slab inventory by species, dimensions, and live-edge profile, since a client who chose a specific walnut slab during a showroom visit needs that exact piece of wood — not a similar-looking one — actually fabricated into their finished table. A maker also has to plan every commission's resin-pour recipe and cure schedule, since a deep river-channel pour has to go in multiple thin layers over several days rather than one deep pour (a single thick pour generates enough curing heat to crack or cloud the resin), and a maker running several commissions at different pour stages simultaneously can't rely on memory for which table is ready for its next layer versus which one still needs another 24 hours of cure time before sanding. Color-batch matching adds its own wrinkle, since a multi-piece commission (a table plus matching side tables) needs the same tinted-resin batch across every piece, and mixing a fresh batch that's even slightly off-shade from the first pour is a mismatch a client notices immediately. A maker running a home workshop with several commissions in progress currently tracks slab assignments, pour-layer schedules, and color-batch recipes on a shop notebook and a mental sense of which table is due for its next pour, and a client's chosen slab getting mixed up with another commission, or a pour scheduled a day too early while the layer beneath is still soft, is a ruined piece and weeks of lost material and shop time.

The tool maintains a slab inventory tracker by species, dimensions, and live-edge profile tied to the specific client commission it's reserved for; a per-commission resin-pour schedule sequencing multi-layer pours against each layer's required cure time before the next pour or sanding stage begins; and a color-batch recipe log ensuring a multi-piece commission's tinted resin matches across every pour. Monetization: $19-29/mo per maker. Distribution: r/woodworking and r/epoxy river table maker Facebook groups, the Association of Woodworking & Furnishings Suppliers (AWFS) member network, and regional maker-fair and custom-furniture trade shows where independent makers already trade slab-sourcing and cure-schedule tips. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 6. Mobile Window Screen & Screen Door Repair Technician Job-Measurement, Material & Route Tracker

An independent mobile technician re-screening window screens and repairing screen doors has to measure each frame's exact dimensions and match it to the right mesh type (standard fiberglass, aluminum, or pet-resistant) and spline diameter before cutting material, since a screen cut a half-inch short or fitted with the wrong-diameter spline won't seat properly in the frame channel and has to be redone on the spot. A technician also has to track mesh-roll and spline inventory by type and color, since a route stop that calls for pet-resistant mesh is a wasted trip if the technician's van is only stocked with standard fiberglass that day, and running out of a specific spline size mid-route means turning away the next job on the schedule. Most jobs are same-day, on-site repairs across multiple stops in a single day, and a technician running a full day of window and door screen jobs across a scattered residential route currently tracks frame measurements and material needs on a notepad and a guess at what's loaded in the van, and a wrong-mesh job that has to be rescheduled, or a route that runs out of spline before the last stop, is a lost afternoon and a customer who calls a competitor for next time.

The tool maintains a per-job frame-measurement record capturing dimensions, mesh type, and spline diameter needed for each window or door; a mesh-roll and spline material inventory tracker flagging a reorder before a route runs a specific type too low; and a daily route board sequencing the day's stops against the material actually loaded for that day's jobs. Monetization: $12-19/mo per technician. Distribution: r/HomeImprovement-adjacent screen-repair and handyman Facebook groups, local hardware-store and Ace-Rental co-op bulletin boards, and Nextdoor contractor communities where independent screen repair technicians already trade material-sourcing and route tips. Time to first dollar: 2 weeks.
