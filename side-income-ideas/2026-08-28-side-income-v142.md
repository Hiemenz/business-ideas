# Side Income Ideas - 2026-08-28 (Round 142)

Simple operational tracker tools for self-employed individuals and micro-businesses. These are NOT compliance tools — they're practical day-to-day workflow trackers that replace the messy spreadsheet, whiteboard, or notebook a solo operator currently uses to run their book of business. Each idea targets a different type of self-employed operator not already covered in this repo, names a real distribution channel, and gives realistic per-account pricing and a time to first dollar.

---

### 1. Bathtub & Tile Reglazing Technician Job-Spec, Color-Match & Cure-Window Tracker

An independent bathtub and tile reglazing technician — resurfacing a worn or outdated tub, sink, or tile surface with a sprayed epoxy or acrylic coating rather than replacing the fixture — has to track each job's exact surface material (cast iron, fiberglass, acrylic, porcelain) and the specific color/finish match ordered, since the prep chemicals and bonding primer differ by substrate and a coating applied with the wrong primer for that material is a peeling, failed job discovered only after the customer starts using the tub again. A technician also has to manage each job's cure-window strictly, since a freshly reglazed surface needs 24-72 hours with no water contact before it's fully cured, and a customer who runs a bath the same evening because nobody flagged the exact cure-complete time on that specific job is a warranty claim over a defect the technician didn't actually cause. Chemical inventory adds a further layer, since epoxy and catalyst components have their own shelf life and mixing ratio, and a technician running several jobs a week across different substrate types needs to know which color/finish stock and which primer type is on hand before quoting a same-week appointment. A technician running a full week of jobs currently tracks substrate type, color-match orders, and cure-window deadlines on a paper work order and a phone reminder, with a wrong-primer job or a cure-window violation discovered only when the customer calls back about a surface that's already failed.

The tool maintains a per-job spec sheet (substrate material, color/finish match, and primer type) generating the correct prep-and-bonding sequence; a cure-window tracker computing each job's water-contact-safe date and sending the customer a reminder; and a chemical/color-stock inventory tracker by product line and mix-ratio batch with a reorder alert. Monetization: $15-25/mo per technician. Distribution: the National Bathtub & Countertop Refinishers Association's (NBCRA) member directory, r/HomeImprovement bathroom-remodel threads, and property-manager and real-estate-flipper referral partnerships. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 2. Baby-Proofing & Childproofing Installer Room-Audit, Product-Spec & Follow-Up Tracker

An independent baby-proofing and childproofing installer conducting an in-home safety walkthrough and installing gates, cabinet locks, outlet covers, furniture anchors, and window guards has to document each room's specific hazards and the exact hardware installed against them, since a full-home installation can involve dozens of individual attachment points across a dozen rooms, and an installer relying on memory during the walkthrough risks missing a hazard the parent specifically flagged (a particular cabinet with cleaning chemicals, a bookshelf that needs anchoring to a stud rather than drywall) once the crew starts working from a mental checklist instead of a documented room-by-room audit. An installer also has to track hardware compatibility, since anchor and gate hardware varies by wall type, stair configuration, and furniture style, and a mismatched anchor kit discovered mid-install means a delayed job and a parent anxious to have the house safe before a mobile baby starts crawling. Follow-up visits add a further layer, since most families need a re-check as a child grows into new hazards (a toddler who starts climbing needs a different safety pass than a crawling infant), and an installer running a full calendar of new-client walkthroughs and returning follow-ups currently tracks room audits, hardware specs, and follow-up scheduling on a paper checklist per home, with a missed hazard or an anchor installed into drywall instead of a stud discovered only after an incident report from a worried parent.

The tool maintains a per-home room-by-room hazard audit checklist generating an installation punch list; a hardware-spec tracker matching anchor and gate types to each room's wall/stair/furniture configuration; and a child-age-based follow-up scheduler flagging when a family is due for a re-check as their child reaches a new mobility milestone. Monetization: $15-25/mo per installer. Distribution: the International Association for Child Safety (IAFCS) member directory, r/beyondthebump and local parent Facebook groups, and pediatrician-office and baby-store referral partnerships. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 3. Invisible & Electric Pet Fence Installation Technician Boundary-Map, Collar-Program & Callback Tracker

An independent installer of underground invisible/electric pet-containment fencing has to map each property's exact boundary-wire loop before burying a foot of cable, since the loop layout has to account for the specific yard shape, any exclusion zones (gardens, pools, driveways), and the transmitter's maximum-loop-length rating, and a loop laid out without checking that rating against the property's actual perimeter means a signal that's too weak at the far end of the yard — a gap in coverage a dog will eventually find and bolt through. A technician also has to program and document each collar's correction-level setting against the specific dog's size, temperament, and coat thickness, since a setting too weak for a large, thick-coated dog won't register, while a setting calibrated for that dog used later on a smaller second pet in the same household is an over-correction the customer will call back about. Every install also comes with a training period — the pet needs supervised boundary-flag training over one to two weeks before the owner can trust the system unsupervised — and a technician running installs across a service area currently tracks boundary maps, collar settings, and training-checklist follow-ups on a paper site sketch and a phone photo, with a coverage gap or a mis-set collar discovered only when a dog gets loose and the customer calls back angry instead of scheduling a paid adjustment.

The tool maintains a per-property boundary-wire map (loop length, exclusion zones, and transmitter model) checked against that transmitter's rated coverage before the job is marked complete; a per-pet collar-setting log matching correction level to the dog's size and coat type; and a boundary-flag training-checklist tracker scheduling the follow-up visits a new install needs before the owner goes unsupervised. Monetization: $12-19/mo per technician. Distribution: the Pet Care Services Association's installer network, r/InvisibleFence and r/dogtraining threads, and local veterinary-clinic and dog-trainer referral partnerships. Time to first dollar: 2 weeks.

### 4. Paint Protection Film (PPF) Installer Panel-Pattern, Film-Batch & Warranty Tracker

An independent paint protection film installer wrapping a vehicle's front end, full body, or high-wear panels in a clear self-healing film — a distinct trade from window tinting and ceramic coating, since PPF is a precision-cut, heat-shrunk film applied panel by panel rather than a sprayed or liquid product — has to track each job's exact panel-pattern cut file matched to the specific vehicle's year, make, model, and trim, since a pattern cut for the wrong trim level (a different front bumper or hood contour) wastes an expensive roll of film and stalls the job mid-install waiting on a re-cut. An installer also has to track which film brand and batch went on which vehicle, since PPF carries a manufacturer warranty against yellowing, cracking, and edge-lifting that can run 10 years, and a customer's warranty claim years later needs the specific film product and lot number on file to file the claim with the manufacturer rather than guessed at from a faded invoice. Film inventory itself runs by roll width and finish (gloss, matte, or color-tint), and an installer running several vehicles a week needs a reorder trigger before a commonly-used roll width runs out mid-week. An installer running a full install queue currently tracks panel-pattern files, film-batch numbers, and warranty terms on a paper job ticket and a folder of pattern-file printouts, with a wrong-pattern cut or a lost batch number turning a same-day install into a wasted roll and an unfileable warranty claim years later.

The tool maintains a per-job panel-pattern tracker matched to the vehicle's confirmed year/make/model/trim; a film-batch and warranty-registration log recording brand, lot number, and coverage term per vehicle for instant warranty-claim lookup; and a film-roll inventory tracker by width and finish with a reorder alert. Monetization: $19-29/mo per installer. Distribution: the International Detailing Association's (IDA) PPF-installer segment, r/AutoDetailing and PPF-installer Facebook groups, and auto-dealership and body-shop referral partnerships. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 5. Drone (UAS) Repair & Maintenance Technician Component-Diagnostic & Calibration Tracker

An independent drone repair technician servicing commercial and hobbyist UAS platforms — diagnosing crashed or malfunctioning multirotor aircraft and replacing motors, ESCs, flight controllers, and gimbal/camera assemblies — has to track each unit's exact make, model, and firmware version before ordering parts, since motor mounts, prop specs, and controller boards vary by model generation and a part ordered against the wrong firmware or hardware revision doesn't fit or won't calibrate correctly once installed. A technician also has to log post-repair calibration results — compass and IMU calibration, motor-balance checks, and a test-flight log — since a drone returned to a commercial client (a real estate photographer or an agricultural survey operator) without documented calibration is a liability the technician owns if the aircraft crashes again on its very next flight, and a repair shop servicing dozens of units a month needs that calibration record on file to stand behind the work. Parts inventory adds a further layer, since motors, ESCs, and props are model-specific down to the exact SKU, and a shop promising a fast turnaround for a commercial client whose livelihood depends on the aircraft needs a reorder trigger before a commonly-needed component runs out. A technician running a full repair bench currently tracks unit specs, calibration results, and parts inventory on a paper repair tag and a supplier order folder, with a mismatched part order or a missing calibration record costing a wasted trip and a client grounded during their next paid job.

The tool maintains a per-unit intake profile (make, model, firmware version, and diagnosed issue) tracked through the repair queue; a post-repair calibration and test-flight log confirming compass, IMU, and motor-balance checks before a unit is released; and a parts inventory tracker by model-specific SKU with a reorder alert before a common motor or ESC runs low. Monetization: $19-29/mo per technician. Distribution: r/drones and r/dji practitioner repair threads, the Commercial Drone Alliance's operator network, and local drone-retailer and photography/surveying-operator referral partnerships. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 6. Custom Stonemason & Architectural Stone Veneer Installer Material-Takeoff & Job-Stage Tracker

An independent stonemason building custom fireplaces, retaining walls, and natural or manufactured stone veneer facades — a distinct trade from paver/hardscape installers, since masonry work is set course by course with mortar rather than dry-laid interlocking units — has to produce an accurate stone-and-mortar material takeoff from each job's square footage and stone pattern before ordering, since natural stone is priced and delivered by the pallet with real lead times, and an underordered pallet discovered mid-job means an idle crew waiting days for a matching-lot restock while a half-built wall sits exposed to weather. A mason also has to track which stone lot or quarry batch went into which job, since natural stone varies in color and vein pattern batch to batch, and a customer who orders more stone later for an addition needs the original lot matched as closely as possible or the new section visibly clashes against the weathered original. The build itself moves through distinct stages — footing/foundation prep, dry-fit layout, mortar setting, and pointing/sealing — and a mason running several concurrent jobs needs each job's current stage tracked against its weather-dependent scheduling window, since mortar work can't proceed in freezing or heavy-rain conditions and a crew dispatched to a job that has to be rescheduled is a wasted day. A mason running a full job queue currently tracks material takeoffs, stone-lot records, and job-stage scheduling on a paper estimate sheet and a supplier delivery ticket, with an underordered pallet or a stone-lot mismatch on a later addition costing a delayed job and a visibly patched-together final result.

The tool maintains a per-job material takeoff (square footage, stone pattern, and mortar/sand quantities) generating an accurate supplier order; a stone-lot/batch tracker recording which quarry batch went into which job for future addition-matching; and a job-stage scheduling board sequencing footing, dry-fit, setting, and pointing against a weather-dependent calendar. Monetization: $19-29/mo per mason. Distribution: the Natural Stone Institute's (NSI) member directory, r/Masonry and r/hardscaping threads, and landscape-architect and hardscape-contractor referral partnerships. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.
