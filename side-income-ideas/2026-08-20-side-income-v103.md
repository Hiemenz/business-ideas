# Side Income Ideas - 2026-08-20 (Session 103)

Simple operational tracker tools for self-employed operators and micro-businesses — scheduling, client records, inventory, billing, and project-pipeline tools, not compliance software. Each idea below covers a self-employed operator type not already present in the repo's prior 102 sessions.

---

### 1. Independent A&P Mechanic (Aircraft Mechanic) Mobile Maintenance Log & Squawk-List Tracker

General aviation aircraft owners at small municipal airports rely on freelance A&P (Airframe & Powerplant) mechanics who travel between multiple airport hangars servicing a rotating roster of privately owned planes. Right now most of these mechanics keep each aircraft's maintenance history, open squawks (reported issues awaiting repair), parts on order, and next annual/100-hour inspection due-date scattered across paper logbooks, a notes app, and memory — which makes it easy to lose track of which plane is waiting on which part, or which owner hasn't been called back about a completed repair.

The tool maintains a per-aircraft profile (tail number, make/model, hours since last annual, next 100-hour/annual due date) with an open squawk list per aircraft, a parts-on-order tracker linked to the squawk that's waiting on it, and a job log per visit (what was inspected, what was signed off, photos of the work). A simple owner-facing view lets the mechanic text a status update — "still waiting on the mag part, ETA Thursday" — pulled straight from the tracked record instead of a memory-jog phone call.

Monetization: $25-45/mo per mechanic. Distribution: EAA (Experimental Aircraft Association) local chapters and AOPA (Aircraft Owners and Pilots Association) mechanic/maintenance forums, where freelance A&Ps already gather to trade referrals. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.

### 2. Independent Concrete Contractor (Flatwork & Foundations) Pour Schedule & Cure-Time Tracker

A small residential concrete crew (driveways, patios, foundations, sidewalks) juggles multiple jobs at different stages simultaneously — one site needs forms set this week, another poured tomorrow depending on weather, and a third is curing and can't be stripped or loaded for a set number of days. Missing a cure-time window (stripping forms too early, or scheduling a follow-on trade like a fence installer to work on a slab before it's ready) causes callbacks and cracked work, and most small crews track this on a whiteboard or a foreman's memory rather than a real schedule.

The tool maintains a per-job pipeline (form-set date, scheduled pour date with a weather-hold flag, concrete order quantity/mix spec, cure-time countdown before strip/load-bearing, and a simple crew assignment view) alongside a ready-mix supplier order log so the contractor never shows up to a pour without confirming the truck. A client-facing status link shows "poured, curing until [date]" so the contractor isn't fielding "is it ready yet" calls.

Monetization: $30-60/mo per crew. Distribution: local concrete contractor associations and NRMCA (National Ready Mixed Concrete Association) affiliated regional groups, plus contractor Facebook groups organized by state licensing requirement. Time to first dollar: 3-5 weeks.

### 3. Independent Deck & Outdoor Living Contractor Project-Stage & Material Takeoff Tracker

A one- or two-person deck-building business runs several projects in parallel through distinct stages — design/quote, permit submission, footings, framing, decking/railing install, and final inspection — and each stage has its own material list (footings need concrete and post bases, framing needs joists and hardware, decking needs board count and fastener type by linear foot). Estimating material quantities by hand for each job and re-deriving them when a homeowner changes the deck size mid-quote wastes hours that a structured takeoff would save, and tracking which of five active decks is waiting on a permit versus ready to frame is currently a mental exercise for the owner.

The tool maintains a per-project stage tracker (quote → permit → footings → framing → decking → inspection) with a material takeoff calculator that derives board count, joist spacing, and hardware quantities from entered deck dimensions, plus a permit-submission and inspection-scheduling log per municipality (since permit turnaround and inspection requirements vary by jurisdiction). A materials shopping list exports per job for the lumber yard run.

Monetization: $35-65/mo per contractor. Distribution: NADRA (North American Deck and Railing Association), whose member base is almost entirely small independent deck-building operations. Time to first dollar: 3-5 weeks.

### 4. Independent Pet Cremation Service Operator Chain-of-Custody & Family Notification Tracker

Small independent pet cremation operators (as distinct from the human funeral homes and pet cemeteries already covered in this repo's compliance track) handle a steady stream of individual pet remains from veterinary clinics and grieving pet owners, and the operational core of the business is making absolutely sure the right ashes go back to the right family — a mix-up is the single worst outcome a pet cremation business can have. Right now most small operators track intake, cremation batch/run number, and pickup/delivery status on paper tags and a logbook, with family notification ("your pet's remains are ready") handled by a phone call someone has to remember to make.

The tool maintains a per-pet intake record (owner name, veterinary clinic of origin, intake date/weight, assigned cremation run number, private vs. communal cremation type) with a chain-of-custody log from intake through cremation to return, an urn/container inventory linked to each completed job, and an automated family-notification trigger (text or email) the moment a job is marked ready for pickup or delivery.

Monetization: $40-70/mo per operator. Distribution: the International Association of Pet Cemeteries & Crematories (IAOPCC) and veterinary clinic referral networks that already send business to these operators. Time to first dollar: 4-6 weeks.

### 5. Antique Mall & Vintage Booth Vendor Multi-Location Inventory & Consignment Tracker

A vendor who rents booth space in one or more antique malls (a common small-business model — many vendors rent booths in 2-4 malls simultaneously to reach different customer bases) has to track which items are placed in which booth, what each mall's monthly rent and commission-on-sale terms are, and — since most antique malls only send a monthly settlement statement rather than real-time sales data — has no easy way to reconcile what actually sold against what they dropped off, or to know their true per-item profit after commission and booth rent are backed out.

The tool maintains a per-item inventory log (item description, acquisition cost, assigned booth/mall, price tag), a per-mall booth-rent and commission-rate profile, and a monthly settlement reconciliation view where the vendor enters the mall's reported sales and the tool calculates true profit per item after rent allocation and commission, flagging items that have sat unsold past a chosen age threshold as markdown candidates.

Monetization: $15-30/mo per vendor. Distribution: regional antique mall vendor Facebook groups and state antique dealer associations, where multi-location booth renters actively trade tips on which malls perform best. Time to first dollar: 2-4 weeks.

### 6. Mobile Fleet & Semi-Truck Washing Route Operator Client Contract & Wash-Frequency Tracker

Mobile fleet washing operators contract with trucking companies, delivery fleets, and construction-equipment yards to wash vehicles on a recurring schedule (weekly, biweekly, or per-DOT-inspection-prep) at the client's yard rather than a fixed carwash location. Managing this means tracking each fleet client's contracted wash frequency and per-vehicle or per-truck pricing, which vehicles in a given fleet were actually washed on a given route visit (fleets add and retire trucks constantly, and washing a truck that's off-lease or missing a route stop wastes water and time), and invoicing based on actual vehicles washed rather than a flat guess.

The tool maintains a per-client fleet roster (vehicle/unit numbers, contracted wash frequency, price per unit), a route-visit checklist that records which units were actually washed on a given date, and an invoice generator that totals actual units washed against the client's contracted rate — plus a simple route-sequencing view for the day's stops across multiple fleet-yard locations.

Monetization: $30-50/mo per operator. Distribution: mobile fleet washing and pressure-washing trade Facebook groups and regional trucking association vendor directories, where fleet-wash contractors already look for referrals into new trucking yards. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.
