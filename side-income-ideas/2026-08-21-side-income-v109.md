# Side Income Ideas - 2026-08-21 (Round 109)

Simple operational tracker tools for self-employed individuals and micro-businesses. These are NOT compliance tools — they're practical day-to-day workflow trackers that replace the messy spreadsheet, whiteboard, or notebook a solo operator currently uses to run their book of business. Each idea targets a different type of self-employed operator not already covered in this repo, names a real distribution channel, and gives realistic per-account pricing and a time to first dollar.

---

### 1. Micro-Roaster & Small-Batch Coffee Roaster Batch Log & Wholesale Café Account Tracker

An independent micro-roaster running a small drum roaster works from a rotating slate of green coffee lots — each with its own origin, harvest/crop year, and a roast profile (charge temperature, first-crack time, development time, drop temperature) that has to be dialed in and then repeated consistently batch after batch, since a wholesale café account will notice if this month's bag tastes different from last month's. A roaster running several origins through the roaster each week while also fulfilling recurring wholesale orders to a handful of local cafés currently tracks green-bean lot inventory, which roast profile belongs to which origin, and each café's standing weekly or biweekly bag order across a paper roast log taped to the roaster and a separate order notebook, and a roast profile that drifts unrecorded or a wholesale account whose restock date got missed is a lost repeat-buyer relationship this business runs on.

The tool maintains a per-lot green-bean inventory record (origin, crop year, remaining weight) linked to a per-batch roast log (roast date, profile parameters, and bag yield) with a searchable roast-profile history per origin for consistency reference, and a wholesale café account tracker showing each account's standing order size, roast/origin preference, and last-delivery date against current roasted-bag inventory.

Monetization: $19-35/mo per roaster. Distribution: the Roasters Guild (a division of the Specialty Coffee Association) member directory and regional micro-roaster Facebook groups, where small roasters already trade roast-log and wholesale-account workflow tips. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.

### 2. Portable Sawmill & Chainsaw Milling Operator Job Log & Board-Foot Yield Tracker

An independent portable-sawmill operator hauls a trailer-mounted bandsaw mill (or a chainsaw milling rig) directly to a customer's property to saw their own downed or standing timber into lumber — a landowner clearing storm-damaged trees, a hobbyist building a barn from their own woodlot — with each job needing an on-site estimate of board-foot yield from the customer's specific logs, a cutting-list spec (dimensional lumber, live-edge slabs, or custom thicknesses the customer wants), and a blade/chain sharpening and maintenance interval that has to be tracked against hours run, since a dull blade ruins a cut and burns a job's profit margin. An operator running several jobs a week across scattered rural properties currently tracks each customer's log count and species, the requested cutting spec, and blade-hours-since-sharpening on a paper job ticket and a mental tally, and a blade run too long past its sharpening interval mid-job, or a cutting spec miscommunicated and cut wrong, wastes a customer's log and the operator's drive time both.

The tool maintains a per-job record (customer, property location, log species/count, and cutting-list spec) with a board-foot yield estimator and running yield log per job, a blade/chain maintenance tracker computing hours run since last sharpening against the mill's service interval, and a route/scheduling view for stacking multiple properties into an efficient driving day.

Monetization: $15-29/mo per operator. Distribution: the Portable Sawmill/Norwood/Wood-Mizer owner forums and regional sawyer Facebook groups, plus the Forest Products Society's small-sawyer network, where independent portable-mill operators already coordinate job-estimating and blade-maintenance workflow. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.

### 3. Pumpkin Patch & Corn Maze Seasonal Agritourism Operator Attraction Booking & Field-Block Tracker

A seasonal fall agritourism operator running a pumpkin patch and corn maze attraction — distinct from a year-round u-pick orchard — plants a maze field and pumpkin patch blocks months ahead of a roughly eight-week fall season, then has to manage a compressed rush of group bookings (school field trips, birthday parties, weekend crowds) against limited daily visitor capacity, while separately tracking which patch blocks are ready for pumpkin harvest/sale versus still maturing and when the corn maze's cut paths need re-mowing or storm-damage repair before the next busy weekend. An operator running dozens of school-group reservations and hundreds of weekend walk-ups across an eight-week window currently tracks group bookings, daily capacity, and block-by-block pumpkin readiness on a paper reservation binder and a whiteboard in the farm stand, and a school group booked into a day that's already at capacity, or a patch block sold down to nothing without anyone noticing before a busy Saturday, is a refunded booking or an empty-handed family that doesn't come back next year.

The tool maintains a group-booking calendar with daily visitor-capacity tracking and a waitlist for sold-out days, a per-field-block pumpkin inventory and ready-for-harvest status view, and a maze-maintenance log tracking path mowing/repair dates against the season's weekly rush pattern.

Monetization: $25-49/mo per farm (priced for the concentrated eight-week season). Distribution: the North American Farmers' Direct Marketing Association (NAFDMA) member directory and conference, and regional agritourism/corn-maze-operator Facebook groups where fall attraction operators already trade booking and capacity-management workflow tips. Time to first dollar: 3-5 weeks (timed ahead of the fall season).

### 4. Seasonal Wild Game Processor Hunter Intake & Cut-Sheet Tracker

A seasonal wild-game processor — a small custom meat-cutting operation that takes in deer, elk, and hogs from hunters during a compressed fall hunting season — receives dozens of animals a day at the season's peak, each tagged to a specific hunter's harvest tag and paired with that hunter's individual cut-sheet preference (ground meat only, specific steak cuts, summer sausage or jerky processing, antler/cape retention for a taxidermist), and has to keep every carcass correctly matched to its hunter and cut-sheet through days of hanging, cutting, packaging, and freezer storage before pickup. A processor running a walk-in cooler full of dozens of concurrent carcasses during peak rifle season currently tracks hunter name, tag number, cut-sheet instructions, and freezer-package location on paper claim tickets stapled to each carcass, and a mismatched cut sheet or a customer who shows up to find their order isn't ready is exactly the kind of mistake a one-location seasonal operation can't afford during its only few weeks of annual revenue.

The tool maintains a per-animal intake record (hunter name/tag number, species, and intake date) linked to a cut-sheet specification (cut preferences, sausage/jerky add-ons, special handling notes) with a processing-status view from hang time through cutting to packaged-and-ready, a freezer-location log for finished packages, and a pickup-ready notification queue sorted by which orders are complete and awaiting customer pickup.

Monetization: $29-49/mo per processor (priced for a short, high-volume season). Distribution: the National Association of Meat Processors (NAMP) member directory and state taxidermist/processor trade shows, plus regional deer-processor Facebook groups where seasonal wild-game processors already coordinate rush-season intake workflow. Time to first dollar: 4-6 weeks (timed ahead of fall hunting season).

### 5. Mobile Equine & Canine Massage/Bodywork Therapist Client Animal Profile & Treatment-History Tracker

A certified equine or canine massage and bodywork therapist travels between barns, training facilities, and clients' homes treating performance horses and working or competition dogs for muscle soreness, recovery after competition, and general maintenance, with each animal needing its own treatment history — problem areas identified session to session, technique and pressure notes, and a recommended re-treatment interval that varies by the animal's workload (a competition horse in active training needs more frequent sessions than a pasture-sound retiree) — and a therapist building a client base of dozens of animals across scattered barns currently tracks per-animal treatment notes and when each one is due for its next session on paper intake cards and a personal appointment book, and an animal whose recommended re-treatment interval quietly slips because no one flagged it is a client relationship that drifts to a competitor who reaches out first.

The tool maintains a per-animal profile (owner, discipline/workload level, and a treatment-history log noting problem areas, technique, and session date) with a recommended-interval "due for next session" view sorted across the therapist's full client roster, and a barn-by-barn route view for scheduling multiple animals at the same location efficiently.

Monetization: $15-29/mo per therapist. Distribution: the Equine Sports Massage Association (ESMA) and International Association of Animal Massage & Bodywork (IAAMB) member directories and regional equine/canine bodywork Facebook groups, where independent practitioners already trade client-tracking and route-scheduling workflow tips. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.

### 6. Hardscape & Paver Installer Material Takeoff & Multi-Phase Job Tracker

An independent hardscape contractor building patios, walkways, and retaining walls out of segmental pavers and block works each job through a fixed sequence — excavation to grade, compacted base-material installation, paver or block laying to pattern, joint sanding, and a final sealing step often scheduled weeks after installation to let the joints settle — with each job's paver square footage and base-material tonnage calculated from the site measurements before ordering, and a contractor running several concurrent jobs at different phases currently tracks each site's material takeoff, which phase it's in, and when the delayed sealing visit is due across a paper estimate pad and a mental list, risking an under-ordered pallet of pavers that stalls a crew mid-job or a sealing visit that's forgotten entirely once the crew has moved on to the next site.

The tool maintains a per-job record (site measurements, paver/block product and quantity, and base-material tonnage calculated from the takeoff) with a phase-status tracker from excavation through sealing, a material-ordering checklist flagging any job whose ordered quantity falls short of the takeoff calculation, and a deferred-sealing-visit calendar flagging jobs coming due for their final seal weeks after installation.

Monetization: $19-35/mo per contractor. Distribution: the Interlocking Concrete Pavement Institute (ICPI, now part of the National Concrete Masonry Association) certified-installer directory and regional hardscape-contractor Facebook groups, where independent installers already trade material-takeoff and multi-phase scheduling workflow tips. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.
