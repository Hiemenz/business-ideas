# Side Income Ideas - 2026-08-06 (Round 38)

### 1. Event Florist Studio Order Management & Wholesale Flower Order Tracker
Event florists who design and install flowers for weddings, corporate events, and parties manage a complex production workflow: for each event, dozens of flower varieties in specific quantities must be ordered from wholesale suppliers 2-3 weeks in advance, received and conditioned, processed into arrangements over 1-3 days, and delivered and installed on the event date. The margin between what was quoted and what was actually spent on wholesale flowers depends on accurate variety-level cost tracking — which most florists manage in a spreadsheet or on paper that doesn't connect the proposal to the wholesale order to the actual spend. This tool maintains a per-event record (event name, client, event date, delivery address, and event type), a proposal line-item list (each arrangement type, included flowers by variety and stem count, labor estimate, and proposed price), a wholesale flower order list auto-generated from the proposal (aggregated stems needed per variety across all arrangements in the event, formatted as a wholesale order list), a receiving log (stems received per variety vs. ordered, with any substitutions noted), and a post-event cost reconciliation (actual wholesale cost per variety × stems used vs. proposed revenue — showing final event margin). A weekly production calendar shows every event with its order deadline (order by this date to receive 2 weeks before the event), processing days, and delivery date. Monetization: $15-25/mo per florist account. Distribution: American Institute of Floral Designers (AIFD) member communities, Floranext and Details Flowers software alternative-seeker communities, r/florists, event florist Facebook groups. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 2. In-Person Personal Trainer Session Log & Client Assessment Tracker
Personal trainers working with clients at a commercial gym or private studio track client programs, session attendance, and progress assessments — but between 10-20 active clients and back-to-back session scheduling, the notes from last Tuesday's session with one client get fuzzy by the following Tuesday. Without a systematic session log, it's easy to lose track of where each client is in their programming cycle, what their last 1-rep max was for a given lift, or what injury or limitation came up two sessions ago. This tool maintains a per-client profile (goal, current program name and week, training history summary, any injuries or movement restrictions, and weekly session frequency), a session log (for each completed session: date, exercises performed with sets/reps/weight, any technique observations, how the client felt, and next session plan), and an assessment tracker (for clients with periodic assessments: assessment date, measurements or performance benchmarks logged — bodyweight, body fat if measured, 1RMs for key lifts, movement screen findings — and comparison to the prior assessment). A client-facing session summary the trainer can text or email after each workout. A weekly dashboard shows the trainer's upcoming client roster and each client's last session date — flagging any client who is overdue for a session. Monetization: $12-18/mo per trainer account (unlimited clients). Distribution: r/personaltraining, NASM and ACE certified trainer communities, personal training Facebook groups, GymDesk and TrainHeroic alternative-seeker communities. Time to first dollar: 1-2 weeks.

### 3. Freelance Videographer Client Project Brief & Delivery Pipeline Tracker
Freelance videographers managing 3-8 active client projects simultaneously — corporate videos, wedding films, event highlight reels, and branded content — track their production pipeline across a calendar app, a shared Google Drive, and email threads. The critical path from shoot to delivery involves: client brief confirmed, shoot day(s) completed, raw footage ingested, rough cut delivered for client review, revisions received, final cut delivered, and final file formats archived and shared. Without a systematic pipeline view, projects fall out of sync — a rough cut delivered 2 weeks ago with no follow-up on revisions means a lost delivery slot or an unhappy client. This tool maintains a per-project record (client name, project type, shoot date(s), deliverables list — number of final videos, formats required, and any platform-specific requirements like vertical cuts for Instagram), a production pipeline tracker (current stage for each project: brief/pre-production/shoot/ingested/rough cut delivered/revisions received/final cut/delivered/archived), a client revision log (when the client provides revision notes: date received, each revision item listed, and the date the revision was addressed), a project deadline calendar showing every project's rough cut due date and final delivery due date, and an invoice status tracker (for each project: quoted price, deposit received, final payment due at delivery, and payment received confirmation). A weekly pipeline view shows every project by stage and flags any project where revision notes have been waiting for action for more than 5 days. Monetization: $12-20/mo per videographer account. Distribution: r/videography, CreativeCOW community forums, FilmRiot and Peter McKinnon creator communities, freelance videographer Facebook groups. Time to first dollar: 1-2 weeks.

### 4. Independent Insurance Agent Client Policy Renewal Reminder & Cross-Sell Tracker
Independent P&C insurance agents managing a book of 150-500 personal lines and commercial clients track renewal dates, coverage gaps, and cross-sell opportunities across a mix of agency management system notes, a spreadsheet reminder calendar, and their own memory. The renewal call — the single highest-leverage moment in an insurance agent's calendar — often arrives too late or gets missed entirely when the agent is managing a claims situation or a new business push. This tool maintains a per-client record (client name, contact, coverage lines held — auto, home, umbrella, life, commercial — and any open cross-sell opportunities noted from prior conversations), a policy renewal calendar (for each policy: insurance carrier, policy number, effective date, expiration/renewal date, annual premium, and a renewal outreach due date set 45 days before expiration), a renewal outreach log (for each policy approaching renewal: date the agent called or emailed, client response, any coverage changes discussed, and renewal confirmed or lost), and a cross-sell opportunity tracker (any coverage line the client doesn't currently hold that was mentioned in a prior conversation or identified by a coverage review — vehicle added but no umbrella, new home purchase, growing business that needs commercial coverage — with a follow-up date). A daily agenda view shows the agent's renewal calls due today and this week ranked by premium size. Annual at-a-glance view: total book premium by month, renewal retention rate, and new cross-sells written. Monetization: $15-25/mo per agent account. Distribution: Independent Insurance Agents & Brokers of America (IIABA/Big I) member communities, r/insurance agent communities, Agency Nation and Rough Notes trade media communities, Applied Epic and HawkSoft alternative-seeker communities. Time to first dollar: 1-2 weeks.

### 5. Amateur Radio Operator Callsign, License Class Upgrade & Contact Logbook
Licensed amateur radio operators — hams — manage their FCC amateur radio license (valid for 10 years, with an online renewal 90 days before expiration via ULS), track contacts (QSOs) in a logbook for award submissions and personal records, and in many cases are working toward a license class upgrade (Technician → General → Amateur Extra, each requiring a separate exam). Most operators log contacts in a paper log or a desktop program like Log4OM or Ham Radio Deluxe that is tied to a single machine and doesn't travel to field days or portable operations. This tool maintains an FCC license tracker (callsign, license class, grant date, expiration date, and a 90-day renewal alert — FCC ULS allows online renewal only in the 90-day window before expiration), a contact logbook (per-QSO: date, UTC time, station worked — callsign and country, frequency, mode — CW/phone/digital, and signal reports sent and received), an award submission tracker (for awards like ARRL DXCC, Worked All States, Worked All Continents — the contacts needed, contacts confirmed, and credits submitted), and an exam preparation tracker (for operators working toward the next license class: the question pool's topic areas, practice test score history, and planned exam date). A log statistics view shows total QSOs by mode, total countries worked, and progress toward any active awards. Monetization: $5-8/mo per operator or $30/year. Distribution: ARRL (American Radio Relay League) member communities, r/amateurradio, eHam.net community forums, QRZ.com operator communities. Time to first dollar: 1-2 weeks.

### 6. Board Game Designer Prototype Playtest Session Tracker
Independent board game designers who are developing a prototype through iterative playtesting run 10-50+ playtest sessions before a game reaches publication readiness — gathering feedback on rules clarity, balance, play time, and player experience at each session. Most designers track this feedback in a notebook or a Google Doc with no systematic way to identify whether a particular mechanic has been flagged in multiple independent playtest groups, or whether the average play time has been trending down over successive design iterations. This tool maintains a per-game record (working title, current version number, player count range, target play time, and design status — concepting/prototype/playtesting/final development), a per-session playtest log (date, session host, player count, actual play time, game version played, and a structured feedback form: rules questions that arose, any rule the group agreed was confusing, balance issues observed, components that needed clarification, and overall enjoyment rating), a mechanic feedback tracker (for each named game mechanic: a running log of feedback mentions across sessions — positive mentions, negative mentions, and suggested modifications — allowing the designer to see which mechanics are repeatedly flagged), and a version changelog (for each new version: what was changed, what prompted the change, and the first playtest session run on the new version). A dashboard view shows total sessions by version, average play time by version, and top flagged mechanics across all sessions. Monetization: $8-12/mo per designer account. Distribution: r/tabletopgamedesign, The Game Crafter and BoardGameGeek designer forums, Unpub Network playtest community, Game Developers Conference (GDC) board game community forums. Time to first dollar: 1-2 weeks.
