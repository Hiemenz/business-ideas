# Side Income Ideas - 2026-08-25 (Round 128)

Simple operational tracker tools for self-employed individuals and micro-businesses. These are NOT compliance tools — they're practical day-to-day workflow trackers that replace the messy spreadsheet, whiteboard, or notebook a solo operator currently uses to run their book of business. Each idea targets a different type of self-employed operator not already covered in this repo, names a real distribution channel, and gives realistic per-account pricing and a time to first dollar.

---

## Ideas

### 1. Mobile Crane & Rigging Operator Load-Chart, Site-Access & Job Scheduling Tracker

An independent owner-operator running a single mobile crane for one-off lifts — sign installs, rooftop HVAC swaps, log cabin or shed placements, tree removals — has to match each job's actual lift weight and radius against that specific crane's load chart before ever showing up on site, since a lift attempted past the chart's rated capacity at that boom angle and radius is the exact failure mode that tips a crane over, and has to confirm site-access details (overhead lines, soft ground, gate clearance width) ahead of time rather than discovering a truck can't actually reach the pick point after arriving with a loaded rig. An operator running several jobs a week across scattered addresses currently tracks load-chart lookups, rigging gear (slings, shackles, spreader bars) assigned to each job, and site-access notes on a paper site-survey sheet and a load-chart binder kept in the truck cab, and a job scheduled without confirming the crane's chart actually covers that day's radius-and-weight combination, or rigging gear sent to the wrong job because two lifts were booked the same morning, is a rescheduled job and a client who now has to explain the delay to their own crew standing around waiting.

The tool maintains a per-job lift-spec record (weight, radius, boom angle) checked against the operator's specific crane's load-chart data, flagging any job outside the chart's safe range before it's confirmed; a site-access checklist per job address (overhead-line clearance, ground conditions, gate/turn-radius notes) filled in ahead of arrival; and a rigging-gear assignment tracker showing which slings, shackles, and spreader bars are allocated to which job on a given day. Monetization: $19-29/mo per operator. Distribution: r/Cranes and heavy-equipment operator Facebook groups, the Specialized Carriers & Rigging Association (SC&RA) member network, and regional crane-operator trade shows where independent owner-operators already trade load-chart and site-survey workflow tips. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 2. Zipline & Canopy Tour Operator Capacity-Booking, Guide-Ratio & Harness-Inspection Tracker

An independent zipline or canopy tour operator running a fixed course of platforms and cables has to book tour groups against the course's actual hourly launch capacity, since a course that can safely run one group every 15 minutes but gets double-booked by a phone reservation and an online booking landing in the same slot is a group standing at the launch platform with nowhere to go, and has to staff every tour with the minimum guide-to-participant ratio the course was designed and insured around, since running short a guide on a busy Saturday is a safety corner nobody wants cut in front of paying customers. Equally important is a rolling harness, cable, and anchor-point inspection log, since the gear directly holding a customer 80 feet in the air needs a predictable inspection cadence rather than "whenever someone remembers," and a course that lets an inspection interval slide during a busy summer week is one incident away from losing the insurance that lets it operate at all. An operator running a full-season tour schedule across several guides currently tracks bookings, guide assignments, and equipment-inspection dates on a paper reservation book and a gear-shed whiteboard, and a tour launched a guide short because nobody cross-checked the day's headcount against the schedule, or a harness that missed its scheduled inspection because the whiteboard got erased, is exactly the kind of gap that turns into a very expensive problem.

The tool maintains a capacity-aware booking calendar preventing any tour slot from being overbooked past the course's rated launch interval; a per-tour guide-assignment tracker enforcing the minimum guide-to-participant ratio before a booking is confirmed; and a per-harness/cable/anchor-point inspection log with a due-date calculator flagging any piece of gear approaching its next scheduled check. Monetization: $25-39/mo per course. Distribution: the Association for Challenge Course Technology (ACCT) member directory and its annual conference, r/aerialadventure and outdoor-recreation-operator Facebook groups, and regional zipline/canopy-tour owner meetups where independent operators already trade booking-capacity and inspection-interval tips. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.

### 3. Caricature Artist Event Booking, Sketch-Throughput & Style-Portfolio Tracker

An independent caricature artist who books birthday parties, corporate mixers, and boardwalk or market spots has to quote each gig based on how many sketches they can realistically turn out in the booked hours at that event's pace — a slow, detailed color piece takes far longer per subject than a quick black-and-white line sketch, and quoting a two-hour corporate event at the wrong per-sketch rate either underprices the artist's time or leaves a line of unhappy guests when the event ends before everyone gets drawn. An artist juggling several bookings a month across birthday parties, corporate events, and casual walk-up spots currently tracks which client booked what date, what style and price tier was quoted, and how the travel fee was calculated on a phone notes app with no link back to the actual booking, and a corporate client who was quoted a color-portrait rate but remembers agreeing to a cheaper line-sketch price, or a double-booked Saturday nobody caught until two clients called the same week, is a disputed invoice or a lost booking in a referral-driven business that lives and dies on word of mouth.

The tool maintains a per-event booking record tracking date, venue, style/price tier quoted, sketch-per-hour throughput assumption, and travel fee; a booking calendar with a double-booking check across the artist's full schedule; and a client contact and repeat-booking history for corporate accounts and recurring party clients. Monetization: $12-19/mo per artist. Distribution: r/caricature and illustrator/street-artist Facebook groups, The Bash and GigSalad vendor community boards where caricature artists already list their services, and regional balloon-artist and party-vendor trade associations that caricature artists frequently cross-book alongside. Time to first dollar: 2 weeks.

### 4. Freediving & Spearfishing Charter Guide Trip-Window, Catch-Log & Gear-Fit Tracker

An independent freediving instructor or spearfishing charter guide has to time trips against real-time swell height and water-visibility conditions rather than a fixed calendar slot, since a breath-hold dive or spearfishing trip run in poor visibility or heavy surge is both a wasted trip and a real safety risk without a scuba tank's margin for error, and has to log each trip's catch by species and estimated weight against state or regional bag-and-size limits, since a charter guide who doesn't track cumulative catch across a multi-stop day can unknowingly put clients over a daily limit. A guide also has to size weight belts, fins, and spearguns to each client's experience level and body type before a trip departs, since handing an inexperienced client gear sized for an advanced diver is exactly how a trip goes sideways underwater. A guide running a rotating client roster across a compressed dive season currently tracks trip bookings against a separate marine-forecast app with no link back to which bookings are actually safe to run, and catch counts on a paper log kept in a dry bag, and a trip confirmed on a day the swell forecast never actually settled into a safe range, or a catch log that quietly drifts past a bag limit because nobody was adding it up mid-day, is a bad review or a fish-and-wildlife citation in a business built entirely on repeat bookings and word of mouth.

The tool maintains a trip-window scheduling board cross-referencing forecasted swell height and water visibility against the operator's safe-conditions threshold before a booking is confirmed; a per-trip catch log tracking species and count against the day's bag-and-size limits in real time; and a per-client gear-fit record matching weight-belt, fin, and speargun sizing to that client's logged experience level. Monetization: $19-29/mo per guide. Distribution: r/Freediving and r/Spearfishing practitioner threads, the Freediving Instructors International (FII) and AIDA-affiliated instructor networks, and regional spearfishing-club Facebook groups where independent guides already trade swell-window and catch-tracking tips. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 5. Mobile Karaoke Host & Rental Operator Equipment-Inventory, Setlist & Booking Tracker

An independent mobile karaoke host who books private parties, bars, and corporate happy hours has to track a full physical equipment inventory — wireless mics, speakers, a video screen or projector, and a licensed song-catalog library — checked out for one event and back in before the next, since a mic that didn't make it back from Friday's party is a Saturday booking short a working microphone with no time to source a replacement. A host running several bookings a week across different venues also has to track each client's song-request history and preferred setlist style (classic rock crowd versus a Top-40 birthday party versus a corporate team-building crowd), since showing up to a repeat client without remembering what worked at their last event is a missed chance to make the booking feel personal in a referral-driven business. A host currently tracks equipment check-out/check-in and booking details on a paper gear checklist taped inside the equipment case and a phone contacts list with no booking history attached, and a piece of gear that didn't get logged back in after a late-night event, discovered only when the next booking's setup comes up short, is an emergency equipment run an hour before a paying gig starts.

The tool maintains a per-event equipment check-out/check-in log covering mics, speakers, screens, and cabling with a due-back alert before the next scheduled booking; a booking calendar with a double-booking check across the host's full equipment inventory; and a per-client song-request and setlist history for repeat bar residencies and corporate accounts. Monetization: $15-25/mo per host. Distribution: r/DJs and mobile-entertainment vendor Facebook groups, The Bash and GigSalad vendor community boards where mobile karaoke hosts already list their services, and regional mobile-DJ and event-entertainer trade associations. Time to first dollar: 2 weeks.

### 6. Custom Skate Ramp & Mini-Ramp Builder Material-Takeoff & Build-Queue Tracker

An independent fabricator building custom skate ramps, mini ramps, and quarter-pipes for backyards, breweries, and skate shops has to calculate a material takeoff — sheets of plywood by thickness, coping length and diameter, steel framing stock — specific to each commission's dimensions and transition radius before ever cutting the first piece, since underordering plywood mid-build stalls a job waiting on a lumber-yard run, and a coping length ordered short is a ramp that sits half-finished until the right stock arrives. A builder running several commissions at different stages at once — design/dimension sign-off, framing, decking, and coping installation — currently tracks each project's material list and build-stage progress on a shop whiteboard and a stack of hand-sketched dimension drawings, and a commission that gets its steel framing order placed against the wrong ramp's dimensions because two jobs' sketches got mixed up on the shop floor is a costly reorder and a blown install date for a customer who scheduled a grand-opening or backyard party around it.

The tool maintains a per-commission material-takeoff calculator generating a plywood, coping, and steel cut list from that ramp's specific dimensions and transition radius; a build-stage tracker sequencing framing, decking, and coping installation against a target install date across multiple concurrent commissions; and a client-facing project-status view showing exactly which build stage a ramp is in. Monetization: $19-29/mo per builder. Distribution: r/skateboarding and DIY-ramp-building forums, regional skatepark-builder and ramp-fabricator Facebook groups, and independent skate-shop networks where custom ramp builders already trade material-takeoff and build-queue tips. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.
