# Side Income Ideas - 2026-08-21 (Round 105)

Simple operational tracker tools for self-employed individuals and micro-businesses. These are NOT compliance tools — they're practical day-to-day workflow trackers that replace the messy spreadsheet, whiteboard, or notebook a solo operator currently uses to run their book of business. Each idea targets a different type of self-employed operator not already covered in this repo, names a real distribution channel, and gives realistic per-account pricing and a time to first dollar.

---

### 1. Freelance Court Reporting Scopist Job-Queue, Turnaround & Style-Preference Tracker

A scopist is a freelance transcript editor who takes a court reporter's raw realtime stenography output — full of untranslated steno outlines, homonym errors, and formatting gaps — and edits it into a clean, court-ready transcript, almost always working for several different reporters simultaneously on a rush basis, since many depositions and hearings need a same-week or next-day turnaround. Each reporter client has their own formatting style preferences (line-numbering convention, Q&A spacing, parenthetical style) the scopist has to remember and apply correctly job to job, and juggling 4-8 concurrent jobs from different reporters with different due dates and style sheets in an email inbox and a personal notebook is how a scopist misses a turnaround deadline or applies the wrong reporter's formatting — either of which can cost the relationship with that reporter for good, since reporters depend entirely on trust that a scopist won't be the reason they miss a court deadline.

The tool maintains a per-reporter client profile (formatting style sheet, standard turnaround expectation, rate per page) and a job queue tracking each transcript's page count, due date/time, priority level (rush vs. standard), and completion status, with a "due today/tomorrow" board sorted by deadline across all active reporter clients. An invoicing view totals completed pages per reporter per pay period against the agreed per-page rate.

Monetization: $15-29/mo per account. Distribution: the Scopists.com community and Facebook group, the National Court Reporters Association (NCRA) scopist listings, and r/courtreporting, where working scopists actively trade workflow tips. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 2. Independent Marine Surveyor Pre-Purchase Inspection & Report-Delivery Tracker

An independent marine surveyor — accredited through SAMS (Society of Accredited Marine Surveyors) or NAMS (National Association of Marine Surveyors) — inspects boats for pre-purchase buyers, insurance companies requiring a condition-and-value survey before binding a policy, and lenders financing a vessel purchase. Each survey has a hard delivery deadline (an insurer or closing attorney waiting on the written report to bind coverage or fund a loan) that a surveyor juggling 6-10 concurrent surveys across different marinas and boat types can't reliably track from memory, and every survey generates a substantial standardized checklist — hull condition, rigging, engine hours and compression-test results, safety equipment, moisture-meter readings by hull section — that has to become a formatted written report with photos, currently assembled by hand from field notes every single time.

The tool maintains a per-survey job record (vessel details, client type — buyer, insurer, or lender — inspection date, and report-due deadline) with a structured findings checklist by category that a surveyor fills in dockside on a mobile device with photo attachments, and a report-generation view that assembles the structured findings into a client-ready PDF. A pipeline board shows every open survey and how many days remain before its report is due.

Monetization: $29-49/mo per account. Distribution: SAMS and NAMS member directories and their annual conferences, plus marine-surveyor forums and regional boating-industry Facebook groups. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.

### 3. Freelance Remote Medical Coder Multi-Client Coding Queue & Productivity Tracker

An independent, AAPC- or AHIMA-certified medical coder who contracts remotely with several small physician practices and ambulatory clinics simultaneously — a common model since few small practices generate enough coding volume to justify a full-time in-house coder — has to track each client's chart-to-code turnaround requirement (some contracts specify same-day coding, others 48-72 hours), the coder's own productivity (charts coded per hour, by client and specialty, since per-chart or hourly billing depends on an accurate log), and, separately, their own CPC/CCS certification renewal and CEU accumulation, since a lapsed credential can end every client contract overnight. Juggling several EHR logins, several practices' chart queues, and a personal CEU-hour count in a spreadsheet is how a coder loses track of which practice's charts have been sitting uncoded past the contracted turnaround.

The tool maintains a per-client chart queue (chart count received, coded, and the client's contracted turnaround window) with an overdue-charts alert, a productivity log tracking charts-coded-per-hour by client and specialty for accurate billing, and a personal CEU/certification tracker with the AAPC or AHIMA renewal deadline and CEU-hours-completed count.

Monetization: $19-35/mo per account. Distribution: AAPC local chapter meetings and its online forum, r/CodingandBilling, and independent medical coder Facebook groups where remote/1099 coders trade client-management advice. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.

### 4. Axe-Throwing & Laser Tag Indoor Recreation Venue Booking, Waiver & Lane-Maintenance Tracker

A small independent axe-throwing venue or laser tag arena runs a constant flow of walk-in and reserved group bookings — birthday parties, corporate team events, bachelor/bachelorette groups — where every participant must sign a liability waiver before playing, and a venue running back-to-back lane or arena time slots on a busy Saturday can't afford front-desk staff manually checking a paper waiver binder against a walk-in group while the next reservation is already waiting. Axe-throwing venues specifically also need a per-lane equipment log — axe handles crack and blades dull with use, and target boards need periodic replacement — that a busy owner running 6-10 lanes has no systematic way to track beyond noticing a problem when a customer complains.

The tool maintains a booking calendar by lane/arena time slot with group size and package type, a digital waiver capture linked to each booking (checking every named participant against a signed waiver before check-in clears), and — for axe-throwing venues — a per-lane equipment condition log tracking axe and target-board wear with a replacement-due flag based on throws-per-item usage.

Monetization: $39-69/mo per venue. Distribution: the World Axe Throwing League (WATL) and National Axe Throwing Federation (NATF) venue-operator communities, plus laser tag operator forums and the IAAPA small-operator member directory. Time to first dollar: 3-5 weeks.

### 5. Freelance Professional Book Indexer Project, Page-Proof & Deadline Tracker

A freelance professional book indexer — hired by publishers or self-publishing authors to write the back-of-book index after page proofs are finalized — works under an unusually compressed deadline structure: the index can only be written once final page proofs are set, and a publisher's production schedule typically gives the indexer just 1-3 weeks before the book goes to print, with several such projects often landing in the same tight window from different publisher clients. An indexer juggling 3-6 concurrent projects has to track each book's page-proofs-received date, the contracted deadline, the agreed rate (per page, per hour, or flat fee), and the specific style guide required (Chicago Manual of Style, publisher house style) — currently tracked in an inbox and a personal calendar with no single view of which project's clock is closest to running out.

The tool maintains a per-project record (client/publisher, page count, page-proofs-received date, deadline, rate structure, and required style guide) with a countdown view sorted by nearest deadline across all active projects, and a completed-project invoicing log totaling pages or hours billed per client.

Monetization: $15-25/mo per account. Distribution: the American Society for Indexing (ASI) member directory and regional chapter listservs, and the INDEX-L professional indexers' mailing list, where freelance indexers already coordinate around overlapping publisher deadlines. Time to first dollar: 2-4 weeks.

### 6. Freelance Session Musician & Accompanist Gig, Chart & Payment Tracker

A freelance session musician, accompanist, or side-player — the working musician who plays weddings, church services, studio sessions, and community theater pit orchestras as hired talent rather than leading their own band — juggles a constantly rotating calendar of one-off and recurring gigs, each with its own charts/setlist, key requirements (a vocalist's song might need transposing to their comfortable key), call time and dress code, and payment terms (some pay day-of in cash, others invoice a contractor 30-45 days out). A musician playing 10-20 gigs a month across different bandleaders and contractors has no single place to confirm what key "that one song" needs to be in for a specific client without digging through old texts and marked-up sheet music.

The tool maintains a gig calendar (date, contractor/bandleader, venue, call time, dress code, and pay rate/terms) linked to a per-gig chart/setlist library noting the required key and any special arrangement notes per song per client, and an outstanding-payment tracker flagging gigs where payment is overdue against the agreed terms.

Monetization: $12-25/mo per account. Distribution: Recording Musicians Association and local AFM musicians' union locals, plus r/WeAreTheMusicMakers and gigging-musician Facebook groups organized by metro area. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.
