# Side Income Ideas - 2026-08-22 (Round 111)

Simple operational tracker tools for self-employed individuals and micro-businesses. These are NOT compliance tools — they're practical day-to-day workflow trackers that replace the messy spreadsheet, whiteboard, or notebook a solo operator currently uses to run their book of business. Each idea targets a different type of self-employed operator not already covered in this repo, names a real distribution channel, and gives realistic per-account pricing and a time to first dollar.

---

### 1. Taxidermist Mount Queue & Specimen Intake Tracker

A taxidermist runs a multi-month job queue where each mount — deer shoulder mount, full-body turkey, fish replica, small-game rug — moves through its own sequence (specimen intake and tagging, skinning/caping, tanning, either in-house or sent to a commercial tannery with its own multi-week turnaround, form selection and fitting, mounting, and finish work) with a customer's specific pose and finish preferences noted at drop-off. A shop running dozens of concurrent mounts at different stages during the fall hunting-season rush currently tracks each specimen's hunter tag, species, stage, and pose preference on paper claim tags stapled to capes stored in a walk-in freezer, and a specimen that sits too long before caping and salting, or a tannery shipment that gets lost because no one tracked the send and return dates, is a ruined cape and a customer's trophy that can never be replaced.

The tool maintains a per-specimen intake record (customer, species, harvest date and location for state tag compliance, and pose/finish preference) with a stage tracker from intake through tannery send/return to final mounting and pickup-ready status, a tannery shipment log with expected-return-date alerts, and a freezer/storage location index so any cape or specimen can be found immediately.

Monetization: $19-35/mo per taxidermist. Distribution: the National Taxidermists Association (NTA) member directory and state taxidermist association trade shows, plus regional taxidermy Facebook groups where independent taxidermists already trade fall-rush intake and tannery-tracking workflow tips. Time to first dollar: 3-5 weeks (timed ahead of fall hunting season).

### 2. Roll-Off Dumpster & Waste Container Rental Route Operator Delivery, Swap & Dump-Fee Tracker

An independent roll-off dumpster rental operator running a fleet of a few roll-off trucks and a yard of containers serves contractors, homeowners doing a cleanout, and small renovation crews, each rental running its own clock from delivery to pickup with a rental-period limit before overage fees kick in, and each container needing a swap-out or final pickup scheduled around the customer's job progress and the landfill or transfer station's operating hours. An operator running fifteen to thirty active container rentals at once currently tracks which container is at which address, the rental start date and included rental period, swap requests as jobs generate more debris than one container holds, and the per-load tipping fee owed at the disposal site on a whiteboard in the yard office and a stack of paper delivery tickets, and a container that sits past its rental period without an overage invoice going out, or a swap request that gets missed because the driver's route sheet was outdated, is revenue the operator never captures and a contractor whose job stalls waiting on a container that should have already been swapped.

The tool maintains a container fleet roster tracking each unit's current location, customer, delivery date, and rental-period countdown with automatic overage-fee flagging, a daily route/dispatch board for deliveries, swaps, and final pickups sequenced by driver, a per-load disposal-site tipping-fee log tied back to the customer invoice, and a customer account history showing repeat-renter patterns for contractors who rent containers on every job.

Monetization: $25-40/mo per operator. Distribution: the National Waste & Recycling Association's independent-hauler resources and regional dumpster-rental owner Facebook groups, where small operators already trade route-scheduling and overage-billing workflow tips. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.

### 3. Septic Tank Pumping Route Operator Service-Interval & Disposal-Manifest Tracker

An independent septic pumping operator services a route of residential and small-commercial septic tanks, each on its own recommended pump-out interval — typically every three to five years depending on tank size and household occupancy — that the operator has to track across hundreds of properties to know who's coming due, while every pump-out load also needs a disposal-facility manifest recording where the septage was hauled and dumped, since most states require septage haulers to document disposal at a permitted treatment facility. An operator running a route of several hundred residential accounts currently tracks which property is due for its next pump-out, each tank's size and access notes (a tank buried under a deck, a long driveway needing extra hose), and per-load disposal manifests on a paper route book and a stack of dump-receipt slips, and a property that quietly slips past its pump-out interval is a backed-up system and an emergency call that could have been a routine scheduled stop, while a missing disposal manifest is exactly what a state environmental inspection targets.

The tool maintains a per-property tank record (size, last pump-out date, access notes, and computed next-due date) with a "due this month" route-planning view sorted by area, a per-load disposal-manifest log tied to the receiving facility, and a customer contact/reminder queue for properties approaching their interval.

Monetization: $19-35/mo per operator. Distribution: the National Association of Wastewater Technicians (NAWT) member directory and state onsite-wastewater association meetings, plus regional septic-service Facebook groups where independent pumpers already trade route-scheduling and disposal-documentation workflow tips. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.

### 4. Water Well Drilling & Pump Installation Contractor Job Log & Route Tracker

An independent water well driller and pump installer runs a rig between scattered rural properties, each job requiring its own site assessment (estimated depth based on neighboring wells, casing and screen specs), a multi-day drilling schedule that can slip with ground conditions, and — once the well is in — a pump selection and installation matched to the well's yield and the home's water demand, with warranty tracking on the pump and pressure-tank equipment installed. A driller running several concurrent jobs across a rural service area currently tracks each site's drilling progress, casing/screen materials used, and pump/equipment warranty start dates on a paper job folder and a truck logbook, and a warranty claim on a pump installed eighteen months ago that nobody can find the install date or model number for is a customer service call that goes nowhere and a manufacturer warranty claim that gets denied for lack of documentation.

The tool maintains a per-job site record (property address, estimated and actual depth, casing/screen materials) with a drilling-progress stage tracker, a pump/equipment installation log capturing model, serial number, and install date per component with a warranty-expiration view, and a route-scheduling board for sequencing multi-day jobs across a rural territory.

Monetization: $19-35/mo per contractor. Distribution: the National Ground Water Association (NGWA) member directory and state water well association meetings, plus regional well-drilling Facebook groups where independent drillers already trade job-scheduling and warranty-tracking workflow tips. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.

### 5. Land Clearing & Forestry Mulching Contractor Job & Equipment-Hours Tracker

A land clearing and forestry mulching contractor runs skid-steer-mounted mulching heads and excavators across rural acreage for landowners clearing pasture, right-of-way, or building sites, quoting each job by the acre with pricing that shifts based on vegetation density and terrain, and needs to track each machine's engine hours against its maintenance schedule since mulching heads and undercarriages take heavy wear that catches up fast if service intervals slip. A contractor running three to six jobs a week across scattered rural properties currently tracks the job queue, acreage quoted versus acreage actually cleared, and each machine's hours-since-last-service on a paper job ticket and a maintenance log taped inside the equipment trailer, and a mulching head that runs past its service interval because no one was tracking hours in the field is an expensive unscheduled breakdown mid-job that stalls every job behind it that week.

The tool maintains a job pipeline by property with acreage quoted, acreage completed, and terrain notes, a per-machine engine-hours tracker with maintenance-interval alerts for blades, undercarriage, and filters, a job-cost calculator comparing quoted acreage price against actual machine hours logged, and a customer/property history for landowners who book recurring right-of-way or pasture maintenance.

Monetization: $20-30/mo per contractor. Distribution: the Forestry Mulching and Land Clearing operator groups on Facebook and the American Loggers Council's affiliated-contractor network, where independent operators already trade job-costing and equipment-maintenance workflow tips. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.

### 6. Enrolled Agent & IRS Tax Resolution Specialist Case Pipeline & Deadline Tracker

An enrolled agent working IRS tax resolution cases — offers in compromise, installment agreements, penalty abatement requests, and audit representation — carries a caseload where each client's case sits at a different stage with its own IRS response deadline, and where missing a 30-day response window on a notice can forfeit a client's appeal rights or let a lien or levy proceed. An EA running fifteen to thirty active resolution cases at once currently tracks which IRS notice was received when, the response deadline it triggers, what forms and financial documentation still need to be collected from the client, and each case's current stage (intake, financial analysis, offer submitted, in review, resolved) on a mix of a paper tickler file and a case-management tool built for tax prep rather than resolution work, and a response deadline that slips past because a notice got buried in a stack of case files is a client's collection hold lifted and a levy that hits their bank account.

The tool maintains a case pipeline by stage with each IRS notice's received date and computed response deadline, a client document-collection checklist tracking which financial disclosure forms and supporting records are still outstanding, a deadline calendar surfacing every case's next action across the full caseload in one view, and a case-note log for tracking calls and correspondence with the IRS on each client's behalf.

Monetization: $25-40/mo per practitioner. Distribution: the National Association of Enrolled Agents (NAEA) member forums and the American Society of Tax Problem Solvers (ASTPS) community, where independent EAs already trade case-tracking and deadline-management workflow tips specific to resolution work as distinct from seasonal tax preparation. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.
