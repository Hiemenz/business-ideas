# Side Income Ideas - 2026-08-17 (Session 88)

Simple operational tracker tools for self-employed individuals and micro-businesses — scheduling, client records, inventory, billing, and project-pipeline tools, not compliance software. Each idea below covers a self-employed operator type not previously addressed in this repo: independent farriers, freelance court reporters, mobile notary/loan signing agents, freelance upholsterers, independent pool service technicians, and freelance voiceover artists.

---

### 1. Independent Farrier Client-Horse Rotation & Shoeing-Cycle Tracker
Farriers work a route of barns and private stables, and every horse on that route needs reshoeing or trimming on its own cycle — typically every 6-8 weeks, but faster for performance horses in heavy work and slower for pasture-sound retirees. A farrier juggling 150-300 horses across dozens of barns currently tracks this on a paper appointment book or a mental model of "who's due," and a missed cycle means a horse goes lame or throws a shoe mid-competition, which damages the relationship with the barn owner and the horse's trainer far more than a late oil change would with a car.

The tool tracks each horse by name, owner, barn location, shoe/trim type, and last-service date, auto-calculates the next-due date from that horse's specific cycle length, and generates a route-optimized worklist for the farrier's next barn visit so nothing gets skipped. It also logs per-horse notes (lameness history, specific shoeing preferences, hoof issues) that matter for continuity when a horse is seen every 6-8 weeks for years, plus a simple invoice generator tied to each visit.
Monetization: $15-25/mo per account. Distribution: the American Farrier's Association (AFA) and regional farrier associations, which run certification programs and have active practitioner directories. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.

### 2. Freelance Court Reporter & Deposition Stenographer Job & Transcript-Turnaround Tracker
Freelance court reporters take assignments from multiple law firms and reporting agencies simultaneously, and each deposition or hearing comes with its own transcript-delivery deadline — standard turnaround is typically 30 days, but expedited requests (7-day, 3-day, or even same-day "daily copy" for trial) are common and priced at a premium. A reporter juggling 8-15 active jobs at a time needs to know, at a glance, which transcripts are due next, which agencies still owe payment, and which jobs still need exhibits or errata sheets processed — currently tracked in a mix of email threads and a personal calendar that doesn't surface the highest-priority deadline clearly.

The tool tracks each job by case name, requesting firm/agency, deposition date, page-rate and turnaround tier, transcript due date, and payment status, and surfaces an urgency-sorted worklist so expedited jobs never slip behind routine ones. It also logs exhibit and errata-sheet status per job and flags overdue invoices by agency, since freelance reporters are typically paid net-30 to net-60 and tracking who owes what across a dozen agencies by hand is error-prone.
Monetization: $12-20/mo per account. Distribution: the National Court Reporters Association (NCRA) and state court reporter associations, which run active freelancer job boards and directories. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.

### 3. Independent Mobile Notary & Loan Signing Agent Appointment & Document-Chain Tracker
Mobile notaries and loan signing agents drive to borrowers' homes, offices, or title companies to complete real estate closing packages, and each signing involves a specific chain of custody: pick up the document package from the title company or signing service, meet the borrower at a scheduled time, notarize and witness the required pages, and return the completed package (often overnight) by a strict deadline the title company sets to fund the loan on time. A signing agent juggling 3-6 appointments a day across different signing services currently tracks pickup times, appointment addresses, and drop-off deadlines across text messages and a paper planner, and a missed drop-off deadline can delay a home closing and burn the relationship with the signing service that sends repeat work.

The tool tracks each signing by borrower, address, appointment time, signing service/title company, document-package pickup and drop-off deadlines, and fee, with a day-view route sequenced by appointment time and drive distance. It also logs a per-signing-service running total of completed jobs and payments received, since signing services typically pay per completed and confirmed signing and payment timing varies by service.
Monetization: $10-18/mo per account. Distribution: the National Notary Association (NNA) and state signing-agent Facebook/forum communities where mobile notaries already coordinate on assignments. Time to first dollar: 2-3 weeks.

### 4. Freelance Upholsterer Job Queue & Fabric/Materials Tracker
Independent upholsterers take on furniture-restoration jobs that run for weeks — strip the old fabric, rebuild springs or webbing if needed, order the client's chosen fabric (often with a multi-week lead time from the supplier), then cut and install — and a single small shop typically has 5-15 pieces in some stage of that pipeline at once. Currently, most upholsterers track this with sticky notes on the piece itself and a paper order pad for fabric, which makes it easy to lose track of which yardage was ordered for which client's sofa, or to quote a completion date without accounting for a fabric backorder.

The tool tracks each job by client, furniture piece and photos, fabric selection and yardage ordered, supplier and expected fabric-arrival date, current pipeline stage (strip, rebuild, cut, install), and quoted versus actual completion date, so the shop always knows what's blocking each piece. It also tracks fabric cost versus labor quote per job so the upholsterer can see which job types are actually profitable.
Monetization: $12-20/mo per account. Distribution: the Upholstery Education Foundation and regional upholsterers' guilds, plus the large "Upholstery" trade Facebook groups where independent shops already exchange sourcing and technique advice. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.

### 5. Independent Pool Service Technician Route, Chemical-Dosing & Equipment Tracker
A solo pool service tech services 60-150 residential and small-commercial pools on a weekly route, and each pool has its own chemical-dosing history, equipment inventory (pump, filter, heater model and age), and quirks (a pool that always runs high on phosphates, a heater due for replacement). Currently most solo techs track this with a paper route sheet and memory, which works until a new pool gets added mid-route or a tech has to hand off a route temporarily and the substitute has no record of what each pool needs.

The tool tracks each pool by address, chemical readings and dosing history logged per visit, equipment inventory with install dates (to flag when a pump or heater is approaching typical end-of-life), and route sequencing for the week. It also flags pools with recent chemical-reading trends drifting out of normal range before they become a visible problem (green water, algae bloom), and generates a simple per-client visit log the tech can text or email as a value-add.
Monetization: $15-25/mo per account. Distribution: the Independent Pool and Spa Service Association (IPSSA), a national association specifically for solo and small pool service operators. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.

### 6. Freelance Voiceover Artist Audition-to-Booking Pipeline & Usage-Rights Tracker
Freelance voiceover artists submit dozens of auditions a week across casting platforms and direct client relationships, and a small fraction convert into paid bookings — each booking then carries its own usage-rights terms (a commercial licensed for regional broadcast for one year is priced and tracked very differently from a national broadcast buyout, and audiobook or corporate e-learning narration work carries yet another rights structure). Freelancers currently track auditions in a spreadsheet at best, and usage-rights expiration dates — which determine when a client needs to renegotiate or stop using the recording — are easy to lose track of entirely, leaving money on the table when a license quietly expires and the client keeps using the audio anyway.

The tool tracks each audition through pipeline stages (submitted, callback, booked, declined) with client and project notes, and for every booked job logs the usage-rights scope (media type, geographic region, duration/buyout) with a renewal or expiration alert so the artist knows exactly when to reach out about a renegotiation. It also tracks per-client lifetime billing so the artist can see which relationships are actually worth prioritizing.
Monetization: $10-18/mo per account. Distribution: the World-Voices Organization (WoVO) and the Voice Actors Network / VO community forums (VOBSS, r/VoiceActing) where freelancers already discuss rate cards and booking workflows. Time to first dollar: 3-4 weeks.
